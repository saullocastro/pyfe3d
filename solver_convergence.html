Convergence criteria of the non-linear solvers (pyfe3d.solver.convergence)#

The equilibrium iterations of Analysis stop when the residual force vector is small, measured by one of two criteria selected with Analysis.convergence:

  • 'crisfield', the default: Crisfield’s criterion, the residual scaled with the diagonal of the linear stiffness matrix and compared with the largest scaled norm of the reference force vectors, the external and the internal ones and, when they apply, the external force vector of the increment of the arc-length solvers and the load factor times \(-\partial \boldsymbol{R}/\partial \lambda\), over the free degrees-of-freedom only, see crisfield_zeta();

  • 'relative': the Euclidean norm of the residual compared with the largest Euclidean norm of the force vectors, Analysis.relTOL, or the largest absolute residual force, Analysis.absTOL.

Notes#

The need for scaling

In beam, plate and shell models the degrees-of-freedom mix translations and rotations, such that the residual mixes forces and moments, and its Euclidean norm \(\|\boldsymbol{R}\|\) adds the squares of forces and of moments: the quantity has no physical meaning and its value depends on the system of units, the moments being scaled by a change of the length unit while the forces are not. Each component is therefore scaled with the corresponding diagonal entry of a reference stiffness matrix, a scaling that [3] (p. 58) attributes to [2] and that he used (his Eqs. 27 and 28) in the convergence test of his arc-length procedure, with the diagonal of the tangent stiffness matrix at the beginning of the increment. [1] (Sec. 9.5.4, Eq. 9.48) presents the same scaling as an alternative to the Euclidean norm of the residual, with a scaling matrix that could, for example, be used to ensure that, for a problem involving rotational variables, all parameters have the same dimensions:

\[d_j = K_{{C0}_{jj}}, \qquad \|v\|_{\boldsymbol{d}} = \sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{n} \frac{v_j^2}{|d_j|}}\]

with \(n\) the number of free degrees-of-freedom and \(d_j\) the entries of the diagonal scaling vector \(\boldsymbol{d}\). \(d_j\) is a stiffness, of the units of the generalized force of the degree-of-freedom \(j\) divided by those of its generalized displacement, such that each term \(v_j^2/|d_j|\) has the units of energy, whether the degree-of-freedom is a translation or a rotation: \(\|v\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}\) is the square root of an energy-like quantity, independent of the units of each degree-of-freedom. The absolute value guards against negative diagonal entries.

Crisfield’s criterion

The iteration \(i\) has converged when

\[\zeta_i = \frac{\|\boldsymbol{R}_i\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}}{\max\left( \|\lambda \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}, \|\boldsymbol{F}_{int}(\boldsymbol{u}_i)\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}, \|\Delta\lambda \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}, \|\lambda \, \partial \boldsymbol{R}/\partial \lambda\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}\right)} \le \epsilon\]

with \(\epsilon\) = Analysis.relTOL, \(\lambda\) the current load factor and \(\boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\) the reference external force vector. The third reference only applies to the arc-length solvers, \(\Delta\lambda\) being the increment of the load factor of the current step, and the fourth one only to load vectors that depend on the load factor, e.g. prescribed displacements imposed exactly, see callbacks. These are also the references of the relative criterion, see check_convergence(), measured here with the scaled norm. The maximum does not degenerate: under self-equilibrated loads \(\|\lambda \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}\) may be negligible whereas \(\|\boldsymbol{F}_{int}\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}\) reflects the internal force carried by the structure; at the beginning of a load-controlled analysis the internal forces are small and the external load is the reference; when the arc-length solvers pass through a load factor close to zero, \(\Delta\lambda \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\) is the reference; and under prescribed displacements alone the external and the free part of the internal force vectors both vanish at equilibrium, whereas \(-\lambda \, \partial \boldsymbol{R}/\partial \lambda\), the out-of-balance force that the prescribed displacements produce with the free degrees-of-freedom held fixed, does not. The norms are evaluated over the free degrees-of-freedom only, since the components of the residual at the constrained ones are the support reactions, not an imbalance. \(\boldsymbol{d}\) is computed once from the linear stiffness matrix calc_kC(), assembled once for the whole analysis, and kept constant during the iterations, such that \(\zeta_i\) measures the progress of the iterations and not the evolution of the tangent stiffness matrix. The internal force vector of the numerator and of the denominator comes from the same evaluation, at no additional cost.

With a consistent tangent stiffness matrix the Newton-Raphson iterations converge quadratically, \(\zeta_{i+1} \sim \zeta_i^2\), and a tight tolerance such as \(\epsilon = 10^{-9}\) is reached in a few iterations; with the modified Newton-Raphson method the convergence is linear ([1], Sections 1.2.2 and 1.2.3, pp. 8-13). A number of iterations consistently larger than three or four for moderate increments indicates a tangent stiffness matrix inconsistent with the internal force vector, which is why \(\zeta_i\) is printed at every iteration.

The criterion applies to the arc-length methods [3], which only change how the load factor is updated, not the residual, with the additional reference \(\Delta\lambda \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\).

The relative criterion

With Analysis.convergence = 'relative', the iteration has converged when \(\|\boldsymbol{R}\| \le\) relTOL \(\max(\|\boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\|, \|\boldsymbol{F}_{int}\|, ...)\), with the Euclidean norm over all the degrees-of-freedom of the solver, or when \(\max|R_j| \le\) absTOL, which depends on the units of the model, see check_convergence().

The round-off floor

The residual is the difference of force vectors computed in floating-point arithmetic. Each component of \(\boldsymbol{F}_{int}\) sums element contributions of the order of \(\sum_k |K_{{C0}_{jk}}| |c_k|\), which may be much larger than the component itself, e.g. under large self-equilibrated axial forces, such that the residual cannot be computed more accurately than about \(\varepsilon \| |\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}| \, |\boldsymbol{c}| \|\), with \(\varepsilon\) the machine precision and \(|\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}|\) and \(|\boldsymbol{c}|\) the elementwise absolute values of the linear stiffness matrix and of the solution, which is the componentwise bound of the rounding error of a matrix-vector product [5]. When this level is above \(\epsilon\) times the references of \(\zeta_i\), e.g. near a null load, where the references, net force vectors, are small and the element contributions are not, the iterations stagnate and every step fails. The iteration \(i\) has therefore also converged when

\[\|\boldsymbol{R}_i\|_{\boldsymbol{d}} \le C \, \varepsilon \, \| |\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}| \, |\boldsymbol{c}_i| \|_{\boldsymbol{d}}\]

with \(C\) = ROUNDOFF_FACTOR \(= 3\), \(c_i\) the solution of the iteration, and the Euclidean norm instead of \(\|\cdot\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}\) with the relative criterion, see roundoff_floor(). Like absTOL, the floor is an alternative to relTOL with both criteria. \(|\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}|\) is computed once, with \(\boldsymbol{d}\), and the floor adds one product by \(|\boldsymbol{c}_i|\) per iteration. It does not change \(\zeta_i\), which may then be larger than \(\epsilon\): the log of the iterations marks the iterations converged at the round-off floor. With prescribed displacements, uk, \(|\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}|\) and \(|\boldsymbol{c}|\) are those of the free degrees-of-freedom, the product \(|K_{ff}| \, |u_f|\) omitting the contribution of the prescribed displacements, \(|K_{fp}| \, |u_p|\): the floor is then lower, i.e. conservative.

Measured on the low sinusoidal arch of Fung and Kaplan [4], hinged at both ends under a sinusoidal load, \(\lambda_1 = 2.2\), modelled with 40 BeamC elements, traced with both arc-length methods and both criteria, with arc-length increments of 0.05 and \(\epsilon = 10^{-9}\), and a reference load of 1.25 times the upper limit load, reached at the load factor 0.80: without the floor, the four analyses stop at load factors of 0.026 to 0.032, close to the null load between the limit points; after the last converged step, the residuals of the iterations that follow the first correction of each attempt remain between 0.29 and 0.47 times \(\varepsilon \| |\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}| \, |\boldsymbol{c}| \|\), with \(\zeta_i \ge 1.0 \times 10^{-9}\), and no residual of the analyses is below 0.21 times this level. With 80 elements they stop at load factors of 0.20 to 0.21, between the limit points, and with 160 elements at 0.67 to 0.71, before the upper limit point. With the floor, all of them reach the load factor 1.0, the floor accepting 9 to 31 of the about 360 iterations with 40 elements, with \(\zeta_i\) up to \(8.8 \times 10^{-9}\) and residuals of 0.30 to 2.9 times \(\varepsilon \| |\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}| \, |\boldsymbol{c}| \|\), and 83 to 86 iterations with 160 elements.

pyfe3d.solver.convergence.CRITERIA = ('crisfield', 'relative')#

Names of the convergence criteria accepted by Analysis.convergence

pyfe3d.solver.convergence.ROUNDOFF_FACTOR = 3.0#

An iteration has also converged when the norm of its residual is not larger than this factor times the machine precision times the norm of \(|\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}| |\boldsymbol{c}|\), the round-off floor of the residual, see roundoff_floor() and check_convergence()

pyfe3d.solver.convergence.check_convergence(an, R, fint, fext_total, fext_ref=None, q_ref=None, w=None, Kc_abs=None, full_output=False)#

Check the convergence of the residual force vector

With an.convergence = 'crisfield' the convergence is achieved when \(\zeta \le\) an.relTOL, see crisfield_zeta(), with the weights w. With an.convergence = 'relative' it is achieved when either:

  • norm(R) <= an.relTOL*max(norm(fext_total), norm(fint), norm(fext_ref), norm(q_ref)), with the Euclidean norm, if an.relTOL is not None;

  • max(abs(R)) <= an.absTOL, if an.absTOL is not None.

an.absTOL is also accepted as an additional criterion with 'crisfield'. With both criteria, when Kc_abs is given, the convergence is also achieved when the norm of R of the criterion is not larger than the round-off floor of roundoff_floor().

Parameters:
anAnalysis

Analysis object with the attributes convergence, relTOL and absTOL.

Rnp.ndarray

Residual force vector, fint - fext_total, 1-D array.

fintnp.ndarray

Internal force vector of the same evaluation, 1-D array.

fext_totalnp.ndarray

External force vector at the current load factor, 1-D array.

fext_refnp.ndarray or None, optional

Additional reference of both criteria, the external force vector of the current increment of the arc-length solvers. Default None.

q_refnp.ndarray or None, optional

Additional reference of both criteria, the load factor times \(-\partial \boldsymbol{R}/\partial \lambda\) when calc_fext accepts lbd, see callbacks. Default None.

wnp.ndarray or None, optional

Weights of crisfield_weights(), required with 'crisfield'. Default None.

Kc_absnp.ndarray or None, optional

\(|\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}| |\boldsymbol{c}|\), the product of the elementwise absolute values of the linear stiffness matrix calc_kC() and of the solution of the evaluation of R, 1-D array. None disables the round-off floor. Default None.

full_outputbool, optional

Whether to also return roundoff. Default False.

Returns:
convergedbool

Whether the criterion is satisfied.

Rnormfloat

Norm of the residual used by the criterion, \(\|\boldsymbol{R}\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}\) with 'crisfield', the Euclidean norm with 'relative', used by the divergence checks of the solvers.

zetafloat

Relative residual, \(\zeta\) with 'crisfield', norm(R) over the largest Euclidean norm of the references with 'relative'.

roundoffbool

Only with full_output=True: whether the convergence is achieved by the round-off floor only, i.e. not by an.relTOL or an.absTOL.

Raises:
ValueError

When an.convergence is not one of CRITERIA, when an.relTOL and an.absTOL are both None, or when w is missing with 'crisfield'.

Notes

The relative criterion is that of the first versions of these solvers; its reference fext_ref is useful when the load factor is close to zero, where fext_total and fint are small. When all its references are null, the relative residual is norm(R) itself.

The round-off floor, see the module documentation, does not change zeta: an iteration that it accepts may have \(\zeta >\) an.relTOL.

pyfe3d.solver.convergence.crisfield_weights(K0)#

Weights \(1/|d_j|\) of the scaled norm of Crisfield’s criterion

The weight of each degree-of-freedom is the inverse of the modulus of the diagonal entry of K0. K0 must have no null row or column, as the matrices given to the solvers, see check_null_rows_cols().

Parameters:
K0sparse_matrix or np.ndarray

Linear stiffness matrix of the free degrees-of-freedom, shape (n, n), normally calc_kC().

Returns:
wnp.ndarray

Weights, 1-D array of size n, of the inverse of the dimension of the diagonal entries of K0.

Raises:
numpy.linalg.LinAlgError

When K0 has a null row or column, see check_null_rows_cols().

ValueError

When a diagonal entry is null in a row or column that is not null, which leaves the scaling of its degree-of-freedom undefined.

See also

crisfield_zeta

the criterion that uses them.

Notes

\(w_j = 1/|K_{{C0}_{jj}}|\), see the module documentation. The constrained degrees-of-freedom are partitioned out before calling the solvers, so that every degree-of-freedom of K0 is free and enters the norms. A null diagonal entry in a row that is not null, e.g. of a Lagrange multiplier, gives no scale to its degree-of-freedom, in which case the relative criterion is the alternative, convergence='relative'.

pyfe3d.solver.convergence.crisfield_zeta(R, fext, fint, w, q=None, fext_inc=None)#

Relative force imbalance \(\zeta\) of Crisfield’s criterion

Parameters:
Rnp.ndarray

Residual force vector, fint - fext, 1-D array of size n.

fextnp.ndarray

External force vector at the current load factor, 1-D array of size n.

fintnp.ndarray

Internal force vector, from the same evaluation as R, 1-D array of size n.

wnp.ndarray

Weights of crisfield_weights(), 1-D array of size n.

qnp.ndarray or None, optional

Additional reference, the load factor times \(-\partial \boldsymbol{R}/\partial \lambda\) when the load vector depends on the load factor, see callbacks. Default None.

fext_incnp.ndarray or None, optional

Additional reference, the external force vector of the increment of the arc-length solvers, \(\Delta\lambda \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\). Default None.

Returns:
zetafloat

\(\|\boldsymbol{R}\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}\) over the largest scaled norm of the references, dimensionless, 0. when the residual and all the references are null.

Notes

The criterion is \(\zeta \le \epsilon\), see the module documentation: the reference is \(\max(\|\boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}, \|\boldsymbol{F}_{int}\|_{\boldsymbol{d}})\), with also \(\|\boldsymbol{q}\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}\) and \(\|\Delta\lambda \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}\) when given. When the reference is null and the residual is not, \(\zeta\) is infinite.
