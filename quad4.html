Quad4 - Quadrilateral element with mixed integration (pyfe3d.quad4)#

The module has three classes: Quad4Data, with the sizes of the sparse arrays that one element fills; Quad4Probe, with the buffers in element coordinates that one or more elements share; and Quad4, the element. The update_* methods of Quad4 write the element contributions in sparse row, column and value arrays, from the positions given by its init_k_* attributes, or add them to a global internal force vector.

The Quad4 is the recommended quadrilateral plane stress finite element.

Another option is the Quad4R with full reduced integration, more efficient, but with an hourglass control that creates artificial stiffness to compensate the reduced integration.

The Quad4 element has 6 degrees-of-freedom (DOF): \(u\), \(v\), \(w\), \(r_x\), \(r_y\), \(r_z\). All DOF are interpolated bi-linearly between the nodes. The element matrices combine membrane, bending, membrane-bending coupling and transverse shear terms with a stiffness for the drilling rotation \(r_z\), whose model is selected with the drilling_model attribute of Quad4: the default 0 is physics-based, any other value gives the fictitious penalty of MSC Nastran and Autodesk Nastran controlled by the K6ROT attribute. The quadrature of the membrane terms depends on the drilling model, and the quadrature of the transverse shear terms on whether the thickness h of the pyfe3d.shellprop.ShellProp is smaller than the square root of the element area.

The transverse shear stiffnesses \(A_{44}\), \(A_{45}\) and \(A_{55}\) are read from the pyfe3d.shellprop.ShellProp object with the shear correction already applied, see pyfe3d.shellprop.ShellProp.calc_transverse_shear_stiffness(), and no shear correction factor is applied by the element. When a material direction is defined, they are brought to the element coordinate system with pyfe3d.shellprop.ShellProp.calc_Ats_element().

Notes#

The element follows the first-order shear deformation theory (FSDT). The displacements at a distance \(z\) from the reference surface are \(u + z r_y\), \(v - z r_x\) and \(w\), which gives, in the element coordinate system, the membrane strains, the curvatures and the transverse shear strains

\[\epsilon_{xx} = u_{,x}, \quad \epsilon_{yy} = v_{,y}, \quad \gamma_{xy} = u_{,y} + v_{,x}\]
\[\kappa_{xx} = {r_y}_{,x}, \quad \kappa_{yy} = -{r_x}_{,y}, \quad \kappa_{xy} = {r_y}_{,y} - {r_x}_{,x}\]
\[\gamma_{xz} = w_{,x} + r_y, \quad \gamma_{yz} = w_{,y} - r_x\]

and the strain energy

\[U = \frac{1}{2} \int_A \left( \boldsymbol{\epsilon}^\top \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{\epsilon} + 2 \boldsymbol{\epsilon}^\top \boldsymbol{B} \boldsymbol{\kappa} + \boldsymbol{\kappa}^\top \boldsymbol{D} \boldsymbol{\kappa} + \boldsymbol{\gamma}^\top \boldsymbol{A}_s \boldsymbol{\gamma} \right) dA + U_{r_z}\]

with \(\boldsymbol{\epsilon} = \{\epsilon_{xx}, \epsilon_{yy}, \gamma_{xy}\}^\top\), \(\boldsymbol{\kappa} = \{\kappa_{xx}, \kappa_{yy}, \kappa_{xy}\}^\top\), \(\boldsymbol{\gamma} = \{\gamma_{yz}, \gamma_{xz}\}^\top\), \(\boldsymbol{A}\), \(\boldsymbol{B}\) and \(\boldsymbol{D}\) the laminate stiffness matrices and \(\boldsymbol{A}_s\) the matrix of \(A_{44}\), \(A_{45}\) and \(A_{55}\), all in the element coordinate system, and \(U_{r_z}\) the drilling term described below. These are the rows of Quad4Probe.update_BL(). All DOF are interpolated bi-linearly between the nodes, such that any of the DOF gradients can be constant over the element when the element is rectangular.

Integration scheme

The stiffness for the degrees of freedom \(w\), \(r_x\) and \(r_y\) is based on the paper of Hughes et al. [1], whose rotations \(\theta_1\) and \(\theta_2\) enter the transverse shear strains as \(w_{,x} - \theta_1\) and \(w_{,y} - \theta_2\), their Eq. (7), such that \(\theta_1 = -r_y\) and \(\theta_2 = r_x\).

Hughes et al. (1977) proposed the following integration scheme:

  • For thin and moderately thick plates, when \(h/\ell < 1\), where \(\ell\) is the element characteristic length, here calculated as the square root of the element area \(\ell = \sqrt{\text{area}}\)

– two-by-two quadrature for the bending energy terms

– one-point quadrature for the transverse shear energy terms

  • For very thick plates, when \(h/\ell >= 1\), the “modified one-point shear” element of the paper (pp. 1541-1542)

– two-by-two quadrature for the bending energy terms

– two-by-two quadrature for the transverse shear terms that are products of the gradients of \(w\), the \((\partial w/\partial x)^2\) and \((\partial w/\partial y)^2\) contributions of the paper and, for a laminate, the \(\partial w/\partial x \, \partial w/\partial y\) term of \(A_{45}\)

– one-point quadrature for the remaining transverse shear terms

The membrane stiffness terms, which are not specified in the paper of Hughes et al. (1977), are integrated with a three-by-three quadrature when the default drilling model is active and with a two-by-two quadrature otherwise, for the reason explained under the drilling stiffness below. The bending term always uses two-by-two, so that the drilling model does not change the bending response of the element.

With the one-point quadrature of the transverse shear terms, the element stiffness matrix has two zero-energy modes besides the rigid-body modes, as reported by Hughes et al. [1]: the hourglass mode \(w = (x - x_c)(y - y_c)\) with zero rotations, and the in-plane twist \(w = 0\), \(r_x = x - x_c\), \(r_y = y - y_c\), where \((x_c, y_c)\) is the centroid. Their transverse shear strains vanish at the centroid. The two-by-two quadrature of the gradient terms for \(h/\ell \ge 1\) gives stiffness to the hourglass mode, the “modified one-point shear” element of the paper. The hourglass mode is the one that survives assembly. It is suppressed by any support that constrains \(w\) along a boundary line, but a strip one element wide loaded in torsion, whose twist is the hourglass pattern, is much too flexible, e.g. the twist case of the straight cantilever of MacNeal and Harder [7].

Hughes et al. [1] note that the transverse shear stiffness is of the order of \((\ell/h)^2\) times the bending stiffness, such that, with a finite word length, the bending stiffness is lost for very thin elements, and propose to multiply the transverse shear stiffness by \((h/\ell)^2\) times the square of a maximum allowed aspect ratio \(\ell/h\), \(10^5/8\) for their plate element in the single precision of their computer, when this ratio is exceeded. This correction is not applied. In double precision, the central deflection of a simply supported square plate under a central point load, with eight by eight elements, changes by less than \(10^{-5}\) from \(\ell/h = 1.25 \times 10^3\) to \(\ell/h = 1.25 \times 10^5\), see tests/test_quad4_thin_limit.py, by \(3 \times 10^{-4}\) at \(\ell/h = 1.25 \times 10^6\), and by \(-32\) % at \(\ell/h = 1.25 \times 10^7\), where the round-off dominates.

Transverse shear stiffness

When a material direction is defined, pyfe3d.shellprop.ShellProp.calc_Ats_element() re-evaluates the equilibrium-based stiffness of Rohwer (1988) [6] with the plies rotated to the element coordinate system, such that the assumed cylindrical bending states are posed along the element axes.

Drilling stiffness

The FSDT kinematics contains no strain measure associated with \(r_z\), so the rows and columns of the drilling degree-of-freedom would be empty and a mesh of coplanar elements would give a singular global stiffness matrix. Two models are available, selected with the drilling_model attribute.

Physics-based, the default (drilling_model = 0). The in-plane displacement field is enriched so that the drilling rotations produce membrane strain energy, following Allman [2], and the independently interpolated \(r_z\) is tied to the rotation of the membrane field by the regularised functional of Hughes and Brezzi [3]. The combination for the quadrilateral is the one of Ibrahimbegovic, Taylor and Wilson [4].

Each edge \(k\), joining nodes \(i\) and \(j\) and of length \(\ell_k\), carries a quadratic normal displacement whose end slopes are identified with the vertex drilling rotations, giving the amplitude

\[a_k = \frac{\ell_k}{8}\left({r_z}_i - {r_z}_j\right)\]

so that the enrichment is not a new degree-of-freedom but the difference of the two drilling rotations already present at the ends of the edge. The in-plane field becomes

\[\begin{split}\left\{\begin{matrix} u \\ v \end{matrix}\right\} = \sum_i S_i \left\{\begin{matrix} u_i \\ v_i \end{matrix}\right\} + \sum_k \hat{S}_k \frac{\ell_k}{8}\left({r_z}_i - {r_z}_j\right) \boldsymbol{n}_k\end{split}\]

with \(\boldsymbol{n}_k\) the unit normal of edge \(k\) and \(\hat{S}_k\) the hierarchical bubble of that edge, equal to unity at its mid-point and zero at every corner, which for the quadrilateral are the mid-side functions of the eight-node serendipity element, see allman_enrichment(). In row form the enrichment populates the drilling columns of the membrane operator, giving \(\tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}_m\), whereas the curvature and transverse shear operators are untouched, because the edge modes act only on the in-plane translations. The drilling residual becomes

\[\tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}_{r_z} = \boldsymbol{S}^{r_z} + \frac{1}{2}\tilde{\boldsymbol{S}}^u_{,y} - \frac{1}{2}\tilde{\boldsymbol{S}}^v_{,x}\]

and the contribution to the element stiffness matrix is

\[\boldsymbol{K}_{C_e}^{r_z} = \gamma_{r_z} \iint_{\xi\eta} \tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}_{r_z}^\top \tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}_{r_z} \det \boldsymbol{J} d\xi d\eta \qquad \text{with} \qquad \gamma_{r_z} = A_{66}\]

The value \(\gamma_{r_z} = A_{66}\) is a modulus and not a user parameter. Hughes and Brezzi identify the shear modulus \(G\) as the natural regularisation parameter of the isotropic problem, and the thickness integration turns \(G\) into \(hG = A_{66}\), which generalises to the \(A_{66}\) of the laminate extensional stiffness matrix. The gamma_rz attribute allows a different value to be used, which is only of interest for the sensitivity study that the literature recommends.

Allman’s enrichment on its own is rank-deficient by one, because the state \(u_i = v_i = 0\) with \({r_z}_i = \omega_0\) makes every amplitude \(a_k\) vanish and therefore produces no membrane strain, although it is not a rigid-body motion. The term above gives that state the energy \(\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{r_z} A_e \omega_0^2\), with \(A_e\) the element area, and restores the rank at the element level. Prescribing the translations does not remove this mode, but in an assembled mesh it is a single global mode, which Allman notes, for his triangle, is very easily suppressed by prescribing any one of the drilling rotations of the model, and often by the displacement boundary conditions themselves ([2], Sec. 5); the term above removes it without such a constraint.

Two quadrature choices matter and both follow Ibrahimbegovic et al. (1990). The membrane term is integrated with three points per direction: the edge modes make \(\tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}_m\) vary linearly, and with two points the alternating pattern of the drilling rotations produces no membrane strain at any of the four points, leaving a zero-energy mode that no value of \(\gamma_{r_z}\) can remove. The constraint term \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C_e}^{r_z}\) is integrated with a single point at the centroid, which is what makes the element insensitive to \(\gamma_{r_z}\): a fully integrated constraint over-constrains \(r_z = \theta_z\) and locks the membrane response as \(\gamma_{r_z}\) grows, whereas with one point the response reaches an asymptote. One point also gives the constraint rank one per element, exactly what is needed to remove the uniform drilling mode.

Unlike the penalty below, the added term is consistent rather than artificial. Stationarity with respect to \(r_z\) gives \(\gamma_{r_z}(r_z - \theta_z) = 0\) pointwise, so the exact solution satisfies \(r_z = \theta_z\) and the term contributes no energy, for any positive \(\gamma_{r_z}\). The nodal moments about the shell normal recovered in the internal force vector are therefore physical, and in-plane moments can be transmitted between shells and beams through the shared drilling degree-of-freedom. Note also that when \(\boldsymbol{B} \neq \boldsymbol{0}\), for an offset reference surface or an unsymmetric laminate, the enriched membrane operator couples \(r_z\) to the curvatures, which is physically correct.

Fictitious penalty (drilling_model = 1), the default before version 0.10.0, following the approach adopted in MSC Nastran and Autodesk Nastran through their K6ROT parameter. It provides a small artificial stiffness whose only purpose is to remove the singularity, so the forces associated with it are spurious and any moment recovered about the shell normal is meaningless. The penalty energy is defined per element as:

\[U_{r_z} = \frac{1}{2} K6ROT \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot \int_A A_{66} (r_z - \theta_z)^2 dA\]

where \(10^{-6}\) is a scaling factor suggested by MSC Nastran’s approach (CQUAD4) to make the artificial drilling stiffness sufficiently small. AUTODESK NASTRAN’s quick reference guide recommends \(K6ROT = 100\) for static analysis. For modal solutions, \(K6ROT = 10^4\) is suggested. MSC NASTRAN’s quick reference guide states that \(K6ROT > 100\) should not be used, thus contradicting AUTODESK NASTRAN. The rotation \(r_z\) represents the drilling degree-of-freedom in element’s coordinates, whereas \(\theta_z\) the in-plane rotation strain, defined as:

\[\theta_z = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\right)\]

The first variation of \(U_{r_z}\) then becomes:

\[\delta U_{r_z} = K6ROT \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot \int_A A_{66} (r_z - \theta_z)(\delta r_z - \delta \theta_z) dA\]

which can be expressed in terms of the shape functions and element degrees-of-freedom (\(u_e\)) as: \(r_z = S^{r_z} u_e\), \(u = S^u u_e\) and \(v = S^v u_e\) as:

\[\delta U_{r_z} = K6ROT \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot u_e^\top \int_A A_{66} (S^{r_z \top} + 1/2 S^{u \top}_{,y} - 1/2 S^{v \top}_{,x})(\delta S^{r_z} + 1/2 \delta S^u_{,y} - 1/2 S^v_{,x}) dA u_e\]

or simply as:

\[\delta U_{r_z} = K6ROT \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot A_{66} u_e^\top \int_A B_{r_z}^\top B_{r_z} dA u_e\]

with:

\[B_{r_z} = S^{r_z} + 1/2 S^u_{,y} - 1/2 S^v_{,x}\]

which is the same operator as \(\tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}_{r_z}\) above, evaluated on the unenriched field. Being built from that operator and not from an addition on the diagonal terms is what keeps the penalty from stiffening a rigid rotation of the element about its normal, so both models represent all rigid-body motions and all constant-strain states exactly. \(A_{66}\) is assumed constant over the element. This term is integrated with the two-by-two quadrature. The approach herein presented is very similar to the one presented in Eq. 2.20 of Adam et al. [5].

Choosing between them. The physics-based model is the default because it is the one that is correct when the drilling moment is part of the load path, when shells are connected to beams or stiffeners that must transmit in-plane moments, or when the mesh is too coarse for the unenriched membrane response to be trusted. It is markedly more accurate in in-plane bending: on Cook’s skew membrane with a two-by-two mesh it gives 20.8 against the reference 23.9, where the penalty gives 11.8. The penalty remains available for reproducing results obtained before 0.10.0, and it is cheaper, since it leaves the membrane term on the two-by-two quadrature.

 ^ y
 |

 |       |
 |       |   --> x
 |       |
 |_______|
1         2

The element coordinate system is determined identically what is explained in Nastran’s quick reference guide for the CQUAD4 element, as illustrated below.

The constructor stores the probe and sets the identification numbers and the positions c1, c2, c3, c4 to -1, the init_k_* positions to 0, the rotation matrix r11, ..., r33 to zero and m11, m12, m21, m22 to the identity. Before computing any element matrix, the caller sets c1, c2, c3, c4 and the init_k_* positions, and calls update_rotation_matrix() and update_probe_xe().

Notes

The default physics-based drilling stiffness, drilling_model = 0, combines the in-plane enrichment of Allman (1984) [1] with the regularisation of Hughes and Brezzi (1989) [2], in the form given for the quadrilateral by Ibrahimbegovic et al. (1990) [3]. With it the drilling rotation is a kinematic variable that carries strain energy, the recovered nodal moments about the shell normal are physical, and no user parameter is involved, since its regularisation parameter is taken from the laminate stiffness. The fictitious penalty of the other values of drilling_model corresponds to \(\gamma_{r_z} = K6ROT \cdot 10^{-6} A_{66}\). Both models are described in the Notes of pyfe3d.quad4.

For K6ROT, AUTODESK NASTRAN’s quick reference guide recommends K6ROT = 100. for static analysis. For modal solutions, K6ROT=1.e4 is suggested. MSC NASTRAN’s quick reference guide states that K6ROT > 100. should not be used, but this is contradicting AUTODESK NASTRAN.

The default gamma_rz, \(A_{66}\), is the value identified by Hughes and Brezzi (1989) [2]. This is a modulus and not a parameter that needs tuning: the element response has a broad plateau of insensitivity around it, and the attribute is exposed for the sensitivity study that the literature recommends rather than for normal use. Very large values over-constrain \(r_z = \theta_z\), and a zero value leaves the Allman enrichment rank-deficient by one.

The matrix is integrated over the element with 2 by 2 Gauss-Legendre quadrature, from the coordinates probe.xe and the rotation matrix, and its values are added to KA_betav at the positions init_k_KA_beta to init_k_KA_beta + KA_BETA_SPARSE_SIZE - 1; the global row and column indices are always written to KA_betar and KA_betac at the same positions. Parameters:

Notes

With \(w_n = T_{13} u + T_{23} v + T_{33} w\) the displacement along the element normal, written with the global translations, the computed matrix is

\[\boldsymbol{K}_{A_\beta} = -\int_A \boldsymbol{S}^\top \left( T_{11} \boldsymbol{S}_{,x} + T_{12} \boldsymbol{S}_{,y} \right) dA\]

acting on the nodal values of \(w_n\), where \(\boldsymbol{S}\) holds the bilinear shape functions and \(\boldsymbol{S}_{,x}\), \(\boldsymbol{S}_{,y}\) their derivatives along the element axes. The element coordinates are \(x = T_{11} X + T_{21} Y + T_{31} Z\) and \(y = T_{12} X + T_{22} Y + T_{32} Z\), such that \(T_{11} \boldsymbol{S}_{,x} + T_{12} \boldsymbol{S}_{,y}\) is the derivative along the global \(X\) axis, the direction of the flow, projected on the plane of the element.

Piston theory

The matrices of update_KA_beta(), update_KA_gamma() and update_CA() are the matrices of the aerodynamic load along the element normal of a supersonic flow along the global \(X\) axis, flowing towards the positive \(X\). The linear piston theory, with Krumhaar’s correction for a cylinder [1], gives the pressure on the side of the flow \(p = \beta w_{,X} + \mu \dot{w} - \gamma w\), with \(w\) the displacement towards the flow, and hence the load per unit area along the normal

\[q = -\beta \, w_{n,X} + \gamma \, w_n - \mu \, \dot{w}_n\]

whichever side of the element the flow is on, because \(q\) and \(w_n\) change sign together. The load vector is then \(\boldsymbol{f}_A = \beta \boldsymbol{K}_{A_\beta} \boldsymbol{u} + \gamma \boldsymbol{K}_{A_\gamma} \boldsymbol{u} + \mu \boldsymbol{C}_A \dot{\boldsymbol{u}}\) and the equations of motion are

\[\boldsymbol{M} \ddot{\boldsymbol{u}} - \mu \boldsymbol{C}_A \dot{\boldsymbol{u}} + \left(\boldsymbol{K} - \beta \boldsymbol{K}_{A_\beta} - \gamma \boldsymbol{K}_{A_\gamma}\right) \boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{0}\]

The coefficients are given by the caller; with the density \(\rho_{\infty}\), speed \(U\) and Mach number \(M\) of the flow, \(\beta = \rho_{\infty} U^2/\sqrt{M^2 - 1}\), \(\mu = \beta (M^2 - 2)/(U (M^2 - 1))\), and \(\gamma = \beta/(2 R \sqrt{M^2 - 1})\) for the flow over the outer, convex, side of a cylinder of radius \(R\), an outward displacement lowering the pressure, or \(\gamma = 0\) for a flat panel. The positive \(\mu\) damps the motion, and the positive \(\gamma\) reduces the stiffness.

References

[1]

Krumhaar, H., “The accuracy of linear piston theory when applied to cylindrical shells”, AIAA Journal, 1(6), 1448-1449, 1963.

Notes

Assuming that KCNL = KC0L + KCL0 + KCLL + KGNL, that is \(\boldsymbol{K}_{CNL} + \boldsymbol{K}_{GNL}\), the book’s nonlinear constitutive stiffness matrix \(\boldsymbol{K}_{CNL}\) of the terms of \(\boldsymbol{B}_{NL}\) plus the geometric \(\boldsymbol{K}_{GNL}\), built from the von Karman membrane strains

\[\epsilon_{xx} = u_{,x} + \frac{1}{2} w_{,x}^2, \quad \epsilon_{yy} = v_{,y} + \frac{1}{2} w_{,y}^2, \quad \gamma_{xy} = u_{,y} + v_{,x} + w_{,x} w_{,y}\]

whose nonlinear part is \(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{NL} = \frac{1}{2} \boldsymbol{B}_{NL} \boldsymbol{u}_e\), with \(\boldsymbol{B}_{NL}\) its variation. With \(\boldsymbol{B}_m\) and \(\boldsymbol{B}_b\) the linear membrane and bending strain-displacement matrices, \(\boldsymbol{G}_x = \partial \boldsymbol{S}^w / \partial x\) and \(\boldsymbol{G}_y = \partial \boldsymbol{S}^w / \partial y\) the gradients of \(w\), and \(\boldsymbol{A}\), \(\boldsymbol{B}\) the laminate matrices:

  • KC0L = \(\boldsymbol{B}_m^\top \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{B}_{NL} + \boldsymbol{B}_b^\top \boldsymbol{B} \boldsymbol{B}_{NL}\)

  • KCL0 = KC0L`^top`

  • KCLL = \(\boldsymbol{B}_{NL}^\top \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{B}_{NL}\)

  • KGNL = \(N_{NL,xx} \boldsymbol{G}_x^\top \boldsymbol{G}_x + N_{NL,yy} \boldsymbol{G}_y^\top \boldsymbol{G}_y + N_{NL,xy} (\boldsymbol{G}_x^\top \boldsymbol{G}_y + \boldsymbol{G}_y^\top \boldsymbol{G}_x)\), with \(\boldsymbol{N}_{NL} = \{N_{NL,xx}, N_{NL,yy}, N_{NL,xy}\}^\top = \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{NL}\)

The first three groups are the constitutive terms coupling the linear and the nonlinear parts of the membrane strain. KGNL is geometric, carrying the stress of the nonlinear membrane strain. It is collected here so that update_KG() stays homogeneous of degree one in the displacements, which is what a linear buckling analysis needs. With it here,

\[\boldsymbol{K}_T = \boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + \boldsymbol{K}_{CNL}(\boldsymbol{u}) + \boldsymbol{K}_{GNL}(\boldsymbol{u}) + \boldsymbol{K}_G(\boldsymbol{u})\]

is the exact Jacobian of the internal forces of update_fint() with nonlinear=1, and a Newton-Raphson iteration built on them converges quadratically. The quadrature of update_KG() is used.

The linear part of the internal forces, KC0*u, keeps the quadratures of KC0, whereas the nonlinear terms of _update_probe_finte_nonlinear(), KCNL and KG use the two-by-two quadrature; each part of the strain energy is differentiated with its own quadrature, so the Jacobian remains exact. The element matrix is computed by _update_probe_KCNLve() and rotated to global coordinates as in update_KC0().

Notes

The three mass matrices follow from the kinetic energy of Brockman 1987 [1], Eq. (20), and have the form of its Eq. (21). With the displacements \(u + z r_y\), \(v - z r_x\) and \(w\) of the Notes of pyfe3d.quad4, the kinetic energy integrated through the thickness,

\[T = \frac{1}{2} \int_A \left[ intrho (\dot{u}^2 + \dot{v}^2 + \dot{w}^2) + 2\, intrhoz (\dot{u} \dot{r}_y - \dot{v} \dot{r}_x) + intrhoz2 (\dot{r}_x^2 + \dot{r}_y^2) \right] dA\]

where intrho, intrhoz and intrhoz2 include the offset, gives, for the nodes \(i\) and \(j\), in element coordinates,

\[\begin{split}M_{ij} = h_{ij} \begin{bmatrix} intrho & 0 & 0 & 0 & intrhoz & 0 \\ 0 & intrho & 0 & -intrhoz & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & intrho & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & -intrhoz & 0 & intrhoz2 & 0 & 0 \\ intrhoz & 0 & 0 & 0 & intrhoz2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad h_{ij} = \int_A S_i S_j dA\end{split}\]

for the DOF \(u, v, w, r_x, r_y, r_z\), the drilling rotation carrying no inertia. The matrix is rotated to global coordinates as in update_KC0(). The integrals \(h_{ij}\) are computed with:

  • mtype=0: two-point Gauss-Legendre quadrature in each direction;

  • mtype=1: two-point Gauss-Lobatto quadrature in each direction, whose points are the nodes, the lumped mass matrix of Brockman [1], giving \(h_{ii}\) equal to the Jacobian determinant at node \(i\) and \(h_{ij} = 0\) for \(i \neq j\);

  • mtype=2: a single point at the centroid, where every \(S_i = 1/4\), giving \(h_{ij} = A/16\) for all \(i, j\).

For mtype=0 the quadrature is exact. With mtype=2 the element mass is placed at the average of the node positions, which is the centroid of the area only for a parallelogram.

Lumped mass matrix

The Gauss-Lobatto rule of mtype=1 is a nodal quadrature. Applied to the whole integrand of \(T\), in which \(S_i\) is 1 at node \(i\) and 0 at the other nodes, it couples no different nodes and gives for node \(i\) the block \(M_{ii}\) above with \(h_{ii}\), the coupling through intrhoz included: the matrix is block-diagonal per node and, as Brockman [1] writes, diagonal only when intrhoz is zero. The weights \(h_{ii}\) sum to the area \(A\), so the lumped matrix has the exact mass and the exact first moment of the mass through the thickness, intrhoz \(A\), which places the centre of mass at the offset from the reference surface. In the plane of the element the first moment is exact when \(h_{ii} = \int_A S_i dA\), which holds for a parallelogram, where \(h_{ii} = A/4\), and not for a general quadrilateral, whose Jacobian determinant varies. Of the rotary inertia, the term intrhoz2 \(A\) about the axes in the plane of the element is exact, while the part from the distribution of intrho over the area, about any axis, is concentrated at the nodes: 3 times the exact value about the centroid of a parallelogram, an error that decreases with the size of the elements.

The coordinates of the four nodes, read from x at the positions c1//2, c2//2, c3//2 and c4//2, are rotated to the element coordinate system with the transpose \(\boldsymbol{T}_{3\times 3}^\top\) of the transformation matrix and written to probe.xe. update_area() is then called, updating area. update_rotation_matrix() must have been called before.

Note

The xe attribute of object Quad4Probe is updated, accessible using .probe.xe.

Parameters:
xarray-like

Array with global nodal coordinates, for a total of \(M\) nodes in the model, this array will be arranged as: \(x_1, y_1, z_1, x_2, y_2, z_2, ..., x_M, y_M, z_M\). 1-D array of size 3*M, dimension of length.

update_rotation_matrix(self, double[: :1] x, double xmati=0., double xmatj=0., double xmatk=0.) → void#

Update the rotation matrix of the element

Attributes r11,r12,r13,r21,r22,r23,r31,r32,r33 are updated, with the entries \(T_{ij}\) of the transformation matrix \(\boldsymbol{T}_{3\times 3}\) from element to global coordinates, whose columns are the element axes.

The element coordinate system is determined, identifying the \(ijk\) components of each axis: \({x_e}_i, {x_e}_j, {x_e}_k\); \({y_e}_i, {y_e}_j, {y_e}_k\); \({z_e}_i, {z_e}_j, {z_e}_k\).

The rotation matrix terms are calculated after solving 9 equations.

The element z axis is along the cross product of the vectors from node 4 to node 2 and from node 1 to node 3, and the element x axis along the sum of these two vectors. When a material direction is given, m11, m12, m21, m22 are updated with the rotation from the material direction projected onto the element to the element x axis. When it is the zero vector, the default, or parallel to the element normal, m11, m12, m21, m22 are not changed.

Parameters:
xarray-like

Array with global nodal coordinates, for a total of \(M\) nodes in the model, this array will be arranged as: \(x_1, y_1, z_1, x_2, y_2, z_2, ..., x_M, y_M, z_M\). 1-D array of size 3*M, dimension of length. The coordinates of each node are read from the positions c1//2, c2//2, c3//2 and c4//2.

xmati, xmatj, xmatkdouble, optional

Vector in global coordinates representing the material direction. This vector is projected onto the plate element, thus becoming the material direction. The \(ABD\) matrix defining the constitutive behavior of the element is rotated from the material direction to the element \(x\) axis while calculating the stiffness matrices. Only the direction of the vector is used. Default 0., the zero vector, which leaves m11, m12, m21, m22 unchanged.

class pyfe3d.quad4.Quad4Probe#

Probe used for local coordinates, local displacements, local stiffness, local stresses etc…

The idea behind using a probe is to avoid allocating larger memory buffers per finite element. The memory buffers are allocated per probe, and one probe can be shared amongst many finite elements, with the information being updated and retrieved on demand.

Note

The probe can be shared amongst more than one finite element, depending on how you defined them. Mind that the probe will always safe the values from the last udpate.

The constructor takes no parameters and allocates every array filled with zeros.

update_BL(self, double xi, double eta, int drilling_model=0) → void#

Update all components of the interpolation matrix \(\boldsymbol{B}_L\) at a given natural coordinate point \(\xi\), \(\eta\).

The rows BLexx, BLeyy, BLgxy, BLkxx, BLkyy, BLkxy, BLgyz_grad, BLgyz_rot, BLgxz_grad, BLgxz_rot and BLdrilling of the probe are computed from its element coordinates xe, which must have been updated with Quad4.update_probe_xe(). Only the entries that depend on xi, eta and xe are written, the other entries keep their values. Sw_x, Sw_y and the other attributes are not changed.

Parameters:
xi, etadouble

Natural coordinates of the evaluation point. Dimensionless, from -1 to 1 over the element.

drilling_modelint, optional

Must match the drilling_model attribute of the finite element that the probe is being used with, such that the recovered strains correspond to the displacement field that produced the stiffness matrix. The default 0 includes the Allman drilling enrichment in the membrane rows and in BLdrilling; any other value gives the unenriched rows used by the K6ROT penalty model.

Notes

With the bilinear shape functions \(S_i\) and their Cartesian derivatives \(S_{i,x}\), \(S_{i,y}\), the products of the rows with ue are the strains of the Notes of pyfe3d.quad4: BLexx, BLeyy and BLgxy give \(\epsilon_{xx} = u_{,x}\), \(\epsilon_{yy} = v_{,y}\) and \(\gamma_{xy} = u_{,y} + v_{,x}\); BLkxx, BLkyy and BLkxy give \(\kappa_{xx} = {r_y}_{,x}\), \(\kappa_{yy} = -{r_x}_{,y}\) and \(\kappa_{xy} = {r_y}_{,y} - {r_x}_{,x}\); BLgxz_grad and BLgxz_rot give the two parts of \(\gamma_{xz} = w_{,x} + r_y\), and BLgyz_grad and BLgyz_rot those of \(\gamma_{yz} = w_{,y} - r_x\); BLdrilling gives \(r_z - \theta_z = r_z + \frac{1}{2}u_{,y} - \frac{1}{2}v_{,x}\), the operator \(B_{r_z}\) of the penalty model.

With drilling_model=0 the \(r_z\) columns of BLexx, BLeyy and BLgxy receive the derivatives of the edge modes of allman_enrichment(), \(\tilde S^u_{,x}\), \(\tilde S^v_{,y}\) and \(\tilde S^u_{,y} + \tilde S^v_{,x}\), and the \(r_z\) columns of BLdrilling receive \(\frac{1}{2}\tilde S^u_{,y} - \frac{1}{2}\tilde S^v_{,x}\), giving the operator \(\tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}_{r_z}\) of the physics-based model.
