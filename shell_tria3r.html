Tria3R: discrete shear gap with the Allman membrane#

Tria3R, module pyfe3d.tria3r, is the recommended triangle of pyfe3d. It has the kinematics and the constant strains of the linear triangle, see Shell elements: kinematics and formulation, and, with the default shear_model = 1, the transverse shear field of Tria3DSG, the discrete shear gap averaged over the three starting nodes, see Tria3DSG: the discrete shear gap triangle. What distinguishes it from Tria3DSG is the membrane, enriched by default with the drilling rotations of Allman (1984), with the regularisation of Hughes and Brezzi (1989), see Drilling rotations.

Quadratures#

The membrane, bending and extension-bending coupling terms, constant over the triangle, are integrated with one point at the centroid, \(S_1 = S_2 = S_3 = 1/3\), with the weight \(1/2\) times \(\det \boldsymbol{J} = 2 A_e\). The transverse shear of the DSG field is added through the same \(18 \times 18\) block as the drilling terms, see the Notes of pyfe3d.tria3r. With the default drilling_model = 0, the terms of the Allman enrichment, whose strains are linear over the element, are integrated with the three-point rule of Cowper (1973), with the area coordinates \((2/3, 1/6, 1/6)\) and their permutations and the weight \(A_e/3\), see Tria3R.update_KC0(); a single point would give the element five zero eigenvalues over its nine in-plane degrees of freedom instead of three. The Hughes-Brezzi term is integrated with one point at the centroid, which makes the element insensitive to \(\gamma_{r_z}\). With drilling_model = 1 the penalty is integrated with the three-point rule.

The Allman membrane#

The edge \(k\), joining the nodes \(i\) and \(j\), carries the hierarchical bubble \(\hat{S}_k = 4 S_i S_j\) with the amplitude \(a_k = \ell_k ({r_z}_i - {r_z}_j)/8\) along its normal, such that the in-plane field is enriched without a new degree of freedom, see Drilling rotations. The edge modes act on the in-plane translations only: the curvatures and the transverse shear strains are not enriched, and on a flat plate in bending, buckling or vibration, where the membrane is decoupled from the bending, Tria3R and Tria3DSG give the same results to round-off. With \(\boldsymbol{B} \ne \boldsymbol{0}\), for an offset or an unsymmetric laminate, the enriched membrane strains couple \(r_z\) to the curvatures. The enrichment enters the membrane forces of the geometric stiffness and the non-linear terms, evaluated at the centroid, which integrates its linear strains exactly with one point, see Tria3R.update_KG().

The enrichment makes the element much better in in-plane bending and better on shells whose response mixes membrane and bending. Measured with quadrilateral meshes split along the diagonal 1-3, normalised by the reference, with Tria3DSG in parentheses:

  • skew membrane of Cook, reference 23.956 of Felippa (2003), \(n \times n\) cells: 0.864, 0.948, 0.982, 0.994 for \(n\) = 4, 8, 16, 32 (0.457, 0.713, 0.896, 0.970);

  • Scordelis-Lo roof of Belytschko et al. (1985), Table 3, \(N \times N\) on the quarter: 0.975, 0.989, 0.994, 0.996 for \(N\) = 8, 16, 32, 64 (0.853, 0.952, 0.984, 0.994);

  • twisted beam of MacNeal and Harder (1985), out-of-plane load: 0.933, 0.988, 0.997 with 12x2, 48x4 and 192x8 cells (0.817, 0.954, 0.990);

  • pinched hemisphere with an 18 degree hole of Belytschko et al. (1985): 0.288, 0.862, 0.998, 1.011 for \(N\) = 8, 16, 32, 64 (0.451, 0.926, 0.998, 1.009).

On the coarse meshes of the doubly curved hemisphere, the drilling rotation of a facet is made of the rotations that bend its neighbours, and the edge modes turn them into membrane strain, which stiffens an inextensional deformation. The penalty model, drilling_model = 1, which has no Allman field, gives 0.929 and 0.988 at \(N\) = 8 and 16, whereas reducing gamma_rz hundredfold does not change the stiffening: it is in the enrichment, not in the Hughes-Brezzi coefficient. It is gone at \(N = 32\). This is why Tria3DSG is the better triangle on a coarse mesh of a doubly curved shell. The assembly of Tria3R.update_KC0() takes about half the time of that of Tria3DSG.

The centroid-sampled shear of shear_model = 0#

With shear_model = 0 the transverse shear strains are sampled at the centroid, and the transverse shear stiffnesses are divided by (4) with alpha_shear_locking, e.g. 0.7. Here the factor does the unlocking, since the centroid sample still locks, and its value is about seven times the values near 0.1 of the references, where the factor sits on top of a field that is already free of locking (Lyly, Stenberg and Vihinen, 1993; Bischoff and Bletzinger, 2004; Castro et al., 2019). The factor tends to zero only when \(\ell\) falls well below \(h\), and on thin plates and shells the error stalls under refinement at a value set by \(\alpha\) and by the mesh pattern: on a simply supported square plate with \(a/h = 1000\) under a uniform pressure, the centre deflection is 14.1, 14.4 and 14.3 % too small with 32x32, 64x64 and 128x128 squares cut into triangles, the buckling load stalls at +16.4 % and the first frequency at +8.0 %, whereas the DSG field converges to +0.02 %. Lyly, Stenberg and Vihinen (1993), p. 348, state that the stabilisation was introduced for a piecewise quadratic deflection and that a linear or bilinear deflection needs a reduction operator of the MITC type with it; the centroid sample is neither. A larger \(\alpha\) moves the stall at the price of a too flexible coarse mesh, and on a thin cylinder in buckling it can make a mode at two elements per wavelength the critical one. The measurements are in the Notes of pyfe3d.tria3r.

Zero-energy modes#

A single free element has seven zero-energy modes: the six rigid-body motions and the mode of the rotations

\[u = v = w = r_z = 0, \qquad r_x = c \, (x - x_c), \qquad r_y = c \, (y - y_c)\]

about the centroid \((x_c, y_c)\), without curvature, \({r_y}_{,x} = {r_x}_{,y} = 0\) and \(-{r_x}_{,x} + {r_y}_{,y} = 0\), and without transverse shear strain. The bending and the transverse shear of the triangle have nine degrees-of-freedom and at most five independent constant strains, three curvatures and two transverse shear strains, so at least four of their modes have no energy, three being rigid-body motions. As the twisting mode of the one-point quadrilateral, Eq. (13) of Brockman (1987), the mode exists for a single element only: in an assembled mesh the neighbouring elements restrain it, see the Notes of pyfe3d.tria3r.
