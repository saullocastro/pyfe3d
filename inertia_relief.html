Inertia relief#

A structure without supports, e.g. an aircraft or a launch vehicle in flight, a spacecraft, or any free-free model, can move as a rigid body: its stiffness matrix \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\) is singular, with the rigid-body modes in its null space, and the linear static system of Linear static analyses cannot be solved as it stands. Under steady loads that are not balanced, e.g. the aerodynamic loads and the weights of an aircraft that is not trimmed, or the thrust of a launch vehicle, the free structure accelerates as a rigid body while it deforms elastically. Inertia relief balances the loads with the d’Alembert forces of this rigid-body acceleration and computes the displacements relative to a reference support, with apply_inertia_relief().

Solvability of the singular system#

The \(n_r\) rigid-body modes, six for a free structure in 3D, are the columns of \(\boldsymbol{\Phi}\), with \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} \boldsymbol{\Phi} = \boldsymbol{0}\) and, by symmetry, \(\boldsymbol{\Phi}^\top \boldsymbol{K}_{C0} = \boldsymbol{0}\). Premultiplying \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} \boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\) by \(\boldsymbol{\Phi}^\top\) shows that a static solution exists only for loads orthogonal to the rigid-body modes,

(1)#\[\boldsymbol{\Phi}^\top \boldsymbol{F}_{ext} = \boldsymbol{0}\]

i.e. without a resultant force or moment (Farhat and Géradin 1998; Cavaliere et al. 2021, Sec. 3, Eqs. (12) and (15)), and that it is then defined up to a rigid-body motion \(\boldsymbol{\Phi} \boldsymbol{\alpha}\). A particular solution is obtained by prescribing \(n_r\) null displacements, the reference support, chosen such that they suppress the rigid-body modes without restraining any deformation: a statically determinate support, for which the rows of \(\boldsymbol{\Phi}\) at the reference dofs form a non-singular square matrix.

Rigid-body modes and accelerations#

The dofs are partitioned as in Boundary conditions and prescribed displacements, into the known set \(k\), the reference support bref together with the dofs bfix that the model itself constrains, e.g. a plane of symmetry, and the free set \(u\). With unit displacements at the reference dofs and zero at those of bfix, the rigid-body modes follow from the stiffness matrix (Barnett et al. 1995, p. 6, Eq. (19)):

(2)#\[\begin{split}\boldsymbol{\Phi}_k = \begin{bmatrix} \boldsymbol{I} \\ \boldsymbol{0} \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \boldsymbol{\Phi}_u = -\boldsymbol{K}_{{C0}_{uu}}^{-1} \boldsymbol{K}_{{C0}_{uk}} \boldsymbol{\Phi}_k\end{split}\]

with the rows of bref first and those of bfix second in \(\boldsymbol{\Phi}_k\), where \(\boldsymbol{K}_{{C0}_{uu}}\) is positive definite only when the supports suppress all the rigid-body modes (Bathe 2014, Sec. 8.2.5). Under steady loads, the acceleration is that of a rigid body, \(\ddot{\boldsymbol{u}} = \boldsymbol{\Phi} \ddot{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\); premultiplying the equations of motion \(\boldsymbol{M} \ddot{\boldsymbol{u}} + \boldsymbol{K}_{C0} \boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\) by \(\boldsymbol{\Phi}^\top\) gives the rigid-body accelerations (Eqs. (21) and (22)),

(3)#\[\boldsymbol{\Phi}^\top \boldsymbol{M} \boldsymbol{\Phi} \, \ddot{\boldsymbol{\alpha}} = \boldsymbol{\Phi}^\top \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\]

and the displacements relative to the reference support, \(\boldsymbol{u}_k = \boldsymbol{0}\), solve the free rows of the static system with the d’Alembert forces added to the loads (Eqs. (23) and (24)):

(4)#\[\boldsymbol{K}_{{C0}_{uu}} \boldsymbol{u}_u = \boldsymbol{F}_{{ext}_u} - \boldsymbol{M}_u \boldsymbol{\Phi} \ddot{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\]

whose total load \(\boldsymbol{F}_{ext} - \boldsymbol{M} \boldsymbol{\Phi} \ddot{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\) satisfies the solvability condition (1). The mass matrix must give every rigid-body mode a mass, \(\boldsymbol{\Phi}^\top \boldsymbol{M} \boldsymbol{\Phi}\) positive definite: e.g. a beam needs a rotary inertia about its axis, intrhoy2 + intrhoz2 of BeamProp. A gravity field enters \(\boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\) as \(\boldsymbol{M} \boldsymbol{a}_g\), with \(\boldsymbol{a}_g\) the uniform acceleration at the translational dofs.

The reference support#

The reactions are the residual at the known dofs, \(\boldsymbol{R} = \boldsymbol{K}_{C0} \boldsymbol{u} - \boldsymbol{F}_{ext} + \boldsymbol{M} \boldsymbol{\Phi} \ddot{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\), as in (3). Since \(\boldsymbol{R}_u = \boldsymbol{0}\) and \(\boldsymbol{\Phi}^\top \boldsymbol{K}_{C0} = \boldsymbol{0}\), premultiplying it by \(\boldsymbol{\Phi}^\top\) shows that the reactions of a statically determinate reference support are zero: the support carries no load and does not change the strains, the stresses and the internal forces; another statically determinate support changes the displacements by a rigid-body motion only. The displacements are thus relative to the chosen support, which moves with the rigid-body acceleration; a support at a point of interest, e.g. the centre of gravity or a payload interface, makes them easier to read. Typical choices are the six dofs of one node, or three translations of one node, two of a second node and one of a third node that are not aligned; with bfix, fewer, e.g. heave and pitch for a half model of an airplane whose plane of symmetry and fore-aft translation are constrained, see tests/test_inertia_relief_ha144a.py.

apply_inertia_relief() checks the choice: with too few reference dofs, or dofs that leave a rigid-body mode free, e.g. two translations along the line joining their nodes, \(\boldsymbol{K}_{{C0}_{uu}}\) is singular, which is detected from the signs and the magnitudes of the pivots of its factorization, see pivot_rtol; with more dofs than the rigid-body modes, the support is statically indeterminate, and a column of \(\boldsymbol{\Phi}\) of (2) is not a rigid-body mode, \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} \boldsymbol{\Phi}_j \ne \boldsymbol{0}\), see rtol. Both raise a numpy.linalg.LinAlgError. \(\boldsymbol{K}_{{C0}_{uu}}\) is factorized once, as in linear_static(), and the factorization gives both \(\boldsymbol{\Phi}_u\) and \(\boldsymbol{u}_u\).

Balanced and unbalanced loads#

For balanced loads, e.g. self-equilibrated loads, the rigid-body accelerations are zero up to the round-off and inertia relief reduces to a fictitious support of the singular static problem: the displacements are those of the structure supported by the reference support, which carries no reaction. For unbalanced loads, the plain linear static solution with the same support would carry the resultant of the loads at the support, with unrealistic reactions and an unrealistic distribution of the internal forces (Cavaliere et al. 2021, Sec. 1). Inertia relief replaces these reactions by the d’Alembert forces of the rigid-body acceleration, distributed as the mass. The element forces of a distributed inertia load contain the element’s own share of it, as for any distributed load. The result is a quasi-static state, valid when the loads are steady and the transient response has been damped out; it is not a dynamic response.

Relation to the prescribed displacements#

The reference support is a set of prescribed null displacements, the known set of Boundary conditions and prescribed displacements with bk = bref | bfix and uk = 0, and the reactions are computed as there, with the d’Alembert forces added to the external forces. The difference is the choice of these dofs: a support of a linear static analysis restrains the structure and carries the loads, whereas the reference support of inertia relief only fixes the rigid-body motion and carries no load. The methods for large singular systems without a reference support, which compute the null space of \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\) during the factorization, are discussed by Farhat and Géradin (1998, Secs. 1-4).

tests/test_inertia_relief.py checks the function against closed-form solutions, three point masses joined by two springs, a chain of springs and masses, free-free beams and bars, and tests/test_inertia_relief_ha144a.py against the published results of the MSC Nastran example HA144A of Rodden and Johnson (1994).
