Co-rotational formulation for large rotations#

The elements of pyfe3d are formulated in element axes fixed in the initial configuration, with linear strains or the von Kármán strains of moderate rotations, see Beam theory and the beam elements. Large displacements and rotations with small strains are obtained without changing them, with the element-independent co-rotational (EICR) formulation of Felippa and Haugen (2005) and Nour-Omid and Rankin (1991), implemented in pyfe3d.corotational: the motion of each element is split into a rigid motion, followed by a co-rotational (CR) frame, and a small deformational motion relative to it, to which the linear element responds. This chapter presents the finite rotations, the kinematics of the split, the internal force and the consistent tangent stiffness matrix, and the frames of the beams, the truss and the spring; how a model is assembled and solved is in Non-linear static analyses.

Finite rotations#

Rotation vector and rotation matrix. The rotational degrees of freedom of a node of a CorotationalModel are the components of its total rotation vector \(\boldsymbol{\psi}\), of angle \(\psi = |\boldsymbol{\psi}|\) about the axis of \(\boldsymbol{\psi}\), added by the solvers like the translations. With \(\mathrm{Spin}(\boldsymbol{a}) \boldsymbol{b} = \boldsymbol{a} \times \boldsymbol{b}\) (spin(), and its inverse axial()), the nodal rotation matrix is the exponential map, Rodrigues’ formula (expso3()):

(1)#\[\boldsymbol{R} = \exp(\mathrm{Spin}(\boldsymbol{\psi})) = \boldsymbol{I} + \frac{\sin \psi}{\psi} \mathrm{Spin}(\boldsymbol{\psi}) + \frac{1 - \cos \psi}{\psi^2} \mathrm{Spin}(\boldsymbol{\psi})^2\]

Its inverse for angles up to \(\pi\), logso3(), extracts the quaternion of \(\boldsymbol{R}\) with Spurrier’s algorithm, accurate at every angle, including 0 and \(\pi\). Below the angles SERIES_ANGLE and SERIES_ANGLE_ETA_MU, the coefficients of these matrices are evaluated with their Taylor series, which avoid the cancellation of the closed forms.

Variations. A variation of the rotation of a node is a spatial spin \(\delta \boldsymbol{\omega}\), \(\delta \boldsymbol{R} \boldsymbol{R}^\top = \mathrm{Spin}(\delta \boldsymbol{\omega})\), related to the variation of the rotation vector by (Lambda())

(2)#\[\delta \boldsymbol{\omega} = \boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{\psi}) \delta \boldsymbol{\psi}, \qquad \boldsymbol{\Lambda} = \boldsymbol{I} + \frac{1 - \cos \psi}{\psi^2} \mathrm{Spin}(\boldsymbol{\psi}) + \frac{\psi - \sin \psi}{\psi^3} \mathrm{Spin}(\boldsymbol{\psi})^2\]

the \(\boldsymbol{H}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\) of Crisfield (1997), Eq. (16.90), and the \(\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\) of Felippa and Haugen (2005), Eq. (100). Its inverse, for the small deformational rotations \(\boldsymbol{\theta}\) in the CR frame, is (Hmat(), Felippa and Haugen, Eq. (101))

(3)#\[\delta \boldsymbol{\theta} = \boldsymbol{H}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) \delta \boldsymbol{\omega}, \qquad \boldsymbol{H} = \boldsymbol{I} - \frac{1}{2} \mathrm{Spin}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) + \eta \, \mathrm{Spin}(\boldsymbol{\theta})^2, \qquad \eta = \frac{1 - (\theta/2) \cot(\theta/2)}{\theta^2}\]

\(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\) is singular at \(\psi = 2 k \pi\), \(k \ge 1\): a node must turn by less than one full revolution, \(|\boldsymbol{\psi}| < 2 \pi\). Since the rotation vectors are total, no state is kept between the calls, and the solvers of Analysis are used unchanged. The derivatives of the transposes of \(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\) and \(\boldsymbol{H}\) times a fixed moment, which enter the tangent stiffness matrix, are dLambdaT_m() and Lmat().

Base and co-rotational frames#

The frames are written as \(3 \times 3\) matrices whose columns are the element axes, the convention of the attributes r11, ..., r33 of the elements (Felippa and Haugen write their transposes): \(\boldsymbol{T}_0\), the base frame, which is that of update_rotation_matrix of the element, and \(\boldsymbol{T}_R\), the CR frame of the current configuration. With the base local coordinates \(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_a = \boldsymbol{T}_0^\top (\boldsymbol{X}_a - \boldsymbol{X}_C)\) of node \(a\), \(\boldsymbol{X}_C\) being the mean of the nodal coordinates, the deformed positions \(\boldsymbol{x}^D_a\) and the nodal rotation matrices \(\boldsymbol{R}_a\), the deformational displacements of node \(a\) in the CR frame are

(4)#\[\bar{\boldsymbol{u}}_{d,a} = \boldsymbol{T}_R^\top (\boldsymbol{x}^D_a - \boldsymbol{x}_C) - \tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_a, \qquad \bar{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{d,a} = \mathrm{axial}\left(\log\left( \boldsymbol{T}_R^\top \boldsymbol{R}_a \boldsymbol{T}_0 \right)\right)\]

with \(\boldsymbol{x}_C\) the mean of \(\boldsymbol{x}^D_a\) (Felippa and Haugen, Eq. (22); Nour-Omid and Rankin, Eq. (14)). In this chapter the overbar marks components in the CR frame, as in Felippa and Haugen. The positions are computed relative to the base centroid, \((\boldsymbol{X}_a - \boldsymbol{X}_C) + \boldsymbol{u}_a\), which keeps their round-off independent of the origin of the coordinates. A rigid motion of the element moves \(\boldsymbol{T}_R\) with it and leaves the deformational displacements \(\boldsymbol{d}\) zero; the element is then evaluated with its local linear stiffness matrix \(\boldsymbol{K}_e\), computed once in the base configuration by its own update_KC0 with the rotation matrix set to the identity.

The CR frame is defined by a frame rule of each element type, CorotationalModel.add() choosing the wrapper:

Wrapper, elements

CR frame

CorotationalShell, triangles

\(x\) along the side 1-2 and \(z\) normal to the plane of the nodes; the solution depends on the numbering of the nodes

CorotationalShell, quadrilaterals

\(z\) along the cross product of the diagonals and \(x\) along their sum, invariant to a cyclic renumbering; a warped element is linked to its flat projection by rigid offsets, see the Notes of CorotationalShell and Choosing an element

CorotationalBeam, BeamC and BeamLR

mean nodal triad, below

CorotationalTruss

'chord': the axis alone, Crisfield’s co-rotational truss; 'mean_triad': as the beams, with the torsion

CorotationalSpring

'axis': as the beams, for an axial spring along its nodes; 'triad': mean of the rotated element axes, also for coincident nodes

Mean nodal triad of the beams. The \(x\) axis of the CR frame follows the chord, and the \(y\) axis lies in the plane of the chord and of the mean of the base \(y\) axis \(\boldsymbol{q}_0 = \boldsymbol{T}_0 \boldsymbol{e}_2\) rotated by the two nodal rotations (Crisfield, 1997, Eqs. (17.11)-(17.14); the midpoint interpolation of Nour-Omid and Rankin, Section 5):

(5)#\[\boldsymbol{e}_1 = \frac{\boldsymbol{x}^D_2 - \boldsymbol{x}^D_1}{l}, \qquad \boldsymbol{q} = \frac{1}{2} (\boldsymbol{R}_1 + \boldsymbol{R}_2) \boldsymbol{q}_0, \qquad \boldsymbol{e}_3 = \frac{\boldsymbol{e}_1 \times \boldsymbol{q}}{|\boldsymbol{e}_1 \times \boldsymbol{q}|}, \qquad \boldsymbol{e}_2 = \boldsymbol{e}_3 \times \boldsymbol{e}_1\]

with \(l\) the current length. The frame is undefined when \(\boldsymbol{q}\) becomes parallel to the chord; below the component \(\bar{q}_2\) of q2_min the wrapper returns NaN forces, so that the solvers reduce the increment, and warns. Nour-Omid and Rankin, Appendix B.3, redefine \(\boldsymbol{q}\) in that case, which a formulation without state cannot do without a jump of the forces.

Internal force and consistent tangent stiffness#

Projector. The variation of the CR frame follows from the variations of the nodal displacements and spins in the CR frame, \(\delta \bar{\boldsymbol{v}}\), through the spin-fitter matrix \(\boldsymbol{G}\), \(\delta \boldsymbol{\omega}_R = \boldsymbol{G} \delta \bar{\boldsymbol{v}}\), derived in pyfe3d for each frame rule and given in the Notes of the wrappers; for the mean nodal triad

(6)#\[\omega_1 = \frac{1}{2 q_2} \sum_i (\bar{q}_{i2} \omega_{i,x} - \bar{q}_{i1} \omega_{i,y}) - \frac{q_1}{q_2} \frac{w_2 - w_1}{l}, \qquad \omega_2 = -\frac{w_2 - w_1}{l}, \qquad \omega_3 = \frac{v_2 - v_1}{l}\]

with \(\bar{\boldsymbol{q}} = \boldsymbol{T}_R^\top \boldsymbol{q} = (q_1, q_2, 0)\), \(\bar{\boldsymbol{q}}_i = \boldsymbol{T}_R^\top \boldsymbol{R}_i \boldsymbol{q}_0\), and \(v_a\), \(w_a\), \(\omega_{i,x}\), \(\omega_{i,y}\) the local translations and spins of the nodes. With the matrix \(\boldsymbol{S}\) of the rigid rotations of the nodes about the centroid, \(\boldsymbol{G} \boldsymbol{S} = \boldsymbol{I}\) (Felippa and Haugen, Eq. (17)), and the projector

(7)#\[\boldsymbol{P} = \boldsymbol{P}_u - \boldsymbol{S} \boldsymbol{G}\]

removes the rigid motions from the variations of the element, \(\boldsymbol{P}_u\) removing the rigid translations (Felippa and Haugen, Eqs. (11), (12), (31)-(34)).

Internal force. With the local force \(\boldsymbol{K}_e \boldsymbol{d}\) transformed from the deformational rotations to the spins by \(\bar{\boldsymbol{H}} = \mathrm{diag}(\boldsymbol{I}, \boldsymbol{H}(\bar{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{d,a}))\), the internal force in the CR frame is that of the consistent symmetrizable equilibrated (CSE) formulation of Felippa and Haugen (2005), Section 6.3, Eq. (53):

(8)#\[\bar{\boldsymbol{p}} = \boldsymbol{P}^\top \bar{\boldsymbol{H}}^\top \boldsymbol{K}_e \boldsymbol{d}\]

Tangent stiffness. Its variation gives the tangent stiffness matrix in the CR frame,

(9)#\[\boldsymbol{K}_R = \underbrace{\boldsymbol{P}^\top \bar{\boldsymbol{H}}^\top \boldsymbol{K}_e \bar{\boldsymbol{H}} \boldsymbol{P}}_{\text{material}} + \underbrace{\boldsymbol{P}^\top \bar{\boldsymbol{L}} \boldsymbol{P} - \boldsymbol{F}_{nm} \boldsymbol{G} - \boldsymbol{G}^\top \boldsymbol{F}_n^\top \boldsymbol{P}}_{\text{geometric}}\]

with \(\bar{\boldsymbol{L}} = \mathrm{diag}(\boldsymbol{0}, \boldsymbol{L}(\bar{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{d,a}, \bar{\boldsymbol{m}}_a))\) of the local nodal moments (Lmat()), the moment-correction stiffness of Felippa and Haugen, Eqs. (46) and (102), \(-\boldsymbol{F}_{nm} \boldsymbol{G}\) the rotational geometric stiffness, Eqs. (44)-(45), and \(-\boldsymbol{G}^\top \boldsymbol{F}_n^\top \boldsymbol{P}\) the equilibrium-projection geometric stiffness, Eq. (47), with \(\boldsymbol{F}_n\) and \(\boldsymbol{F}_{nm}\) built with the spins of the forces and moments of \(\bar{\boldsymbol{p}}\). Equation (47) is exact when \(\boldsymbol{G}\) does not depend on the nodal rotations, their condition (37), which holds for the frames of the shells. The mean nodal triad depends on them, and CorotationalBeam uses, with exact_KGP=True (the default), the exact variation of \(\boldsymbol{P}^\top \boldsymbol{p}\),

(10)#\[\boldsymbol{K}_{GP} = -\frac{\partial (\boldsymbol{G}^\top \boldsymbol{z})}{\partial \bar{\boldsymbol{v}}} - \boldsymbol{G}^\top \boldsymbol{F}_n(\boldsymbol{p})^\top \boldsymbol{P}, \qquad \boldsymbol{z} = \boldsymbol{S}^\top \boldsymbol{p}, \qquad \boldsymbol{p} = \bar{\boldsymbol{H}}^\top \boldsymbol{K}_e \boldsymbol{d}\]

derived in pyfe3d, whose first term Eq. (47) misses, a term of the order of the unbalanced force (Felippa and Haugen, Eq. (48)).

Additive rotation vectors. The force and the stiffness are transformed to global components, \(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{T}} = \mathrm{diag}(\boldsymbol{T}_R)\), and from the spins to the variations of the rotation vectors, \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} = \mathrm{diag}(\boldsymbol{I}, \boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{\psi}_a))\) (Crisfield, 1997, Section 17.4.2, Eqs. (17.176)-(17.177)):

(11)#\[\boldsymbol{f} = \hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^\top \boldsymbol{\mathcal{T}} \bar{\boldsymbol{p}}, \qquad \boldsymbol{K} = \hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^\top \boldsymbol{\mathcal{T}} \boldsymbol{K}_R \boldsymbol{\mathcal{T}}^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} + \mathrm{diag}\left(\boldsymbol{0}, \frac{\partial (\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^\top \boldsymbol{m}_a)}{\partial \boldsymbol{\psi}_a}\right)\]

with \(\boldsymbol{m}_a\) the global moments of node \(a\) (dLambdaT_m()). \(\boldsymbol{K}\) is the Jacobian of \(\boldsymbol{f}\), exact for the frames of the shells, the chord of the truss and, with exact_KGP=True, the nodal triads of the beams and the springs, and unsymmetric away from equilibrium; its material part enters calc_kC and its geometric part calc_kG of the model. The steps are those of the kernel _force_tangent_c of pyfe3d.corotational, and each tangent is checked against central finite differences in tests/test_corotational_core.py.

Truss and spring#

With frame='chord', CorotationalTruss is the co-rotational truss of Crisfield (1991), Section 3.6, written for the space truss of Section 3.7: with \(l_0\) and \(l\) the initial and current lengths, \(\boldsymbol{e}\) the unit vector from node 1 to node 2 and the axial force \(N = (E A/l_0)(l - l_0)\),

(12)#\[\begin{split}\boldsymbol{f} = N \begin{Bmatrix} -\boldsymbol{e} \\ \boldsymbol{e} \end{Bmatrix}, \qquad \boldsymbol{K}_t = \frac{E A}{l_0} \boldsymbol{e} \boldsymbol{e}^\top + \frac{N}{l} (\boldsymbol{I} - \boldsymbol{e} \boldsymbol{e}^\top)\end{split}\]

on the translations, \(\boldsymbol{K}_t\) and \(-\boldsymbol{K}_t\) being the blocks of the two nodes, material and geometric. The rotations receive no force, as in the linear element; with a torsional stiffness, 'auto' chooses 'mean_triad', the frame of the beams.

The linear Spring does not balance the moment of its transverse forces between distinct nodes, see Truss and spring elements; the projector of CorotationalSpring adds it, so that its tangent at the base configuration differs from KC0. Its 'triad' frame replaces the chord of Eq. (5) by the mean of the rotated base \(x\) axes, which depends on the nodal rotations only and is defined for coincident nodes, up to a relative rotation of the nodes of 180 degrees.

Von Kármán strains in the co-rotational frame#

With kinematics='corotational_von_karman' of CorotationalModel.add() (local='von_karman' of the wrappers), the local response is that of the von Kármán strains of the element, step 8 of the procedure of Crisfield (1997), Section 17.1.5: the element’s own update_probe_finte(prop, nonlinear=1), update_KCNL and update_KG are evaluated at \(\boldsymbol{d}\), and the local force \(\bar{\boldsymbol{f}}(\boldsymbol{d})\) and the local tangent \(\boldsymbol{K}_e + \boldsymbol{K}_{CNL}(\boldsymbol{d}) + \boldsymbol{K}_G(\boldsymbol{d})\) replace \(\boldsymbol{K}_e \boldsymbol{d}\) and \(\boldsymbol{K}_e\) in Eqs. (8) and (9). In the CR frame of a beam the deformational translations are along the chord, \(\bar{v} = \bar{w} = 0\):

  • BeamLR interpolates \(v\) and \(w\) linearly, so its von Kármán terms vanish and the result is that of 'corotational' to round-off; the same holds for the shells, whose transverse translations in the CR frame are zero or the hourglass pattern of the warp;

  • BeamC interpolates them with the nodal rotations, and its element-mean axial strain of Beam theory and the beam elements, Eq. (18), adds the local higher-order terms of Crisfield (1997), Section 17.1.5, Eqs. (17.56a)-(17.56b). On the cantilever under an end force of Sze, Liu and Lo (2004), Section 3.1, 16 elements give tip errors of -0.02 % and +0.05 % with 'corotational' and +0.002 % and +0.007 % with 'corotational_von_karman', see CorotationalBeam.

The truss and the spring, without von Kármán strains of their own, take 'corotational_von_karman' as 'corotational'.

Limits of validity#

  • Small strains: the local response is linear, or von Kármán, in the CR frame; large strains are not represented.

  • Each nodal rotation vector below one revolution, \(|\boldsymbol{\psi}_a| < 2 \pi\).

  • The mean nodal triad is undefined when the mean of the rotated \(y\) axes turns parallel to the chord, q2_min; for a truss without torsion, 'chord' avoids it.

  • A node shared with an element of another kinematics is valid only while its rotations remain small, since that element reads the rotation vector as a small rotation; a warning is issued.

  • Moments in the constant external load vector fext are conjugate to the rotation vectors; moments about fixed axes and follower moments depend on the configuration and are added with CorotationalModel.add_moment(), see Non-linear static analyses.
