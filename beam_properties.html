Beam properties#

A BeamProp holds everything of the cross section that the elements BeamC, BeamLR and Truss read: either the integrals of a rigid, homogeneous cross section with its moduli, or a full sectional stiffness matrix, and the mass integrals. This chapter states what each attribute means in the beam model of Beam theory and the beam elements, how the sectional stiffness matrix is built from them, and when it must be given instead.

Cross-section integrals#

The integrals are taken over the cross section in the element axes \(y\), \(z\), measured from the beam axis that joins the two nodes:

Attribute

Integral

Role in the strain energy

A

\(\int dA\)

axial and shear stiffness, \(E A\), \(G_{12} A\), \(G_{13} A\)

Ay, Az

\(\int y \, dA\), \(\int z \, dA\)

coupling of the axial strain with the curvatures and of the shear strains with the twist; zero for a centroidal axis

Izz, Iyy

\(\int y^2 dA\), \(\int z^2 dA\)

bending in the \(x y\) plane (\(v\), \(r_z\)) and in the \(x z\) plane (\(w\), \(r_y\))

Iyz

\(\int y z \, dA\)

coupling of the two planes of bending; zero for principal axes

J

torsion constant

torsion, through GJ when GJ is not set

The first moments are named after the coordinate they integrate, Ay \(= \int y \, dA\), which some texts call \(Q_z\). The moduli are E, the Young modulus along the axis, and G, the shear modulus, with three derived stiffnesses:

  • G12, the transverse shear modulus of the element plane \(x y\), and G13, that of the plane \(x z\); both read G until they are set;

  • GJ, the torsional stiffness; it reads G*J until it is set.

Once set, each keeps its value independently of G and J, until it is deleted, e.g. del prop.G12. A property given with G and J alone keeps the meaning it had before these attributes existed.

Shear correction and torsion#

With the rigid section of Beam theory and the beam elements, the shear strain is uniform over the section and the strain energy of the shear stresses, per unit length, is \(\frac{1}{2} \boldsymbol{\gamma}^\top \boldsymbol{D}_s \boldsymbol{\gamma}\) with \(\boldsymbol{\gamma} = \{\bar{\gamma}_y, \bar{\gamma}_z, \kappa_x\}^\top\) and

(1)#\[\begin{split}\boldsymbol{D}_s = \begin{bmatrix} G_{12} A & 0 & -G_{12} A_z \\ 0 & G_{13} A & G_{13} A_y \\ -G_{12} A_z & G_{13} A_y & G_{12} I_{yy} + G_{13} I_{zz} \end{bmatrix}\end{split}\]

The two corrections that a real section needs enter here:

  • the shear stiffnesses of the actual, non-uniform, shear stress distribution are \(k_y G A\) and \(k_z G A\), with shear correction factors that depend on the shape of the section, the material and, for laminates, the stacking sequence; the elements apply none, and the user sets G12 \(= k_y G\) and G13 \(= k_z G\), e.g. \(k_y = k_z = 5/6\) for a rectangle;

  • the torsional entry of the rigid section is the polar moment of the stiffness, exact only for circular sections and far too stiff for open thin-walled ones; it is replaced by GJ, e.g. \(G J\) with the Saint-Venant torsion constant \(J\), in which the section warps freely.

Applying a correction factor to G instead of G12 and G13 also scales GJ when GJ is not set. For an arbitrary section, the shear stiffnesses, the torsional stiffness and the couplings follow from the cross-sectional analysis of pyfe3d.vabs without assumed factors, see Cross-sectional analysis of beams.

Sectional stiffness matrix#

The elements read the stiffness only through the \(6 \times 6\) sectional stiffness matrix C of the strains \(\boldsymbol{\epsilon} = \{\bar{\varepsilon}, \bar{\gamma}_y, \bar{\gamma}_z, \kappa_x, \kappa_y, \kappa_z\}^\top\) of Beam theory and the beam elements, Eq. (4). Until it is set, reading C returns the matrix of the 11 stiffness attributes, Eq. (6), i.e. the integral of the rigid section with \(G J\) = GJ in place of the torsional entry of Eq. (1). Once set, C keeps its own 21 independent entries, and the elements no longer read the 11 attributes, until del prop.C; the mass integrals are independent of C. The pattern of Eq. (6) cannot hold the couplings of a composite or otherwise anisotropic section, which arise from the interaction of the anisotropy with the warping of the section:

The units of an entry are force times length to the number of its indices that are 3, 4 or 5, e.g. force for C[0, 0] and force times length squared for C[4, 4].

A set C must be symmetric to 1e-10 times its largest entry, its symmetric part being stored, and, set or built, it must be positive semi-definite, since the strain energy per unit length cannot be negative. BeamC and BeamLR check it each time they read it, with a Cholesky factorization with diagonal pivoting of the matrix scaled to a unit diagonal, to a tolerance of 1e-10, and raise ValueError otherwise; zero rows, e.g. GJ = 0 in a planar model whose out-of-plane degrees of freedom are constrained, are allowed. With couplings other than C[0, 3], BeamC inverts C to build its shape functions and needs it positive definite, see Beam theory and the beam elements. Truss reads only C[0, 0] and C[3, 3] and does not check C.

From the cross-sectional analysis to the property#

pyfe3d.vabs orders the strains as \(\{\gamma_{11}, 2\gamma_{12}, 2\gamma_{13}, \kappa_1, \kappa_2, \kappa_3\}\), with \(x_2 = y\) and \(x_3 = z\), and its curvatures are the components of the curvature vector, \(\kappa_2 = \kappa_z\) and \(\kappa_3 = -\kappa_y\). Hence the VABS indices 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 become 0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 4 and the entries that couple \(\kappa_3\) with another strain change sign, which yu_to_pyfe3d() does. beamprop_from_vabs() creates a BeamProp whose C is a matrix in the order of pyfe3d, e.g. yu_to_pyfe3d(sec.Kt), fills its 11 attributes from the entries of C for information, modulus-weighted with a reference Young modulus, and, given the CrossSection, copies the mass integrals of CrossSection.calc_mass(). After del prop.C the 11 attributes alone represent the section: the shear centre is then the tension centre and the couplings are lost. See Cross-sectional analysis of beams.

Mass integrals#

The kinetic energy of the beam elements, Eq. (16), uses six integrals of the density \(\rho(y, z)\) over the section: intrho \(= \int \rho \, dA\), the mass per unit length; intrhoy \(= \int \rho y \, dA\) and intrhoz \(= \int \rho z \, dA\), which couple the inertia of the translations and of the rotations when the axis is not through the centre of mass; intrhoy2 \(= \int \rho y^2 dA\), intrhoz2 \(= \int \rho z^2 dA\) and intrhoyz \(= \int \rho y z \, dA\), the rotary inertia, with intrhoy2 + intrhoz2 the polar inertia of the twist. For a homogeneous section of density \(\rho\), intrho \(= \rho A\), intrhoy2 \(= \rho I_{zz}\), intrhoz2 \(= \rho I_{yy}\) and intrhoyz \(= \rho I_{yz}\). The lumped mass matrices drop intrhoy, intrhoz and intrhoyz, see Beam theory and the beam elements.
