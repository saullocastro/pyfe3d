Tria3DSG: the discrete shear gap triangle#

Tria3DSG, module pyfe3d.tria3dsg, is a flat three-node triangle with the FSDT kinematics of Shell elements: kinematics and formulation, linear interpolation of the six degrees of freedom and transverse shear strains obtained with the discrete shear gap (DSG) method of Bletzinger, Bischoff and Ramm (2000). Its membrane is that of the constant-strain triangle, without drilling enrichment. The same transverse shear field is the default of Tria3R, which adds the Allman membrane, see Tria3R: discrete shear gap with the Allman membrane.

Constant strains of the linear triangle#

With the area coordinates \(S_1\), \(S_2\), \(S_3\) and \(\det \boldsymbol{J} = x_1(y_2 - y_3) + x_2(y_3 - y_1) + x_3(y_1 - y_2)\), twice the signed area, the derivatives \(S_{1,x} = (y_2 - y_3)/\det \boldsymbol{J}\), \(S_{1,y} = (x_3 - x_2)/\det \boldsymbol{J}\) and their cyclic permutations are constant, and so are the membrane strains and the curvatures of (3). One point integrates them exactly, and the triangle has no hourglass modes. The signed area makes the operators correct for both orderings of the nodes. The transverse shear strains of the same interpolation are not usable: \(\nabla w\) is a single constant vector and the rotations are linear, such that no nodal values give \(\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(0)} = \boldsymbol{0}\) in a state of constant curvature, and the element locks, see Shell elements: kinematics and formulation.

The discrete shear gap#

With \(\boldsymbol{\phi} = (-r_y, r_x)\), the transverse shear strains of (3) are \(\{\gamma_{xz}, \gamma_{yz}\} = \nabla w - \boldsymbol{\phi}\). The shear gap is the part of the increase of the deflection between two points that is due to shear rather than to bending (Bletzinger et al., 2000, Sec. 2, Eq. (3)). On the triangle, its nodal values are obtained by integrating the shear strains along the straight edges that join a starting node \(p\) to the other two nodes \(q\), with the rotations interpolated linearly along the edge, such that their integral is the mean of the two nodal values times the edge vector:

(1)#\[\Delta w_p = 0, \qquad \Delta w_q = (w_q - w_p) + \frac{1}{2} (r_{y,p} + r_{y,q}) \, \Delta x - \frac{1}{2} (r_{x,p} + r_{x,q}) \, \Delta y, \qquad \Delta x = x_q - x_p, \quad \Delta y = y_q - y_p\]

The assumed transverse shear strain is the gradient of the interpolated gap,

(2)#\[\begin{split}\begin{Bmatrix} \gamma_{xz} \\ \gamma_{yz} \end{Bmatrix} = \nabla \left( \sum_i S_i \Delta w_i \right) = \sum_{q \ne p} \Delta w_q \begin{Bmatrix} S_{q,x} \\ S_{q,y} \end{Bmatrix}\end{split}\]

which is constant over the element and a linear combination of the nodal values only. The following properties follow from (1) and (2):

  • the rigid-body modes that involve \(w\), a translation and the two rotations about the in-plane axes, give \(\Delta w_q = 0\) and no shear;

  • a state of constant transverse shear is reproduced exactly;

  • the nodal values of a Kirchhoff state of constant curvature, \(w\) quadratic and \(r_y = -w_{,x}\), \(r_x = w_{,y}\), give \(\Delta w_q = 0\): the element stores no parasitic shear energy in pure bending, for any thickness, which is the locking-free property;

  • the operator is formed once per element, without quadrature.

The transverse shear stiffness \(\boldsymbol{A}_{ts}\) of the ShellProp is therefore used as the laminate gives it, and the shear correction keeps its meaning.

Averaging over the starting node. The gap measured from one node makes the operator depend on which node that is, a dependence inherent to the method, whose influence decreases with refinement (Bletzinger et al., 2000, Sec. 4.1.1): on an irregular triangle the element matrix changes by 14 and 17 % under the two cyclic relabellings of its nodes. pyfe3d uses the mean of the three operators,

(3)#\[\begin{split}\begin{Bmatrix} \gamma_{xz} \\ \gamma_{yz} \end{Bmatrix} = \frac{1}{3} \sum_{p=1}^{3} \sum_{q \ne p} \Delta w_q^{(p)} \begin{Bmatrix} S_{q,x} \\ S_{q,y} \end{Bmatrix}\end{split}\]

which inherits the four properties, since each operator has them and they are linear in the operator, and which does not depend on the numbering of the nodes, see the Notes of pyfe3d.tria3dsg.

The transverse shear strains have no von Kármán terms, such that the DSG operator appears only in KC0. Every operator of the element is constant over the triangle, and KCNL, KG and the non-linear internal forces of Shell elements: kinematics and formulation are integrated exactly by a multiplication by the area.

Optional rescaling of the transverse shear stiffness#

The attribute alpha_shear_locking \(= \alpha\), 0. by default, divides the transverse shear stiffnesses in the element axes by

(4)#\[1 + \alpha \frac{\ell^2}{h^2}\]

with \(\ell\) the longest edge and \(h\) the thickness, the factor of Lyly, Stenberg and Vihinen (1993), Eq. (3.12), called Stenberg’s method by Bischoff and Bletzinger (2004), Sec. 3.1, in the form of Castro et al. (2019), Eq. (26). In these references the factor sits on top of an assumed-strain or DSG field that is already free of locking, with values of \(\alpha\) near 0.1. On this element it is a mild coarse-mesh stabilisation: \(\alpha = 0.07\), the lower bound of the range of Castro et al., takes the first natural frequency of a thin plate with a \(5 \times 7\) mesh from 12.06 to 7.93 % above the analytical value, and its buckling load from +1.71 to +0.11 %. The factor is unbounded in \(\ell/h\), and a non-zero \(\alpha\) removes nearly all the transverse shear stiffness of a practical shell mesh: the energy of a constant transverse shear state drops to \(1/(1 + \alpha \ell^2/h^2)\) of the correct one, and on a plate with \(a/h = 5\), where the transverse shear carries 19 % of the deflection, the error grows monotonically with \(\alpha\). alpha_shear_locking is therefore zero by default, and 0.07 is the value recorded for coarse meshes of thin plates only, see the Notes of Tria3DSG.

Membrane and drilling#

The membrane is the constant-strain triangle, stiff in in-plane bending: on the skew membrane of Cook with \(n \times n\) cells split along a diagonal, the tip displacement is 0.457, 0.713, 0.896 and 0.970 of the reference for \(n\) = 4, 8, 16 and 32, against 0.864, 0.948, 0.982 and 0.994 for Tria3R. The drilling rotation is tied to the in-plane rotation \(\theta_z = (v_{,x} - u_{,y})/2\) by the Hughes-Brezzi term with \(\gamma_{r_z} = A_{66}\), or with the penalty \(K6ROT \cdot 10^{-6} A_{66}\) for drilling_model different from zero, integrated with the three-point rule: one point would constrain one combination of the three \(r_z\) only, and an assembled patch would have eight zero eigenvalues instead of six, see Drilling rotations and the Notes of Tria3DSG.update_KC0().

Zero-energy modes#

A single free element has seven zero-energy modes: the six rigid-body motions and the mode of the rotations

\[u = v = w = r_z = 0, \qquad r_x = c \, (x - x_c), \qquad r_y = c \, (y - y_c)\]

about the centroid \((x_c, y_c)\), without curvature, \({r_y}_{,x} = {r_x}_{,y} = 0\) and \(-{r_x}_{,x} + {r_y}_{,y} = 0\), and without transverse shear strain. The bending and the transverse shear of the triangle have nine degrees-of-freedom and at most five independent constant strains, three curvatures and two transverse shear strains, so at least four of their modes have no energy, three being rigid-body motions. As the twisting mode of the one-point quadrilateral, Eq. (13) of Brockman (1987), the mode exists for a single element only: in an assembled mesh the neighbouring elements restrain it, see the Notes of pyfe3d.tria3dsg.
