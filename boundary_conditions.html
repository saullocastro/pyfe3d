Boundary conditions and prescribed displacements#

The elements assemble the matrices and vectors of all the N dofs of the model, see Overview and workflow, without any support. The supports and the prescribed displacements are imposed afterwards, by partitioning the dofs into the free ones, whose displacements are unknown, and the known ones, whose displacements are prescribed. This page gives the partition in the linear analyses, its extension to the non-linear analyses, where the prescribed displacements grow with the load factor and are imposed exactly, the reactions, and the errors that the solvers report when the supports are insufficient.

The partition of the dofs#

At each dof either the external force or the displacement is known. The Boolean array bu of size N marks the free dofs, with the subscript \(u\), and its complement bk = ~bu the known ones, with the subscript \(k\), whose prescribed displacements \(\bar{\boldsymbol{u}}_k\) are given in uk[bk]; a fixed support is the particular case \(\bar{\boldsymbol{u}}_k = \boldsymbol{0}\). The dofs are not reordered: the blocks are selected with the masks, e.g. KC0uu = KC0[bu, :][:, bu] and KC0uk = KC0[bu, :][:, bk]. Written as if the free dofs came first, the linear static system \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} \boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\) of Linear static analyses reads (Bathe 2014, Sec. 4.2.2, Eq. (4.42), with other subscripts)

(1)#\[\begin{split}\begin{bmatrix} \boldsymbol{K}_{{C0}_{uu}} & \boldsymbol{K}_{{C0}_{uk}} \\ \boldsymbol{K}_{{C0}_{ku}} & \boldsymbol{K}_{{C0}_{kk}} \end{bmatrix} \begin{Bmatrix} \boldsymbol{u}_u \\ \bar{\boldsymbol{u}}_k \end{Bmatrix} = \begin{Bmatrix} \boldsymbol{F}_{{ext}_u} \\ \boldsymbol{F}_{{ext}_k} + \boldsymbol{R}_k \end{Bmatrix}\end{split}\]

with \(\boldsymbol{F}_{{ext}_k}\) the loads applied at the known dofs and \(\boldsymbol{R}_k\) the unknown reactions. The first block row is the system of the free dofs, in which the prescribed displacements act as the equivalent forces \(-\boldsymbol{K}_{{C0}_{uk}} \bar{\boldsymbol{u}}_k\) (Eq. (4.43)), and the second gives the reactions (Eq. (4.45)):

(2)#\[\boldsymbol{K}_{{C0}_{uu}} \boldsymbol{u}_u = \boldsymbol{F}_{{ext}_u} - \boldsymbol{K}_{{C0}_{uk}} \bar{\boldsymbol{u}}_k\]
(3)#\[\boldsymbol{R}_k = \boldsymbol{K}_{{C0}_{ku}} \boldsymbol{u}_u + \boldsymbol{K}_{{C0}_{kk}} \bar{\boldsymbol{u}}_k - \boldsymbol{F}_{{ext}_k}\]

Only \(\boldsymbol{K}_{{C0}_{uu}}\) is factorized. In terms of the residual \(\boldsymbol{R} = \boldsymbol{F}_{int} - \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\), with \(\boldsymbol{F}_{int} = \boldsymbol{K}_{C0} \boldsymbol{u}\) in a linear analysis, the first row is the equilibrium \(\boldsymbol{R}_u = \boldsymbol{0}\) of the free dofs and \(\boldsymbol{R}_k\) is the residual at the known ones. In code, with the assembled KC0, fext and uk:

u = np.zeros(N)
u[bk] = uk[bk]
u[bu] = solve(KC0[bu, :][:, bu], fext[bu] - KC0[bu, :][:, bk] @ uk[bk])
Rk = KC0[bk, :] @ u - fext[bk]

where KC0[bk, :] @ u is the internal force vector at the known dofs, which the update_fint methods of the elements also give, see step 7 of Overview and workflow. The matrices of the eigenvalue analyses are partitioned in the same way and only their free-free blocks are used, see Linear buckling and natural frequencies: the eigenvalue problems are homogeneous, and the known dofs have null displacements in the buckling and vibration modes.

Prescribed displacements in the non-linear analyses#

Analysis performs the partition itself when it receives bu, and imposes the prescribed displacements uk exactly. The callables of the model then receive and return the vectors and matrices of all the N dofs, as assembled by the elements, and FreeDofs restricts them to the free dofs for the solvers. The prescribed displacements grow with the load factor \(\lambda\), \(\boldsymbol{u}_k = \lambda \bar{\boldsymbol{u}}_k\), with uk[bk] the values \(\bar{\boldsymbol{u}}_k\) at \(\lambda = 1\), such that the configuration of the full model is the null-space reduction

(4)#\[\begin{split}\boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{Z} \boldsymbol{c} + \lambda \bar{\boldsymbol{u}}_k, \qquad \boldsymbol{Z} = \begin{bmatrix} \boldsymbol{I}_{uu} \\ \boldsymbol{0}_{ku} \end{bmatrix}\end{split}\]

with \(\boldsymbol{c} = \boldsymbol{u}_u\) the unknowns of the solvers, see FreeDofs.full(), and \(\bar{\boldsymbol{u}}_k\) placed at the known dofs. The solvers see the reduced residual, its Jacobian and its derivative with respect to the load factor:

(5)#\[\boldsymbol{R}_u(\boldsymbol{c}, \lambda) = \boldsymbol{F}_{{int}_u}(\boldsymbol{u}) - \lambda \boldsymbol{F}_{{ext}_u}, \qquad \frac{\partial \boldsymbol{R}_u}{\partial \boldsymbol{c}} = \boldsymbol{K}_{T_{uu}}, \qquad -\frac{\partial \boldsymbol{R}_u}{\partial \lambda} = \boldsymbol{F}_{{ext}_u} - \boldsymbol{K}_{T_{uk}} \bar{\boldsymbol{u}}_k\]

with \(\boldsymbol{K}_T\) the tangent stiffness matrix of the full model at the current configuration and load factor, whose blocks are returned by FreeDofs.calc_kC() and FreeDofs.calc_kG(). The last vector, the out-of-balance force that a unit increment of the load factor produces at the free dofs held fixed, depends on the configuration and on the load factor. It is passed to the solvers as a load vector that depends on the load factor, calc_fext(inc=1., c=c, lbd=lbd), see Load- and configuration-dependent loads, and it is what drives an analysis under prescribed displacements alone, where \(\boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\) is null: it gives the predictor of the first Newton-Raphson increment, the scaling of the arc length and a reference of the convergence criteria, see The Newton-Raphson method, Arc-length methods and Convergence criteria. In the linear analysis, with \(\boldsymbol{K}_T = \boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\), the system solved is (2), with the right-hand side of FreeDofs.linear_fext().

The prescribed values are imposed exactly at every iteration: the corrections of the solvers vanish at the known dofs, and the norms of the convergence criteria are evaluated over the free dofs only. At a converged configuration the reactions are the residual at the known dofs,

(6)#\[\boldsymbol{R}_k = \boldsymbol{F}_{{int}_k}(\boldsymbol{u}) - \lambda \boldsymbol{F}_{{ext}_k}\]

given by the calc_fint of the full model, which reduces to (3) in a linear analysis. The solution vectors an.cs of Analysis.static() have size N, with \(\lambda \bar{\boldsymbol{u}}_k\) at the known dofs. A model without load, calc_fext returning zeros, and with a prescribed displacement is displacement controlled: the load factor then measures the prescribed displacement, and the reaction follows from (6); with the arc-length methods, the load factor, here the prescribed displacement, may also decrease along the path. tests/test_dome_prescribed_displacement.py traces with Riks’ method the reaction of two shells pressed down by a deflection prescribed at their crown.

The same reduction applies to any linear constraint \(\boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{Z} \boldsymbol{c} + \boldsymbol{u}_{0} + \lambda \boldsymbol{u}_{1}\), e.g. symmetry conditions or a restriction of a model to some of its modes, implemented by the user in the callables, see pyfe3d.solver.callbacks; FreeDofs is the case of \(\boldsymbol{Z}\) made of columns of the identity matrix.

Without bu, the callables return the vectors and matrices of the free dofs only, and the partition is done by the user; uk then cannot be given.

Errors of the supports#

The solvers of pyfe3d.solver do not remove null rows and columns. A dof left without stiffness, e.g. an unattached node, an unstiffened drilling rotation or a missing boundary condition, is a modelling error that makes the matrix singular, and it raises a numpy.linalg.LinAlgError naming the dofs, see check_null_rows_cols(). Insufficient supports that leave a rigid-body mode free, a mechanism, or distorted elements, e.g. re-entrant quadrilaterals, leave \(\boldsymbol{K}_{{C0}_{uu}}\) with a null or a negative eigenvalue. For a symmetric matrix this is detected from the signs of the pivots of its factorization, by Sylvester’s law of inertia (is_positive_definite()), and linear_static(), the linear Analysis.static() and linear_buckling() raise a numpy.linalg.LinAlgError that names these likely causes, see Linear static analyses. A singular matrix whose round-off pivots all come out positive is not detected by this test.

A structure without supports, e.g. in flight, has a singular \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\) by nature. It is solved with inertia relief, whose reference support is a set of prescribed null displacements chosen to suppress the rigid-body modes without restraining any deformation, see Inertia relief.
