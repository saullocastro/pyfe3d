Load- and configuration-dependent loads#

A dead load keeps its magnitude and direction during the deformation. A follower load does not: a pressure normal to the deformed surface and acting on the deformed area, a force that follows the rotation of the node it is applied to, or the aerodynamic load of the piston theory. The load vector then depends on the configuration, its derivative adds the load stiffness matrix to the tangent stiffness matrix, and the arc-length methods must follow the load vector of the current configuration. A load vector may also depend on the load factor, which is how prescribed displacements are imposed exactly. This page gives the theory and how such loads are passed to Analysis, through the optional keyword arguments of pyfe3d.solver.callbacks.

The load stiffness matrix#

With a load \(\lambda \boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{u})\) proportional to the load factor, the residual and its derivatives are

(1)#\[\boldsymbol{R}(\boldsymbol{u}, \lambda) = \boldsymbol{F}_{int}(\boldsymbol{u}) - \lambda \boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{u}), \qquad \frac{\partial \boldsymbol{R}}{\partial \boldsymbol{u}} = \boldsymbol{K}_C + \boldsymbol{K}_G + \boldsymbol{K}_F, \qquad -\frac{\partial \boldsymbol{R}}{\partial \lambda} = \boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{u})\]

with the load stiffness matrix \(\boldsymbol{K}_F = -\lambda \, \partial \boldsymbol{F}/\partial \boldsymbol{u} = \lambda \boldsymbol{K}_{F_{ref}}\), of the sign that makes a load growing along the displacement it produces reduce the tangent stiffness. The integrand of \(\boldsymbol{K}_F\) has the interpolation of the virtual displacements on one side and the derivative of the load, e.g. through the slopes of the loaded surface, on the other, and \(\boldsymbol{K}_F\) is symmetric only when the load derives from a potential, i.e. when \(\partial F_i/\partial u_j = \partial F_j/\partial u_i\).

For a pressure normal to the deformed surface, Hibbitt (1979, p. 938) splits the load stiffness of a uniform pressure into a symmetric surface integral and an antisymmetric integral along the boundary of the loaded surface, which vanishes where the displacements are prescribed: a uniform pressure on a closed surface, or on a surface whose boundaries have prescribed displacements, gives a symmetric \(\boldsymbol{K}_F\), whereas free edges, where the pressure changes, and a non-uniform pressure give unsymmetric terms (p. 939). In Table 2 of Schweizerhof and Ramm (1984), the domain terms are symmetric for a uniform pressure, and for a non-uniform one only when the pressure field is fixed in space, and the boundary terms vanish under one of the conditions of their Sec. 3.2.1, Eqs. (32)-(35): the pressure is zero on the whole boundary; at least two displacement components are prescribed at the edge; the edge is restrained normal to a continuous surface, e.g. the undeformed surface, \(w = 0\) for a plate on the \(XY\) plane; or the boundary terms add up to zero.

pyfe3d does not symmetrise \(\boldsymbol{K}_F\). The solvers factorize the tangent stiffness matrix with a sparse LU factorization, which accepts unsymmetric matrices, such that a consistent \(\boldsymbol{K}_F\) keeps the quadratic convergence of the Newton-Raphson method, verified by test_newton_raphson_quadratic_with_follower_load of tests/test_solver_follower_loads.py. The symmetric part of \(\boldsymbol{K}_F\) would lead to the same converged solution, controlled by the residual alone, with a linear convergence.

Passing the load to the solvers#

The callables of Analysis may declare three optional keyword arguments, which the solvers pass only to the callables that declare them explicitly in their signature, see pyfe3d.solver.callbacks.accepts_kwarg(); a **kwargs does not count, and the simplest callables are called with the minimum set of arguments:

  • inc, the current load factor, passed to calc_fint, calc_kC and calc_kG, see pyfe3d.solver.callbacks.calc_fint() and pyfe3d.solver.callbacks.calc_kT(). calc_fint(c, inc=lbd) then contains the configuration-dependent part of the load, \(-\lambda (\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{c}) - \boldsymbol{F}_{ext})\), with \(\boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\) the vector of calc_fext(inc=1.), and calc_kC + calc_kG its derivative, the load stiffness matrix \(\boldsymbol{K}_F\). The residual that the solvers form, calc_fint(c, inc=lbd) - lbd*calc_fext(inc=1.), is then (1).

  • c, the current configuration, passed to calc_fext, which then returns the load vector of that configuration for a unit load factor, \(\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{c}) = -\partial \boldsymbol{R}/\partial \lambda\), see pyfe3d.solver.callbacks.calc_load_vector() and pyfe3d.solver.callbacks.is_load_configuration_dependent(). The arc-length methods use it in the predictor and in the bordered system of each iteration, see Arc-length methods, and the linear analysis uses it in its load correction iterations, see Linear static analyses.

  • lbd, the current load factor, passed to calc_fext together with c, for a vector \(-\partial \boldsymbol{R}/\partial \lambda\) that also depends on the load factor, see pyfe3d.solver.callbacks.is_load_factor_dependent(). inc keeps its meaning, the factor that multiplies the returned vector. The vector is then evaluated together with the tangent stiffness matrix, at the same configuration and load factor, right after calc_kC and calc_kG, such that the callables may share their computations; its linear solution at \(\boldsymbol{c} = \boldsymbol{0}\), \(\lambda = 0\) gives the predictor of the first Newton-Raphson increment and the scaling of the arc length, and \(\lambda\) times it is a reference of the convergence criteria, see Convergence criteria. The linear analysis never passes lbd.

With the elements, inc and c are the arguments of a follower load; lbd is used by FreeDofs for the prescribed displacements.

Prescribed displacements as a load-factor-dependent load#

A linear constraint imposed exactly, \(\boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{Z} \boldsymbol{c} + \boldsymbol{u}_0 + \lambda \boldsymbol{u}_1\), gives the reduced residual and its derivative with respect to the load factor

(2)#\[\boldsymbol{R}_r(\boldsymbol{c}, \lambda) = \boldsymbol{Z}^\top \left( \boldsymbol{F}_{int}(\boldsymbol{Z} \boldsymbol{c} + \boldsymbol{u}_0 + \lambda \boldsymbol{u}_1) - \lambda \boldsymbol{F}_{ext} \right), \qquad -\frac{\partial \boldsymbol{R}_r}{\partial \lambda} = \boldsymbol{Z}^\top \left( \boldsymbol{F}_{ext} - \boldsymbol{K}_T \boldsymbol{u}_1 \right)\]

with \(\boldsymbol{K}_T\) at the current configuration and load factor: the vector that calc_fext(inc=1., c=c, lbd=lbd) returns. Under a prescribed displacement alone, the reference vector \(\boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\) and the reduced internal force vector both vanish at equilibrium, and only this vector gives the solvers a scale. It is not the reaction: it acts on the free dofs, whereas the reaction is the residual of the full model at the constrained dofs. FreeDofs implements the case of \(\boldsymbol{Z}\) made of columns of the identity, \(\boldsymbol{u}_0 = \boldsymbol{0}\) and \(\boldsymbol{u}_1 = \bar{\boldsymbol{u}}_k\), see Boundary conditions and prescribed displacements; tests/test_solver_load_factor_dependent_load.py checks a general constraint on hardening springs in series against their exact solution.

Follower loads of pyfe3d#

  • The aerodynamic load of the piston theory, \(\lambda \beta \boldsymbol{K}_{A_\beta} \boldsymbol{u}\), assembled with update_KA_beta of Quad4 and Quad4R, is linear in the configuration and gives the constant, unsymmetric load stiffness \(-\beta \boldsymbol{K}_{A_\beta}\) per unit load factor, see test_piston_theory_load_unsymmetric_tangent of tests/test_solver_nonlinear_static.py; its stability is studied with the eigenvalue analyses of Linear buckling and natural frequencies.

  • The concentrated moments of CorotationalModel.add_moment() with kind='fixed_axes', about the fixed global axes, which are non-conservative and give an unsymmetric tangent, or kind='follower', rotating with the nodal triad, depend on the configuration and enter calc_fint, calc_kG and calc_fext(c=...) of the model as described above; the moments in the attribute fext of the model, conjugate to the rotation vectors, are constant.

  • A pressure or any other follower load of the user enters the callables in the same way; when it is affine in the displacements, \(\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{u}) = \boldsymbol{F}_0 - \boldsymbol{K}_{F_{ref}} \boldsymbol{u}\), its load stiffness is constant and the geometrically linear problem is the unsymmetric linear system of Linear static analyses.

In a linear buckling analysis, the load stiffness of the reference load adds to the geometric stiffness matrix, and a non-symmetric \(\boldsymbol{K}_F\) may lead to flutter instead of divergence, see Linear buckling and natural frequencies.
