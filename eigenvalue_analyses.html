Linear buckling and natural frequencies#

The eigenvalue analyses of pyfe3d linearize the behaviour of the structure about a reference state: the linear buckling analysis seeks the load multipliers at which the stiffness of a linear pre-buckling state vanishes, the natural frequency analysis the free vibrations about a state at rest, possibly pre-stressed, and the complex frequency analysis the free vibrations of a damped system. They use the free-free blocks of the assembled matrices, e.g. KC0uu = KC0[bu, :][:, bu], see Boundary conditions and prescribed displacements, and are solved with linear_buckling(), natural_frequency() and complex_frequency() of pyfe3d.solver.eigen, which return the eigenvectors with the size of the matrices received, to be expanded by the user, u[bu] = eigvecs[:, i]. The algorithms that solve and verify them are described in Eigenvalue solvers and their verification.

Linear buckling#

At a stable equilibrium the second variation of the total potential energy is positive for every variation of the configuration; the neutral equilibrium criterion, \(\delta^2 V = \delta \boldsymbol{u}^\top \boldsymbol{K}_T \, \delta \boldsymbol{u} = 0\) for at least one \(\delta \boldsymbol{u} \ne \boldsymbol{0}\), marks the loss of stability, where the tangent stiffness matrix of Non-linear static analyses becomes singular. The linear buckling analysis takes a linear pre-buckling state: the solution \(\boldsymbol{u}_0\) of the linear static system of a reference load, \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} \boldsymbol{u}_0 = \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\), with the pre-buckling rotations neglected, such that \(\boldsymbol{K}_{CNL} = \boldsymbol{0}\) and \(\boldsymbol{K}_{GNL} = \boldsymbol{0}\). The geometric stiffness matrix of that state, KG, is linear in its stresses, hence in the load, and the tangent stiffness matrix along the pre-buckling path is \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + \lambda \boldsymbol{K}_G\). It is singular at the load multipliers of

(1)#\[(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + \lambda \boldsymbol{K}_G) \boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{0}\]

whose lowest positive eigenvalue \(\lambda_1\) gives the critical load \(\lambda_1 \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\) and whose eigenvector is the buckling mode. The analysis has three steps:

  1. the linear static solution \(\boldsymbol{u}_0\) of the reference load, see Linear static analyses;

  2. the geometric stiffness matrix of \(\boldsymbol{u}_0\): for each element, update_probe_xe(x), update_probe_ue(u0) and update_KG(KGr, KGc, KGv, prop); for the shells, a known constant membrane state can be given instead with update_KG_given_stress(Nxx, Nyy, Nxy, KGr, KGc, KGv), with the stress resultants in the element coordinate system of each element, see Conventions;

  3. the eigenvalue problem (1) of the free dofs, eigvals, eigvecs = linear_buckling(KC0uu, KGuu, num_eigvalues=...).

linear_buckling() returns the positive load multipliers first, in increasing order, followed, when fewer than num_eigvalues positive ones exist, by the negative ones closest to zero, which are the critical load multipliers of the reversed reference load: a tensile reference load, which has no positive multiplier, thus gives the critical multipliers of the compressive one. The infinite load multipliers, e.g. of dofs where KG has null rows, are discarded by the sparse solvers of symmetric matrices, and returned as -inf, last, by the dense solvers.

Preloads and combined loads. A load that is not scaled by \(\lambda\), e.g. a preload with the geometric stiffness matrix \(\boldsymbol{K}_{G0}\), belongs to the first matrix, since the geometric stiffness matrix is linear in the stresses and the stresses of a linear pre-buckling state add up:

(2)#\[(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + \boldsymbol{K}_{G0} + \lambda \boldsymbol{K}_G) \boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{0}\]

solved with linear_buckling(KC0uu + KG0uu, KGuu), which is how the critical value of one load component is found with the other components kept constant, e.g. the compressive load of a panel under a given shear load. A preload beyond its own buckling load makes the first matrix indefinite, which linear_buckling() reports, see Eigenvalue solvers and their verification.

Elements. The beams have update_KG for the axial force of the pre-buckling state and take the slopes of their geometric stiffness matrices from the interpolations of the transverse displacements, such that the buckling load includes the reduction due to shear deformation, Eq. (2-57) of Timoshenko and Gere (1961, Sec. 2.17, p. 133), see BeamC and BeamLR; Truss and Spring have no geometric stiffness matrix. The integration of the geometric stiffness matrices of the shells, the slopes at the centroid of the elements with reduced transverse shear and the eccentricity term of the rigid offsets of warped quadrilaterals are described with each element, see Quad4.update_KG(), Quad4R.update_KG() and Choosing an element.

Limits. The linear pre-buckling state neglects the pre-buckling rotations and the change of the stresses along the path. The critical load is that of a bifurcation from the linear path; a structure whose pre-buckling path is markedly non-linear, or that is sensitive to imperfections, has a limit load that is traced with the non-linear analyses of Non-linear static analyses and Arc-length methods. A geometric stiffness matrix built at a converged non-linear state, calc_kG(c, NLgeom=True), linearizes the stability about that state instead.

Follower loads and flutter#

With a configuration-dependent load, the load stiffness matrix of the reference load, \(\boldsymbol{K}_{F_{ref}}\), see Load- and configuration-dependent loads, adds to the geometric stiffness matrix:

(3)#\[\left(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + \lambda (\boldsymbol{K}_G + \boldsymbol{K}_{F_{ref}})\right) \boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{0}\]

an unsymmetric problem when \(\boldsymbol{K}_{F_{ref}}\) is, whose load multipliers may be complex; linear_buckling() detects the unsymmetric matrices and uses its solvers of unsymmetric matrices, which find the lowest positive real load multiplier, the divergence load. A non-conservative system may lose its stability by flutter, a dynamic instability that this static criterion does not detect: Ziegler’s double pendulum under a follower force has no finite load multiplier, its stiffness matrix being singular for no value of the load, and loses its stability by flutter, see tests/test_solver_linear_buckling.py. Its stability is studied with the dynamic method below. For a uniform pressure on a closed surface, or on a surface whose edges satisfy the conditions of Load- and configuration-dependent loads, \(\boldsymbol{K}_{F_{ref}}\) is symmetric and the static criterion applies.

Natural frequencies#

The free vibrations \(\boldsymbol{u} \sin(\omega_n t)\) about a state at rest give the generalized eigenvalue problem

(4)#\[(\boldsymbol{K} - \omega_n^2 \boldsymbol{M}) \boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{0}\]

solved by omegan, eigvecs = natural_frequency(Kuu, Muu, num_eigvalues=...), which returns the circular frequencies \(\omega_n\), in rad/s with consistent units, in increasing order. \(\boldsymbol{K}\) is \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\), plus the geometric stiffness matrix of a pre-stress when it applies, e.g. KC0uu + KGuu: a compressive pre-stress lowers the frequencies, and the lowest one vanishes at the critical load multiplier of (1), see tests/test_solver_natural_frequency.py. A pre-stress beyond the buckling load gives negative \(\omega_n^2\), returned as null frequencies. The mass matrix is assembled with update_M, whose mtype selects the consistent, lumped or reduced-integration mass matrix of each element, see Choosing an element. A lumped mass matrix without rotary inertia is singular: its massless dofs have infinite eigenvalues, which the solvers, seeking the lowest ones, are not troubled by, but which the lumped matrix places where its factorization puts them, so a consistent mass matrix is preferable when the lowest frequencies matter. The unconstrained rigid-body modes of a free structure give null frequencies.

Dynamic stability. With \(\boldsymbol{K}\) the tangent stiffness matrix at a load level, including the load stiffness of a follower load, e.g. with the aerodynamic stiffness matrix of the piston theory, \(\boldsymbol{K}\) is unsymmetric and the eigenvalues \(\omega_n^2\) may become complex; omegan is then the principal square root of the complex eigenvalues. Below the flutter load the \(\omega_n^2\) are real and positive; at the flutter load two frequencies coalesce, a double root of the frequency equation, and above it they become a complex conjugate pair, one of which corresponds to a growing motion. This is the dynamic method of Bolotin (1963, Introduction, Secs. 6 and 7, pp. 12-17), where \(\omega_n\) is the frequency \(\Omega\) of the characteristic exponent \(s = i\Omega\); a real negative \(\omega_n^2\), i.e. a real positive \(s\), indicates divergence (p. 17). The flutter load of Ziegler’s double pendulum and the frequencies below it are checked against their analytical values in tests/test_solver_natural_frequency.py.

Damped frequencies#

With a damping matrix \(\boldsymbol{C}\), the free vibrations \(\boldsymbol{u} e^{\lambda t}\) of \(\boldsymbol{M} \ddot{\boldsymbol{q}} + \boldsymbol{C} \dot{\boldsymbol{q}} + \boldsymbol{K} \boldsymbol{q} = \boldsymbol{0}\) give the quadratic eigenvalue problem

(5)#\[(\lambda^2 \boldsymbol{M} + \lambda \boldsymbol{C} + \boldsymbol{K}) \boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{0}\]

solved by eigvals, eigvecs = complex_frequency(Kuu, Cuu, Muu, num_eigvalues=...), which returns the complex eigenvalues \(\lambda\), one per complex conjugate pair, with \(\mathrm{Im}(\lambda) \ge 0\), and the complex eigenvectors. With \(\lambda = -\zeta \omega_n \pm i \omega_d\), the damped circular frequency is \(\omega_d = |\mathrm{Im}(\lambda)|\), abs(eigvals.imag), and the damping ratio \(\zeta = -\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)/|\lambda|\), -eigvals.real/abs(eigvals): a single dof, \(m \lambda^2 + c \lambda + k = 0\), has the roots \(\lambda = -\zeta \omega_n \pm i \omega_n \sqrt{1 - \zeta^2}\) for \(\zeta < 1\), with \(\omega_n = \sqrt{k/m} = |\lambda|\) and \(\zeta = c/(2\sqrt{k m})\). A real \(\lambda\), e.g. of an overdamped mode, gives \(\omega_d = 0\), and a positive \(\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)\) a growing motion, i.e. an unstable system, by flutter when \(\lambda\) is complex. With \(\boldsymbol{C} = \boldsymbol{0}\), \(\lambda = i \omega_n\), with the \(\omega_n\) of natural_frequency(). This \(\zeta\) is not the \(\zeta\) of the convergence criterion of the non-linear analyses.

The damping matrix is assembled by the user, e.g. Rayleigh damping \(\boldsymbol{C} = \alpha \boldsymbol{M} + \beta \boldsymbol{K}\), or the aerodynamic damping of the piston theory, Cuu = -mu*CAuu with update_CA of Quad4 and Quad4R, together with the aerodynamic stiffness in Kuu = KC0uu - beta*KA_betauu - gamma*KA_gammauu, see update_KA_beta. A flutter analysis then follows the largest \(\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)\) over the flow speed: the aerodynamic damping moves the flutter onset beyond the coalescence of the frequencies of natural_frequency(), see tests/test_quad4_piston_theory.py. C may also contain gyroscopic terms; K, C and M may be unsymmetric.
