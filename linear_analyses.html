Linear static analyses#

A linear static analysis assumes small displacements and rotations, a linear elastic material and loads that do not change with the deformation. The equilibrium is then a single linear system, solved in one step, which is also the first iteration of every non-linear analysis. This page derives the system, describes how pyfe3d solves it and checks its matrix, and treats the case of a load that depends on the configuration of a structure that otherwise remains linear.

The linear static system#

The total potential energy of an elastic body under dead loads, \(V = U - W_{ext}\), is stationary at equilibrium, \(\delta V = \delta U - \delta W_{ext} = 0\). With the linear strains of the elements, \(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} = \boldsymbol{B}_L \boldsymbol{u}\), the stresses \(\boldsymbol{\sigma} = \boldsymbol{C} \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\) and the displacement field interpolated from the nodal dofs \(\boldsymbol{u}\), the first variation must vanish for every \(\delta \boldsymbol{u}\), which gives

(1)#\[\boldsymbol{R} = \boldsymbol{F}_{int} - \boldsymbol{F}_{ext} = \boldsymbol{K}_{C0} \boldsymbol{u} - \boldsymbol{F}_{ext} = \boldsymbol{0}, \qquad \boldsymbol{K}_{C0} = \int_{\Omega} \boldsymbol{B}_L^\top \boldsymbol{C} \boldsymbol{B}_L \, d\Omega\]

with \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\) the linear constitutive stiffness matrix, KC0, assembled with the update_KC0 methods of the elements, see Overview and workflow, and \(\boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\) the external force vector, assembled by the user. The residual \(\boldsymbol{R}\) keeps the sign convention of the non-linear analyses, \(\boldsymbol{R} = \boldsymbol{F}_{int} - \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\), whose Jacobian is the tangent stiffness matrix, here \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\), see Non-linear static analyses. The linear internal force vector of the elements, update_fint(..., nonlinear=0), is \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} \boldsymbol{u}\).

\(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\) of an unsupported structure is symmetric and positive semi-definite, with the rigid-body modes in its null space. The supports are imposed by the partition of Boundary conditions and prescribed displacements, which gives the system of the free dofs, Eq. (2),

(2)#\[\boldsymbol{K}_{{C0}_{uu}} \boldsymbol{u}_u = \boldsymbol{F}_{{ext}_u} - \boldsymbol{K}_{{C0}_{uk}} \bar{\boldsymbol{u}}_k\]

whose matrix is positive definite when the supports suppress every rigid-body mode (Bathe 2014, Sec. 8.2.5, pp. 726-727), and the reactions, Eq. (3). A structure that has no supports is solved with inertia relief, see Inertia relief.

Constant terms that are not proportional to the displacements of the analysis belong to the matrix: e.g. the geometric stiffness matrix of a known preload, KC0uu + KG0uu, see Linear buckling and natural frequencies, or the aerodynamic stiffness matrix of the piston theory of Quad4 and Quad4R; a follower load affine in the displacements, \(\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{u}) = \boldsymbol{F}_0 - \boldsymbol{K}_F \boldsymbol{u}\), gives the unsymmetric system \((\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + \boldsymbol{K}_F) \boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{F}_0\), see linear_static() and Load- and configuration-dependent loads.

Solvers#

Three functions solve the system (2):

  • solve() solves a sparse system with the LU factorization of scipy.sparse.linalg.splu(), symmetric or not, with a real or complex right-hand side, and raises a numpy.linalg.LinAlgError for a null row or column, see check_null_rows_cols(), or for an exactly singular matrix, where scipy.sparse.linalg.spsolve() would return NaN values;

  • linear_static() solves K @ u = fext of the free dofs, after checking that a symmetric K is positive definite, and returns [1.] and [u], the outputs of Analysis.static();

  • Analysis.static() with NLgeom=False solves the same system from the callables of a model, calc_kC() and calc_fext(), see Non-linear static analyses; with Analysis(..., bu=bu, uk=uk) the callables work with all the N dofs, the right-hand side of (2) is formed by FreeDofs.linear_fext(), and cs[0] has size N, with uk[bk] at the known dofs.

The check of positive definiteness costs nothing: a symmetric matrix is factorized once with a symmetric permutation and without row interchanges, \(\boldsymbol{\Pi} \boldsymbol{K} \boldsymbol{\Pi}^\top = \boldsymbol{L} \boldsymbol{D} \boldsymbol{L}^\top\), whose number of negative pivots in \(\boldsymbol{D}\) is the number of negative eigenvalues of \(\boldsymbol{K}\) by Sylvester’s law of inertia, see is_positive_definite(). When all the pivots are positive, the same factorization, then that of Cholesky up to the scaling of the rows, gives the solution, see pyfe3d.solver.static._solve_linear_static(). A negative or a zero pivot raises a numpy.linalg.LinAlgError naming the likely causes: insufficient boundary conditions, a mechanism, distorted elements, e.g. re-entrant quadrilaterals, or a preload beyond the buckling load included in the matrix. An unsymmetric matrix is solved with solve() without this check.

The factorization is the cost of the analysis; the reactions, Eq. (3), and the element forces of the post-processing, update_probe_ue and update_fint of the elements, are products by the assembled matrices or loops over the elements.

Loads that depend on the configuration#

A load whose direction or magnitude follows the deformation, e.g. a pressure normal to the deformed surface, makes the equilibrium of a structure that is otherwise linear, \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} \boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}(\boldsymbol{u})\), a non-linear system. When calc_fext declares the keyword argument c, see Load- and configuration-dependent loads, Analysis.static() with NLgeom=False solves it with the load correction iterations

(3)#\[\boldsymbol{u}_{j+1} = \boldsymbol{u}_j + \boldsymbol{K}_{C0}^{-1} \left( \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}(\boldsymbol{u}_j) - \boldsymbol{K}_{C0} \boldsymbol{u}_j \right), \qquad \boldsymbol{u}_0 = \boldsymbol{K}_{C0}^{-1} \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\]

which start from the solution of the reference load calc_fext() and reuse one factorization of \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\), until the convergence criterion of the non-linear solvers is satisfied, see Convergence criteria, or maxNumIter iterations are done; a warning is printed and the last iterate returned when they do not converge. This is a fixed-point iteration without the load stiffness in the iteration matrix. For a load affine in the displacements, \(\boldsymbol{F}_{ext}(\boldsymbol{u}) = \boldsymbol{F}_0 - \boldsymbol{K}_F \boldsymbol{u}\), its limit is the solution of \((\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + \boldsymbol{K}_F) \boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{F}_0\), and the error is multiplied at each iteration by \(-\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}^{-1} \boldsymbol{K}_F\), whose spectral radius is \(1/\min|\lambda|\) over the load multipliers of \((\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + \lambda \boldsymbol{K}_F) \boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{0}\), negative and complex ones included: the iterations converge, linearly, below the load of the smallest \(|\lambda|\) and diverge beyond it, see Analysis.static(). For an affine load whose \(\boldsymbol{K}_F\) is known, solving the unsymmetric system with linear_static() avoids the iterations; with geometric non-linearity, the load stiffness enters the tangent stiffness matrix instead, see Load- and configuration-dependent loads.

Verification#

tests/test_solver_static.py checks solve() and linear_static() against exact solutions of small systems and the deflection of a Ritz plate against the Navier solution of Timoshenko and Woinowsky-Krieger (1959), Art. 29; tests/test_prescribed_displacement_linear.py checks a cantilever with a prescribed tip deflection, and tests/test_solver_analysis.py and tests/test_solver_follower_loads.py the linear analysis of Analysis with prescribed displacements and with a configuration-dependent load.
