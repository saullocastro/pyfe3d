Beam theory and the beam elements#

A beam model replaces the three-dimensional analysis of a slender member by a one-dimensional problem along a reference axis, in which the cross section enters only through a few sectional properties. The reduction holds when the dimensions of the cross section are small compared with the length of the member and with the length over which the deformation varies along it; it does not hold near clamped ends, concentrated loads and abrupt changes of section, nor when the walls of the cross section deform or buckle locally, where a shell model is needed.

pyfe3d has two Timoshenko beam elements, BeamC and BeamLR, which share the kinematics, the sectional stiffness matrix of a BeamProp and the degrees of freedom, and differ in the interpolation along the element. This chapter derives the beam model they implement, the two discretizations, their mass, geometric and non-linear stiffness matrices, and closes with the choice between them. The cross-section properties are detailed in Beam properties, the truss and the spring in Truss and spring elements, the computation of the sectional stiffness matrix of an arbitrary cross section in Cross-sectional analysis of beams, and the large rotations of beams in Co-rotational formulation for large rotations.

Element coordinates and degrees of freedom#

The element coordinate system has its \(x\) axis from node 1 to node 2, the beam axis, and its \(y\) axis in the plane of \(x\) and of the vector vxyi, vxyj, vxyk given to update_rotation_matrix; the cross section lies in the \(y z\) plane, see Conventions. Each of the two nodes has the six degrees of freedom of every node of pyfe3d, and the element displacements, in element coordinates, are ordered as

(1)#\[\boldsymbol{u}_e = \{u_1, v_1, w_1, {r_x}_1, {r_y}_1, {r_z}_1, u_2, v_2, w_2, {r_x}_2, {r_y}_2, {r_z}_2\}^\top\]

the attribute ue of BeamCProbe and BeamLRProbe. The element length \(L\) is the attribute length, and \(\xi = x/L\) is the coordinate along the element.

Kinematics of the rigid cross section#

The cross section translates and rotates as a rigid body. With \(u, v, w\) the translations and \(r_x, r_y, r_z\) the rotations of the beam axis, right-handed about the element axes, the displacements of the point \((y, z)\) of the cross section are

(2)#\[u_x = u - y r_z + z r_y, \qquad u_y = v - z r_x, \qquad u_z = w + y r_x\]

Linear strains of the three-dimensional body, with the in-plane strains of the cross section zero by assumption, give

(3)#\[\epsilon_{xx} = u_{,x} + y \kappa_y + z \kappa_z, \qquad \gamma_{xy} = \gamma_{xy}^0 - z \kappa_x, \qquad \gamma_{xz} = \gamma_{xz}^0 + y \kappa_x\]

with the six generalized strains of the beam axis

(4)#\[\begin{split}\boldsymbol{\epsilon} = \begin{Bmatrix} \bar{\varepsilon} \\ \bar{\gamma}_y \\ \bar{\gamma}_z \\ \kappa_x \\ \kappa_y \\ \kappa_z \end{Bmatrix} = \begin{Bmatrix} u_{,x} \\ v_{,x} - r_z \\ w_{,x} + r_y \\ {r_x}_{,x} \\ -{r_z}_{,x} \\ {r_y}_{,x} \end{Bmatrix}\end{split}\]

the axial strain, the transverse shear strains \(\gamma_{xy}^0 = \bar{\gamma}_y\) and \(\gamma_{xz}^0 = \bar{\gamma}_z\) of the planes \(x y\) and \(x z\), the twist rate and the two bending curvatures. The curvatures are named after the coordinate they multiply in the axial strain, as in Luo (2008), not after the axis of the rotation they come from: \(\kappa_y\) is the curvature of the bending in the \(x y\) plane, governed by \(v\) and \(r_z\), and \(\kappa_z\) that of the bending in the \(x z\) plane, governed by \(w\) and \(r_y\). A positive \(\kappa_y\) stretches the fibres at \(y > 0\). The torsion is that of Saint-Venant, uniform and without restrained warping; the warping of the cross section enters only through the torsional stiffness, see Beam properties.

Sectional stiffness matrix and strain energy#

The strain energy of the element is the integral along the axis of a quadratic form of the generalized strains,

(5)#\[U = \frac{1}{2} \int_0^L \boldsymbol{\epsilon}^\top \boldsymbol{C} \boldsymbol{\epsilon} \, dx, \qquad \boldsymbol{F} = \boldsymbol{C} \boldsymbol{\epsilon} = \{N, V_y, V_z, M_x, M_z, M_y\}^\top\]

where \(\boldsymbol{C}\) is the \(6 \times 6\) sectional stiffness matrix, the attribute C of BeamProp, symmetric with 21 independent entries \(C_{ij}\), \(i, j = 0, \ldots, 5\), in the order of \(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\), and \(\boldsymbol{F}\) the work-conjugate sectional forces: the axial force, the two shear forces, the twisting moment and the bending moments \(M_z = \int \sigma_{xx} y \, dA\) and \(M_y = \int \sigma_{xx} z \, dA\), both positive when they stretch the fibres at positive \(y\) or \(z\). Integrating the strain energy density of the rigid cross section, with Eq. (3), gives the matrix of the 11 cross-section attributes of BeamProp (see Beam properties):

(6)#\[\begin{split}\boldsymbol{C} = \begin{bmatrix} E A & 0 & 0 & 0 & E A_y & E A_z \\ 0 & G_{12} A & 0 & -G_{12} A_z & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & G_{13} A & G_{13} A_y & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & -G_{12} A_z & G_{13} A_y & G J & 0 & 0 \\ E A_y & 0 & 0 & 0 & E I_{zz} & E I_{yz} \\ E A_z & 0 & 0 & 0 & E I_{yz} & E I_{yy} \end{bmatrix}\end{split}\]

with \(A_y = \int y \, dA\) and \(A_z = \int z \, dA\) zero when the axis passes through the centroid, and \(I_{yz} = 0\) for principal axes. The rigid section gives \(G_{12} I_{yy} + G_{13} I_{zz}\) for the torsional entry, which is exact only for circular sections; it is replaced by the torsional stiffness \(G J\) (GJ) given by the user. Equation (6) has 11 parameters: the extension-shear, extension-twist, shear-shear, bending-shear and bending-twist couplings of an anisotropic or non-homogeneous section are given by setting C itself, e.g. with beamprop_from_vabs().

The elements apply no shear correction. In the rigid section the transverse shear stress is uniform, and the shear stiffnesses are \(G A\), against the \(k_y G A\) and \(k_z G A\) of the actual, non-uniform, distributions, with the shear correction factors \(k_y\), \(k_z\), e.g. \(5/6\) for a rectangle. The user applies them to the moduli, \(G_{12} = k_y G\) and \(G_{13} = k_z G\), which leaves GJ unchanged; for an arbitrary section the cross-sectional analysis of pyfe3d.vabs gives the shear entries, the torsional stiffness and the couplings without assumed factors.

Equilibrium of an element loaded at its nodes#

Stationarity of the total potential energy with Eq. (5), for a prismatic element without distributed loads, gives the equilibrium equations of the sectional forces,

(7)#\[N_{,x} = {V_y}_{,x} = {V_z}_{,x} = {M_x}_{,x} = 0, \qquad {M_z}_{,x} = V_y, \qquad {M_y}_{,x} = V_z\]

i.e. constant axial force, shear forces and twisting moment, and linear bending moments. For a diagonal \(\boldsymbol{C}\), the bending in the \(x y\) plane reduces to \((C_{11} \bar{\gamma}_y)_{,x} = 0\) and \(C_{11} \bar{\gamma}_y = C_{44} {\kappa_y}_{,x}\), i.e. \(G_{12} A \bar{\gamma}_y = E I_{zz} {\kappa_y}_{,x}\), and likewise for the \(x z\) plane with \(C_{22}\) and \(C_{55}\). An interpolation that solves these equations for any nodal values makes one element exact for a prismatic member loaded at its nodes; this is the principle of BeamC.

The ratio of the shear to the bending flexibility of an element of length \(L\) is measured by

(8)#\[\alpha_y = -\frac{12 C_{44}}{C_{11} L^2}, \qquad \alpha_z = -\frac{12 C_{55}}{C_{22} L^2}\]

negative, i.e. \(-12 E I_{zz}/(G_{12} A L^2)\) and \(-12 E I_{yy}/(G_{13} A L^2)\) for Eq. (6). With the radii of gyration \(i_z = \sqrt{I_{zz}/A}\) and \(i_y = \sqrt{I_{yy}/A}\), \(|\alpha_y| = 12 (E/G_{12}) (i_z/L)^2\): the shear deformation decays with the square of the slenderness, and the Euler-Bernoulli beam, \(\bar{\gamma}_y = \bar{\gamma}_z = 0\), is its limit \(\alpha_y = \alpha_z = 0\). For a rectangle of height \(h\) along \(z\) and an isotropic material, \(|\alpha_z| = 2 (1 + \nu) (h/L)^2\), about 0.026 for \(L/h = 10\) and \(\nu = 0.3\). The shear deformation matters for short members, sandwich and composite sections with low transverse shear moduli, and the higher vibration modes, whose half-wave length takes the place of \(L\).

BeamC: consistent shape functions#

Uncoupled section. When the only coupling of \(\boldsymbol{C}\) is the extension-twist entry \(C_{03}\), e.g. without C and with Ay, Az and Iyz zero, BeamC uses the consistent shape functions of Luo (2008): \(u\) and \(r_x\) linear, and, with \(\beta_y = 1/(1 - \alpha_y)\),

(9)#\[v = \left[1 + \beta_y (2\xi^3 - 3\xi^2 + \alpha_y \xi)\right] v_1 + L \beta_y \left(\xi^3 + \frac{\alpha_y - 4}{2} \xi^2 + \left(1 - \frac{\alpha_y}{2}\right) \xi \right) {r_z}_1 + \beta_y (-2\xi^3 + 3\xi^2 - \alpha_y \xi) v_2 + L \beta_y \left(\xi^3 - \frac{\alpha_y + 2}{2} \xi^2 + \frac{\alpha_y}{2} \xi \right) {r_z}_2\]
(10)#\[r_z = \frac{6 \beta_y}{L} (\xi^2 - \xi) (v_1 - v_2) + \left[1 + \beta_y (3\xi^2 + (\alpha_y - 4) \xi)\right] {r_z}_1 + \beta_y (3\xi^2 - (\alpha_y + 2) \xi) {r_z}_2\]

and the same functions for \(w\) and \(r_y\), with \(v\) replaced by \(w\), \(r_z\) by \(-r_y\), \(\alpha_y\) by \(\alpha_z\) and \(\beta_y\) by \(\beta_z = 1/(1 - \alpha_z)\). In the code they are alphay, alphaz, betay and betaz. Cubic \(v\) and quadratic \(r_z\) share the same four nodal values, which is what makes the interpolation consistent: the shear strain is constant along the element,

(11)#\[\bar{\gamma}_y = \alpha_y \beta_y \left( \frac{v_1 - v_2}{L} + \frac{{r_z}_1 + {r_z}_2}{2} \right)\]

and vanishes for \(\alpha_y = 0\), where Eq. (9) becomes the cubic Hermite interpolation of the Euler-Bernoulli beam and \(r_z = v_{,x}\). The element is therefore free of shear locking with exact integration. These functions solve Eq. (7) with a diagonal \(\boldsymbol{C}\) only with the negative sign of Eq. (8); the same solution is Eq. (17) of Luo (2008) and Eq. (3.236) of Petyt (2010), whereas Eq. (21) of Luo (2008) prints \(\alpha_y\), \(\alpha_z\) with the positive sign, and his Eq. (20) the functions of \(w\), \(r_y\) with the signs of those of \(v\), \(r_z\), see the Notes of BeamC. One element gives the tip deflection \(P L^3/(3 E I_{zz}) + P L/(G_{12} A)\) of a cantilever under a tip force \(P\), Eq. (1.2) of Bathe (2014), and the stiffness matrix KC0 of BeamC.update_KC0() is the exact integral of Eq. (5) with these functions, evaluated in closed form.

Coupled section. With any other coupling, including those of Ay, Az and Iyz, Eqs. (9) and (10) no longer solve the equilibrium equations, and BeamC derives its shape functions from Eq. (7) with the full \(\boldsymbol{C}\), which must then be positive definite. Integrating the equilibrium equations from node 1, the sectional forces are

(12)#\[\boldsymbol{C} \boldsymbol{\epsilon} = (\boldsymbol{I} + x \boldsymbol{Z}) \boldsymbol{q}, \qquad \boldsymbol{\epsilon} = \boldsymbol{S} (\boldsymbol{I} + x \boldsymbol{Z}) \boldsymbol{q}, \qquad \boldsymbol{q} = \{N, V_y, V_z, M_x, M_z(0), M_y(0)\}^\top\]

with \(\boldsymbol{S} = \boldsymbol{C}^{-1}\) and the only non-zero entries of \(\boldsymbol{Z}\) (indices from 0) \(Z_{41} = Z_{52} = 1\). Integrating the kinematic relations of Eq. (4) from node 1,

(13)#\[u = \int \epsilon_0 \, dx, \quad r_x = \int \epsilon_3 \, dx, \quad r_z = -\int \epsilon_4 \, dx, \quad r_y = \int \epsilon_5 \, dx, \quad v = \int (\epsilon_1 + r_z) \, dx, \quad w = \int (\epsilon_2 - r_y) \, dx\]

and adding the rigid-body motion \(\boldsymbol{R}(x)\) of node 1, the field \(\boldsymbol{d} = \{u, v, w, r_x, r_y, r_z\}^\top\) is \(\boldsymbol{d} = \boldsymbol{R}(x) \boldsymbol{u}_1 + \boldsymbol{P}(x) \boldsymbol{q}\), quadratic in \(x\) for \(u\) and the rotations and cubic for \(v\) and \(w\), with coefficients that depend on all the entries of \(\boldsymbol{S}\). The statics of the element clamped at node 1 give \(\boldsymbol{q} = \boldsymbol{T} \boldsymbol{P}_2\) for the loads \(\boldsymbol{P}_2\) at node 2, and \(\boldsymbol{F}_c = \boldsymbol{P}(L) \boldsymbol{T}\) is the flexibility of this cantilever. With \(\boldsymbol{G} = [-\boldsymbol{R}(L), \boldsymbol{I}]\), which takes the rigid-body motion of node 1 out of the displacements of node 2,

(14)#\[\boldsymbol{q} = \boldsymbol{H}_f \boldsymbol{u}_e, \qquad \boldsymbol{H}_f = \boldsymbol{T} \boldsymbol{F}_c^{-1} \boldsymbol{G}, \qquad \boldsymbol{d} = \left( [\boldsymbol{R}(x), \boldsymbol{0}] + \boldsymbol{P}(x) \boldsymbol{H}_f \right) \boldsymbol{u}_e, \qquad \boldsymbol{K}_{C0_e} = \boldsymbol{G}^\top \boldsymbol{F}_c^{-1} \boldsymbol{G}\]

\(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0_e}\) is the Hessian of Eq. (5) with these shape functions, and the mass, geometric and non-linear matrices are integrated analytically with the same polynomials. Without couplings other than \(C_{03}\) they reduce to Eqs. (9) and (10), whose closed-form expressions are then used. Otherwise \(\boldsymbol{S}\), \(\boldsymbol{F}_c^{-1}\), \(\boldsymbol{H}_f\) and the coefficients of the shape functions are evaluated numerically for each element and call, in the C kernel _coupled_fields of pyfe3d.beamc, at a cost of a few microseconds per element. The derivation is that of pyfe3d, given step by step in the Notes of BeamC.

One element of BeamC is thus exact under nodal loads for any positive definite \(\boldsymbol{C}\), and the axial force \(N = q_0\) is uniform along the element. The functions of Luo (2008) evaluated with the diagonal of a coupled \(\boldsymbol{C}\) would not be: for single couplings of a rectangular section, and for the bending-shear couplings of a composite box beam, their tip displacements err by up to 8.9e-2 and 0.42, see the Notes of BeamC and tests/test_beam_sectional_stiffness.py.

BeamLR: linear interpolation and reduced integration#

BeamLR interpolates the six displacements of the axis linearly, \(d = (1 - \xi) d_1 + \xi d_2\). The axial strain, the twist and the curvatures are then constant in each element, and the shear strains \(\bar{\gamma}_y = v_{,x} - r_z\) and \(\bar{\gamma}_z = w_{,x} + r_y\) linear. With exact integration, the shear terms of the linear interpolation would lock in slender elements: a constant curvature, i.e. a linear \(r_z\), cannot be combined with a linear \(v\) into a shear strain that vanishes along the element. BeamLR evaluates the strain energy at the centre of the element,

(15)#\[\boldsymbol{K}_{C0_e} = L \boldsymbol{B}_L^\top \boldsymbol{C} \boldsymbol{B}_L, \qquad \boldsymbol{\epsilon} = \boldsymbol{B}_L \boldsymbol{u}_e \text{ at } \xi = 1/2\]

which is exact for the constant strains and imposes the shear strains only at the centre, see BeamLR.update_KC0(). The interpolation does not depend on \(\boldsymbol{C}\), so any \(\boldsymbol{C}\), positive semi-definite, is used as it is, and every coupling is represented with the same convergence as an uncoupled section. The price is a constant curvature in each element: the error decreases as the square of the element length, e.g. 8.3e-2, 5.2e-3 and 3.3e-4 with 1, 4 and 16 elements for the coupling \(C_{15}\) under a transverse tip load, against one element of BeamC, see the Notes of BeamC. Since BeamLR does not use \(\alpha_y\), \(\alpha_z\), it is an independent check of their sign: refined meshes converge to the same tip deflections and frequencies as BeamC, tests/test_timoshenko_beam_literature.py.

Mass matrices#

The kinetic energy of the displacement field of Eq. (2), integrated over the cross section with the mass integrals of BeamProp, is

(16)#\[T = \frac{1}{2} \int_0^L \Big[ m (\dot{u}^2 + \dot{v}^2 + \dot{w}^2) + m_{yy} \dot{r}_z^2 + m_{zz} \dot{r}_y^2 - 2 m_{yz} \dot{r}_y \dot{r}_z - 2 m_y \dot{u} \dot{r}_z + 2 m_z \dot{u} \dot{r}_y - 2 m_z \dot{v} \dot{r}_x + 2 m_y \dot{w} \dot{r}_x + (m_{yy} + m_{zz}) \dot{r}_x^2 \Big] dx\]

with \(m\) = intrho, \(m_y\) = intrhoy, \(m_z\) = intrhoz, \(m_{yy}\) = intrhoy2, \(m_{zz}\) = intrhoz2 and \(m_{yz}\) = intrhoyz. The consistent mass matrix, mtype=0 of BeamC.update_M() and BeamLR.update_M(), is the Hessian of \(T\) with respect to the nodal velocities, with the shape functions of each element, integrated exactly and with the rotary inertia: for BeamC it is the matrix of Petyt (2010), Sections 3.3, 3.4 and 3.9, in each plane of bending, and with couplings it uses the shape functions of Eq. (14); for BeamLR, e.g. \(L m/6\) times \([2, 1; 1, 2]\) for each translation. The lumped mass matrix, mtype=1, is the two-point Gauss-Lobatto quadrature of \(T\), whose points are the nodes, after Brockman (1987): diagonal, with \(L m/2\) for each translation, \(L (m_{yy} + m_{zz})/2\) for \(r_x\), \(L m_{zz}/2\) for \(r_y\) and \(L m_{yy}/2\) for \(r_z\) at each node, and without the first moments \(m_y\), \(m_z\) and the product \(m_{yz}\), which would make it non-diagonal.

Geometric stiffness and geometrically non-linear analysis#

The geometric non-linearity of both elements is that of the von Kármán axial strain of the beam axis,

(17)#\[\epsilon_{xx} = u_{,x} + \frac{1}{2} \left( v_{,x}^2 + w_{,x}^2 \right) + y \kappa_y + z \kappa_z\]

valid for small strains and moderate rotations in the fixed element axes; the curvatures and the shear strains stay linear. The internal forces of update_fint(..., nonlinear=1) are the gradient of the strain energy with Eq. (17), and KC0 + KCNL + KG is its exact, symmetric Jacobian: KG is the geometric stiffness of the axial force of the linear strains, homogeneous of degree one in the displacements, and KCNL holds the constitutive terms of the non-linear part of the strain together with the geometric term of its axial force, see BeamC.update_KCNL(). The splitting keeps KG suitable for the linear buckling problem \((\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + \lambda \boldsymbol{K}_G) \boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{0}\), see Linear buckling and natural frequencies.

BeamC: element mean of the non-linear strain. With a linear \(u\) and cubic \(v\), \(w\), the pointwise \((v_{,x}^2 + w_{,x}^2)/2\) is of degree four in \(x\), which the constant \(u_{,x}\) cannot balance: the axial strain cannot vanish in inextensional bending and coarse meshes are too stiff, the membrane locking of Crisfield (1991), Section 7.1.3. BeamC replaces the non-linear part by its element mean,

(18)#\[\epsilon_{xx} = u_{,x} + \bar{\epsilon}_{NL} + y \kappa_y + z \kappa_z, \qquad \bar{\epsilon}_{NL} = \frac{1}{L} \int_0^L \frac{1}{2} \left( v_{,x}^2 + w_{,x}^2 \right) dx = \frac{1}{2} \boldsymbol{u}_e^\top \boldsymbol{H}_m \boldsymbol{u}_e\]

Crisfield (1991), Eqs. (7.31)-(7.32), and (1997), Eqs. (17.56a)-(17.56b), with \(\boldsymbol{H}_m\) the mean of the products of the slope rows of the consistent shape functions, which include the shear deformation. The geometric stiffness carries the element mean \(N_m\) of the axial force,

(19)#\[\boldsymbol{K}_G = L N_m \boldsymbol{H}_m\]

and, since the shape functions solve the equilibrium equations, \(N\) is uniform and \(N_m = N\) for any \(\boldsymbol{C}\); e.g. \(N_m = E A (u_2 - u_1)/L\) for a centroidal uncoupled section, see BeamC.update_KG().

BeamLR: slopes of the linear interpolation. BeamLR takes the slopes \(v_{,x}\) and \(w_{,x}\) from the linear interpolations of \(v\) and \(w\), constant in each element, so that its geometric stiffness matrix is that of a string, \(N/L\) times \([1, -1; -1, 1]\) for \(v\) and for \(w\) of the two nodes, see BeamLR.update_KG(). The axial strain can vanish in each element, and the element is free of membrane locking without averaging. Because the slopes, not the rotations, enter the axial strain, the buckling load includes the shear deformation and converges, as the square of the element length, to \(P_e/(1 + P_e/(k G A))\), Eq. (2-57) of Timoshenko and Gere (1961), instead of the Euler load \(P_e\), see the Notes of BeamLR; BeamC, whose slopes include the shear strains of Eq. (11), reproduces Eq. (2-57) to 4.2e-7 with 20 elements for a clamped-free column, tests/test_beam_linear_buckling_3D_column.py.

Transformation to global coordinates#

All the matrices and internal forces are computed in element coordinates and transformed with the block-diagonal matrix \(\boldsymbol{T}\) of four copies of \(\boldsymbol{T}_{3\times 3}\), whose columns are the element axes, \(\boldsymbol{K} = \boldsymbol{T} \boldsymbol{K}_e \boldsymbol{T}^\top\) and \(\boldsymbol{F} = \boldsymbol{T} \boldsymbol{F}_e\), see Conventions. The element axes are not updated with the displacements: for large rotations the elements are wrapped by the co-rotational layer, see Co-rotational formulation for large rotations.

Limits of validity#

  • Rigid cross section: no in-plane deformation of the section, no shear lag, no local buckling of the walls; the warping enters only through GJ or through a C computed by pyfe3d.vabs.

  • Saint-Venant torsion: no warping degree of freedom, hence no restrained warping, whose stiffness can dominate the torsion of thin-walled open sections; the generalized Vlasov stiffness of CrossSection.calc_vlasov() is not used by the elements.

  • Prismatic elements: one constant C per element; a tapered or curved member is modelled by several straight elements.

  • Linear elasticity and small strains; with nonlinear=1, moderate rotations in the fixed element axes. The shear correction factors are the user’s choice unless C comes from a cross-sectional analysis.

BeamC is the recommended beam element: it needs the fewest elements, and with a coupled sectional stiffness matrix it remains exact under nodal loads. BeamLR is the choice when C is only positive semi-definite with couplings, as an independent check of a BeamC model, or where a simple element with constant strains is preferred. Both can be mixed with the shells, the truss and the springs in one model, and both are wrapped by CorotationalBeam for large rotations, see Co-rotational formulation for large rotations and Choosing an element.
