Quad4R: one-point integration and hourglass control#

Quad4R, module pyfe3d.quad4r, has the kinematics, interpolation and constitutive relation of Quad4, see Shell elements: kinematics and formulation, but integrates the membrane, coupling, bending and transverse shear terms with a single point at the centroid, \(\xi = \eta = 0\) with the weight 4, \(4 \det \boldsymbol{J}_0\) being the area \(A\) of a parallelogram. One point makes the internal forces and the stiffness matrices cheaper than those of Quad4, and it leaves hourglass modes, deformations with no strain at the centroid, which must be given stiffness. The strain energy is that of (5) plus the hourglass energy \(U_{hg}\):

  • the hourglass modes of the bending and of the transverse shear, of \(w\), \(r_x\) and \(r_y\), have the physical stabilisation of Belytschko and Leviathan (1994), without parameter;

  • the hourglass modes of the membrane, of \(u\) and \(v\), have the artificial stiffness of Brockman (1987).

The arguments hgfactor_physical and hgfactor_membrane of Quad4R.update_KC0(), Quad4R.update_probe_finte() and Quad4R.update_fint() multiply the two; 1., the default, is the formulation described here, other values serve verification and sensitivity studies.

Decomposition of the bilinear field#

Belytschko and Leviathan (1994), Eq. (3.23), write the bilinear shape functions as a linear field plus an hourglass term,

(1)#\[S_I = \Delta_I + x \, b_{xI} + y \, b_{yI} + h \, \gamma_I, \qquad h = \xi \eta, \qquad \gamma_I = \frac{1}{4} \left( h_I - (\boldsymbol{h}^\top \boldsymbol{x}) b_{xI} - (\boldsymbol{h}^\top \boldsymbol{y}) b_{yI} \right)\]

with \(b_{xI}\), \(b_{yI}\) the derivatives at the centroid, \(\boldsymbol{h} = \{1, -1, 1, -1\}\) and \(\boldsymbol{x}\), \(\boldsymbol{y}\) the nodal coordinates, such that \(S_{I,x} = b_{xI} + h_{,x} \gamma_I\), Eq. (3.25), with \(\{h_{,x}, h_{,y}\}^\top = \boldsymbol{J}^{-1} \{\eta, \xi\}^\top\). The vector \(\gamma_I\) is orthogonal to the linear fields: \(\boldsymbol{\gamma}^\top\) applied to the nodal values of any rigid motion or linear field is zero, on any quadrilateral. The constant part of the strains is integrated at the centroid, and the stabilisation is the energy of their non-constant part.

Physical stabilisation of the transverse shear#

The transverse shear strains are the assumed covariant strains of Dvorkin and Bathe (1984), (1), tied at the mid-points of the edges, here with the labels of Belytschko and Leviathan, \(B\) \((0, -1)\), \(D\) \((0, 1)\), \(A\) \((-1, 0)\) and \(C\) \((1, 0)\), and split into a constant and a non-constant part, Eqs. (3.35)-(3.39):

(2)#\[e_\xi = \frac{e_B + e_D}{2} + \eta \frac{e_D - e_B}{2}, \qquad e_\eta = \frac{e_A + e_C}{2} + \xi \frac{e_C - e_A}{2}\]

both mapped to the element axes with the Jacobian \(\boldsymbol{J}_0\) at the centroid. The constant part replaces the displacement-based shear strains at the centroid, Eq. (4.12), and the non-constant part gives the stabilisation of Eq. (4.13), integrated exactly with \(\int \xi^2 dA = \int \eta^2 dA = A/3\) and \(\int \xi \eta \, dA = 0\):

(3)#\[\begin{split}\boldsymbol{K}^s_{stab} = f_p \frac{A}{3} \left( \boldsymbol{D}_1^\top \boldsymbol{A}_s \boldsymbol{D}_1 + \boldsymbol{D}_2^\top \boldsymbol{A}_s \boldsymbol{D}_2 \right), \qquad \boldsymbol{D}_1 = \boldsymbol{J}_0^{-1} \begin{Bmatrix} (e_D - e_B)/2 \\ 0 \end{Bmatrix}, \quad \boldsymbol{D}_2 = \boldsymbol{J}_0^{-1} \begin{Bmatrix} 0 \\ (e_C - e_A)/2 \end{Bmatrix}\end{split}\]

with \(f_p\) = hgfactor_physical and \(\boldsymbol{A}_s\) the matrix of \(A_{55}\), \(A_{45}\) and \(A_{44}\) for \(\gamma_{xz}\) and \(\gamma_{yz}\); the coupling of the constant and non-constant parts is omitted, as in the paper. On a parallelogram this is the transverse shear of Quad4 with two by two points, see Quad4: assumed transverse shear strains (MITC4).

Physical stabilisation of the bending#

The non-constant part of the curvatures, Eq. (3.28), is

(4)#\[\kappa_{xx} = h_{,x} q_y, \qquad \kappa_{yy} = -h_{,y} q_x, \qquad \kappa_{xy} = h_{,y} q_y - h_{,x} q_x, \qquad q_x = \boldsymbol{\gamma}^\top \boldsymbol{r}_x, \quad q_y = \boldsymbol{\gamma}^\top \boldsymbol{r}_y\]

with \(\boldsymbol{r}_x\), \(\boldsymbol{r}_y\) the nodal rotations, and its energy gives the stiffness of Eqs. (4.13), (B.17) and (B.18),

(5)#\[K^b_{r_{xI} r_{xJ}} = f_p Q_{xx} \gamma_I \gamma_J, \quad K^b_{r_{yI} r_{yJ}} = f_p Q_{yy} \gamma_I \gamma_J, \quad K^b_{r_{xI} r_{yJ}} = f_p Q_{xy} \gamma_I \gamma_J\]
\[Q_{xx} = H_{xx} D_{66} + H_{yy} D_{22} + 2 H_{xy} D_{26}, \quad Q_{yy} = H_{xx} D_{11} + H_{yy} D_{66} + 2 H_{xy} D_{16}, \quad Q_{xy} = -H_{xx} D_{16} - H_{yy} D_{26} - H_{xy} (D_{12} + D_{66})\]

with \(H_{xx}\), \(H_{yy}\), \(H_{xy}\) the integrals of \(h_{,x}^2\), \(h_{,y}^2\) and \(h_{,x} h_{,y}\) over the element, Eq. (4.14), evaluated with two by two points, and \(D_{ij}\) the entries of the bending stiffness with zero membrane forces, \(\boldsymbol{D} - \boldsymbol{B} \boldsymbol{A}^{-1} \boldsymbol{B}\), which is \(\boldsymbol{D}\) for a symmetric laminate and does not depend on the offset of the ShellProp. On a parallelogram the one-point bending plus this stabilisation is the bending integrated with two by two points.

Neither stabilisation has a parameter. The rigid-body motions and every state of constant strain, of constant curvature, including the uniform twist \(w = kxy\), \(r_x = kx\), \(r_y = -ky\), and of constant transverse shear have no non-constant strain, on any quadrilateral, and the assumed shear strains of a constant curvature vanish too: the element passes the constant-twist patch test and the thin-plate bending patch test of Belytschko and Leviathan (1994), Section 6.1, for any thickness and unit of length, and a single element has only the six rigid-body modes as zero-energy modes. Both stiffnesses scale with the unit of length as the physical ones do.

Hourglass control of the membrane#

The hourglass modes of \(u\) and \(v\) have the artificial stiffness of Brockman (1987), with the orthogonalised operator of his Eq. (14), quoted from Belytschko and Tsay (1983), normalised as in his Eq. (15):

(6)#\[K_{ij} = A \, E^{(h)} \gamma_i \gamma_j, \qquad \gamma_i = S_{1,xy} \left( h_i - h_x S_{i,x} - h_y S_{i,y} \right), \qquad h_x = x_1 - x_2 + x_3 - x_4, \quad h_y = y_1 - y_2 + y_3 - y_4\]

for each of \(u\) and \(v\), with the derivatives at the centroid, the correction terms in \(h_x\), \(h_y\) vanishing for a parallelogram. The generalised stiffnesses of Brockman, \(0.10 E t/(1 + 1/A)\), are not dimensionally homogeneous, and tend to the area \(A\) only for \(A \ll 1\) in the unit of length of the model; pyfe3d uses the area itself and, for a laminate, the membrane moduli of the extensional compliance \(\boldsymbol{A}^{-1} = [a_{ij}]\) in the element axes,

(7)#\[E^{(h)}_u = 0.10 \, f_m E_{1eq} t A, \qquad E^{(h)}_v = 0.10 \, f_m E_{2eq} t A, \qquad E_{1eq} = \frac{1}{t a_{11}}, \quad E_{2eq} = \frac{1}{t a_{22}}\]

with \(f_m\) = hgfactor_membrane and \(t\) the thickness, which reduce to \(E\) for an isotropic plate and make the response independent of the unit of length. The physical stabilisation of the membrane of Belytschko and Leviathan (1994), Eqs. (3.27) and (B.13), which on a flat element is the bilinear membrane with two by two points, was measured stiffer still in in-plane bending, and is not used. Neither form is free of locking in the in-plane bending of strips one element deep, e.g. 0.119, 0.033 and 0.064 of the reference tip displacement of the straight cantilever of MacNeal and Harder (1985), 6 by 1 elements, on the regular, trapezoidal and parallelogram meshes; Quad4 does not lock there.

Drilling stiffness without enrichment#

The drilling rotation is tied to the in-plane rotation of the membrane by the Hughes-Brezzi term, integrated with two by two points, with \(\gamma_{r_z} = A_{66}\) for drilling_model = 0 and \(K6ROT \cdot 10^{-6} A_{66}\) otherwise, see Drilling rotations. Unlike Quad4 and Tria3R, the membrane is not enriched with the drilling rotations: the enrichment removes the excessive in-plane bending stiffness of the bilinear field, which the one-point integration already removes. On the skew membrane of Cook, reference 23.96, the tip displacement is 30.14, 24.66, 24.08 and 23.97 with 2x2 to 16x16 elements, against 20.75, 22.83, 23.54 and 23.81 for the enriched Quad4. An enrichment sampled at one point would leave the spurious mode \(u \propto x\), \(v \propto -y\), \(r_z \propto \boldsymbol{h}\), whose membrane strain at the centroid is cancelled by the enrichment, see the Notes of pyfe3d.quad4r.

Geometric stiffness#

The membrane forces of (12) are integrated with two by two points, and the slopes of \(w\) are taken at the centroid, the single point of the curvatures and of the transverse shear in KC0, see Quad4R.update_KG(). The hourglass pattern \(w = \xi \eta\) then has no geometric stiffness, as it has no stiffness in KC0 besides the stabilisation of the transverse shear; with the slopes at the two by two points, a linear buckling analysis could return a spurious mode of \(w\) alternating in sign from node to node, e.g. 41 % below the Mindlin solution of a simply supported square plate with \(b/h = 10\) and 16 by 16 elements. With a warped element, update_KG and update_KCNL take the hgfactor_* of update_KC0 for the nodal forces of the eccentricity term of the rigid offsets, see Choosing an element.
