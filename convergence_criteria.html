Convergence criteria#

The iterations of The Newton-Raphson method and of Arc-length methods stop when the residual \(\boldsymbol{R} = \boldsymbol{F}_{int} - \lambda \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\) is small enough. The test is on the residual, the actual imbalance of forces, and not on the size of the corrections, which measures only how far the last iteration moved. Analysis.convergence selects one of the two criteria of pyfe3d.solver.convergence, applied by pyfe3d.solver.convergence.check_convergence(): 'crisfield', the default, or 'relative'.

Why the residual must be scaled#

The dofs of beam, plate and shell models mix translations and rotations, and the residual mixes forces and moments. Its Euclidean norm adds the squares of forces and of moments, a quantity without physical meaning, whose value depends on the system of units: a change of the length unit scales the moments but not the forces, and changes the relative weight of the two in the norm. Each component is therefore scaled with the diagonal entry of a reference stiffness matrix, a scaling that Crisfield (1981, p. 58) attributes to Peano and Riccioni (1978) and that Crisfield (1991, Sec. 9.5.4, Eq. (9.48)) presents as an alternative to the Euclidean norm of the residual. With the diagonal scaling vector \(\boldsymbol{d}\) of the linear stiffness matrix of the free dofs,

(1)#\[d_j = K_{{C0}_{jj}}, \qquad \|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\boldsymbol{d}} = \sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{n} \frac{v_j^2}{|d_j|}}\]

with \(n\) the number of free dofs, see crisfield_weights() and pyfe3d.solver.convergence.scaled_norm(). \(d_j\) is a stiffness, of the units of the generalized force of the dof \(j\) divided by those of its generalized displacement, such that each term \(v_j^2/|d_j|\) has the units of energy, whether the dof is a translation or a rotation: the scaled norm is the square root of an energy-like quantity, independent of the units of each dof. The absolute value guards against negative diagonal entries. A null diagonal entry in a row that is not null, e.g. of a Lagrange multiplier, leaves its dof without a scale and raises a ValueError; the relative criterion is then the alternative.

Crisfield’s criterion#

The iteration \(i\) has converged when

(2)#\[\zeta_i = \frac{\|\boldsymbol{R}_i\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}}{\max\left( \|\lambda \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}, \|\boldsymbol{F}_{int}(\boldsymbol{u}_i)\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}, \|\Delta\lambda \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}, \|\lambda \, \partial \boldsymbol{R}/\partial \lambda\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}\right)} \le \epsilon\]

with \(\epsilon\) = Analysis.relTOL, \(10^{-9}\) by default, see crisfield_zeta(). The third reference applies only to the arc-length methods, with \(\Delta\lambda\) the increment of the load factor of the current step, and the fourth only to load vectors that depend on the load factor, e.g. prescribed displacements imposed exactly, see Boundary conditions and prescribed displacements. The maximum does not degenerate:

  • under self-equilibrated loads \(\|\lambda \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}\) may be negligible, whereas \(\|\boldsymbol{F}_{int}\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}\) reflects the internal forces carried by the structure;

  • at the beginning of a load-controlled analysis the internal forces are small and the external load is the reference;

  • when an arc-length method passes through a load factor close to zero, \(\Delta\lambda \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\) is the reference;

  • under prescribed displacements alone, the external and the free part of the internal force vectors both vanish at equilibrium, whereas \(-\lambda \, \partial \boldsymbol{R}/\partial \lambda\), the out-of-balance force that the prescribed displacements produce with the free dofs held fixed, does not.

The norms are evaluated over the free dofs only: the components of the residual at the known dofs are the support reactions, not an imbalance. \(\boldsymbol{d}\) is computed once from the linear stiffness matrix calc_kC(), assembled once for the analysis, and kept constant, such that \(\zeta_i\) measures the progress of the iterations and not the evolution of the tangent stiffness matrix; the internal force vector of the numerator and of the denominator comes from the same evaluation, at no additional cost. tests/test_solver_nonlinear_static.py checks that scaling the rotational dofs of a plate model changes neither the iterations nor \(\zeta\), whereas it changes the relative criterion, and that the norms ignore the reactions.

The criterion is the same for the arc-length methods, which change how the load factor is updated but not the residual, with the additional reference \(\Delta\lambda \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\) (Crisfield 1981).

The relative criterion#

With Analysis.convergence = 'relative', the iteration has converged when

(3)#\[\|\boldsymbol{R}\| \le \epsilon \max\left(\|\lambda \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\|, \|\boldsymbol{F}_{int}\|, \|\Delta\lambda \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\|, \|\lambda \, \partial \boldsymbol{R}/\partial \lambda\|\right)\]

with the Euclidean norm over the free dofs and the same references, or \(\|\boldsymbol{R}\|\) itself when they are all null. It depends on the units of the dofs, as discussed above, and remains for models whose dofs have no stiffness scale, e.g. with Lagrange multipliers.

With both criteria, Analysis.absTOL, None by default, also accepts the iterations whose largest absolute residual is not larger than it, in the units of force of the model, and relTOL = None leaves only that test.

Choice of the tolerance#

With a consistent tangent stiffness matrix, the full Newton-Raphson iterations converge quadratically, \(\zeta_{i+1} \sim \zeta_i^2\), and a tolerance as tight as \(\epsilon = 10^{-9}\) is reached in two or three iterations from the \(\zeta\) of \(10^{-2}\) to \(10^{-3}\) of a moderate increment; with the modified Newton-Raphson method the convergence is linear (Crisfield 1991, Secs. 1.2.2 and 1.2.3, pp. 8-13) and looser tolerances are more realistic. The decay of \(\zeta\), printed at every iteration, is a diagnostic: a number of iterations consistently larger than three or four for moderate increments reveals a tangent stiffness matrix inconsistent with the internal force vector, e.g. a missing calc_kG, which changes the iterations but not the converged solution. A tight tolerance is recommended when the converged state feeds an eigenvalue analysis, whose geometric stiffness matrix is built from its stresses.

The round-off floor#

The tolerance cannot be made arbitrarily small, because the residual is the difference of force vectors computed in floating-point arithmetic. Each component of \(\boldsymbol{F}_{int}\) sums element contributions of the order of \(\sum_k |K_{{C0}_{jk}}| |u_k|\), which may be much larger than the component itself, e.g. under large self-equilibrated axial forces, and the rounding error of such a sum is bounded componentwise by the product of the absolute values times the machine precision \(\varepsilon\) and a factor of the number of terms (Higham 2002, Sec. 3.5, Eqs. (3.12) and (3.13)). The residual thus cannot be computed more accurately than about \(\varepsilon \| |\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}| \, |\boldsymbol{u}| \|\), with \(|\cdot|\) the elementwise absolute values. When this level is above \(\epsilon\) times the references of (2), e.g. near a null load, where the references, net force vectors, are small and the element contributions are not, the iterations stagnate and every step fails. An iteration has therefore also converged when

(4)#\[\|\boldsymbol{R}_i\|_{\boldsymbol{d}} \le C \, \varepsilon \, \| |\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}| \, |\boldsymbol{u}_i| \|_{\boldsymbol{d}}, \qquad C = 3\]

with \(C\) = ROUNDOFF_FACTOR and the Euclidean norm with the relative criterion, see pyfe3d.solver.convergence.roundoff_floor(). Like absTOL, the floor is an alternative to relTOL; it does not change \(\zeta_i\), which may then be larger than \(\epsilon\), and the log marks the iterations converged at the round-off floor. \(|\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}|\) is computed once with \(\boldsymbol{d}\), and the floor costs one product per iteration. With prescribed displacements, the product is over the free dofs and omits the contribution of the prescribed ones, which makes the floor lower, i.e. conservative.

On the low sinusoidal arch of Fung and Kaplan (1952), traced with both arc-length methods and both criteria with \(\epsilon = 10^{-9}\), the analyses stop close to the null load between the two limit points without the floor, with residuals at 0.29 to 0.47 times \(\varepsilon \| |\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}| \, |\boldsymbol{u}| \|\), and all of them reach the load factor 1.0 with it, see the module documentation of pyfe3d.solver.convergence and tests/test_solver_arc_length_fung_kaplan.py.

The floor assumes that calc_fint computes the internal forces from the displacements, whose round-off is then of the order of \(\varepsilon |\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}| |\boldsymbol{u}|\). It cannot cover a round-off that depends on the nodal coordinates \(\boldsymbol{X}\), e.g. of current positions computed as \(\boldsymbol{X} + \boldsymbol{u}\), since a translation of the origin changes none of the quantities the criterion receives. Such a model should compute the positions relative to a nearby point, as pyfe3d.corotational does with the base centroid of each element, or else set absTOL.
