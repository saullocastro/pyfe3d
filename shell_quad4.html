Quad4: assumed transverse shear strains (MITC4)#

Quad4, module pyfe3d.quad4, is the recommended quadrilateral of pyfe3d: a flat four-node element with the FSDT kinematics of Shell elements: kinematics and formulation, bilinear interpolation of the six degrees of freedom, the assumed transverse shear strains of the MITC4 element of Dvorkin and Bathe (1984) and, by default, the drilling enrichment of the membrane of Allman (1984) with the regularisation of Hughes and Brezzi (1989), see Drilling rotations. Its name in the code, “mixed integration”, refers to the different quadratures of its terms.

Quadratures#

The terms of the stiffness matrix (9) are integrated with Gauss-Legendre points:

  • bending, \(\boldsymbol{B}_b^\top \boldsymbol{D} \boldsymbol{B}_b\): two by two points, with every drilling model, such that the drilling model does not change the bending response;

  • membrane and membrane-bending coupling: three by three points with the default drilling_model = 0, whose edge modes make the membrane strains vary linearly, and two by two points with the penalty drilling_model = 1, see Drilling rotations;

  • transverse shear: the assumed strains below, with two by two points, with the default shear_model = 1;

  • the geometric stiffness, KCNL and the non-linear internal forces: two by two points for the membrane forces, with the slopes of \(w\) of Geometric stiffness below.

Assumed transverse shear strains#

The covariant transverse shear strains of the bilinear field, with the interpolated \(w\), \(r_x\), \(r_y\) and coordinates,

(1)#\[\gamma_{\xi z} = x_{,\xi} \gamma_{xz} + y_{,\xi} \gamma_{yz} = w_{,\xi} + x_{,\xi} r_y - y_{,\xi} r_x, \qquad \gamma_{\eta z} = w_{,\eta} + x_{,\eta} r_y - y_{,\eta} r_x\]

are evaluated at the mid-points of the edges, the tying points \(A\) \((0, -1)\) and \(C\) \((0, 1)\) for \(\gamma_{\xi z}\) and \(D\) \((-1, 0)\) and \(B\) \((1, 0)\) for \(\gamma_{\eta z}\), and interpolated linearly across the element,

(2)#\[\gamma_{\xi z} = \frac{1 - \eta}{2} \gamma_{\xi z}^{A} + \frac{1 + \eta}{2} \gamma_{\xi z}^{C}, \qquad \gamma_{\eta z} = \frac{1 - \xi}{2} \gamma_{\eta z}^{D} + \frac{1 + \xi}{2} \gamma_{\eta z}^{B}\]

such that \(\gamma_{\xi z}\) is constant along \(\xi\) and \(\gamma_{\eta z}\) along \(\eta\). At each integration point they are brought to the element axes with the inverse Jacobian of that point, \(\{\gamma_{xz}, \gamma_{yz}\}^\top = \boldsymbol{J}^{-1} \{\gamma_{\xi z}, \gamma_{\eta z}\}^\top\), and the shear energy of (9) is integrated with two by two points. The strains are exact for a pure twist and for a pure bending of a rectangle, the element passes the patch test and it has no zero-energy mode besides the six rigid-body modes (Bathe and Dvorkin, 1985, pp. 372-373).

On a parallelogram, whose Jacobian is constant, the assumed strain at the centroid is the displacement-based one, and the two by two integral of the assumed shear energy is the one-point shear term of Hughes et al. (1977) plus the energy of the parts of the assumed strains linear in \(\eta\), for \(\gamma_{\xi z}\), and in \(\xi\), for \(\gamma_{\eta z}\): a matrix of rank two, the physical stabilisation of Belytschko and Leviathan (1994), Eq. (4.13), which Quad4R uses and which vanishes for every state of constant transverse shear and of constant curvature, see Quad4R: one-point integration and hourglass control. The element is then that of Hughes et al. with the energy of the transverse shear that varies linearly over the element added, the energy that a one-point rule discards.

The one-point transverse shear of shear_model = 0#

With shear_model = 0 the transverse shear is integrated with one point at the centroid when the thickness of the property and the length \(\ell = \sqrt{A}\) of the element give \(h/\ell < 1\), the selective scheme of Hughes et al. (1977), and with the assumed strains above otherwise. The one-point rule has two zero-energy modes besides the rigid-body modes (Hughes et al., 1977, p. 1542; Hughes, Cohen and Haroun, 1978, Sec. 3.6): the hourglass mode \(w = (x - x_c)(y - y_c)\) with zero rotations and the in-plane twist \(w = 0\), \(r_x = x - x_c\), \(r_y = y - y_c\), \((x_c, y_c)\) being the centroid, whose curvatures vanish and whose shear strains vanish at the centroid. A single element then has eight zero eigenvalues instead of six. The hourglass mode survives assembly. It is suppressed by any support that constrains \(w\) along a line, but a strip one element wide in torsion, whose twist is the hourglass pattern, is far too flexible: 377 times the reference twist of the straight cantilever of MacNeal and Harder (1985), against 0.941 with the assumed strains, the value of their QUAD4 element.

On other quadrilaterals the displacement-based strain at the centroid does not vanish under a constant curvature, and the one-point rule fails the thin-plate bending patch test: 0.87 to 1.22 times the exact deflection of the interior nodes on the patch of Belytschko and Leviathan (1994), and an energy error of 580 % on the patch of MacNeal and Harder (1985), which the assumed strains pass to round-off. For very thick elements, \(h/\ell \ge 1\), Hughes et al. propose a “modified one-point shear” with two by two points for the products of the slopes of \(w\); mixing two quadratures in one energy stores the spurious shear energy \(A_{55} \ell^4 \kappa^2/12\) in the pure twist \(w = \kappa x y\), \(r_x = \kappa x\), \(r_y = -\kappa y\) of a square element, \((5/12)(\ell/h)^2\) times its twist energy for an isotropic plate, which is why the assumed strains are used there with both values of shear_model. The Notes of pyfe3d.quad4 give the results of both rules on the problems of MacNeal and Harder (1985).

Geometric stiffness#

The membrane forces of (12) are integrated with two by two points. The slopes \(w_{,x}\) and \(w_{,y}\) are taken at the same points with the default shear_model = 1, and at the centroid with shear_model = 0 or centroid_slopes = 1, see Quad4.update_KG(). The reason is the hourglass pattern \(w = \xi \eta\) with zero rotations, which has no slope at the centroid. The one-point shear gives it no stiffness in KC0. With the slopes at the two by two points it has the geometric stiffness of a full quadrature: in a supported plate, the pattern modulated by a smooth envelope costs only the shear energy of the envelope, independent of the element size \(\ell\), whereas its geometric work grows as \(1/\ell^2\), and a linear buckling analysis can return a spurious mode of \(w\) alternating in sign from node to node below the physical buckling load once the transverse shear stiffness is low compared with the bending stiffness, as in a sandwich plate. Taken at the centroid, the slopes give the pattern no geometric stiffness, consistently with KC0. The assumed shear strains of the default give the pattern its shear stiffness at every element size, and the slopes at the two by two points can be used: they integrate the geometric stiffness of a smooth mode more accurately, while the slopes at the centroid give higher buckling loads on coarse meshes. Measured on a simply supported plate with 5 elements per half-wave along the load, the buckling load is 4.31 % above the Kirchhoff solution with the default, 6.81 % with centroid_slopes = 1 and 4.67 % with shear_model = 0.

The same slopes enter update_KG, update_KCNL and the non-linear internal forces, so that KC0 + KCNL + KG remains the exact Jacobian for every rule. In the co-rotational frame of CorotationalShell, with local='von_karman', the transverse translations of a warped element are the hourglass pattern of its warp, not a deflection, and the wrapper sets centroid_slopes = 1 while it calls the element.

Membrane and drilling#

With the default drilling_model = 0 the in-plane displacements are enriched with the edge modes of Allman, the mid-side functions of the eight-node serendipity element, whose amplitudes are differences of the drilling rotations of the nodes, see Drilling rotations. The enrichment removes the excessive in-plane bending stiffness of the bilinear membrane: on the skew membrane of Cook with two by two elements the tip displacement is 20.8 against the reference 23.9, where the penalty model gives 11.8, and the element does not lock in the in-plane bending of strips one element deep, unlike Quad4R. The curvature and transverse shear strains are not enriched; with \(\boldsymbol{B} \ne \boldsymbol{0}\) the enriched membrane strains couple \(r_z\) to the curvatures. The enrichment enters the membrane forces of the geometric stiffness as well, see Quad4.update_KG(), and the strains recovered with Quad4Probe.update_BL() must be given the drilling_model of the element.

Warped elements#

The matrices are those of the flat projection of the element, linked to the warped nodes by rigid offsets with the default warp_offsets = 1, see Choosing an element, section Warped quadrilaterals, and the Notes of Quad4.
