Shell elements: kinematics and formulation#

The four shell elements of pyfe3d, the quadrilaterals Quad4 and Quad4R and the triangles Tria3DSG and Tria3R, are flat facets with the six degrees of freedom \(u\), \(v\), \(w\), \(r_x\), \(r_y\), \(r_z\) per node, such that they share the nodes of the beams, of the springs and of each other, see Conventions. A curved shell is modelled as an assembly of flat facets, each one a plate in which the membrane and the bending stiffnesses are superimposed and coupled through the laminate. This chapter gives the theory that the four elements have in common: the kinematics, the constitutive relation, the interpolation, the element matrices of the linear and of the geometrically non-linear analyses, and the reason why each element treats the transverse shear differently. The pages of the elements give what is specific to each one: Quad4: assumed transverse shear strains (MITC4), Quad4R: one-point integration and hourglass control, Tria3DSG: the discrete shear gap triangle and Tria3R: discrete shear gap with the Allman membrane; the stiffness of the drilling rotation and the mass matrices are treated in Drilling rotations and Mass matrices of the shells, the properties in Shell properties, and the choice of an element in Choosing an element.

Facets and element coordinates#

Every element matrix is computed in the element coordinate system \(x\), \(y\), \(z\), with \(z\) normal to the facet, and rotated to the global one with the transformation matrix \(\boldsymbol{T}\) of the element, see Conventions:

(1)#\[\boldsymbol{K} = \boldsymbol{T} \boldsymbol{K}_e \boldsymbol{T}^\top, \qquad \boldsymbol{F} = \boldsymbol{T} \boldsymbol{F}_e\]

The three nodes of a triangle define its plane. The four nodes of a quadrilateral on a curved surface are in general not coplanar, and the matrices of Quad4 and Quad4R are those of the flat element in the mean plane of the four nodes, which is parallel to both diagonals; the rigid offsets that link the flat element to the warped nodes are described in Choosing an element, section Warped quadrilaterals. The coordinates of the nodes in the element system are the attribute xe of the probe of each element, e.g. Quad4Probe, set by update_probe_xe.

Kinematics of the first-order shear deformation theory#

The elements follow the first-order shear deformation theory (FSDT). The displacements of a point at the distance \(z\) from the reference surface are

(2)#\[u(x, y, z) = u(x, y) + z \, r_y(x, y), \qquad v(x, y, z) = v(x, y) - z \, r_x(x, y), \qquad w(x, y, z) = w(x, y)\]

with \(r_x\) and \(r_y\) the right-hand rotations about the element axes. In terms of the rotations of the transverse normal of the FSDT, \(\phi_x\) and \(\phi_y\), which give \(u + z\phi_x\) and \(v + z\phi_y\), they are \(r_y = \phi_x\) and \(r_x = -\phi_y\). Keeping only the linear terms, the in-plane strains are the sum of a part constant through the thickness and of a part linear in \(z\), and the transverse shear strains are constant through the thickness:

(3)#\[\begin{split}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{(0)} = \begin{Bmatrix} \varepsilon_{xx} \\ \varepsilon_{yy} \\ \gamma_{xy} \end{Bmatrix} = \begin{Bmatrix} u_{,x} \\ v_{,y} \\ u_{,y} + v_{,x} \end{Bmatrix}, \qquad \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{(1)} = \begin{Bmatrix} \kappa_{xx} \\ \kappa_{yy} \\ \kappa_{xy} \end{Bmatrix} = \begin{Bmatrix} r_{y,x} \\ -r_{x,y} \\ r_{y,y} - r_{x,x} \end{Bmatrix}, \qquad \boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(0)} = \begin{Bmatrix} \gamma_{yz} \\ \gamma_{xz} \end{Bmatrix} = \begin{Bmatrix} w_{,y} - r_x \\ w_{,x} + r_y \end{Bmatrix}\end{split}\]

The membrane strains \(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{(0)}\) depend on \(u\) and \(v\), the curvatures \(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{(1)}\), written \(\boldsymbol{\kappa}\) in the Notes of the elements, on \(r_x\) and \(r_y\), and the transverse shear strains \(\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(0)}\) on \(w\), \(r_x\) and \(r_y\). The transverse shear strains are ordered \(\{\gamma_{yz}, \gamma_{xz}\}\), as the transverse shear stiffnesses \(A_{44}\), \(A_{45}\), \(A_{55}\) of ShellProp, index 4 for \(yz\) and 5 for \(xz\). The drilling rotation \(r_z\) appears in none of the strains of (3): its stiffness is the subject of Drilling rotations. The rows of these strains are the attributes BLexx, BLeyy, BLgxy, BLkxx, BLkyy, BLkxy and of the transverse shear of the probes, see Quad4Probe.update_BL().

Stress resultants and constitutive relation#

With the membrane forces \(\boldsymbol{N} = \{N_{xx}, N_{yy}, N_{xy}\}\), the moments \(\boldsymbol{M} = \{M_{xx}, M_{yy}, M_{xy}\}\) and the transverse shear forces \(\boldsymbol{Q} = \{Q_y, Q_x\}\), the thickness integrals of the stresses, of the stresses times \(z\) and of the transverse shear stresses, and the plane-stress stiffnesses of the plies, the constitutive relation of the element is

(4)#\[\begin{split}\begin{Bmatrix} \boldsymbol{N} \\ \boldsymbol{M} \end{Bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \boldsymbol{A} & \boldsymbol{B} \\ \boldsymbol{B} & \boldsymbol{D} \end{bmatrix} \begin{Bmatrix} \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{(0)} \\ \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{(1)} \end{Bmatrix}, \qquad \boldsymbol{Q} = \boldsymbol{A}_{ts} \boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(0)}, \qquad \boldsymbol{A}_{ts} = \begin{bmatrix} A_{44} & A_{45} \\ A_{45} & A_{55} \end{bmatrix}\end{split}\]

with the extensional, extension-bending coupling and bending stiffness matrices \(\boldsymbol{A}\), \(\boldsymbol{B}\), \(\boldsymbol{D}\) and the transverse shear stiffness matrix \(\boldsymbol{A}_{ts}\), which contains the shear correction, of a ShellProp, see Shell properties. The coupling matrix \(\boldsymbol{B}\) is not to be confused with the strain-displacement matrices below, which always carry a subscript. The elements read the four matrices in their own coordinate system through the rotation m11, m12, m21, m22 from the material direction to the element \(x\) axis, see ShellProp.calc_constitutive_element() and ShellProp.calc_Ats_element(), and apply no shear correction factor of their own.

The strain energy of the element is

(5)#\[U = \frac{1}{2} \int_A \left( \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{(0)\top} \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{(0)} + 2 \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{(0)\top} \boldsymbol{B} \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{(1)} + \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{(1)\top} \boldsymbol{D} \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{(1)} + \boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(0)\top} \boldsymbol{A}_{ts} \boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(0)} \right) dA + U_{r_z}\]

with \(U_{r_z}\) the energy of the drilling term, to which Quad4R adds the energy of its hourglass control, see Quad4R: one-point integration and hourglass control.

Interpolation and element matrices#

All six fields are interpolated with the same shape functions \(S_i\), the bilinear functions of the natural coordinates \(\xi\), \(\eta\) for the quadrilaterals,

(6)#\[S_1 = \frac{(1 - \xi)(1 - \eta)}{4}, \quad S_2 = \frac{(1 + \xi)(1 - \eta)}{4}, \quad S_3 = \frac{(1 + \xi)(1 + \eta)}{4}, \quad S_4 = \frac{(1 - \xi)(1 + \eta)}{4}\]

and the area coordinates \(S_1\), \(S_2\), \(S_3\) for the triangles, such that \(u = \boldsymbol{S}^u \boldsymbol{u}_e\), …, \(r_z = \boldsymbol{S}^{r_z} \boldsymbol{u}_e\), with \(\boldsymbol{u}_e\) the element displacements in the order of Conventions. The same functions map the geometry of the quadrilateral, and the Cartesian derivatives follow from the Jacobian matrix,

(7)#\[\begin{split}\begin{Bmatrix} \partial/\partial x \\ \partial/\partial y \end{Bmatrix} = \boldsymbol{J}^{-1} \begin{Bmatrix} \partial/\partial \xi \\ \partial/\partial \eta \end{Bmatrix}, \qquad \boldsymbol{J} = \begin{bmatrix} x_{,\xi} & y_{,\xi} \\ x_{,\eta} & y_{,\eta} \end{bmatrix}, \qquad dx \, dy = \det \boldsymbol{J} \, d\xi \, d\eta\end{split}\]

whereas the derivatives of the area coordinates are constant, e.g. \(S_{1,x} = (y_2 - y_3)/(2 A_e)\) and \(S_{1,y} = (x_3 - x_2)/(2 A_e)\), with \(A_e\) the area of the triangle. The strains (3) become \(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{(0)} = \boldsymbol{B}_m \boldsymbol{u}_e\), \(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{(1)} = \boldsymbol{B}_b \boldsymbol{u}_e\) and \(\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(0)} = \boldsymbol{B}_s \boldsymbol{u}_e\), with

(8)#\[\begin{split}\boldsymbol{B}_m = \begin{bmatrix} \boldsymbol{S}^u_{,x} \\ \boldsymbol{S}^v_{,y} \\ \boldsymbol{S}^u_{,y} + \boldsymbol{S}^v_{,x} \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \boldsymbol{B}_b = \begin{bmatrix} \boldsymbol{S}^{r_y}_{,x} \\ -\boldsymbol{S}^{r_x}_{,y} \\ \boldsymbol{S}^{r_y}_{,y} - \boldsymbol{S}^{r_x}_{,x} \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \boldsymbol{B}_s = \begin{bmatrix} \boldsymbol{S}^w_{,y} - \boldsymbol{S}^{r_x} \\ \boldsymbol{S}^w_{,x} + \boldsymbol{S}^{r_y} \end{bmatrix}\end{split}\]

and the constitutive stiffness matrix of the element, the Hessian of (5), is

(9)#\[\begin{split}\boldsymbol{K}_{C0_e} = \int_A \begin{bmatrix} \boldsymbol{B}_m \\ \boldsymbol{B}_b \end{bmatrix}^\top \begin{bmatrix} \boldsymbol{A} & \boldsymbol{B} \\ \boldsymbol{B} & \boldsymbol{D} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \boldsymbol{B}_m \\ \boldsymbol{B}_b \end{bmatrix} dA + \int_A \boldsymbol{B}_s^\top \boldsymbol{A}_{ts} \boldsymbol{B}_s \, dA + \boldsymbol{K}_{C0_e}^{r_z}\end{split}\]

computed by update_KC0, e.g. Quad4.update_KC0(), and rotated with (1). With \(\boldsymbol{B} = \boldsymbol{0}\) the first integral splits into a membrane term on \(u\), \(v\) and a bending term on \(r_x\), \(r_y\); with an offset of the reference surface or an unsymmetric laminate they are coupled. The internal forces of update_fint are the gradient of (5), \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0_e} \boldsymbol{u}_e\) in the linear case.

The triangles are linear: their strains \(\boldsymbol{B}_m\), \(\boldsymbol{B}_b\) and \(\boldsymbol{B}_s\) are constant, and one point integrates them exactly, with the weight \(A_e\), i.e. \(1/2\) times \(\det \boldsymbol{J} = 2 A_e\). The bilinear quadrilateral has strains that vary over the element, and the choice of the quadrature is what controls its locking and its spurious modes.

Shear locking and spurious modes#

The transverse shear stiffness scales with \(h\) and the bending stiffness with \(h^3\), such that their ratio grows as \((\ell/h)^2\), \(\ell\) a length of the element. In bending, the FSDT approaches the constraint \(\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(0)} \to \boldsymbol{0}\) of the Kirchhoff plate. If the interpolated \(\boldsymbol{B}_s\) cannot vanish in a state of pure bending, the element stores parasitic shear energy that grows as \((\ell/h)^2\) relative to its bending energy, and becomes far too stiff: this is shear locking. The bilinear quadrilateral integrated with two by two points locks, because its displacement-based shear strains contain bilinear terms that a constant curvature cannot switch off; the linear triangle locks because its \(\nabla w\) is a single constant vector while the rotations are linear, such that no nodal values satisfy \(\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(0)} = \boldsymbol{0}\) in a state of constant curvature.

Integrating the shear with fewer points removes the parasitic terms, but leaves zero-energy (hourglass) modes, deformation patterns with no strain at the integration points: with one point for the shear, the bilinear quadrilateral has two of them besides the rigid-body modes (Hughes et al., 1977). pyfe3d uses four remedies, each with its own page:

  • assumed transverse shear strains, the mixed interpolation of the covariant shear strains of Dvorkin and Bathe (1984), tied at the mid-points of the edges: Quad4, free of shear locking and of spurious modes, see Quad4: assumed transverse shear strains (MITC4);

  • one-point integration with physical stabilisation: Quad4R integrates every term at the centroid and gives the hourglass modes of the bending and of the transverse shear the stiffness of the non-constant part of the strains, after Belytschko and Leviathan (1994), with the same assumed shear strains, see Quad4R: one-point integration and hourglass control;

  • discrete shear gap of Bletzinger, Bischoff and Ramm (2000): the shear strains of both triangles are the gradient of an interpolated shear gap, which vanishes identically in a state of constant curvature, see Tria3DSG: the discrete shear gap triangle;

  • algebraic rescaling of the transverse shear stiffness by \(1/(1 + \alpha \ell^2/h^2)\), Lyly, Stenberg and Vihinen (1993), available on the triangles with alpha_shear_locking, 0. by default, see Tria3DSG: the discrete shear gap triangle and Tria3R: discrete shear gap with the Allman membrane.

The first three remove the locking in the strain operator, such that the transverse shear stiffness of the ShellProp is used as the laminate gives it, and the shear correction keeps its meaning.

Geometrically non-linear terms#

The geometrically non-linear analyses use the von Kármán membrane strains of the facet,

(10)#\[\varepsilon_{xx} = u_{,x} + \frac{1}{2} w_{,x}^2, \qquad \varepsilon_{yy} = v_{,y} + \frac{1}{2} w_{,y}^2, \qquad \gamma_{xy} = u_{,y} + v_{,x} + w_{,x} w_{,y}\]

whose non-linear part is \(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{NL} = \frac{1}{2} \boldsymbol{B}_{NL} \boldsymbol{u}_e\), with the rows \(w_{,x} \boldsymbol{G}_x\), \(w_{,y} \boldsymbol{G}_y\) and \(w_{,x} \boldsymbol{G}_y + w_{,y} \boldsymbol{G}_x\) of \(\boldsymbol{B}_{NL}\), \(\boldsymbol{G}_x = \partial \boldsymbol{S}^w/\partial x\) and \(\boldsymbol{G}_y = \partial \boldsymbol{S}^w/\partial y\) being the rows of the slopes of \(w\), the attributes Sw_x and Sw_y of the probes. The curvatures and the transverse shear strains remain linear. The tangent stiffness matrix of the internal forces of update_fint with nonlinear=1 is split into three matrices,

(11)#\[\boldsymbol{K}_T = \boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + \left( \boldsymbol{K}_{CNL} + \boldsymbol{K}_{GNL} \right) + \boldsymbol{K}_G\]

computed by update_KC0, update_KCNL and update_KG, e.g. Quad4.update_KCNL(). The geometric stiffness matrix of the membrane forces \(\{N_{xx}, N_{yy}, N_{xy}\} = \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{(0)} + \boldsymbol{B} \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{(1)}\) of the linear strains is

(12)#\[\boldsymbol{K}_G = \int_A \left( N_{xx} \boldsymbol{G}_x^\top \boldsymbol{G}_x + N_{yy} \boldsymbol{G}_y^\top \boldsymbol{G}_y + N_{xy} \left( \boldsymbol{G}_x^\top \boldsymbol{G}_y + \boldsymbol{G}_y^\top \boldsymbol{G}_x \right) \right) dA\]

which involves only \(w\), the displacement along the normal of the element, as in the Donnell theory. It is linear in the displacements, such that \((\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + \lambda \boldsymbol{K}_G) \boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{0}\) is the linear buckling problem of a pre-buckling state multiplied by \(\lambda\), see Linear buckling and natural frequencies; update_KG_given_stress computes it for given constant membrane forces. \(\boldsymbol{K}_{CNL}\) collects the constitutive terms that contain \(\boldsymbol{B}_{NL}\), \(\boldsymbol{B}_m^\top \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{B}_{NL} + \boldsymbol{B}_b^\top \boldsymbol{B} \boldsymbol{B}_{NL}\), its transpose and \(\boldsymbol{B}_{NL}^\top \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{B}_{NL}\), and \(\boldsymbol{K}_{GNL}\) is (12) with the membrane forces \(\boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{NL}\) of the non-linear strains; it is computed with \(\boldsymbol{K}_{CNL}\) so that \(\boldsymbol{K}_G\) stays linear. Crisfield (1991), Section 8.1.5, groups the same terms differently, with the total membrane forces in his initial-stress matrix, and the sum is the same. Each part of the strain energy is differentiated with its own quadrature and with the same slopes of \(w\), such that (11) is the exact Jacobian of the internal forces and the Newton-Raphson iterations of Non-linear static analyses converge quadratically. In Quad4 and Quad4R the point where the slopes of \(w\) are taken matters, because of the hourglass pattern \(w = \xi \eta\), which has no slope at the centroid, see Quad4: assumed transverse shear strains (MITC4).

The von Kármán strains hold for moderate rotations. For large rotations, every shell element can be wrapped in the element-independent co-rotational formulation of CorotationalShell, see Non-linear static analyses.

Aerodynamic matrices of the piston theory#

Quad4 and Quad4R give the matrices of the aerodynamic load of a supersonic flow along the global \(X\) axis, flowing towards the positive \(X\). The linear piston theory, with the correction of Krumhaar (1963) for a cylinder, gives the load per unit area along the normal of the element

(13)#\[q = -\beta \, w_{n,X} + \gamma \, w_n - \mu \, \dot{w}_n\]

with \(w_n = T_{13} u + T_{23} v + T_{33} w\) the displacement along the element normal written with the global translations, whatever the side of the element on which the flow is. With the bilinear functions \(\boldsymbol{S}\) acting on the nodal values of \(w_n\), the matrices of Quad4.update_KA_beta(), Quad4.update_KA_gamma() and Quad4.update_CA() are

(14)#\[\boldsymbol{K}_{A_\beta} = -\int_A \boldsymbol{S}^\top \left( T_{11} \boldsymbol{S}_{,x} + T_{12} \boldsymbol{S}_{,y} \right) dA, \qquad \boldsymbol{K}_{A_\gamma} = \int_A \boldsymbol{S}^\top \boldsymbol{S} \, dA, \qquad \boldsymbol{C}_A = -\int_A \boldsymbol{S}^\top \boldsymbol{S} \, dA\]

\(T_{11} \boldsymbol{S}_{,x} + T_{12} \boldsymbol{S}_{,y}\) being the derivative along \(X\) projected on the element. The matrices contain no aerodynamic coefficient, and the equations of motion are

(15)#\[\boldsymbol{M} \ddot{\boldsymbol{u}} - \mu \boldsymbol{C}_A \dot{\boldsymbol{u}} + \left( \boldsymbol{K} - \beta \boldsymbol{K}_{A_\beta} - \gamma \boldsymbol{K}_{A_\gamma} \right) \boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{0}\]

with, for the density \(\rho_\infty\), the speed \(U\) and the Mach number \(M\) of the flow, \(\beta = \rho_\infty U^2/\sqrt{M^2 - 1}\), \(\mu = \beta (M^2 - 2)/(U (M^2 - 1))\), and \(\gamma = \beta/(2 R \sqrt{M^2 - 1})\) for the flow over the convex side of a cylinder of radius \(R\), \(\gamma = 0\) for a flat panel. The system has the form of the damped problem of complex_frequency(), see Linear buckling and natural frequencies. The triangles have no aerodynamic matrices.

Limits of validity#

  • Facets. The curvature of a shell is represented only by the angles between neighbouring facets, and the membrane-bending coupling of a curved shell appears only through them; the drilling enrichment of Quad4 and Tria3R stiffens coarse meshes of doubly curved shells, see Tria3R: discrete shear gap with the Allman membrane. A warped quadrilateral is represented by its flat projection, corrected with rigid offsets up to the warping ratios given in Choosing an element.

  • FSDT. Plane stress in each ply, an inextensible transverse normal and transverse shear strains constant through the thickness, whose energy is made consistent by the shear correction of the ShellProp; the transverse shear stresses must be recovered from the shear forces, see Shell properties.

  • Material. Linear elastic, small strains, no thermal strains.

  • Von Kármán strains. Moderate rotations about the in-plane axes; larger rotations need the co-rotational formulation.

  • Thin limit. Hughes et al. (1977) note that the transverse shear stiffness is of the order of \((\ell/h)^2\) times the bending stiffness, such that round-off removes the bending stiffness of very thin elements. pyfe3d applies no correction: in double precision the central deflection of a square plate with eight by eight Quad4 elements changes by \(10^{-4}\) from \(\ell/h = 1.25 \times 10^3\) to \(1.25 \times 10^5\), and by -33 % at \(\ell/h = 1.25 \times 10^7\), where the round-off dominates, see the Notes of pyfe3d.quad4.
