Eigenvalue solvers and their verification#

The eigenvalue problems of Linear buckling and natural frequencies are large and sparse, and only a few eigenvalues are wanted: the lowest positive load multipliers, the lowest frequencies, the eigenvalues of a damped system nearest a shift. The iterative solvers of ARPACK, through scipy.sparse.linalg.eigsh() and scipy.sparse.linalg.eigs(), find them from one factorization, but they return the eigenvalues nearest a shift, not necessarily the lowest ones, may miss copies of repeated eigenvalues, and do not raise when they do. pyfe3d.solver.eigen therefore transforms each problem into one that the sparse solvers solve reliably, verifies every result, and uses a dense solver only as a last resort. This page describes the transformations, the verifications and the solver policy shared by linear_buckling(), natural_frequency() and complex_frequency().

Equilibration of the diagonal#

The matrices of beam and shell models mix translations and rotations, such that the diagonal of \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\) spans many orders of magnitude; the fictitious drilling stiffness of the shells widens the spread further. With precondition=True, the default, the problems are solved with the matrices \(\boldsymbol{P} \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{P}\) and \(\boldsymbol{P} \boldsymbol{B} \boldsymbol{P}\) of the Jacobi scaling

(1)#\[P_{ii} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\max(|K_{ii}|, \mathrm{floor})}}\]

of diagonal_preconditioner(), whose \(\boldsymbol{P} \boldsymbol{K} \boldsymbol{P}\) has a unit diagonal up to the signs. Since \(\boldsymbol{P}\) is non-singular, \(\boldsymbol{P} \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{P} \boldsymbol{y} = w \boldsymbol{P} \boldsymbol{B} \boldsymbol{P} \boldsymbol{y}\) is \(\boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{u} = w \boldsymbol{B} \boldsymbol{u}\) with \(\boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{P} \boldsymbol{y}\): the eigenvalues are unchanged, the eigenvectors are transformed back, and the verification always uses the original matrices. The equilibration usually reduces the condition number and often decides whether the iterative solver converges.

Linear buckling: the Cayley mode#

linear_buckling() writes (1) as

(2)#\[\boldsymbol{K}_G \boldsymbol{u} = \mu \boldsymbol{K}_{C0} \boldsymbol{u}, \qquad \lambda = -\frac{1}{\mu}\]

whose lowest positive load multipliers are the most negative \(\mu\). For symmetric matrices with a positive definite \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\), it calls scipy.sparse.linalg.eigsh() in the Cayley mode, in which ARPACK computes the eigenvalues \((\mu + \sigma)/(\mu - \sigma)\) of the operator \((\boldsymbol{K}_G - \sigma \boldsymbol{K}_{C0})^{-1}(\boldsymbol{K}_G + \sigma \boldsymbol{K}_{C0})\) (Lehoucq et al. 1998, Appendix A, Sec. A.1), and which='SM' selects those of smallest modulus. When the shift \(\sigma\) is larger than \(|\mu|\) of all the eigenvalues, \((\mu + \sigma)/(\mu - \sigma)\) increases in modulus with \(\mu\) in \((-\sigma, \sigma)\), and the most negative \(\mu\), i.e. the lowest positive load multipliers, come first; otherwise the eigenvalues closest to \(-\sigma\) come back instead, without warning, see pyfe3d.solver.eigen._eigsh_cayley(). The shift is therefore estimated from the matrices by estimate_cayley_sigma(): power iterations on \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}^{-1} \boldsymbol{K}_G\), self-adjoint in the inner product of \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\), whose ratio of \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\)-norms grows towards the spectral radius \(\max|\mu|\), multiplied by a safety factor of 10. The second sparse attempt uses a 10 times larger shift.

The negative load multipliers, those of the reversed load, are the positive \(\mu\). When fewer than num_eigvalues positive multipliers exist, the remaining ones are computed with \(-\boldsymbol{K}_G\); the number requested of each sign is limited by the count of the multipliers of that sign, from the inertia below, since ARPACK may not converge when it is asked for more eigenvalues of one sign than exist, see pyfe3d.solver.eigen._eigsh_both_signs().

Verification: residuals and the inertia count#

Every eigenpair returned by a sparse solver is verified, see check_eigenpairs():

  • the relative residual of each eigenpair, e.g. for buckling

    (3)#\[\frac{\|\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} \boldsymbol{u} + \lambda \boldsymbol{K}_G \boldsymbol{u}\|} {\|\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} \boldsymbol{u}\| + |\lambda| \, \|\boldsymbol{K}_G \boldsymbol{u}\|}\]

    must not exceed check_rtol, \(10^{-3}\) for linear_buckling() and \(10^{-6}\) for the frequencies; for a rigid-body mode, whose residual and denominator are both round-off errors, the residual is taken relative to the matrix norms, see pyfe3d.solver.eigen._relative_residuals();

  • for symmetric matrices, the absence of an eigenvalue missed below the highest one returned, from the inertia of a shifted matrix. With a positive definite \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\), \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + s \boldsymbol{K}_G\) is congruent to \(\boldsymbol{I} + s \boldsymbol{K}_{C0}^{-1/2} \boldsymbol{K}_G \boldsymbol{K}_{C0}^{-1/2}\), whose eigenvalues are \(1 - s/\lambda_i\), negative exactly when \(0 < \lambda_i < s\): the number of negative pivots of the factorization of

    (4)#\[\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + s \boldsymbol{K}_G \quad \text{(buckling)}, \qquad \boldsymbol{K} - s \boldsymbol{M} \quad \text{(frequencies)}\]

    is, by Sylvester’s law of inertia, the number of load multipliers in \((0, s)\), or of the \(\omega_n^2 < s\), which must equal the number returned below \(s\), with \(s\) just below the highest eigenvalue returned. This is the Sturm sequence check of the eigenvalue solvers of structural analysis (Bathe 2014, Sec. 10.2.2, p. 849, Sec. 11.4.3, p. 943, and Sec. 11.6, p. 954), and it is what distinguishes a converged answer from a plausible-looking one. The factorization is that of is_positive_definite(), with a symmetric permutation and without row interchanges.

An eigenpair of natural_frequency() or complex_frequency() whose relative residual is too large still passes when its normwise backward error

(5)#\[\eta = \frac{\|\boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{u} + \lambda \boldsymbol{B} \boldsymbol{u}\|}{(\|\boldsymbol{A}\|_F + |\lambda| \, \|\boldsymbol{B}\|_F) \|\boldsymbol{u}\|}\]

is not larger than the machine precision: the eigenpair is then exact for matrices that differ from the given ones by perturbations of the order of the round-off of their entries (Tisseur 2000, Theorem 1, Eq. (2.3)), e.g. an eigenvalue close to zero of a model pre-stressed close to buckling, see pyfe3d.solver.eigen._backward_errors() and pyfe3d.solver.eigen._quadratic_backward_errors(). The buckling analysis keeps the relative residual only: a finite load multiplier is never null, and a backward error at the round-off does not tell that an eigenpair is accurate when the matrix norms are set by stiff penalty springs.

Repeated eigenvalues#

Structures with a symmetry have eigenvalues equal up to the round-off, e.g. the pairs of modes \(\cos n\theta\) and \(\sin n\theta\) of a cylinder with a rotationally symmetric mesh. The Lanczos process of ARPACK, started from a single vector, finds in exact arithmetic one eigenvector of each eigenspace only, and the other copies only through the rounding errors. When the inertia count finds eigenvalues missing below the highest one returned, they are computed by deflated passes before the next solver, see pyfe3d.solver.eigen._complete_repeated(): each pass calls deflated_eigsh(), the shift-invert mode with the eigenvectors already found deflated from the operator and another starting vector, which returns the eigenvalues nearest the shift not found yet, and the eigenvectors are combined with a Rayleigh-Ritz projection that keeps them orthonormal; the result is verified as any other. tests/test_solver_linear_buckling.py checks the completion on a Quad4 cylinder under torsion.

Natural frequencies: shift-invert mode#

natural_frequency() computes the eigenvalues \(\omega_n^2\) nearest the shift \(\sigma = -1\) in the shift-invert mode, with scipy.sparse.linalg.eigsh() for symmetric matrices and scipy.sparse.linalg.eigs() otherwise, factorizing \(\boldsymbol{K} + \boldsymbol{M}\): these are the lowest ones when \(\boldsymbol{K}\) is positive semi-definite and \(\boldsymbol{M}\) positive definite, and \(\boldsymbol{K} + \boldsymbol{M}\) is not singular when \(\boldsymbol{K}\) is singular only because of unconstrained rigid-body modes that have mass. An indefinite \(\boldsymbol{K}\), e.g. pre-stressed beyond buckling, is accepted, its negative \(\omega_n^2\) giving null frequencies. The eigenpairs are verified with the residuals, the backward error and, for symmetric matrices, the inertia count of (4), see pyfe3d.solver.eigen._check_freq().

Unsymmetric problems#

The load stiffness of a follower load makes the buckling problem unsymmetric, with possibly complex load multipliers, many of which may have a smaller modulus than the critical one. linear_buckling() then searches along the positive real axis: scipy.sparse.linalg.eigs() finds the multipliers nearest a shift \(s_j\) within a radius \(r_j\); the next shift is placed beyond the interval of the real axis already covered, until the lowest positive real multiplier found is not beyond the covered interval, see pyfe3d.solver.eigen._eigs_shift_search(). A parity check completes the verification of the residuals:

(6)#\[\det(\boldsymbol{K} + s \boldsymbol{K}_G) = \det \boldsymbol{K} \prod_i (1 + s \mu_i)\]

where a complex conjugate pair gives the positive factor \(|1 + s \mu_i|^2\) and a real \(\mu_i\) a negative factor exactly when \(0 < \lambda_i < s\), so \(\det(\boldsymbol{K} + s \boldsymbol{K}_G)\) just below the critical multiplier must have the sign of \(\det \boldsymbol{K}\). The signs come from the LU factorizations. This check cannot detect two missing real multipliers, nor a missing complex pair.

Damped frequencies: linearization#

complex_frequency() writes the quadratic problem (5), with \(\boldsymbol{z} = \{\boldsymbol{u}^\top, \lambda \boldsymbol{u}^\top\}^\top\), as the generalized eigenvalue problem of size \(2n\)

(7)#\[\begin{split}\begin{bmatrix} \boldsymbol{0} & \boldsymbol{I} \\ -\boldsymbol{K} & -\boldsymbol{C} \end{bmatrix} \boldsymbol{z} = \lambda \begin{bmatrix} \boldsymbol{I} & \boldsymbol{0} \\ \boldsymbol{0} & \boldsymbol{M} \end{bmatrix} \boldsymbol{z}\end{split}\]

the first companion form of Tisseur and Meerbergen (2001, Sec. 3.4, Eq. (3.6)), valid for any \(\boldsymbol{C}\), symmetric or not, and requiring neither a non-singular \(\boldsymbol{K}\) nor a non-singular \(\boldsymbol{M}\), whose singularity only gives infinite eigenvalues, discarded. Its shift-invert operator needs the factorization of the \(n \times n\) matrix \(\boldsymbol{K}_{dyn}(\sigma) = \sigma^2 \boldsymbol{M} + \sigma \boldsymbol{C} + \boldsymbol{K}\) only (Sec. 6.1, Eq. (6.3)), see pyfe3d.solver.eigen._eigs_qep(). The default shift \(\sigma = 1\) rad/s is the counterpart of the shift \(-1\) of natural_frequency(): with \(\boldsymbol{C} = \boldsymbol{0}\) the factorized matrix is the same, \(\boldsymbol{K} + \boldsymbol{M}\), and, for \(\lambda = \omega_n(-\zeta \pm i \sqrt{1 - \zeta^2})\), \(|\lambda - 1|^2 = 1 + 2 \zeta \omega_n + \omega_n^2\) grows with \(\omega_n\) for \(\omega_n > -\zeta\), such that the lowest frequencies are found first. The symmetric linearizations of symmetric matrices give indefinite pencils, unless the system is overdamped (Sec. 3.8.2), and exclude the unsymmetric matrices of the piston theory. The residuals and the backward errors are verified, but no inertia count applies to the complex eigenvalues of a damped system: that no eigenvalue nearer the shift was missed is not verified, and the dense solution, sparse_solver=False, is the reference.

Solver policy and dense fallback#

With sparse_solver=True, the default, the sparse solvers run first for every problem size, and each result must pass the verification. Only when all of them failed, e.g. a failed verification, an ARPACK error or a singular factorization, a dense solver of SciPy is used, if the dense problem has at most max_dense_size dofs, 2000 by default, with a DenseFallbackWarning giving the reasons of the failures; its result is verified as well. Otherwise a RuntimeError lists every attempted solver, such that no unverified result is returned silently, see pyfe3d.solver.eigen._sparse_first(). The dense fallback of the buckling problem condenses out the dofs where KG has null rows, see pyfe3d.solver.eigen._condense(). sparse_solver=False solves the full problem with a dense solver and returns all the eigenvalues, without verification, as a reference for validations, and check=False returns the first sparse result without verification. The fallback can be turned into an error, e.g. in a test suite, with warnings.simplefilter('error', DenseFallbackWarning).

For symmetric matrices, linear_buckling() requires a positive definite \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\), the matrix of its Cayley mode and of its inertia count: with check=True a negative or a zero pivot raises a numpy.linalg.LinAlgError naming the likely causes, insufficient boundary conditions, a mechanism, distorted elements or a preload beyond the buckling load, see Boundary conditions and prescribed displacements. When the solvers of natural_frequency() fail and \(\boldsymbol{K}\) is not positive definite, its RuntimeError starts with the same diagnosis.

A dof without stiffness, a null row or column of \(\boldsymbol{K}\), raises a numpy.linalg.LinAlgError naming the dofs, see check_null_rows_cols(): with the same null row of \(\boldsymbol{K}_G\) or \(\boldsymbol{M}\) the pencil is singular, every value being an eigenvalue, and otherwise it gives a spurious null eigenvalue. \(\boldsymbol{K}_G\), \(\boldsymbol{C}\) and \(\boldsymbol{M}\) may have null rows, e.g. a lumped mass matrix without rotary inertia. is_symmetric() selects the solvers of symmetric or unsymmetric matrices when symmetric=None.

ARPACK and Intel MKL#

With Intel MKL 2024.2.0 to 2025.0.0, the routine dsteqr, which the symmetric drivers of ARPACK call on the tridiagonal matrix of the Lanczos process, returns wrong eigenvectors for matrices larger than 32 by 32, and scipy.sparse.linalg.eigsh() returns wrong eigenpairs, without error, whenever its number of Lanczos vectors ncv exceeds 32. These versions are detected, see dsteqr_is_faulty() and arpack_max_ncv(), and ncv is then capped at 32: capped_eigsh() computes more than 15 eigenpairs in passes with the eigenvectors already found deflated, combined with a Rayleigh-Ritz projection, see pyfe3d.solver.arpackutils, which also describes the override with the environment variable PYFE3D_ARPACK_MAX_NCV. The verification above catches such a failure too, which is another reason to keep it on.
