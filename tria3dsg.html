Tria3DSG - Triangular element with discrete shear gap (pyfe3d.tria3dsg)#

The module has three classes: Tria3DSGData, with the sizes of the sparse arrays that one element fills; Tria3DSGProbe, with the buffers in element coordinates that one or more elements share; and Tria3DSG, the element. The update_* methods of Tria3DSG write the element contributions in sparse row, column and value arrays, from the positions given by its init_k_* attributes, or add them to a global internal force vector.

Three-node triangular shell element with 6 degrees-of-freedom per node, \(u\), \(v\), \(w\), \(r_x\), \(r_y\), \(r_z\), first-order shear deformation theory, and a transverse shear field obtained with the discrete shear gap (DSG) method.

The membrane, bending and transverse shear operators of the element are formed once per element, without a quadrature loop. The transverse shear stiffnesses of the pyfe3d.shellprop.ShellProp are used as given, unless the alpha_shear_locking attribute of Tria3DSG, 0. by default, is set. The drilling rotation \(r_z\) is tied to the in-plane rotation of the membrane field with a coefficient selected by the drilling_model attribute: by default the in-plane shear stiffness A66 of the laminate, otherwise a small fictitious penalty scaled by K6ROT. The in-plane displacement field has no drilling enrichment; for problems dominated by in-plane bending, pyfe3d.tria3r.Tria3R and pyfe3d.quad4.Quad4 carry one. Geometrically nonlinear analyses with the von Karman membrane strains are supported by Tria3DSG.update_fint() with nonlinear=1, Tria3DSG.update_KCNL() and Tria3DSG.update_KG().

Notes#

The transverse shear field is obtained with the discrete shear gap (DSG) method of Bletzinger, Bischoff and Ramm (2000) [1].

Why this element exists

pyfe3d.tria3r.Tria3R takes its transverse shear from a single point at the centroid, which still locks, and cures the locking by dividing the transverse shear stiffnesses by \(1 + \alpha \ell^2/h^2\) with \(\alpha = 0.7\), following Lyly, Stenberg and Vihinen (1993) [2]. That works for plate bending, where the error it introduces is \(O(1/n^2)\) in the number of elements per span, but it leaves the element unable to reproduce a state of constant transverse shear: measured on an 8 by 8 patch, its stiffness against a transverse displacement field with all rotations at zero is \(3 \times 10^{-1}\), \(2 \times 10^{-2}\) and \(1 \times 10^{-3}\) of the pyfe3d.quad4.Quad4 value at \(\ell/h\) of 1.25, 6.25 and 25. A Donnell geometric stiffness matrix works on \(\partial w/\partial x\), which is exactly that subspace, so thin-shell buckling suffers badly. See tests/test_transverse_shear_stiffness.py.

The DSG removes the locking in the operator instead of in the constitutive matrix, so no stabilisation parameter is needed and the transverse shear stiffness is used as the laminate gives it.

The algebraic stabilisation of Lyly, Stenberg and Vihinen (1993) [2], called Stenberg’s method by Bischoff and Bletzinger (2004) [3] (Sec. 3.1), is nevertheless available through the alpha_shear_locking attribute, in the form of Castro et al. (2019) [4] (Eq. (26)), which is 0. by default and then has no effect whatsoever. It is there because Castro et al. apply the factor on top of a discrete shear gap field, as this one, and Lyly et al. on top of the assumed-strain field of the MITC4 quadrilateral, so setting it reproduces the stabilisation they describe, and because on a coarse mesh of a thin plate it does improve the answer: \(\alpha = 0.07\), the lower bound of the range recommended by Castro et al., takes the first natural frequency of a \(5 \times 7\) mesh from \(12.06\) to \(7.93\) per cent above the analytical value and the buckling load of a thin plate from \(+1.71\) to \(+0.11\) per cent. That is the opposite of its role in pyfe3d.tria3r.Tria3R, where the same factor is applied to a centroid-sampled shear field and has to do the unlocking itself, which is why that element defaults to \(\alpha = 0.7\).

The default here is zero because the factor is unbounded in \(\ell/h\), so any non-zero \(\alpha\) removes nearly all of the transverse shear stiffness on a practical shell mesh and with it the three properties listed above. The Notes of Tria3DSG carry the measurements and the recommended value of alpha_shear_locking.

The transverse shear stiffnesses \(A_{44}\), \(A_{45}\) and \(A_{55}\) are read from the pyfe3d.shellprop.ShellProp object with the shear correction already applied, see pyfe3d.shellprop.ShellProp.calc_transverse_shear_stiffness(), and brought to the element coordinate system with pyfe3d.shellprop.ShellProp.calc_Ats_element(), which, with shear_correction='rohwer' and the plies available, re-evaluates the equilibrium-based stiffness of Rohwer (1988) [7] with the plies rotated to the element coordinate system. No shear correction factor is applied by the element.

The discrete shear gap

With \(\boldsymbol{\phi} = (-r_y, r_x)\) the transverse shear strains of this element’s convention are

\[\gamma_{xz} = \frac{\partial w}{\partial x} + r_y \qquad \gamma_{yz} = \frac{\partial w}{\partial y} - r_x\]

that is \(\boldsymbol{\gamma} = \nabla w - \boldsymbol{\phi}\). The shear gap \(\Delta w\) is the part of the increase of the deflection between two points that is due to shear rather than to bending ([1], Sec. 2, Eq. (3)). On the three-node triangle its nodal values are obtained by integrating the shear strains along the straight edges that join node 1 to the other two nodes ([1], Sec. 4.1.1), with \(\boldsymbol{\phi}\) interpolated linearly,

\[\Delta w_1 = 0 \qquad \Delta w_i = (w_i - w_1) - \frac{1}{2}(\boldsymbol{\phi}_1 + \boldsymbol{\phi}_i) \cdot (\boldsymbol{r}_i - \boldsymbol{r}_1)\]

The assumed shear strain is then the gradient of the interpolated gap,

\[\boldsymbol{\gamma} = \nabla \left( \sum_i S_i \Delta w_i \right) = \sum_i \Delta w_i \nabla S_i\]

which is constant over the element because the shape functions are linear. Four properties follow, all of them verified symbolically and then numerically in tests/test_tria3dsg.py:

  • the three rigid-body modes that involve \(w\) give \(\boldsymbol{\gamma} = 0\) exactly;

  • a state of constant transverse shear is reproduced exactly, which is what Tria3R cannot do;

  • a state of constant curvature gives \(\boldsymbol{\gamma} = 0\) exactly, so the element develops no parasitic shear in pure bending. This is the locking-free property, and it is the reason no stabilisation parameter appears anywhere in this element;

  • the operator is a linear functional of the nodal values only and is constant over the element, so it is formed once per element.

The gap is integrated away from one node, which makes the plain operator depend on which node that is, a dependence inherent to the method whose influence on the solution diminishes with mesh refinement ([1], Sec. 4.1.1): measured on an irregular triangle, the element matrix moves by 14 and 17 per cent under the two cyclic relabellings, against \(3 \times 10^{-16}\) for pyfe3d.tria3r.Tria3R, whose shear comes from a symmetric centroid evaluation. A mesh generator orders the nodes of a triangle as it pleases, so this element averages the three operators, one per starting node. The averaging costs nothing: each of the three is separately exact for all of the properties listed above, and those are linear in the operator, so the mean inherits them, which the tests confirm. What it buys is invariance, measured at \(6 \times 10^{-16}\) in tests/test_tria3dsg.py::test_node_numbering_invariance.

Membrane, bending and drilling

The in-plane and curvature fields are the constant-strain ones of the linear triangle, so no locking arises there and one point integrates them exactly. The drilling rotation \(r_z\) is given stiffness with the regularisation of Hughes and Brezzi (1989) [5], tying \(r_z\) to the in-plane rotation \(\theta_z = (v_{,x} - u_{,y})/2\), exactly as in pyfe3d.quad4.Quad4 and pyfe3d.tria3r.Tria3R.

drilling_model = 0, the default, uses the physics-based coefficient \(\gamma_{r_z} = A_{66}\), a modulus of the laminate and not an adjustable parameter. Any other value uses the fictitious penalty \(\gamma_{r_z} = K6ROT \cdot 10^{-6} A_{66}\). With either coefficient the term \(\gamma_{r_z} \int_A (r_z - \theta_z)^2 dA\) is integrated with the three-point rule, see Tria3DSG._update_probe_KC0ve(), whereas the physics-based model of pyfe3d.tria3r.Tria3R, which adds the Allman enrichment, integrates it with a single point at the centroid.

Note

This element does not carry the hierarchical edge-mode enrichment of Allman (1984) [6] that pyfe3d.tria3r.Tria3R and pyfe3d.quad4.Quad4 apply to the in-plane displacement field, so its membrane response in in-plane bending is the stiff one of the constant-strain triangle. The element is aimed at the transverse shear behaviour; for in-plane dominated problems prefer the enriched elements.

Geometrically nonlinear analysis

The element carries the von Karman membrane strains,

\[\epsilon_{xx} = u_{,x} + \frac{1}{2} w_{,x}^2, \quad \epsilon_{yy} = v_{,y} + \frac{1}{2} w_{,y}^2, \quad \gamma_{xy} = u_{,y} + v_{,x} + w_{,x} w_{,y}\]

with the same split of the tangent stiffness as pyfe3d.quad4.Quad4 and pyfe3d.tria3r.Tria3R,

\[\boldsymbol{K}_T = \boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + \boldsymbol{K}_{CNL}(\boldsymbol{u}) + \boldsymbol{K}_{GNL}(\boldsymbol{u}) + \boldsymbol{K}_G(\boldsymbol{u})\]

which is the exact Jacobian of the internal forces of Tria3DSG.update_fint() called with nonlinear=1. \(\boldsymbol{K}_G\) is left homogeneous of degree one in the displacements so that a linear buckling analysis can use it directly, the stress of the nonlinear membrane strain being collected in \(\boldsymbol{K}_{GNL}\) instead, which Tria3DSG.update_KCNL() computes together with \(\boldsymbol{K}_{CNL}\).

Two things are simpler here than in the quadrilateral. First, the transverse shear strains of first-order shear deformation theory have no von Karman terms, so the discrete shear gap operator appears in \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\) alone and the nonlinear methods never touch it. Second, every operator of this element is constant over the triangle, so \(\boldsymbol{K}_{CNL} + \boldsymbol{K}_{GNL}\), its internal forces and \(\boldsymbol{K}_G\) are all integrated exactly by a single multiplication by the area, with no quadrature loop and no quadrature to keep consistent between the matrix and the residual. The drilling term is the only one that needs more than one point, and it is linear.

class pyfe3d.tria3dsg.Tria3DSG#

Three-node triangular shell element with a discrete shear gap

The constructor stores the probe and sets the identification numbers and the positions c1, c2, c3 to -1, the init_k_* positions to 0, and the rotation matrices r11, ..., r33 and m11, m12, m21, m22 to the identity. Before computing any element matrix, the caller sets c1, c2, c3 and the init_k_* positions, and calls update_rotation_matrix() and update_probe_xe().

Position of each node’s first degree-of-freedom in the global displacement vector. The coordinates of a node are read at the positions c1//2, c2//2 and c3//2 of the coordinate array.

Notes

drilling_model = 0 uses \(\gamma_{r_z} = A_{66}\) of Hughes and Brezzi [1], and any other value \(K6ROT \cdot 10^{-6} A_{66}\), see the module documentation. Overriding \(A_{66}\) with gamma_rz is what makes the sensitivity study recommended in the literature possible.

alpha_shear_locking is the optional algebraic stabilisation of the transverse shear stiffness, \(\hat{A}_{ts} = A_{ts}/(1 + \alpha \ell^2/h^2)\) with \(\ell\) the longest edge and \(h\) the total thickness. The default 0. is the element as described in the module documentation: the discrete shear gap is already locking-free, so nothing has to be rescaled.

A non-zero value applies the factor of Stenberg’s method, as Bischoff and Bletzinger (2004) [2] (Sec. 3.1) call the stabilisation of Lyly, Stenberg and Vihinen, on top of a discrete shear gap field, as Castro et al. (2019) [3] (Eq. (26)) do, which is not how pyfe3d.tria3r.Tria3R uses the same symbol. There the factor is applied to a centroid-sampled shear field and has to do the unlocking itself, which is why its default is 0.7, seven times the literature value, and why it is load bearing there and merely helpful here.

Recommended value. 0.07, the lower bound of the range recommended by Castro et al. (2019), on a thin plate discretised coarsely, and only there. Measured with this element, with the remaining columns from tests/test_tria3dsg.py and the study described in the module documentation:

alpha                     0      0.07     0.10     0.15
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
omega_11,  5 x 7     +12.06%   +7.93%   +6.73%   +4.86%
omega_11,  9 x 11     +2.75%   +1.69%   +1.27%   +0.60%
omega_11, 33 x 41     -0.61%   -0.72%   -0.76%   -0.84%
Nxx_cr,   l/h = 30    +1.71%   +0.11%   -0.33%   -1.02%
w_max,    a/h = 5     +9.81%  +10.14%  +10.30%  +10.51%

the first three rows being the first natural frequency of a thin plate under refinement, the fourth the buckling load of a thin plate and the last the deflection of a plate thick enough for transverse shear to carry 19 per cent of it, every entry a relative error against the analytical value.

The buckling row crosses from the stiff to the soft side between \(0.07\) and \(0.1\), which is the behaviour Castro et al. report above \(\alpha = 0.09\), reproduced here on a discrete shear gap field rather than on the ES-PIM of that paper.

Why the default is nevertheless zero. The factor \(\alpha \ell^2/h^2\) is unbounded in \(\ell/h\), so a non-zero \(\alpha\) removes essentially all of the transverse shear stiffness on any practical shell mesh, and with it three properties this element was built to have. Measured at the default \(\alpha = 0\) against \(\alpha = 0.07\):

  • the constant transverse shear state of test_constant_transverse_shear_state_is_exact, exact to \(10^{-12}\) at every element size, drops to \(1/(1 + factor)\) of the correct energy, which is \(0.067\) at \(L/h = 10\) and \(7 \times 10^{-8}\) at \(L/h = 10^4\) for the right triangles of leg \(L\) of that test, whose longest edge is \(\ell = \sqrt{2} L\), i.e. \(0.125\) at \(\ell/h = 10\) and \(1.4 \times 10^{-7}\) at \(\ell/h = 10^4\);

  • the sensitivity of \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\) to \(A_{ts}\) falls by about three hundred on the geometry of test_transverse_shear_stays_out_of_the_nonlinear_terms, that is the element uses a fraction of a per cent of the transverse shear stiffness the laminate gives it;

  • the last row of the table above: where transverse shear is real physics rather than a penalty, \(\alpha\) can only add error, and it does so monotonically.

So the gain is confined to coarse meshes of thin plates, where the element is already accurate, and it is paid for in the regime the FSDT exists to describe. Set it deliberately, per problem class, as for pyfe3d.tria3r.Tria3R, and never as a constant of the element.

update_KC0(self, long[: :1] KC0r, long[: :1] KC0c, double[: :1] KC0v, ShellProp prop, int update_KC0v_only=0) → void#

Update the sparse vectors of the constitutive stiffness matrix

The element-frame matrix is built by _update_probe_KC0ve() into the KC0ve attribute of the probe and rotated to global coordinates here.

Before this method is called, update_probe_xe() must have been used to set the element-frame coordinates of the probe.

The values in global coordinates are added to KC0v at the positions init_k_KC0 to init_k_KC0 + KC0_SPARSE_SIZE - 1. With update_KC0v_only=0 the global row and column indices are written to KC0r and KC0c at the same positions. The rotation matrix of update_rotation_matrix() is used. The area attribute and the strain rows of the probe are updated as well.

Parameters:
KC0r, KC0carray-like

Row and column positions of the sparse values. 1-D arrays of integers (INT) of size at least init_k_KC0 + KC0_SPARSE_SIZE.

KC0varray-like

Sparse values. 1-D array of DOUBLE, same size as KC0r. The values are added to the existing ones, with dimension of force per unit length between translations, of force between a translation and a rotation, and of force times length between rotations.

proppyfe3d.shellprop.ShellProp

Shell property.

update_KC0v_onlyint, optional

0, the default, also fills KC0r and KC0c.

Notes

The formulation of the element matrix is given in _update_probe_KC0ve(). With \(\boldsymbol{T}\) the transformation matrix of the element, block diagonal with the matrix \(\boldsymbol{T}_{3\times 3}\) of the entries \(T_{ij}\) stored in r11, ..., r33, whose columns are the element axes, for the translations and for the rotations of each node, the global values are \(\boldsymbol{K}_g = \boldsymbol{T} \boldsymbol{K}_e \boldsymbol{T}^\top\), i.e. \(K_{g,mn} = T_{mi} K_{e,ij} T_{nj}\).

update_KCNL(self, long[: :1] KCNLr, long[: :1] KCNLc, double[: :1] KCNLv, ShellProp prop, int update_KCNLv_only=0) → void#

Update the sparse vectors of the nonlinear stiffness matrix

The nonlinear stiffness matrix KCNL is the part of the tangent stiffness matrix, besides update_KC0() and update_KG(), that depends on the displacements through the von Karman membrane strains, evaluated at the displacements ue of the probe. The sum of the three is the tangent stiffness matrix of the internal forces of update_fint() with nonlinear=1.

Before this method is called, update_probe_xe() must have been used with the node coordinates and update_probe_ue() with the current displacements.

The element matrix is computed in element coordinates into the probe attribute KCNLve, rotated to global coordinates, and its values are added to KCNLv at the positions init_k_KCNL to init_k_KCNL + KCNL_SPARSE_SIZE - 1. With update_KCNLv_only=0 the global row and column indices are written to KCNLr and KCNLc at the same positions.

Parameters:
KCNLr, KCNLcarray-like

Row and column positions of the sparse values. 1-D arrays of integers (INT) of size at least init_k_KCNL + KCNL_SPARSE_SIZE.

KCNLvarray-like

Sparse values. 1-D array of DOUBLE, same size as KCNLr. The values are added to the existing ones, with dimension of force per unit length between translations, of force between a translation and a rotation, and of force times length between rotations.

proppyfe3d.shellprop.ShellProp

Shell property.

update_KCNLv_onlyint, optional

0, the default, also fills KCNLr and KCNLc.

Notes

Assuming that KCNL = KC0L + KCL0 + KCLL + KGNL, that is \(\boldsymbol{K}_{CNL} + \boldsymbol{K}_{GNL}\), the book’s nonlinear constitutive stiffness matrix \(\boldsymbol{K}_{CNL}\) of the terms of \(\boldsymbol{B}_{NL}\) plus the geometric \(\boldsymbol{K}_{GNL}\), built from the von Karman membrane strains

\[\epsilon_{xx} = u_{,x} + \frac{1}{2} w_{,x}^2, \quad \epsilon_{yy} = v_{,y} + \frac{1}{2} w_{,y}^2, \quad \gamma_{xy} = u_{,y} + v_{,x} + w_{,x} w_{,y}\]

whose nonlinear part is \(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{NL} = \frac{1}{2} \boldsymbol{B}_{NL} \boldsymbol{u}_e\), with \(\boldsymbol{B}_{NL}\) its variation. With \(\boldsymbol{B}_m\) and \(\boldsymbol{B}_b\) the linear membrane and bending strain-displacement matrices, \(\boldsymbol{G}_x = \partial \boldsymbol{S}^w / \partial x\) and \(\boldsymbol{G}_y = \partial \boldsymbol{S}^w / \partial y\) the gradients of \(w\), and \(\boldsymbol{A}\), \(\boldsymbol{B}\) the laminate matrices:

  • KC0L = \(\boldsymbol{B}_m^\top \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{B}_{NL} + \boldsymbol{B}_b^\top \boldsymbol{B} \boldsymbol{B}_{NL}\)

  • KCL0 = KC0L`^top`

  • KCLL = \(\boldsymbol{B}_{NL}^\top \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{B}_{NL}\)

  • KGNL = \(N_{NL,xx} \boldsymbol{G}_x^\top \boldsymbol{G}_x + N_{NL,yy} \boldsymbol{G}_y^\top \boldsymbol{G}_y + N_{NL,xy} (\boldsymbol{G}_x^\top \boldsymbol{G}_y + \boldsymbol{G}_y^\top \boldsymbol{G}_x)\), with \(\boldsymbol{N}_{NL} = \{N_{NL,xx}, N_{NL,yy}, N_{NL,xy}\}^\top = \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{NL}\)

The first three groups are the constitutive terms coupling the linear and the nonlinear parts of the membrane strain. KGNL is geometric, carrying the stress of the nonlinear membrane strain. It is collected here so that update_KG() stays homogeneous of degree one in the displacements, which is what a linear buckling analysis needs. With it here,

\[\boldsymbol{K}_T = \boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + \boldsymbol{K}_{CNL}(\boldsymbol{u}) + \boldsymbol{K}_{GNL}(\boldsymbol{u}) + \boldsymbol{K}_G(\boldsymbol{u})\]

is the exact Jacobian of the internal forces of update_fint() with nonlinear=1, and a Newton-Raphson iteration built on them converges quadratically.

Note

The transverse shear strains of first-order shear deformation theory carry no von Karman terms, so the discrete shear gap operator of this element enters \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\) alone and needs nothing here. The membrane rows have no \(r_z\) entries, this element having no Allman enrichment, so the drilling regularisation is likewise untouched. Both are unlike pyfe3d.quad4.Quad4, whose enriched membrane rows have to be carried into its nonlinear tangent.

Every operator is constant over the triangle, so each term is integrated exactly by a multiplication by the element area, see _update_probe_KCNLve().

update_KG(self, long[: :1] KGr, long[: :1] KGc, double[: :1] KGv, ShellProp prop, int update_KGv_only=0) → void#

Update the geometric stiffness from the current displacements

The membrane stress resultants are recovered from the probe displacements, which update_probe_ue() must have updated, using the same strain operators and the same membrane constitutive relation that update_fint() uses, and then handed to update_KG_given_stress(). Only the linear strains contribute; the stress of the von Karman terms of the membrane strain is carried by update_KCNL() instead.

The values are added to KGv at the positions init_k_KG to init_k_KG + KG_SPARSE_SIZE - 1. With update_KGv_only=0 the global row and column indices are written to KGr and KGc at the same positions. Before this method is called, update_rotation_matrix() and update_probe_xe() must also have been used.

Parameters:
KGr, KGcarray-like

Row and column positions of the sparse values. 1-D arrays of integers (INT) of size at least init_k_KG + KG_SPARSE_SIZE.

KGvarray-like

Sparse values. 1-D array of DOUBLE, same size as KGr. The values are added to the existing ones, with dimension of force per unit length.

proppyfe3d.shellprop.ShellProp

Shell property.

update_KGv_onlyint, optional

0, the default, also fills KGr and KGc.

Notes

The stress resultants are

\[\{N_{xx}, N_{yy}, N_{xy}\}^\top = \boldsymbol{A}\{\epsilon_{xx}, \epsilon_{yy}, \gamma_{xy}\}^\top + \boldsymbol{B}\{\kappa_{xx}, \kappa_{yy}, \kappa_{xy}\}^\top\]

with the linear strains of _update_probe_BL_G() and \(\boldsymbol{A}\), \(\boldsymbol{B}\) in the element coordinate system. Both the strains and the curvatures are constant over the triangle, so the resulting stress state is constant too, which is what update_KG_given_stress() assumes.

Only the linear strains contribute, which leaves this matrix homogeneous of degree one in the displacements, as a linear buckling analysis needs.

update_KG_given_stress(self, double Nxx, double Nyy, double Nxy, long[: :1] KGr, long[: :1] KGc, double[: :1] KGv, int update_KGv_only=0) → void#

Update the sparse vectors of the geometric stiffness matrix

A constant membrane stress state \(N_{xx}, N_{yy}, N_{xy}\) is assumed within the element. The operator involves only the gradients of the element-frame \(w\), which are taken from the Sw_x, Sw_y rows of the probe and turned into global translations with the third row of the rotation matrix, so only the translational degrees-of-freedom appear.

Warning

The stress resultants are in the element coordinate system, and for a triangle that is easy to get wrong. The element \(x\) axis runs from node 1 to node 2, so in a structured mesh whose cells are split along a diagonal the first triangle of each cell has its frame along the mesh while the second has it along the diagonal, some 45 degrees away. Handing the same \((N_{xx}, 0, 0)\) to both then loads half the elements along the diagonal, and the answer comes to depend on how the mesh generator happened to order the nodes of each triangle. A uniaxial state along a global direction has to be rotated to the element coordinate system with the rotation matrix r11, ..., r33 of this object.

update_KG() is free of this, recovering the stress from the element’s own strains in its own frame.

The values are added to KGv at the positions init_k_KG to init_k_KG + KG_SPARSE_SIZE - 1. With update_KGv_only=0 the global row and column indices are written to KGr and KGc at the same positions. Before this method is called, update_rotation_matrix() and update_probe_xe() must have been used. The area attribute and the strain rows of the probe are updated as well.

Parameters:
Nxx, Nyy, Nxydouble

Membrane stress resultants in the element coordinate system. Force per unit length.

KGr, KGcarray-like

Row and column positions of the sparse values. 1-D arrays of integers (INT) of size at least init_k_KG + KG_SPARSE_SIZE.

KGvarray-like

Sparse values. 1-D array of DOUBLE, same size as KGr. The values are added to the existing ones, with dimension of force per unit length.

update_KGv_onlyint, optional

0, the default, also fills KGr and KGc.

Notes

The operator is the Donnell one. With \(S_{i,x}\) and \(S_{i,y}\) the constant derivatives of the shape functions, \(A_e\) the element area and \(\{T_{13}, T_{23}, T_{33}\}\) the element normal in global coordinates, the third column of \(\boldsymbol{T}_{3\times 3}\), the entry of the global translation \(m\) of node \(i\) and \(n\) of node \(j\) is

\[\begin{split}K_{G} = A_e T_{m3} T_{n3} \begin{bmatrix} S_{i,x} & S_{i,y} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} N_{xx} & N_{xy} \\ N_{xy} & N_{yy} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} S_{j,x} \\ S_{j,y} \end{bmatrix}\end{split}\]

the integrand being constant over the triangle.

A uniaxial state \(N\) along a global direction \(\boldsymbol{d}\) has to be rotated. With \(\boldsymbol{T}_{3\times 3}\) the transformation matrix of this object, whose columns are the element axes, \(\boldsymbol{a} = \boldsymbol{T}_{3\times 3}^\top \boldsymbol{d}\) is that direction in element coordinates and the state \(N \boldsymbol{a} \boldsymbol{a}^\top\) has components

\[N^e_{xx} = N a_x^2, \quad N^e_{yy} = N a_y^2, \quad N^e_{xy} = N a_x a_y\]

which reduces to \((N, 0, 0)\) when the element \(x\) axis is along \(\boldsymbol{d}\), so a quadrilateral mesh aligned with the load needs nothing. Measured on the cylinder of tests/test_quad4_linear_buckling_cylinder_displ.py, whose cells are nearly square so the diagonal sits at 44.3 degrees, the three cyclic numberings of one triangle give buckling loads spanning a factor of fifteen without this rotation and agree to 2e-3 with it. This is checked by test_buckling_load_does_not_depend_on_triangle_node_numbering in tests/test_tria3dsg.py.

update_M(self, long[: :1] Mr, long[: :1] Mc, double[: :1] Mv, ShellProp prop, int mtype=0) → void#

Update the sparse vectors of the mass matrix

The element matrix is computed from intrho, intrhoz and intrhoz2 of prop and from the area of the element coordinates probe.xe, which is also stored in area; intrhoz couples the translations u and v with the rotations r_y and r_x, and intrhoz2 enters only the rotations r_x and r_y. The matrix is rotated to global coordinates and its values are added to Mv at the positions init_k_M to init_k_M + M_SPARSE_SIZE - 1; the global row and column indices are always written to Mr and Mc at the same positions. Before this method is called, update_rotation_matrix() and update_probe_xe() must have been used.

Parameters:
Mr, Mcarray-like

Row and column positions of the sparse values. 1-D arrays of integers (INT) of size at least init_k_M + M_SPARSE_SIZE.

Mvarray-like

Sparse values. 1-D array of DOUBLE, same size as Mr. The values are added to the existing ones, with dimension of mass between translations, of mass times length between a translation and a rotation, and of mass times length squared between rotations.

proppyfe3d.shellprop.ShellProp

Shell property. intrho, intrhoz and intrhoz2 are read from it.

mtypeint, optional

0 for the consistent mass matrix and 1 for the lumped one, which puts a third of the element area on each node. Default 0. The element has no reduced-integration mass matrix, 2.

Raises:
ValueError

If mtype is not 0 or 1.

Notes

With the displacements \(u + z r_y\), \(v - z r_x\) and \(w\) through the thickness, consistent with the curvatures of _update_probe_BL_G(), the kinetic energy integrated through the thickness is that of Eq. (20) of Brockman [1],

\[T = \frac{1}{2} \int_A \left[ I_\rho (\dot{u}^2 + \dot{v}^2 + \dot{w}^2) + 2 I_{\rho z} (\dot{u} \dot{r}_y - \dot{v} \dot{r}_x) + I_{\rho z^2} (\dot{r}_x^2 + \dot{r}_y^2) \right] dA\]

with \(I_\rho\), \(I_{\rho z}\) and \(I_{\rho z^2}\) given by intrho, intrhoz and intrhoz2, which include the offset. With \(h_{ij}\) the integral of the products of the linear shape functions over the element, the element matrix has \(I_{\rho} h_{ij}\) between the translations \(u\), \(v\), \(w\) of nodes \(i\) and \(j\), \(I_{\rho z^2} h_{ij}\) between the rotations \(r_x\) and between the rotations \(r_y\), \(+I_{\rho z} h_{ij}\) between \(u\) and \(r_y\), and \(-I_{\rho z} h_{ij}\) between \(v\) and \(r_x\). The drilling rotation \(r_z\) has no inertia. For the consistent mass matrix, \(h_{ij} = \int S_i S_j dA\) is \(A/6\) when \(i = j\) and \(A/12\) otherwise. The lumped mass matrix integrates \(T\) with the vertex rule, the nodal quadrature with the weight \(A/3\) at each node, where \(S_i\) is 1 at node \(i\) and 0 at the other nodes, giving \(h_{ii} = A/3\) and \(h_{ij} = 0\) for \(i \neq j\). The coupling through \(I_{\rho z}\) stays in the block of each node, such that the lumped matrix is block-diagonal per node, diagonal only when \(I_{\rho z} = 0\), and has the exact mass and the exact first moment of the mass, with the centre of mass at the offset from the reference surface. The rotary inertia from the distribution of \(I_\rho\) over the area is concentrated at the nodes, 4 times the exact value about the centroid of the element; the term \(I_{\rho z^2} A\) is exact.

References

[1]

Brockman, R. A., Dynamics of the Bilinear Mindlin Plate Element, International Journal for Numerical Methods in Engineering, 24(12), 2343-2356, 1987. https://doi.org/10.1002/nme.1620241208

class pyfe3d.tria3dsg.Tria3DSGProbe#

Probe carrying the element-frame coordinates, displacements and operators

The idea behind using a probe is to avoid allocating one set of buffers per finite element. The buffers belong to the probe, and one probe can be shared amongst many elements, the contents being updated and read on demand. Mind that the probe always holds the values of the last update.

The constructor takes no parameters and allocates every array filled with zeros.

xe,array-like

ue,array-like

finte,array-like

Element-frame internal forces corresponding to ue, 18 values, updated by Tria3DSG.update_probe_finte(). Forces, and moments (force times length) for the rotations.

KCNLve,array-like

BLkxx, BLkyy, BLkxyarray-like

BLgxz, BLgyzarray-like

BLdrilling: ‘double[::1]’

Sw_x, Sw_yarray-like

Rows of 18 values giving \(w_{,x}\) and \(w_{,y}\), used by the geometrically nonlinear terms. Sw_x holds \(\boldsymbol{G}_x = \partial \boldsymbol{S}^w/\partial x\) and Sw_y holds \(\boldsymbol{G}_y = \partial \boldsymbol{S}^w/\partial y\), the underscore denoting the partial derivative. Their products with ue are dimensionless. The rows above are written by the methods of Tria3DSG that compute stiffness matrices or internal forces.

Notes

Every one of these operators is constant over the triangle, because all three shape functions are linear, so each is formed once per element and no quadrature loop appears anywhere in this module except for the drilling term. BLdrilling is the residual of the regularisation of Hughes and Brezzi [1], whose \(r_z\) part varies over the element; its stiffness is integrated exactly with the three-point rule, see Tria3DSG._update_probe_KC0ve(). BLgxz and BLgyz are the discrete shear gap operators averaged over the three starting nodes, see Tria3DSG._update_probe_BL_G().
