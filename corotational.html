Co-rotational kinematics, EICR (pyfe3d.corotational)#

The element-independent co-rotational (EICR) formulation turns the linear elements Tria3R, Tria3DSG, Quad4, Quad4R, BeamC, BeamLR, Truss and Spring into elements for large displacements and large rotations with small strains, without changing them: the local linear stiffness matrix of each element is taken once in its base configuration, and the rigid motion of the element is removed from the nodal displacements and rotations at every evaluation. With kinematics='corotational_von_karman' the local response is that of the von Kármán strains of the element instead, evaluated by its own routines at the deformational displacements, see CorotationalShell; this changes the results of BeamC only, since the von Kármán terms of the other elements vanish in their co-rotational frame.

  • CorotationalModel assembles a model whose elements are added with kinematics='corotational', 'corotational_von_karman', 'von_karman' or 'linear', and provides the callables calc_fext, calc_fint, calc_kC and calc_kG of pyfe3d.solver.Analysis, which is used unchanged.

  • CorotationalShell, CorotationalBeam, CorotationalTruss and CorotationalSpring wrap one element each.

  • The finite-rotation utilities spin(), axial(), expso3(), logso3(), Lambda(), dLambdaT_m(), Hmat() and Lmat() are used by the wrappers and by the applied-moment helpers of CorotationalModel.

The module is a Cython extension, as the elements: the kinematics, the projector, and the internal force and tangent stiffness matrix of each wrapper are computed by typed C kernels, of which the finite-rotation utilities are the Python interface. The classes are Python classes, whose attributes can be read and changed as usual.

The rotational degrees-of-freedom of the nodes are total rotation vectors, \(\boldsymbol{\psi}_a\), whose nodal rotation matrix is \(\boldsymbol{R}_a = \exp(\mathrm{Spin}(\boldsymbol{\psi}_a))\), and they are added by the solvers like the translations. No state is kept between the calls. The map is singular at \(|\boldsymbol{\psi}_a| = 2 k \pi\), \(k \ge 1\), which limits a node to less than one full revolution.

Notes#

Notation: \(\boldsymbol{T}_0\) and \(\boldsymbol{T}_R\) are the base and the current co-rotational (CR) frames of an element, as 3x3 matrices whose columns are the element axes, the convention of the r11..r33 of the elements, such that global components are \(\boldsymbol{T}\) times local components. Felippa and Haugen [1] write the transposes of these matrices; Nour-Omid and Rankin [2] write \(\boldsymbol{E}_0\) and \(\boldsymbol{E}\).

The spatial spin \(\delta \boldsymbol{\omega}\) of a node, \(\delta \boldsymbol{R} \boldsymbol{R}^T = \mathrm{Spin}(\delta \boldsymbol{\omega})\), is related to the variation of the rotation vector by \(\delta \boldsymbol{\omega} = \boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{\psi}) \delta \boldsymbol{\psi}\), see Lambda(). This \(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\) is the \(\boldsymbol{H}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\) of Crisfield [3], Eq. (16.90), and the \(\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\) of [1], Eq. (100); here \(\boldsymbol{H}\) is always the matrix of [1], Eq. (101), see Hmat().

The internal force and the consistent tangent stiffness matrix of an element are those of the consistent symmetrizable equilibrated (CSE) formulation of [1], Section 6.3, Eq. (53), with the local linear stiffness matrix of the element, written in terms of the spins, and transformed to the additive rotation vectors with \(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\) as in [3], Section 17.4.2, Eqs. (17.173)-(17.177), see CorotationalShell.

Notes

The CR frame is the mean nodal triad, Crisfield [1], Eqs. (17.11)-(17.14), the “midpoint interpolation” of Nour-Omid and Rankin [2], Section 5:

\[\boldsymbol{e}_1 = \frac{\boldsymbol{x}^D_2 - \boldsymbol{x}^D_1}{l}, \qquad \boldsymbol{q} = \frac{1}{2} (\boldsymbol{R}_1 + \boldsymbol{R}_2) \boldsymbol{q}_0, \qquad \boldsymbol{e}_3 = \frac{\boldsymbol{e}_1 \times \boldsymbol{q}}{|\boldsymbol{e}_1 \times \boldsymbol{q}|}, \qquad \boldsymbol{e}_2 = \boldsymbol{e}_3 \times \boldsymbol{e}_1\]

With \(\bar{\boldsymbol{q}} = \boldsymbol{T}_R^T \boldsymbol{q} = (q_1, q_2, 0)\) and \(\bar{\boldsymbol{q}}_i = \boldsymbol{T}_R^T \boldsymbol{R}_i \boldsymbol{q}_0\), its spin-fitter matrix (derived here) is

\[\omega_1 = \frac{1}{2 q_2} \sum_i (\bar{q}_{i2} \omega_{i,x} - \bar{q}_{i1} \omega_{i,y}) - \frac{q_1}{q_2} \frac{w_2 - w_1}{l}, \qquad \omega_2 = -\frac{w_2 - w_1}{l}, \qquad \omega_3 = \frac{v_2 - v_1}{l}\]

with \(\omega_{i,x}, \omega_{i,y}\) the local spins of the nodes. The frame is undefined when \(\boldsymbol{q}\) becomes parallel to the chord, \(q_2 \to 0\), see q2_min; [2], Appendix B.3, redefine \(\boldsymbol{q}\) in that case, which a stateless formulation cannot do without a jump of the forces.

\(\boldsymbol{G}\) depends on the nodal rotations, so the condition (37) of Felippa and Haugen [3] does not hold and their Eq. (47) misses a term of the order of the unbalanced force ([3], Eq. (48)). With exact_KGP=True the projection geometric stiffness is the exact variation of \(\boldsymbol{P}^T \boldsymbol{p}\) (derived here),

\[\boldsymbol{K}_{GP} = -\frac{\partial (\boldsymbol{G}^T \boldsymbol{z})}{\partial \bar{\boldsymbol{v}}} - \boldsymbol{G}^T \boldsymbol{F}_n(\boldsymbol{p})^T \boldsymbol{P}, \qquad \boldsymbol{z} = \boldsymbol{S}^T \boldsymbol{p}\]

with \(\boldsymbol{p} = \bar{\boldsymbol{H}}^T \boldsymbol{K}_e \boldsymbol{d}\) the unprojected force, \(\delta l = \delta \bar{u}_2 - \delta \bar{u}_1\) and \(\delta \bar{\boldsymbol{q}}_i = \mathrm{Spin}(\bar{\boldsymbol{q}}_i)(\boldsymbol{G} \delta \bar{\boldsymbol{v}} - \delta \bar{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_i)\); it is checked by finite differences in tests/test_corotational_core.py.

With local='von_karman' (see CorotationalShell), the deformational translations of the nodes are along the chord, \(\bar{v} = \bar{w} = 0\). BeamLR interpolates \(v\) and \(w\) linearly, so its von Kármán terms vanish and the result is that of 'linear' to round-off. BeamC interpolates them with the nodal rotations, and its von Kármán axial strain \(\epsilon_{xx} = u_{,x} + \bar{\epsilon}_{NL}\), with \(\bar{\epsilon}_{NL}\) the element mean of \((v_{,x}^2 + w_{,x}^2)/2\) (see pyfe3d.beamc.BeamC.update_KCNL()), gives the local “higher-order terms” of [1], Section 17.1.5: with an infinite transverse shear stiffness, it is the strain of [1], Eqs. (17.56a)-(17.56b), for the initially straight element, cubic \(v\) and \(w\), which is checked in tests/test_corotational_core.py. Measured on the cantilever of Sze, Liu and Lo [4], Section 3.1, at the end force P_max, against the elastica: with 'linear', 8 and 16 BeamC give errors of \(-U\) and \(W\) of -0.07 %, +0.17 % and -0.02 %, +0.05 %; with 'von_karman', 4, 8 and 16 BeamC give -0.013 %, -0.010 %; +0.001 %, +0.006 % and +0.002 %, +0.007 %.

Notes

The residual seen by the solvers is

\[\boldsymbol{R}(\boldsymbol{c}, \lambda) = \boldsymbol{F}_{int}(\boldsymbol{c}) - \lambda \left(\boldsymbol{F}_0 + \sum_k \boldsymbol{F}_k(\boldsymbol{c})\right)\]

with \(\boldsymbol{F}_0\) the attribute fext and \(\boldsymbol{F}_k(\boldsymbol{c})\) the configuration-dependent moments of add_moment(). As described in pyfe3d.solver.callbacks, calc_fint() returns \(\boldsymbol{F}_{int} - \lambda \sum_k (\boldsymbol{F}_k(\boldsymbol{c}) - \boldsymbol{F}_k(\boldsymbol{0}))\), calc_kG() contains the load stiffness \(-\lambda \sum_k \partial \boldsymbol{F}_k/\partial \boldsymbol{c}\), and calc_fext(c=c) returns \(\boldsymbol{F}_0 + \sum_k \boldsymbol{F}_k(\boldsymbol{c}) = -\partial \boldsymbol{R}/\partial \lambda\).

The contributions of the kinematics are:

  • 'corotational': CorotationalShell.evaluate() or CorotationalBeam.evaluate(), the material part in calc_kC() and the geometric part in calc_kG();

  • 'corotational_von_karman': the same, with the von Kármán strains of the element in its CR frame (local='von_karman' of the wrappers, see CorotationalShell); the local K_e + KCNL(d) enters the material part and the local KG(d) the geometric part;

  • 'von_karman': update_fint(..., nonlinear=1) of the element, KC0 + KCNL in calc_kC() and KG in calc_kG();

  • 'linear': KC0 u and KC0 in calc_kC().

Limits of validity: 'von_karman' keeps the rotations of the elements small to moderate, the assumption of the von Kármán strains; the two co-rotational kinematics allow large displacements and rotations with small strains, each nodal rotation vector staying below one full revolution, \(|\boldsymbol{\psi}_a| < 2 \pi\), see pyfe3d.corotational. In the CR frame, the von Kármán terms of the shells and of BeamLR vanish, and 'corotational_von_karman' gives the results of 'corotational' for them, to round-off; it changes the results of BeamC only, see CorotationalBeam.

The rotational degrees-of-freedom of a node shared by a co-rotational element and an element of another kinematics are interpreted by the latter as small rotations, valid only while the rotations of that node remain small; a warning is issued at the first evaluation.

Examples

One co-rotational beam element; a rigid rotation of 90 degrees about \(z\) gives no internal forces:

>>> import numpy as np
>>> from pyfe3d import BeamLR, BeamLRProbe, CorotationalModel, DOF
>>> from pyfe3d.beamprop import BeamProp
>>> prop = BeamProp()
>>> prop.A, prop.E, prop.G = 0.0025, 203e9, 78e9
>>> prop.Iyy = prop.Izz = 0.05**4/12
>>> prop.J = prop.Iyy + prop.Izz
>>> x = np.array([0., 0., 0., 2., 0., 0.])
>>> beam = BeamLR(BeamLRProbe())
>>> beam.c1, beam.c2 = 0, DOF
>>> beam.update_rotation_matrix(0., 1., 0., x)
>>> beam.update_probe_xe(x)
>>> model = CorotationalModel(x)
>>> wrapper = model.add(beam, prop, kinematics='corotational')
>>> model.KC0.shape
(12, 12)
>>> u = np.zeros(model.N)
>>> u[DOF:DOF + 2] = [-2., 2.]
>>> u[5::DOF] = 0.5*np.pi
>>> fint = model.calc_fint(u)
>>> bool(np.allclose(fint, 0., atol=1e-6))
True

add_moment(self, node, M, kind='rotation_vector')#

Apply a concentrated moment at a node

A constant moment conjugate to the rotation vector is added to fext; the configuration-dependent moments are stored and enter calc_fext(), calc_fint() and calc_kG().

Parameters:
nodeint

Node index, whose rotational degrees-of-freedom are 6*node + 3 to 6*node + 5.

Marray_like

Moment vector, size 3, in global components at the base configuration, for a unit load factor.

kindstr, optional

  • 'rotation_vector': added to fext, conjugate to the rotation vector \(\boldsymbol{\psi}\) of the node, constant;

  • 'fixed_axes': moment about fixed global axes, configuration-dependent;

  • 'follower': moment that rotates with the node, \(\boldsymbol{R}(\boldsymbol{\psi}) \boldsymbol{M}\), configuration-dependent.

Default 'rotation_vector'.

Raises:
ValueError

When kind is not one of the three, or M is not of size 3.

Notes

The virtual work of a spatial moment \(\boldsymbol{m}\) is \(\boldsymbol{m} \cdot \delta \boldsymbol{\omega} = (\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^T \boldsymbol{m}) \cdot \delta \boldsymbol{\psi}\), see Lambda(). Hence:

  • 'rotation_vector': \(\boldsymbol{F} = \boldsymbol{M}\); it is the moment about fixed axes when the rotation vector keeps a fixed direction, since \(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^T \boldsymbol{n} = \boldsymbol{n}\) for \(\boldsymbol{\psi} \parallel \boldsymbol{n}\), e.g. in plane problems;

  • 'fixed_axes': \(\boldsymbol{F} = \boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{\psi})^T \boldsymbol{M}\), with the load stiffness \(-\partial \boldsymbol{F}/\partial \boldsymbol{\psi}\), see dLambdaT_m(). Moments about fixed axes are non-conservative (Crisfield [1], Section 17.5.2, Fig. 17.4), so the tangent stiffness matrix is unsymmetric;

  • 'follower': \(\boldsymbol{m} = \boldsymbol{R} \boldsymbol{M}\) and \(\boldsymbol{F} = \boldsymbol{\Lambda}^T \boldsymbol{R} \boldsymbol{M} = \boldsymbol{\Lambda} \boldsymbol{M}\), since \(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^T \boldsymbol{R} = \boldsymbol{\Lambda}\) (derived here and checked in the tests); the load stiffness is \(-\partial (\boldsymbol{\Lambda} \boldsymbol{M})/\partial \boldsymbol{\psi} = \partial (\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^T \boldsymbol{M})/\partial \boldsymbol{\psi}\) evaluated at \(-\boldsymbol{\psi}\), since \(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{\psi}) = \boldsymbol{\Lambda}(-\boldsymbol{\psi})^T\). This is the moment counterpart of the follower force of [1], Eqs. (17.211)-(17.212), following the nodal triad; the follower moments of [1], Section 17.5.2, Eqs. (17.216)-(17.218), follow the element axes instead.

The configuration-dependent moments enter calc_fint(), calc_kG() and calc_fext() as described in CorotationalModel; use calc_fext() as the calc_fext of pyfe3d.solver.Analysis.

Notes

Base configuration, Felippa and Haugen [1], Table 3, step 1. At a configuration with the deformed nodal positions \(\boldsymbol{x}^D_a\) and the nodal rotation matrices \(\boldsymbol{R}_a = \exp(\mathrm{Spin}(\boldsymbol{\psi}_a))\), the deformational displacements in the CR frame \(\boldsymbol{T}_R\) are ([1], Section 3.8, Eq. (22), Table 3, steps 2-3; Nour-Omid and Rankin [2], Eqs. (11), (14), (19))

\[\bar{\boldsymbol{u}}_{d,a} = \boldsymbol{T}_R^T (\boldsymbol{x}^D_a - \boldsymbol{x}_C) - \tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_a, \qquad \bar{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{d,a} = \mathrm{axial}(\log( \boldsymbol{T}_R^T \boldsymbol{R}_a \boldsymbol{T}_0))\]

with \(\boldsymbol{x}_C\) the mean of \(\boldsymbol{x}^D_a\). The positions \(\boldsymbol{x}^D_a\) are computed relative to the base centroid of the element, \((\boldsymbol{X}_a - \boldsymbol{X}_C) + \boldsymbol{u}_a\), which leaves \(\bar{\boldsymbol{u}}_d\) and \(\boldsymbol{T}_R\) unchanged and makes their round-off independent of the origin of the coordinates: from \(\boldsymbol{X}_a + \boldsymbol{u}_a\) it is of the order of \(\varepsilon |\boldsymbol{X}_a|\), with \(\varepsilon\) the machine precision, and the Newton-Raphson iterations stagnate above the round-off floor of pyfe3d.solver.convergence on fine meshes far from the origin (derived here, measured with test_cantilever_end_shear_translated of tests/test_corotational_benchmarks.py). The CR frame follows the frame rule of the element, applied to \(\boldsymbol{x}^D_a\):

  • Tria3R and Tria3DSG, side 1-2 alignment ([1], Appendix B.2.1, Eq. (115); Battini and Pacoste [3], Eqs. (2)-(5)). The frame, and thus the solution, depends on the numbering of the nodes ([1], B.2.1; [3], Section 1).

  • Quad4 and Quad4R, \(z\) along \(\boldsymbol{v}_{42} \times \boldsymbol{v}_{13}\) and \(x\) along \((\boldsymbol{v}_{13} + \boldsymbol{v}_{42})/2\), invariant to a cyclic renumbering of the nodes.

The spin-fitter matrix \(\boldsymbol{G}\) of these frames, \(\delta \boldsymbol{\omega}_R = \boldsymbol{G} \delta \bar{\boldsymbol{v}}\), was derived here; with \(\bar{\boldsymbol{x}}_a\) the current local coordinates and \(w, v\) the local translations:

  • triangle, \(\bar{\boldsymbol{x}}_2 - \bar{\boldsymbol{x}}_1 = (a_1, 0, 0)\), \(\bar{\boldsymbol{x}}_3 - \bar{\boldsymbol{x}}_1 = (a_2, b_2, 0)\), \(n = a_1 b_2\): \(\omega_1 = -(a_2 (w_2 - w_1) - a_1 (w_3 - w_1))/n\), \(\omega_2 = -(w_2 - w_1)/a_1\), \(\omega_3 = (v_2 - v_1)/a_1\), the last one as in [3], Eq. (5);

  • quadrilateral, \(\boldsymbol{d}_1 = \bar{\boldsymbol{x}}_3 - \bar{\boldsymbol{x}}_1 = (a_1, b_1, 0)\), \(\boldsymbol{d}_2 = \bar{\boldsymbol{x}}_2 - \bar{\boldsymbol{x}}_4 = (a_2, b_2, 0)\), \(n_z = a_2 b_1 - a_1 b_2\), \(c = |\boldsymbol{d}_1 + \boldsymbol{d}_2|/2\): \(\omega_1 = -(a_1 (w_2 - w_4) - a_2 (w_3 - w_1))/n_z\), \(\omega_2 = (b_2 (w_3 - w_1) - b_1 (w_2 - w_4))/n_z\), \(\omega_3 = ((v_3 - v_1) + (v_2 - v_4))/(2 c)\).

Both satisfy \(\boldsymbol{G} \boldsymbol{S} = \boldsymbol{I}\) ([1], Eq. (17)) and the condition (37) of [1], such that the projection geometric stiffness of [1], Eq. (47), is exact, which is verified by finite differences in tests/test_corotational_core.py.

The internal force and the tangent stiffness matrix are those of the CSE formulation of [1], Section 6.3, Eq. (53), with the projector \(\boldsymbol{P} = \boldsymbol{P}_u - \boldsymbol{S} \boldsymbol{G}\) ([1], Eqs. (11), (12), (31)-(34)):

\[\bar{\boldsymbol{p}} = \boldsymbol{P}^T \bar{\boldsymbol{H}}^T \boldsymbol{K}_e \boldsymbol{d}, \qquad \boldsymbol{K}_R = \boldsymbol{P}^T \bar{\boldsymbol{H}}^T \boldsymbol{K}_e \bar{\boldsymbol{H}} \boldsymbol{P} + \boldsymbol{P}^T \bar{\boldsymbol{L}} \boldsymbol{P} - \boldsymbol{F}_{nm} \boldsymbol{G} - \boldsymbol{G}^T \boldsymbol{F}_n^T \boldsymbol{P}\]

with \(\bar{\boldsymbol{H}} = \mathrm{diag}(\boldsymbol{I}, \boldsymbol{H}(\bar{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{d,a}))\) ([1], Eq. (20)), \(\bar{\boldsymbol{L}} = \mathrm{diag}(\boldsymbol{0}, \boldsymbol{L}(\bar{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{d,a}, \bar{\boldsymbol{m}}_a))\) with the moments of \(\boldsymbol{K}_e \boldsymbol{d}\) ([1], Eqs. (21), (46), (102)), and \(\boldsymbol{F}_{nm}\), \(\boldsymbol{F}_n\) built with \(\bar{\boldsymbol{p}}\) ([1], Eq. (44)). The first term is the material stiffness ([1], Eq. (43)), the others the geometric stiffness ([1], Eqs. (45)-(47)). With \(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{T}} = \mathrm{diag}(\boldsymbol{T}_R)\) and \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} = \mathrm{diag}(\boldsymbol{I}, \boldsymbol{\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{\psi}_a))\), the force and the stiffness in the additive degrees-of-freedom are (Crisfield [4], Section 17.4.2, Eqs. (17.176)-(17.177))

\[\boldsymbol{f} = \hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^T \boldsymbol{\mathcal{T}} \bar{\boldsymbol{p}}, \qquad \boldsymbol{K} = \hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^T \boldsymbol{\mathcal{T}} \boldsymbol{K}_R \boldsymbol{\mathcal{T}}^T \hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}} + \mathrm{diag}\left(\boldsymbol{0}, \frac{\partial (\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^T \boldsymbol{m}_a)}{\partial \boldsymbol{\psi}_a}\right)\]

with \(\boldsymbol{m}_a\) the moments of \(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{T}} \bar{\boldsymbol{p}}\), see dLambdaT_m(). evaluate returns the material part \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^T \boldsymbol{\mathcal{T}} \boldsymbol{K}_M \boldsymbol{\mathcal{T}}^T \hat{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}\) and the geometric part, the rest. The matrix is unsymmetric away from equilibrium ([1], Section 6; [4], Section 17.4.2).

Warped quadrilaterals. \(\boldsymbol{K}_e\) and KC0 use the flat projection of the element (update_KC0 ignores the local \(z\)), whose nodes are at \(\tilde{z}_a\) from the warped nodes along the normal of the mean plane, with \(\tilde{z}_1 = \tilde{z}_3 = -\tilde{z}_2 = -\tilde{z}_4\) for the frame of the diagonals. They do not annihilate the rigid rotations of the warped element, which makes the linear element too stiff in warped meshes, the “warping sensitivity” of [2], Section 1. With warping=None the flat element is evaluated at the warped nodes, and the projector built on the warped geometry restores the invariance to rigid motions ([2], Section 1; [1], Sections 4.2 and 6.5): the tangent at the base configuration is \(\boldsymbol{P}^T \boldsymbol{K}_e \boldsymbol{P}\). With warping='offsets', the default, the flat element is linked to the nodes by rigid offsets that rotate with the nodal triads, the eccentric nodes of [1], Sections 5.1 and 6, whose formulation (their reference [37]) is not given there. The following was derived here. With \(\boldsymbol{e}_a = \tilde{z}_a \boldsymbol{T}_0 \boldsymbol{e}_3\) and \(\boldsymbol{r}_a = \boldsymbol{R}_a \boldsymbol{e}_a\), the flat element has the nodes \(\boldsymbol{x}^D_a - \boldsymbol{r}_a\), the rotations \(\boldsymbol{R}_a\) and the base local coordinates \((\tilde{x}_a, \tilde{y}_a, 0)\), from which \(\boldsymbol{d}\) is measured; its degrees-of-freedom vary as

\[\delta \boldsymbol{u}^f_a = \delta \boldsymbol{u}_a + \mathrm{Spin}(\boldsymbol{r}_a) \boldsymbol{\Lambda}_a \delta \boldsymbol{\psi}_a, \qquad \delta \boldsymbol{\psi}^f_a = \delta \boldsymbol{\psi}_a\]

i.e. \(\delta \boldsymbol{v}^f = \boldsymbol{J} \delta \boldsymbol{v}\). With \(\boldsymbol{f}^f\) and \(\boldsymbol{K}^f\) the force and the tangent of the flat element given above, and \(\boldsymbol{n}_a\) the nodal forces of \(\boldsymbol{f}^f\),

\[\boldsymbol{f} = \boldsymbol{J}^T \boldsymbol{f}^f, \qquad \boldsymbol{K} = \boldsymbol{J}^T \boldsymbol{K}^f \boldsymbol{J} + \mathrm{diag}\left(\boldsymbol{0}, \frac{\partial (\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_a^T (\boldsymbol{n}_a \times \boldsymbol{r}_a))}{\partial \boldsymbol{\psi}_a}\right)\]

The last term, the eccentricity geometric stiffness of [1], Section 5.1, is \(\partial(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_a^T \boldsymbol{m})/\partial \boldsymbol{\psi}_a\) at \(\boldsymbol{m} = \boldsymbol{n}_a \times \boldsymbol{r}_a\) (dLambdaT_m()) minus \(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_a^T \mathrm{Spin}(\boldsymbol{n}_a) \mathrm{Spin}(\boldsymbol{r}_a) \boldsymbol{\Lambda}_a\), from \(\delta \boldsymbol{r}_a = -\mathrm{Spin}(\boldsymbol{r}_a) \boldsymbol{\Lambda}_a \delta \boldsymbol{\psi}_a\). The material part is \(\boldsymbol{J}^T \boldsymbol{K}^f_M \boldsymbol{J}\). At the base configuration \(\boldsymbol{K}\) is KC0 transformed by \(\boldsymbol{J}\), the linear flat element with rigid links, which annihilates the rigid motions of the warped element; it differs from KC0 by the order of the warp ratio. Both treatments keep the non-zero eigenvalues of KC0 at the base configuration. The wrapper sets the attribute warp_offsets of a Quad4 or Quad4R to 0, which switches off the linear rigid offsets of the element itself: those of 'offsets' are the exact ones above, and None uses the flat element without offsets. The stress recovery after update_probe() is therefore that of the flat element. KC0 of a CorotationalModel is computed with the element’s own offsets, warp_offsets = 1, for 'offsets', giving \(\boldsymbol{J}^T \boldsymbol{K}_{C0} \boldsymbol{J}\), the tangent of 'offsets' at the base configuration, and with warp_offsets = 0 for None. In the linear analysis of a cantilever twisted by 90 degrees (length 12, width 1.1, thickness 0.32, tip loads), with 6x1 Quad4 elements (warp ratio 0.049), the tip displacements with 'offsets' are within 0.7 % of those of a 96x16 mesh, 8-11 % too small with None and 74-91 % too small with the KC0 of the flat element, warp_offsets = 0; a coarser case is checked in tests/test_corotational_core.py.

Von Kármán local strains. With local='von_karman' the local element computation is step 8 of the procedure of [4], Section 17.1.5: the existing element routines are entered with the local displacements and give the local internal force vector and the local tangent stiffness matrix, which [4] transforms as \(\boldsymbol{K}_t = \boldsymbol{F}^T \boldsymbol{K}_{tl} \boldsymbol{F} + \boldsymbol{K}_{t\sigma}\) (step 11). Here the element’s own update_probe_finte(prop, nonlinear=1), update_KCNL and update_KG are evaluated with probe.ue = d, by the identity trick of \(\boldsymbol{K}_e\), giving \(\bar{\boldsymbol{f}}(\boldsymbol{d})\) and the local tangent \(\boldsymbol{K}_e + \boldsymbol{K}_{CNL}(\boldsymbol{d}) + \boldsymbol{K}_G(\boldsymbol{d})\), the exact Jacobian of \(\bar{\boldsymbol{f}}\) (update_fint of the elements). \(\bar{\boldsymbol{f}}\) replaces \(\boldsymbol{K}_e \boldsymbol{d}\) in \(\bar{\boldsymbol{p}}\), \(\bar{\boldsymbol{L}}\), \(\boldsymbol{F}_n\) and \(\boldsymbol{F}_{nm}\) above, and the local tangent replaces \(\boldsymbol{K}_e\) in the material stiffness. [1] obtain the material stiffness from the variation of the local force, their Eq. (41), with \(\boldsymbol{K}_e = \partial \boldsymbol{p}_e/\partial \boldsymbol{v}_d\) (their Eq. (42)), and note that their Eq. (43) holds only if \(\boldsymbol{K}_e\) does not depend on the deformational freedoms; the chain rule in Eq. (41) then gives \(\boldsymbol{P}^T \bar{\boldsymbol{H}}^T (\partial \bar{\boldsymbol{f}}/\partial \boldsymbol{d}) \bar{\boldsymbol{H}} \boldsymbol{P}\) (derived here), whereas the geometric terms of their Eqs. (44)-(47), which depend on the force and not on its derivative, keep their form. The material part returned by evaluate() takes \(\boldsymbol{K}_e + \boldsymbol{K}_{CNL}\) and the geometric part takes \(\boldsymbol{K}_G\), both transformed as the material stiffness: the split of kinematics='von_karman' in CorotationalModel. KCNL of the elements includes the geometric term of the axial force of the non-linear strains, see their update_KCNL.

In the CR frames of the shells the transverse translations of \(\boldsymbol{d}\) are zero (triangles, whose frame contains the three nodes) or the hourglass pattern \(\pm \bar{z}\) (quadrilaterals, whose \(x y\) plane is parallel to both diagonals and contains the mean of the nodes), the warp of the element and not a deflection, and the von Kármán terms of Tria3R, Tria3DSG, Quad4 and Quad4R take the slopes of \(w\) from the nodal translations only, at the centroid for the quadrilaterals, where the hourglass pattern has no slope. Quad4R takes them there by construction. Quad4 takes them at the two-by-two points with its default shear_model = 1, see the Notes of Quad4.update_KG(), where the pattern has a slope; the wrapper therefore sets the attribute centroid_slopes of a Quad4 to 1 while it calls the element, in the same way as it sets warp_offsets to 0, and restores it afterwards, also when the call raises, such that an element used outside the wrapper keeps its own rule. These terms therefore vanish, and local='von_karman' gives the internal force and the tangent of 'linear' to round-off, for both values of shear_model of Quad4 (derived here, checked in tests/test_corotational_core.py, where the element alone at the same \(\boldsymbol{d}\) with the slopes at the two-by-two points gives von Kármán forces of 1.4 % of the linear ones); the same holds for BeamLR, see CorotationalBeam.

The local stiffness matrix \(\boldsymbol{K}_e\) is that of the element, by the identity trick of CorotationalShell: six uncoupled springs between the relative translations and rotations of the nodes, in the element axes \(\boldsymbol{T}_0\), i.e. the user orientation. For distinct nodes with transverse stiffnesses, \(\boldsymbol{K}_e\) does not annihilate the rigid rotations, since the linear element does not balance the moment of the transverse forces; the projector adds that moment (Felippa and Haugen [1], Sections 4.2 and 6.2), so the tangent at the base configuration differs from KC0.

The 'triad' frame is derived here. With \(\boldsymbol{t}_1, \boldsymbol{t}_2\) the first two columns of \(\boldsymbol{T}_0\) and \(\boldsymbol{R}_i\) the nodal rotations,

\[\boldsymbol{a} = \frac{1}{2} (\boldsymbol{R}_1 + \boldsymbol{R}_2) \boldsymbol{t}_1, \qquad \boldsymbol{b} = \frac{1}{2} (\boldsymbol{R}_1 + \boldsymbol{R}_2) \boldsymbol{t}_2, \qquad \boldsymbol{e}_1 = \frac{\boldsymbol{a}}{|\boldsymbol{a}|}, \qquad \boldsymbol{e}_3 = \frac{\boldsymbol{e}_1 \times \boldsymbol{b}}{|\boldsymbol{e}_1 \times \boldsymbol{b}|}, \qquad \boldsymbol{e}_2 = \boldsymbol{e}_3 \times \boldsymbol{e}_1\]

i.e. the frame of CorotationalBeam with the chord replaced by the mean of the nodal \(x\) axes. It depends on the nodal rotations only, so it is defined for coincident nodes. With the local vectors \(\bar{\boldsymbol{p}}_i = \boldsymbol{T}_R^T \boldsymbol{R}_i \boldsymbol{t}_1\) and \(\bar{\boldsymbol{q}}_i = \boldsymbol{T}_R^T \boldsymbol{R}_i \boldsymbol{t}_2\), their means \(\bar{\boldsymbol{a}} = (A, 0, 0)\) and \(\bar{\boldsymbol{b}} = (B_1, B_2, 0)\), and \(\bar{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_i\) the local spins of the nodes, the spin-fitter matrix follows from the conditions that \(\bar{\boldsymbol{a}}\) stays along \(x\) and \(\bar{\boldsymbol{b}}\) in the \(x y\) plane:

\[\omega_3 = -\frac{1}{2 A} \sum_i \mathrm{Spin}(\bar{\boldsymbol{p}}_i)_2 \bar{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_i, \qquad \omega_2 = \frac{1}{2 A} \sum_i \mathrm{Spin}(\bar{\boldsymbol{p}}_i)_3 \bar{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_i, \qquad \omega_1 = \frac{1}{B_2} \left(B_1 \omega_2 - \frac{1}{2} \sum_i \mathrm{Spin}(\bar{\boldsymbol{q}}_i)_3 \bar{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_i\right)\]

with \(\mathrm{Spin}(\cdot)_k\) the row \(k\) of the matrix. It satisfies \(\boldsymbol{G} \boldsymbol{S} = \boldsymbol{I}\) ([1], Eq. (17)). The projection geometric stiffness is the exact one of CorotationalBeam, with \(\partial(\boldsymbol{G}^T \boldsymbol{z})/\partial \bar{\boldsymbol{v}}\) obtained from \(\delta \bar{\boldsymbol{p}}_i = \mathrm{Spin}(\bar{\boldsymbol{p}}_i) (\boldsymbol{G} \delta \bar{\boldsymbol{v}} - \delta \bar{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_i)\) and the same for \(\bar{\boldsymbol{q}}_i\); the rest is the CSE formulation of CorotationalShell. The deformational rotations of the two nodes are opposite, \(\pm \boldsymbol{\phi}/2\), so the rotational springs act on \(\boldsymbol{\phi}\), close to the relative rotation of the nodes. Zero internal force under rigid motions and the consistency of the tangent with central finite differences are checked in tests/test_corotational_core.py. The frame is undefined when the relative rotation of the nodes approaches 180 degrees (\(A \to 0\) or \(B_2 \to 0\)), see q2_min.

pyfe3d.corotational.spin(v)#

Skew-symmetric (spin) matrix of a 3-vector

Returns the matrix whose product with a vector is the cross product of v with that vector; axial() is its inverse.

Parameters:
varray_like

Vector of size 3.

Returns:
Snp.ndarray

Shape (3, 3), such that S @ w == np.cross(v, w).
