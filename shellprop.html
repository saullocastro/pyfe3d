Shell property module (pyfe3d.shellprop)#

Highly based on the composites. module.

Classes of the material (MatLamina), of the ply (Lamina) and of the shell property (ShellProp), of the lamination parameters (LaminationParameters) and of their gradients (GradABD), and functions that create a ShellProp from lamination parameters. The shell elements of pyfe3d take their constitutive matrices from a ShellProp. The functions of pyfe3d.shellprop_utils create these objects from the ply data.

calc_LP_grad(self, double thickness, MatLamina mat, LaminationParameters lp) → void#

Gradients of the shell stiffnesses with respect to the thickness and lamination parameters

Updates gradAij, gradBij, gradDij and gradAtsij with the derivatives of the stiffnesses computed by shellprop_from_LaminationParameters() from thickness, from the invariants u1 to u7 of mat and from the lamination parameters of lp; column 0 contains the derivatives with respect to the thickness. The entries [0, 2], [1, 1] and [2, 2] of gradAtsij, which do not depend on the lamination parameters, are not updated. All six rows of gradAij, gradBij and gradDij are updated, including row 5 (A66, B66, D66), whose derivatives with respect to \(\xi_{A3}\), \(\xi_{B3}\) and \(\xi_{D3}\) are \(-h u_3\), \(-h^2/4 \, u_3\) and \(-h^3/12 \, u_3\).

Parameters:

The total thickness of the laminate, length

matMatLamina object

Material object

lpLaminationParameters object

The container class with all lamination parameters already defined

Returns:
None

The attributes of the object are updated.

Notes

The stiffnesses of shellprop_from_LaminationParameters() are \(A = h \, U \{1, \xi_A\}\), \(B = h^2/4 \, U \{0, \xi_B\}\), \(D = h^3/12 \, U \{1, \xi_D\}\) and \(\bar{A}_{ts} = h \, U_{ts} \{1, \xi_{Ats}\}\), with \(U\) the rows of the in-plane terms of MatLamina.get_invariant_matrix() and \(U_{ts}\) those of the transverse shear terms. At constant lamination parameters, \(\partial A/\partial h = U \{1, \xi_A\}\), \(\partial B/\partial h = h/2 \, U \{0, \xi_B\}\), \(\partial D/\partial h = h^2/4 \, U \{1, \xi_D\}\) and \(\partial \bar{A}_{ts}/\partial h = U_{ts} \{1, \xi_{Ats}\}\), and at constant thickness the derivatives with respect to the lamination parameters are the columns of \(U\) multiplied by \(h\), \(h^2/4\) and \(h^3/12\), respectively, and those of \(U_{ts}\) by \(h\).

Notes

With the ply angle \(\theta\) measured from the laminate \(x\) axis to the material direction 1, as in the stiffnesses of rebuild(), the material direction 1 is \(\{\cos\theta, \sin\theta, 0\}\) in the laminate axes, such that \(\{u_1, u_2, u_3\}^\top = T \{u_x, u_y, u_z\}^\top\). Notes

With the stress components ordered as \(\{\sigma_{xx}, \sigma_{yy}, \sigma_{zz}, \tau_{yz}, \tau_{xz}, \tau_{xy}\}\), the order of MatLamina.get_constitutive_matrix(), the matrix is the transformation of the stresses from the laminate axes to the material axes for a rotation by the ply angle \(\theta\) about \(z\), e.g. \(\sigma_{11} = \cos^2\theta \sigma_{xx} + \sin^2\theta \sigma_{yy} + \sin 2\theta \tau_{xy}\) [1]. rebuild(self) → void#

Update constitutive matrices

Computes cost, sint, cos2t, sin2t, cos4t and sin4t from thetadeg, and the ply stiffnesses q11L to q55L in the laminate axes from the plane-stress stiffnesses of matlamina in the material axes, which are computed from its e1, e2, nu12, nu21, g12, g13 and g23.

Notes

The plane-stress reduced stiffnesses in the material axes are

\[Q_{11} = \frac{e_1}{1 - \nu_{12}\nu_{21}}, \quad Q_{12} = \frac{\nu_{12} e_2}{1 - \nu_{12}\nu_{21}}, \quad Q_{22} = \frac{e_2}{1 - \nu_{12}\nu_{21}}, \quad Q_{66} = g_{12}, \quad Q_{44} = g_{23}, \quad Q_{55} = g_{13}\]

such that e3, nu13 and nu23 are not used, and thermal expansion is not considered. With \(c = \cos\theta\) and \(s = \sin\theta\), \(\theta\) being the ply angle, the stiffnesses in the laminate axes are [1]:

\[\begin{split}q_{11L} = Q_{11} c^4 + 2 (Q_{12} + 2 Q_{66}) s^2 c^2 + Q_{22} s^4 \\ q_{12L} = (Q_{11} + Q_{22} - 4 Q_{66}) s^2 c^2 + Q_{12} (s^4 + c^4) \\ q_{22L} = Q_{11} s^4 + 2 (Q_{12} + 2 Q_{66}) s^2 c^2 + Q_{22} c^4 \\ q_{16L} = (Q_{11} - Q_{12} - 2 Q_{66}) s c^3 + (Q_{12} - Q_{22} + 2 Q_{66}) s^3 c \\ q_{26L} = (Q_{11} - Q_{12} - 2 Q_{66}) s^3 c + (Q_{12} - Q_{22} + 2 Q_{66}) s c^3 \\ q_{66L} = (Q_{11} + Q_{22} - 2 Q_{12} - 2 Q_{66}) s^2 c^2 + Q_{66} (s^4 + c^4) \\ q_{44L} = Q_{44} c^2 + Q_{55} s^2, \quad q_{45L} = (Q_{55} - Q_{44}) s c, \quad q_{55L} = Q_{55} c^2 + Q_{44} s^2\end{split}\]

References

[1]

Reddy, J. N., Mechanics of Laminated Composite Plates and Shells: Theory and Analysis, Second Edition, CRC Press, 2003. https://doi.org/10.1201/b12409

Builds the matrix of the invariants u1 to u7 computed by rebuild(). The rows correspond to q11, q22, q12, q66, q16, q26, q44, q45, q55 and the columns to the factors of 1 and of the lamination parameters, in the same combinations used by shellprop_from_LaminationParameters().

rebuild(self) → void#

Update constitutive and invariant terms

Computes, from the moduli e1, e2, e3, g12, g13, g23 and the Poisson’s ratios nu12, nu21, nu13, nu31, nu23, nu32, the stiffness constants c11 to c66, the constitutive constants q11 to q66 and the invariants u1 to u7, with the dimension of force per unit area. e1 and e2 must be non-zero.

Notes

With \(\Delta = 1 - \nu_{12}\nu_{21} - \nu_{13}\nu_{31} - \nu_{23}\nu_{32} - \nu_{12}\nu_{23}\nu_{31} - \nu_{13}\nu_{21}\nu_{32}\), the constitutive constants are the stiffnesses of the orthotropic material in its principal axes [2]:

\[\begin{split}q_{11} = e_1 (1 - \nu_{23}\nu_{32})/\Delta, \quad q_{12} = e_1 (\nu_{21} + \nu_{23}\nu_{31})/\Delta, \quad q_{13} = e_1 (\nu_{31} + \nu_{21}\nu_{32})/\Delta \\ q_{21} = e_2 (\nu_{12} + \nu_{13}\nu_{32})/\Delta, \quad q_{22} = e_2 (1 - \nu_{13}\nu_{31})/\Delta, \quad q_{23} = e_2 (\nu_{32} + \nu_{12}\nu_{31})/\Delta \\ q_{31} = e_3 (\nu_{13} + \nu_{12}\nu_{23})/\Delta, \quad q_{32} = e_3 (\nu_{23} + \nu_{13}\nu_{21})/\Delta, \quad q_{33} = e_3 (1 - \nu_{12}\nu_{21})/\Delta \\ q_{44} = g_{23}, \quad q_{55} = g_{13}, \quad q_{66} = g_{12}\end{split}\]

which are symmetric, \(q_{ij} = q_{ji}\), when the Poisson’s ratios satisfy the reciprocity \(\nu_{ij}/e_i = \nu_{ji}/e_j\), see read_laminaprop(); the matrix of \(q_{ij}\), \(i, j = 1, 2, 3\), is then the inverse of the upper-left block of the compliance matrix given below. With \(e_3 = 0\), as given by read_laminaprop() for 2 or 6 entries, \(\nu_{31} = \nu_{32} = 0\) and \(q_{11}\), \(q_{12}\), \(q_{22}\) are the plane-stress reduced stiffnesses \(e_1/(1 - \nu_{12}\nu_{21})\), \(e_1 \nu_{21}/(1 - \nu_{12}\nu_{21})\) and \(e_2/(1 - \nu_{12}\nu_{21})\) used by Lamina.rebuild().

The stiffness constants are computed with \(\delta = (1 - \nu_{12}\nu_{21} - \nu_{23}\nu_{32} - \nu_{31}\nu_{13} - 2\nu_{21}\nu_{32}\nu_{13})/(e_1 e_2)\) [1]:

\[\begin{split}c_{11} = (1 - \nu_{23}\nu_{32})/(\delta e_2), \quad c_{12} = (\nu_{21} + \nu_{31}\nu_{23})/(\delta e_2), \quad c_{13} = (\nu_{31} + \nu_{21}\nu_{32})/(\delta e_2) \\ c_{22} = (1 - \nu_{13}\nu_{31})/(\delta e_1), \quad c_{23} = (\nu_{32} + \nu_{12}\nu_{31})/(\delta e_1), \quad c_{33} = e_3 (1 - \nu_{12}\nu_{21})/(\delta e_1 e_2) \\ c_{44} = g_{23}, \quad c_{55} = g_{13}, \quad c_{66} = g_{12}\end{split}\]

With the reciprocity, \(\delta e_1 e_2 = \Delta\) and \(c_{ij} = q_{ij}\). The matrix of the stiffness constants, see get_constitutive_matrix(), is then the inverse of the orthotropic compliance matrix, with \(S_{11} = 1/e_1\), \(S_{22} = 1/e_2\), \(S_{33} = 1/e_3\), \(S_{12} = -\nu_{12}/e_1\), \(S_{13} = -\nu_{13}/e_1\), \(S_{23} = -\nu_{23}/e_2\), \(S_{44} = 1/g_{23}\), \(S_{55} = 1/g_{13}\) and \(S_{66} = 1/g_{12}\) [1].

The invariants \(u_1\) to \(u_5\) are those of Jones [3], and \(u_6\), \(u_7\) the transverse shear invariants of the ABAQUS manual [4]:

\[\begin{split}u_1 = (3 q_{11} + 3 q_{22} + 2 q_{12} + 4 q_{66})/8, \quad u_2 = (q_{11} - q_{22})/2 \\ u_3 = (q_{11} + q_{22} - 2 q_{12} - 4 q_{66})/8, \quad u_4 = (q_{11} + q_{22} + 6 q_{12} - 4 q_{66})/8 \\ u_5 = (u_1 - u_4)/2, \quad u_6 = (q_{44} + q_{55})/2, \quad u_7 = (q_{44} - q_{55})/2\end{split}\]

They give the stiffnesses of a ply at any angle, see get_invariant_matrix().

The intended use is plane stress, with the default \(e_3 = 0\) of read_laminaprop() for 2 or 6 entries: \(q_{11}\), \(q_{12}\), \(q_{22}\) are then the plane-stress reduced stiffnesses also used by Lamina.rebuild(), and the invariants give the plane-stress stiffnesses \(q_{ijL}\) of the ply in the laminate axes. With \(e_3 \ne 0\), \(q_{11}\), \(q_{12}\), \(q_{22}\) are the three-dimensional stiffnesses \(c_{11}\), \(c_{12}\), \(c_{22}\), and the invariants give the components 11, 22, 12, 66, 16, 26, 44, 45 and 55 of the three-dimensional stiffness matrix of get_constitutive_matrix() rotated by the ply angle about the 3 axis, which differ from the plane-stress \(q_{ijL}\) of Lamina.rebuild(). This is meant to compute three-dimensional properties, e.g. for continuum shell elements integrated with a user material (UMAT).

Trace-normalize the lamina properties for plane stress

Modify the original MatLamina object with a trace-normalization: the constitutive constants q11, q12, q22, q44, q55, q66 and the invariants u1 to u7 are divided by the trace tr = q11 + q22 + 2*q66, such that they become dimensionless trace-normalized values. calc_Ats_element(self, double thetadeg)#

Transverse shear stiffness in an element coordinate system

The element coordinate system is such that the material direction makes an angle \(\theta\) with the element \(x\) axis, measured towards the element \(y\) axis. This is the same rotation used by the shell elements, where \(m_{11} = \cos\theta\), \(m_{12} = -\sin\theta\), \(m_{21} = \sin\theta\) and \(m_{22} = \cos\theta\).

When shear_correction='rohwer' and the plies are available, the stiffness corresponds to the plies rotated to the element frame, i.e. all ply angles shifted by \(\theta\), and it is evaluated from Fourier coefficients of its inverse calculated once per laminate by calc_transverse_shear_stiffness(), such that the cost per element does not depend on the number of plies.

In all other cases, i.e. any other shear_correction, see calc_transverse_shear_stiffness(), or when no plies exist, e.g. for a property created from lamination parameters or with A44, A45, A55 given directly, A44, A45, A55 are rotated as the components of a second-order tensor.

Note

Changes to A44, A45, A55 made after calc_constitutive_matrix() are only used for elements with \(m_{12} = 0\) when the plies are available and shear_correction='rohwer', because the stiffness is otherwise obtained from the Fourier coefficients calculated from the plies.

Parameters:
thetadegfloat

Angle \(\theta\) in degrees.

Returns:
Atsnp.ndarray

Matrix [[A44, A45], [A45, A55]] in the element coordinate system.

Notes

The transverse shear stiffness obtained with the equilibrium approach of Rohwer (1988) [1] is not invariant to a rotation of the reference frame, because the two cylindrical bending states are tied to the \(x\) and \(y\) axes. Therefore, when shear_correction='rohwer' and the plies are available, the stiffness corresponds to the plies rotated to the element frame, i.e. all ply angles shifted by \(\theta\), such that the assumed static state and the element kinematics refer to the same pair of directions.

Re-evaluating the method of Rohwer for each element has a cost proportional to the number of plies. Instead, the compliance \(S = A_{ts}^{-1}\) in the element frame is represented exactly by its Fourier series:

\[S(\theta) = S_0 + \sum_{n=1}^{5} \left( S_{cn} \cos 2n\theta + S_{sn} \sin 2n\theta \right)\]

because the equilibrium distribution \(f^{(k)}(z)\) is a polynomial of degree 4 in \(\cos\theta\), \(\sin\theta\) and \((C_s^{(k)})^{-1}\) of degree 2, such that \(S\) is a trigonometric polynomial of degree 10 with a period of 180 degrees. The coefficients are calculated once per laminate by calc_transverse_shear_stiffness(), from 16 evaluations in rotated frames, and the cost per element becomes independent of the number of plies. With \(M = 16\) equally spaced samples \(\theta_j = j \pi/M\), the discrete Fourier coefficients of the harmonics \(2n\theta\), \(n \le 5\), are exact, since the aliased harmonics would be \(M - n \ge 11\).

In all other cases the stiffness is rotated as a second-order tensor:

\[\begin{split}A_{ts}^e = T_\gamma^\top A_{ts} T_\gamma \qquad T_\gamma = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\varphi & \sin\varphi \\ -\sin\varphi & \cos\varphi \end{bmatrix} \qquad \varphi = -\theta\end{split}\]

in the ordering \(\{\gamma_{yz}, \gamma_{xz}\}\), as in the book, with \(\varphi\) the rotation of the element frame relative to the frame of A44, A45, A55. The rotation is exact for the constant-strain stiffness.

References

[1]

Rohwer, K., 1988, Improved transverse shear stiffness for layered finite elements, DFVLR-FB 88-32.

Transverse shear strain energy of the recovered stresses

With the stresses of calc_equilibrium_transverse_shear() at the three Gauss-Legendre points of each ply, computes at each point the transverse shear strain energies per unit area of reference surface in the directions 13 and 23, and the resultants of the stresses, all integrated through the thickness with three Gauss-Legendre points per ply.

Returns:

The energies and the resultants \(\int \bar\tau dz\) of the recovered stresses, with shape (npts,). Energy per unit area for U13 and U23, force per unit length for Qy and Qx.

Raises:
ValueError

If a ply has a singular transverse shear constitutive matrix \(C_s^{(k)}\), e.g. g13 = 0 or g23 = 0.

Notes

With the stresses of calc_equilibrium_transverse_shear() and the strains \(\{\bar\gamma_{yz}, \bar\gamma_{xz}\} = (C_s^{(k)})^{-1} \{\bar\tau_{yz}, \bar\tau_{xz}\}\), the energies per unit area of reference surface of Noor and Peters (1989) [1], Eq. (7):

\[\bar{U}_{13} = \int \frac{1}{2} C_{55} \bar\gamma_{xz}^2 dz \qquad \bar{U}_{23} = \int \frac{1}{2} C_{44} \bar\gamma_{yz}^2 dz\]

integrated exactly with three Gauss-Legendre points per ply, since the recovered stresses are quadratic in \(z\) within each ply. The a posteriori factors follow by equating their integral over the domain with that of the FSDT, \(\frac{1}{2} Q_x^2/\bar{A}_{55}\) and \(\frac{1}{2} Q_y^2/\bar{A}_{44}\), i.e. Eqs. (8)-(9) of Noor and Peters (1989), with \(Q = k^0 \bar{A} \gamma^0\) the shear forces of the FSDT. The resultants are \(Q_y = \int \bar\tau_{yz} dz\) and \(Q_x = \int \bar\tau_{xz} dz\).

calc_constitutive_matrix(self) → void#

Calculate the laminate constitutive terms

This is the commonly called ABD matrix with shape=(6, 6). \[\begin{split}(A_{ij}, B_{ij}, D_{ij}) = \sum_k \int_{z_k}^{z_{k+1}} q_{ijL}^{(k)} (1, z, z^2) dz, \quad ij = 11, 12, 16, 22, 26, 66 \\ (intrho, intrhoz, intrhoz2) = \sum_k \int_{z_k}^{z_{k+1}} \rho^{(k)} (1, z, z^2) dz\end{split}\]

calc_equilibrium_transverse_shear(self, eps_x, eps_y, z=None)#

Transverse shear stresses recovered from the actual strain gradients

Integrates through the thickness, from the bottom face, the equilibrium of the in-plane stresses of the plies given by the derivatives eps_x and eps_y of the generalized strains, e.g. those of an FSDT solution, at npts points, and returns the transverse shear stresses at the heights z. The stresses vanish at the bottom face; at the top face they do not vanish in general.

Parameters:
eps_x, eps_yarray-like, shape (6,) or (npts, 6)

Derivatives with respect to \(x\) and \(y\) of \(\{\varepsilon_{xx}^{(0)}, \varepsilon_{yy}^{(0)}, \gamma_{xy}^{(0)}, \varepsilon_{xx}^{(1)}, \varepsilon_{yy}^{(1)}, \gamma_{xy}^{(1)}\}\), at npts points. The first three entries are per unit length and the last three per unit length squared.

zarray-like or None, optional

Heights from the reference surface. If None, the three Gauss-Legendre points of each ply are used. Default None. Length.

Returns:
z, tau_yz, tau_xznp.ndarray

The heights, with shape (nz,), and the stresses, with shape (npts, nz). With z=None, nz is three times the number of plies. Length for z and force per unit area for the stresses.

Raises:
ValueError

If a value of z is outside the laminate.

See also

calc_aposteriori_energy

Notes

Integrates the three-dimensional equilibrium equations from the bottom face, where the tractions vanish, with the in-plane stresses of each ply \(\sigma^{(k)} = C^{(k)} (\varepsilon^{(0)} + z\varepsilon^{(1)})\):

\[\begin{split}\tau_{xz}(z) = -\int_{z_1}^{z} \left( c_1 \left[ \varepsilon^{(0)}_{,x} + \bar{z} \varepsilon^{(1)}_{,x} \right] + c_3 \left[ \varepsilon^{(0)}_{,y} + \bar{z} \varepsilon^{(1)}_{,y} \right] \right) d\bar{z} \\ \tau_{yz}(z) = -\int_{z_1}^{z} \left( c_2 \left[ \varepsilon^{(0)}_{,y} + \bar{z} \varepsilon^{(1)}_{,y} \right] + c_3 \left[ \varepsilon^{(0)}_{,x} + \bar{z} \varepsilon^{(1)}_{,x} \right] \right) d\bar{z}\end{split}\]

where \(c_1\), \(c_2\) and \(c_3\) are the rows of \(C^{(k)}\) that give \(\sigma_{xx}\), \(\sigma_{yy}\) and \(\tau_{xy}\), respectively, and \(z_1\) is the bottom face. It uses the gradients of the generalized strains of the actual solution, as in the a posteriori approach of Noor and Peters (1989) [1], instead of the two cylindrical bending states of calc_transverse_shear_stiffness(). The top face is traction free only if the gradients satisfy the in-plane equilibrium of the stress resultants. The integrals of each ply are computed exactly by _equilibrium_integrals(), with \(z\) measured from the mid-surface and the gradients \(\varepsilon^{(0)}\) shifted by offset times \(\varepsilon^{(1)}\), which avoids the round-off of large values of offset.

References

[1]

Noor, A. K. and Peters, J. M., 1989, A posteriori estimates for the shear correction factors in multi-layered composite cylinders, Journal of Engineering Mechanics, 115, pp. 1225-1244.

\[\begin{split}\{\xi_{A1}, \xi_{A2}, \xi_{A3}, \xi_{A4}\} = \int \{\cos 2\theta, \sin 2\theta, \cos 4\theta, \sin 4\theta\} d\bar{z} \\ \{\xi_{B1}, \xi_{B2}, \xi_{B3}, \xi_{B4}\} = 4 \int \{\cos 2\theta, \sin 2\theta, \cos 4\theta, \sin 4\theta\} \bar{z} d\bar{z} \\ \{\xi_{D1}, \xi_{D2}, \xi_{D3}, \xi_{D4}\} = 12 \int \{\cos 2\theta, \sin 2\theta, \cos 4\theta, \sin 4\theta\} \bar{z}^2 d\bar{z} \\ \{\xi_{Ats,1}, \xi_{Ats,2}\} = \int \{\cos 2\theta, \sin 2\theta\} d\bar{z}\end{split}\]

integrated exactly ply by ply, such that xiAts1 = xiA1 and xiAts2 = xiA2. With these definitions, the stiffnesses of the laminate are linear in the lamination parameters, see shellprop_from_LaminationParameters().

The \(\xi_{Ai}\) and \(\xi_{Di}\) are those of Krupa et al. [1], who integrate from \(z = -h/2\) to \(z = h/2\), i.e. with offset = 0. The \(\xi_{Bi}\) and \(\xi_{Ats,i}\), not defined there, follow the same pattern, with the factor 4 of \(\xi_{Bi}\) corresponding to the factor \(h^2/4\) of \(B_{ij}\) in shellprop_from_LaminationParameters().

calc_transverse_shear_stiffness(self) → void#

Update the transverse shear stiffnesses A44, A45, A55

Called at the end of calc_constitutive_matrix(), and computed once per laminate. The attributes A44, A45, A55 are the transverse shear stiffnesses of the first-order shear deformation theory (FSDT) with the shear correction already applied, and no shear correction factor is applied to them by the elements.

Conventions: \(\{\tau_{yz}, \tau_{xz}\}^\top\), \(\{Q_y, Q_x\}^\top\), \(A_{ts} = [[A_{44}, A_{45}], [A_{45}, A_{55}]]\), index 4 corresponds to \(yz\) and index 5 to \(xz\). The coordinate \(z\) is measured from the reference surface, with the plies running from \(z_1 = -h/2 + offset\) to \(z_{N+1} = +h/2 + offset\), and \(C_s^{(k)} = [[q_{44L}, q_{45L}], [q_{45L}, q_{55L}]]\), which is in general a full matrix. None of the methods depends on offset.

The method is selected by the attribute shear_correction:

  • 'rohwer' (default): equilibrium approach, giving the full 2x2 matrix. The shell elements use it evaluated in the element coordinate system, see calc_Ats_element(), whose coefficients are also computed here, together with the through-thickness distribution used by calc_transverse_shear_stress().

  • 'vlachoutsis': the scalar factors k13, k23 are applied to the constant-strain stiffness, A55 = k13*Abar55, A44 = k23*Abar44, and A45 = (k13 + k23)/2*Abar45.

  • 'whitney': the result is identical to 'vlachoutsis'.

  • 'chow': for laminates symmetric about the mid-surface only, for which the result is identical to 'vlachoutsis'.

  • 'birman_bert': average shear strain factors, applied as in 'vlachoutsis'. It gives \(k = 1\) for a homogeneous plate.

  • 'thickness_shear': dynamic factors, applied as in 'vlachoutsis'. It gives \(k = \pi^2/12\) for a homogeneous plate. The ply densities must be positive.

  • 'constant': \(k = 5/6\), i.e. A44 = 5/6*Abar44, A45 = 5/6*Abar45, A55 = 5/6*Abar55.

  • None: no correction, A44 = Abar44, A45 = Abar45, A55 = Abar55.

Except for 'rohwer', the elements rotate A44, A45, A55 to the element coordinate system as the components of a second-order tensor, see calc_Ats_element().

The following attributes are also updated: Abar44, Abar45, Abar55 (constant strain), Abarbar44, Abarbar45, Abarbar55 (constant stress, nan if a ply has a singular \(C_s^{(k)}\)), and the ratios scf_k13 = A55/Abar55 and scf_k23 = A44/Abar44, which are informative only.

Without plies, A44, A45, A55, Abar44, Abar45, Abar55, Abarbar44, Abarbar45 and Abarbar55 are set to zero and nothing else is updated.

Raises:
ValueError

If shear_correction is not recognized; if a ply has a singular transverse shear constitutive matrix \(C_s^{(k)}\), e.g. g13 = 0 or g23 = 0 (all methods except 'constant' and None); if the ABD matrix of the laminate is singular ('rohwer'); if the laminate is not symmetric ('chow'); or if a ply has a non-positive density ('thickness_shear'). Also if the transverse shear compliance obtained with 'rohwer' is singular, in the material or in a rotated coordinate system, or if the sum of q11L (direction 1) or q22L (direction 2) times the ply thicknesses is not positive ('vlachoutsis', 'whitney', 'chow', 'birman_bert').

Notes

All of the methods are evaluated with \(z\) measured from the mid-surface, which avoids the round-off of large values of offset. The constant-strain stiffness \(\bar{A}_{ts} = \sum_k C_s^{(k)} h_k\) corresponds to transverse shear strains constant through the thickness, and the constant-stress stiffness \(\bar{\bar{A}}_{ts} = h^2 [\sum_k (C_s^{(k)})^{-1} h_k]^{-1}\) to the constant stresses \(\{\tau_{yz}, \tau_{xz}\} = \{Q_y, Q_x\}/h\), with the same complementary energy.

  • 'rohwer': equilibrium approach of Rohwer (1988) [1]. The transverse shear stresses are obtained from the equilibrium of two cylindrical bending states, with zero tractions at the bottom and top faces and continuity at every interface, \(\{\tau_{yz}, \tau_{xz}\}^\top = f^{(k)}(z) \{Q_y, Q_x\}^\top\), see _rohwer(). The 2x2 stiffness is obtained from the complementary energy:

    \[A_{ts} = \left[ \sum_k \int_{z_k}^{z_{k+1}} f^{(k)T} \left(C_s^{(k)}\right)^{-1} f^{(k)} dz \right]^{-1}\]

    The integrand is a polynomial of degree 4 in \(z\) within each ply, such that the 3-point Gauss-Legendre rule used per ply is exact. The method is valid for arbitrary anisotropic and unsymmetric laminates, and the result does not depend on offset. The result is not invariant to a rotation of the reference frame, because the two cylindrical bending states are tied to the \(x\) and \(y\) axes. For this reason, the shell elements use it evaluated in the element coordinate system, see calc_Ats_element(), from the Fourier coefficients of the compliance computed here with 16 rotations of the plies.

  • 'vlachoutsis': the scalar factors \(k_{13}\), \(k_{23}\) of Vlachoutsis (1992) [2] are applied to the constant-strain stiffness, A55 = k13*Abar55, A44 = k23*Abar44, and the ad-hoc A45 = (k13 + k23)/2*Abar45, which cannot represent the coupling of angle-ply laminates with Abar45 = 0. The factors use \(C_{11}\) and \(C_{22}\) of each ply in laminate axes and the direction-wise neutral surfaces, being exact only for specially orthotropic plies. For the direction 1, with \(C_{11} = q_{11L}\) and \(C_{55} = q_{55L}\) of each ply:

    \[z_n = \frac{\int C_{11} z dz}{\int C_{11} dz}, \quad R = \int C_{11} (z - z_n)^2 dz, \quad g(z) = -\int_{z_1}^{z} C_{11} (\bar{z} - z_n) d\bar{z}, \quad k_{13} = \frac{R^2}{\bar{A}_{55} \int g^2/C_{55} dz}\]

    i.e. the energy of the transverse shear stress \(\tau_{xz} = Q_x g(z)/R\) of the one-dimensional equilibrium \(\tau_{xz,z} = -\sigma_{xx,x}\) is equated with that of the FSDT, \(Q_x^2/(k_{13} \bar{A}_{55})\). Analogously for \(k_{23}\) with \(C_{22} = q_{22L}\), \(C_{44} = q_{44L}\) and \(\bar{A}_{44}\). The integrals of \(g\) are evaluated with three Gauss-Legendre points per ply, which is exact, \(g^2\) being a polynomial of degree 4 within each ply.

  • 'whitney': the scalar factors of Whitney (1973) [3], Eqs. (3)-(7), extending Chow (1971) [4] to unsymmetric orthotropic laminates in cylindrical bending. The derivative of the bending stress, Eq. (3), \(\sigma_{xx,x} = -C_{11}(B_{11} - A_{11}z) Q_x/(D_{11}A_{11} - B_{11}^2)\), vanishes at the neutral surface \(z_n = B_{11}/A_{11}\) and, since \(R = (D_{11}A_{11} - B_{11}^2)/A_{11}\), equals \(C_{11}(z - z_n) Q_x/R\). Integrated with the equilibrium \(\sigma_{xx,x} + \tau_{xz,z} = 0\), Eq. (4), it gives the \(\tau_{xz}\) of 'vlachoutsis', Eq. (5), and the energy equivalence, Eqs. (6)-(7), gives the same \(k_{13}\). The result is identical to 'vlachoutsis'.

  • 'chow': the scalar factors of Chow (1971) [4], Eqs. (11)-(12), with the transverse shear stress of a symmetric laminate obtained from the one-dimensional equilibrium \(\tau_{xz} = Q_x g(z)/D_{11}\), \(g(z) = -\int_{-h/2}^{z} C_{11} \bar{z} d\bar{z}\), and \(k_{13} = D_{11}^2 / (\bar{A}_{55} \int g^2/C_{55} dz)\), analogously for \(k_{23}\) with \(C_{22}\), \(D_{22}\) and \(C_{44}\). The coordinate is measured from the mid-surface and the laminate must be symmetric about it, see _check_symmetric(). For symmetric laminates the result is identical to 'vlachoutsis', whose neutral surfaces then coincide with the mid-surface. The factors are applied as in 'vlachoutsis'.

  • 'birman_bert': the average shear strain factors of Birman and Bert (2002) [5], Eq. (13) and Appendix B, obtained by equating the thickness integral of the transverse shear strain of the equilibrium distribution with that of the FSDT, \(k_{13} = R_1 h / (\bar{A}_{55} \int g_1/C_{55} dz)\), analogously for \(k_{23}\). The equilibrium distribution \(g_\alpha(z)\) and the bending stiffness \(R_\alpha\) about the direction-wise neutral surface are the same as in 'vlachoutsis', which generalizes Eq. (13), derived for symmetric cross sections, to unsymmetric laminates. It gives \(k = 1\) for a homogeneous plate. The factors are applied as in 'vlachoutsis'. Note that the value recommended by Birman and Bert (2002) for sandwich structures, \(K = 1\) applied to \(\bar{A}_{55} = \sum_k C_{55}^{(k)} h_k\) (their Eqs. A5-A7), is None.

  • 'thickness_shear': dynamic factors of Yang, Norris and Stavsky (1966) [6], as described by Sun and Whitney (1973) [7], extending the criterion of Mindlin (1951) [8] to laminates, i.e. matching the frequency of the first thickness-shear mode of the FSDT, \(\omega^2 = k_{13} \bar{A}_{55} / \bar{I}_2\), with the exact frequency of the laminate, computed with a transfer matrix through the plies for the anti-plane motion \(u(z)\), with \(\rho^{(k)} \ddot{u} = (C_{55}^{(k)} u_{,z})_{,z}\), traction-free faces and continuity of \(u\) and \(\tau_{xz}\) at every interface, see _thickness_shear_frequency(). The rotatory inertia about the centre of mass, \(\bar{I}_2 = I_2 - I_1^2/I_0\), with \(I_n = \int \rho z^n dz\), makes the result independent of offset. Analogously for \(k_{23}\) with \(C_{44}\). The coupling \(C_{45}\) between the two directions is neglected in the eigenproblem, and the factors are applied as in 'vlachoutsis'. It gives \(k = \pi^2/12\) for a homogeneous plate. The ply densities must be positive.

  • 'constant': \(k = 5/6\), the factor that 'rohwer', 'vlachoutsis', 'whitney' and 'chow' give for a homogeneous plate.

calc_transverse_shear_stress(self, double z, double Qy, double Qx) → tuple#

Transverse shear stresses at a given height

Evaluates, for the transverse shear forces Qy and Qx, the through-thickness distribution of the transverse shear stresses of the equilibrium approach used by shear_correction='rohwer', see calc_transverse_shear_stiffness(), in the material coordinate system, which is quadratic within each ply, vanishes at the bottom and top faces and is continuous at the ply interfaces, e.g. for failure criteria.

The distribution only depends on the in-plane stiffnesses of the plies and is the same for every shear_correction. It is computed by calc_transverse_shear_stiffness() when shear_correction is 'rohwer', or on the first call otherwise, and it is reset by calc_constitutive_matrix(), which must be called again if the plies are modified.

Parameters:
zfloat

Height measured from the reference surface, within \([-h/2 + offset, +h/2 + offset]\). At a ply interface both plies give the same result.

Qy, Qxfloat

Transverse shear forces per unit length, \(Q_y\) and \(Q_x\), e.g. {Qy, Qx} = Ats @ {gamma_yz, gamma_xz}.

Returns:
tau_yz, tau_xztuple of float

Transverse shear stresses.

Raises:
ValueError

If z is outside the laminate, if the laminate has no plies, or if the ABD matrix of the laminate is singular.

Notes

Evaluates the equilibrium distribution of Rohwer (1988) [1], in the material coordinate system, see _rohwer(), stored with \(z\) measured from the mid-surface:

\[\begin{split}\begin{Bmatrix} \tau_{yz} \\ \tau_{xz} \end{Bmatrix} = f^{(k)}(z) \begin{Bmatrix} Q_y \\ Q_x \end{Bmatrix}\end{split}\]

where \(f^{(k)}(z)\) is quadratic within each ply, vanishes at the bottom and top faces and is continuous at the ply interfaces. This is the consistent way to recover \(\tau_{xz}\) and \(\tau_{yz}\), e.g. for failure criteria, since \(C_s \gamma\) is constant within each ply and non-zero at the free surfaces. For a homogeneous plate it gives the parabola \(\tau_{xz} = 3 Q_x/(2h) (1 - 4 \bar{z}^2/h^2)\), with \(\bar{z}\) measured from the mid-surface.

make_balanced(self) → void#

Make a balanced laminate

The attributes A16, A26, Abar45 and Abarbar45, and A45 unless shear_correction='rohwer', are set to zero to make a balanced laminate. Abarbar45 is kept when it is nan, i.e. when a ply has a singular \(C_s^{(k)}\). The other attributes are not modified, among them B16, B26, D16 and D26, which do not vanish in a balanced laminate in general, e.g. B16 and B26 of an antisymmetric \([+45/-45]\) laminate.

Raises:
RuntimeError

If offset is not zero.

Notes

In a balanced laminate, for every ply at \(+\theta\) there is a ply at \(-\theta\) of the same material and thickness, anywhere in the stack. \(q_{16L}\), \(q_{26L}\) and \(q_{45L}\) are odd functions of the ply angle and the other \(q_{ijL}\) are even, see Lamina.rebuild(). The contributions of such a pair of plies, of thickness \(t\) and with bottom faces at \(z_a\) and \(z_b\), to the integral of \(q_{16L} z^n\) add up to

\[q_{16L}(\theta) \int_{z_a}^{z_a + t} z^n dz + q_{16L}(-\theta) \int_{z_b}^{z_b + t} z^n dz = q_{16L}(\theta) \left( \int_{z_a}^{z_a + t} z^n dz - \int_{z_b}^{z_b + t} z^n dz \right)\]

which vanishes for any \(z_a\), \(z_b\) only for \(n = 0\), see calc_constitutive_matrix(). Therefore, \(A_{16}\), \(A_{26}\) and \(\bar{A}_{45} = \sum_k q_{45L}^{(k)} h_k\) vanish in every balanced laminate, whereas the integrals with \(n \ge 1\), \(B_{16}\), \(B_{26}\), \(D_{16}\) and \(D_{26}\), are proportional to \(z_a - z_b\) and depend on the positions of the plies. The determinant of \(C_s^{(k)}\) being even in \(\theta\), the off-diagonal term of \((C_s^{(k)})^{-1}\) is odd, its thickness integral vanishes and so does \(\bar{\bar{A}}_{45}\). A45 is proportional to Abar45 for every shear_correction other than 'rohwer', see calc_transverse_shear_stiffness(), and vanishes with it. With 'rohwer', A45 follows from the through-thickness distribution of the transverse shear stresses, which depends on the positions of the plies and on the whole ABD matrix, and does not vanish in general, e.g. for a \([+30/-30/0]_s\) laminate, even with g13 = g23, i.e. \(q_{45L} = 0\) in every ply; it is not modified, nor are the coefficients used in the element coordinate system, see calc_Ats_element().

pyfe3d.shellprop.shellprop_from_LaminationParameters(double thickness, MatLamina mat, LaminationParameters lp) → ShellProp#

Return a ShellProp object based in the thickness, material and lamination parameters

In a new ShellProp object, the stiffnesses A11 to D66 and Abar44, Abar45, Abar55 are computed from thickness, from the invariants u1 to u7 of mat and from the lamination parameters of lp, with the combinations of MatLamina.get_invariant_matrix(). No plies are created, offset is zero, and the mass integrals intrho, intrhoz, intrhoz2 and the equivalent properties e1, e2, g12, nu12, nu21 are not computed, remaining zero. The transverse shear stiffnesses A44, A45, A55 are equal to the constant-strain Abar44, Abar45, Abar55, shear_correction is None, scf_k13 = scf_k23 = 1 and the constant-stress Abarbar44, Abarbar45, Abarbar55 are nan. If a shear correction is desired, A44, A45, A55 can be modified directly, and the elements will rotate them as a tensor.

Parameters:
thicknessfloat

The total thickness of the laminate, length

matMatLamina object

Material object

lpLaminationParameters object

The container class with all lamination parameters already defined

Returns:
lamShellProp

laminate with the ABD and Ats matrices already calculated

Notes

The stiffnesses of a ply are linear in the trigonometric functions of its angle, with the invariants of mat as coefficients, see MatLamina.get_invariant_matrix(), and the lamination parameters are their thickness integrals, see ShellProp.calc_lamination_parameters(). For a laminate of a single material, with \(h\) = thickness, \(\int dz = h \int d\bar{z}\), \(\int z dz = h^2/4 \cdot 4\int \bar{z} d\bar{z}\) and \(\int z^2 dz = h^3/12 \cdot 12 \int \bar{z}^2 d\bar{z}\) give:

\[\begin{split}A_{11} = h (u_1 + u_2 \xi_{A1} + u_3 \xi_{A3}), \quad A_{12} = h (u_4 - u_3 \xi_{A3}), \quad A_{22} = h (u_1 - u_2 \xi_{A1} + u_3 \xi_{A3}) \\ A_{16} = h (u_2/2 \, \xi_{A2} + u_3 \xi_{A4}), \quad A_{26} = h (u_2/2 \, \xi_{A2} - u_3 \xi_{A4}), \quad A_{66} = h (u_5 - u_3 \xi_{A3}) \\ B_{11} = \frac{h^2}{4} (u_2 \xi_{B1} + u_3 \xi_{B3}), \quad B_{12} = -\frac{h^2}{4} u_3 \xi_{B3}, \quad B_{22} = \frac{h^2}{4} (-u_2 \xi_{B1} + u_3 \xi_{B3}) \\ B_{16} = \frac{h^2}{4} (u_2/2 \, \xi_{B2} + u_3 \xi_{B4}), \quad B_{26} = \frac{h^2}{4} (u_2/2 \, \xi_{B2} - u_3 \xi_{B4}), \quad B_{66} = -\frac{h^2}{4} u_3 \xi_{B3} \\ \bar{A}_{44} = h (u_6 + u_7 \xi_{Ats,1}), \quad \bar{A}_{45} = -h u_7 \xi_{Ats,2}, \quad \bar{A}_{55} = h (u_6 - u_7 \xi_{Ats,1})\end{split}\]

and \(D_{ij}\) as \(A_{ij}\), with the factor \(h^3/12\) instead of \(h\) and \(\xi_{Di}\) instead of \(\xi_{Ai}\). Since the through-thickness distribution of the plies is not known from the lamination parameters, no shear correction can be computed, hence the uncorrected transverse shear stiffnesses. Without plies, the elements rotate them as a tensor, see ShellProp.calc_Ats_element().

pyfe3d.shellprop.shellprop_from_lamination_parameters(double thickness, MatLamina matlamina, double xiA1, double xiA2, double xiA3, double xiA4, double xiB1, double xiB2, double xiB3, double xiB4, double xiD1, double xiD2, double xiD3, double xiD4, double xiAts1=0, double xiAts2=0) → ShellProp#

Return a ShellProp object based in the thickness, material and lamination parameters

The 14 lamination parameters according to the first-order shear deformation theory are \(\xi_{A1} \cdots \xi_{A4}\), \(\xi_{B1} \cdots \xi_{B4}\), \(\xi_{D1} \cdots \xi_{D4}\), \(\xi_{Ats,1}\) and \(\xi_{Ats,2}\). They are stored in a new LaminationParameters object, passed to shellprop_from_LaminationParameters().

Parameters:
thicknessfloat

The total thickness of the plate, length

matlaminaMatLamina object

Material object

xiA1, xiA2, xiA3, xiA4float

Lamination parameters \(\xi_{A1} \cdots \xi_{A4}\), dimensionless.

xiB1, xiB2, xiB3, xiB4float

Lamination parameters \(\xi_{B1} \cdots \xi_{B4}\), dimensionless.

xiD1, xiD2, xiD3, xiD4float

Lamination parameters \(\xi_{D1} \cdots \xi_{D4}\), dimensionless.

xiAts1, xiAts2float, optional

Lamination parameters \(\xi_{Ats,1}\) and \(\xi_{Ats,2}\), dimensionless. Default 0.

Returns:
lamShellProp

Shell property with the ABD and Ats matrices already calculated. See shellprop_from_LaminationParameters() for the transverse shear stiffnesses.

Notes

Note that \(\xi_{Ats,1}\) and \(\xi_{Ats,2}\) are optional and usually equal to zero, becoming important only when the transverse shear modulus is different in the two directions, i.e. when \(G_{13} \ne G{23}\), since they multiply the invariant \(u_7 = (q_{44} - q_{55})/2 = (g_{23} - g_{13})/2\), see shellprop_from_LaminationParameters().
