Mass matrices of the shells#

The mass matrices of the shell elements, computed by update_M, e.g. Quad4.update_M(), follow from the kinetic energy of the FSDT displacement field (2), integrated through the thickness, Brockman (1987), Eq. (20):

(1)#\[T = \frac{1}{2} \int_A \left[ I_\rho \left( \dot{u}^2 + \dot{v}^2 + \dot{w}^2 \right) + 2 I_{\rho z} \left( \dot{u} \dot{r}_y - \dot{v} \dot{r}_x \right) + I_{\rho z^2} \left( \dot{r}_x^2 + \dot{r}_y^2 \right) \right] dA\]

with \(I_\rho\), \(I_{\rho z}\) and \(I_{\rho z^2}\) the thickness integrals of the density times \(1\), \(z\) and \(z^2\), the attributes intrho, intrhoz and intrhoz2 of the ShellProp, measured from the reference surface and therefore including the offset, see Shell properties. With all the fields interpolated by the same shape functions \(S_i\), the block of the nodes \(i\) and \(j\) is, in element coordinates and for \(u, v, w, r_x, r_y, r_z\), as in Eq. (21) of Brockman (1987),

(2)#\[\begin{split}\boldsymbol{M}_{ij} = h_{ij} \begin{bmatrix} I_\rho & 0 & 0 & 0 & I_{\rho z} & 0 \\ 0 & I_\rho & 0 & -I_{\rho z} & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & I_\rho & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & -I_{\rho z} & 0 & I_{\rho z^2} & 0 & 0 \\ I_{\rho z} & 0 & 0 & 0 & I_{\rho z^2} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad h_{ij} = \int_A S_i S_j \, dA\end{split}\]

rotated to global coordinates with (1). The drilling rotation carries no inertia. The signs of \(I_{\rho z}\) follow from \(u + z r_y\) and \(v - z r_x\), see Conventions.

Integration schemes#

The argument mtype of update_M selects how \(h_{ij}\) is integrated:

mtype

Quadrilaterals, Quad4, Quad4R

Triangles, Tria3R, Tria3DSG

0, consistent

two by two Gauss-Legendre points, exact

three points of Cowper (1973), exact: \(h_{ii} = A_e/6\), \(h_{ij} = A_e/12\)

1, lumped

two by two Gauss-Lobatto points, the nodes: \(h_{ii}\) the Jacobian determinant at node \(i\), \(h_{ij} = 0\)

the vertices: \(h_{ii} = A_e/3\), \(h_{ij} = 0\)

2, one point

centroid, \(h_{ij} = A/16\)

centroid, \(h_{ij} = A_e/9\), Tria3R only

Any other value, and 2 for Tria3DSG, raises a ValueError. With mtype = 2 the mass of a quadrilateral is placed at the average of its node positions, the centroid of its area only for a parallelogram.

Lumped mass matrices#

The lumped matrices integrate the whole kinetic energy (1) with a nodal quadrature, in which \(S_i\) is 1 at node \(i\) and 0 at the other nodes: the Gauss-Lobatto points of the lumped matrix of Brockman (1987) for the quadrilaterals and the vertex rule for the triangles. They therefore couple no different nodes, and node \(i\) carries the block (2) with \(h_{ii}\), the coupling through \(I_{\rho z}\) included: the matrix is block-diagonal per node, and diagonal only when \(I_{\rho z} = 0\), as Brockman writes, e.g. for a homogeneous plate without offset. The weights sum to the area, such that the lumped matrix has the exact mass and the exact first moment of the mass through the thickness, \(I_{\rho z} A\), which keeps the centre of mass at the offset from the reference surface. In the plane of the element the first moment of the mass is exact for the triangles, since \(\sum_i (A_e/3) \boldsymbol{x}_i = \int_A \boldsymbol{x} \, dA\), and for a parallelogram, where \(h_{ii} = A/4 = \int_A S_i \, dA\), but not for a general quadrilateral, whose Jacobian determinant varies. Of the rotary inertia, the term \(I_{\rho z^2} A\) about the axes in the plane of the element is exact, whereas the part due to the distribution of \(I_\rho\) over the area, about any axis, is concentrated at the nodes: 3 times the exact value about the centroid of a parallelogram and 4 times that of a triangle, an error that decreases with the size of the elements.

The mass matrix of a warped quadrilateral with warp_offsets = 1 is \(\boldsymbol{J}^\top \boldsymbol{M} \boldsymbol{J}\), with the rigid offsets of Choosing an element; it keeps the form (2) with \(I_{\rho z}\) and \(I_{\rho z^2}\) replaced by the moments of the mass about the warped nodes, see Quad4.update_M(), and the lumped matrix remains block-diagonal per node.

Choosing a mass matrix#

The consistent matrices integrate the kinetic energy exactly and are the default. The lumped matrices give the exact mass and, for the triangles and the parallelograms, the exact centre of mass, with no coupling between nodes, and overestimate the rotary inertia of the in-plane distribution of mass. The one-point matrices concentrate the mass at the centroid, or at the average of the nodes of a quadrilateral, and couple all nodes equally. The natural frequency analyses of Linear buckling and natural frequencies and the inertia relief of Inertia relief take the assembled matrix of any scheme.
