Shell properties#

The shell elements read their constitutive matrices and mass integrals from a ShellProp, module pyfe3d.shellprop, passed to every update_* method that needs it; the functions of pyfe3d.shellprop_utils create it from the ply data. This chapter gives the equivalent single-layer theory behind these classes: the stiffnesses of a ply, their integration through the thickness, the offset of the reference surface, the transverse shear stiffness with its correction, the rotation to the element axes and the lamination parameters. The use of these matrices by the elements is described in Shell elements: kinematics and formulation; the beam properties in Beam properties.

Stiffness of a ply#

A ply is described by a MatLamina, usually created by read_laminaprop() from laminaprop = (E11, E22, nu12, G12, G13, G23), (E, nu) for an isotropic material, for which \(G = E/(2(1 + \nu))\), or nine entries with E33, nu13 and nu23, and by a Lamina with its thickness and angle. The shell elements use the plane-stress reduced stiffnesses, \(\sigma_{33} = 0\), in the material axes 1, 2 of the ply (Reddy, 2003, Eq. (2.4.4); Jones, 1999, Eq. (2.66)),

(1)#\[Q_{11} = \frac{E_{11}}{1 - \nu_{12} \nu_{21}}, \quad Q_{12} = \frac{\nu_{12} E_{22}}{1 - \nu_{12} \nu_{21}}, \quad Q_{22} = \frac{E_{22}}{1 - \nu_{12} \nu_{21}}, \quad Q_{66} = G_{12}, \quad Q_{44} = G_{23}, \quad Q_{55} = G_{13}\]

with \(\nu_{21} = \nu_{12} E_{22}/E_{11}\), which depend on E11, E22, nu12, G12, G13 and G23 only, also when laminaprop gives E33, nu13 and nu23, see Lamina.rebuild() and MatLamina.rebuild(). With the ply angle \(\theta\) measured from the laminate \(x\) axis to the fibre direction 1, \(c = \cos\theta\) and \(s = \sin\theta\), the stiffnesses in the laminate axes are (Reddy, 2003)

(2)#\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} q_{11L} &= Q_{11} c^4 + 2 (Q_{12} + 2 Q_{66}) s^2 c^2 + Q_{22} s^4, &\quad q_{22L} &= Q_{11} s^4 + 2 (Q_{12} + 2 Q_{66}) s^2 c^2 + Q_{22} c^4, \\ q_{12L} &= (Q_{11} + Q_{22} - 4 Q_{66}) s^2 c^2 + Q_{12} (s^4 + c^4), &\quad q_{66L} &= (Q_{11} + Q_{22} - 2 Q_{12} - 2 Q_{66}) s^2 c^2 + Q_{66} (s^4 + c^4), \\ q_{16L} &= (Q_{11} - Q_{12} - 2 Q_{66}) s c^3 + (Q_{12} - Q_{22} + 2 Q_{66}) s^3 c, &\quad q_{26L} &= (Q_{11} - Q_{12} - 2 Q_{66}) s^3 c + (Q_{12} - Q_{22} + 2 Q_{66}) s c^3, \\ q_{44L} &= Q_{44} c^2 + Q_{55} s^2, \quad q_{45L} = (Q_{55} - Q_{44}) s c, &\quad q_{55L} &= Q_{55} c^2 + Q_{44} s^2 \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

The same stiffnesses are linear in \(\cos 2\theta\), \(\sin 2\theta\), \(\cos 4\theta\) and \(\sin 4\theta\), with the invariants of Tsai and Pagano, as given by Jones (1999), Eq. (2.99),

(3)#\[u_1 = \frac{3 Q_{11} + 3 Q_{22} + 2 Q_{12} + 4 Q_{66}}{8}, \quad u_2 = \frac{Q_{11} - Q_{22}}{2}, \quad u_3 = \frac{Q_{11} + Q_{22} - 2 Q_{12} - 4 Q_{66}}{8}, \quad u_4 = \frac{Q_{11} + Q_{22} + 6 Q_{12} - 4 Q_{66}}{8}\]

with \(u_5 = (u_1 - u_4)/2\), and the transverse shear invariants \(u_6 = (Q_{44} + Q_{55})/2\), \(u_7 = (Q_{44} - Q_{55})/2\), the attributes u1 to u7 of MatLamina, which give the stiffnesses of the lamination parameters below. MatLamina.rebuild(plane_stress=False) gives the three-dimensional stiffnesses and invariants instead, e.g. for continuum shell elements of other codes; the shell elements of pyfe3d do not use them. Thermal expansion is not modelled. A zero modulus is admitted as the limit of a positive-definite material, e.g. the antiplane core laminaprop = (0, 0, nu12, 0, G13, G23), which adds only transverse shear stiffness to a laminate.

Stiffness of the laminate#

With the plies of a ShellProp stacked from the bottom, ply \(k\) occupying \(z_k \le z \le z_{k+1}\), \(z_1 = -h/2 + e\) and \(z_{N+1} = h/2 + e\), \(h\) the total thickness and \(e\) = offset, the stiffnesses of (4) and the mass integrals are, see ShellProp.calc_constitutive_matrix(),

(4)#\[(A_{ij}, B_{ij}, D_{ij}) = \sum_k \int_{z_k}^{z_{k+1}} q_{ijL}^{(k)} (1, z, z^2) \, dz, \qquad (I_\rho, I_{\rho z}, I_{\rho z^2}) = \sum_k \int_{z_k}^{z_{k+1}} \rho^{(k)} (1, z, z^2) \, dz\]

for \(ij = 11, 12, 16, 22, 26, 66\), the attributes A11 to D66, intrho, intrhoz and intrhoz2. A property is created by:

  • isotropic_plate(), from thickness, E, nu and, optionally, rho, offset and shear_correction;

  • laminated_plate(), from the stacking sequence stack, the ply angles in degrees from the bottom to the top ply, the ply thickness plyt or plyts, and laminaprop or laminaprops;

  • shellprop_from_LaminationParameters(), from the thickness, a MatLamina and the LaminationParameters, see below.

The equivalent moduli e1, e2, g12, nu12, nu21 of ShellProp.calc_equivalent_properties() are those of the membrane compliance with zero moments, e.g. \(e_1 = 1/(h a_{11})\) with \(\boldsymbol{a} = [[\boldsymbol{A}, \boldsymbol{B}], [\boldsymbol{B}, \boldsymbol{D}]]^{-1}\).

Offset of the reference surface#

The nodes of the shell elements are on the reference surface, and \(z\) is measured from it along the element normal. With offset \(= e\), the mid-surface of the laminate is at the distance \(e\) from the nodes. For a single isotropic layer, (4) gives

(5)#\[A_{11} = A_{22} = \frac{E h}{1 - \nu^2}, \quad A_{12} = \nu A_{11}, \quad A_{66} = G h, \qquad \boldsymbol{B} = e \boldsymbol{A}, \qquad \boldsymbol{D} = \left( \frac{h^2}{12} + e^2 \right) \boldsymbol{A}, \qquad I_{\rho z} = \rho h e, \qquad I_{\rho z^2} = \rho \left( \frac{h^3}{12} + h e^2 \right)\]

and, for any laminate, with \(\boldsymbol{B}^0\) and \(\boldsymbol{D}^0\) about the mid-surface (Vasiliev and Morozov, 2001, Eq. (5.28)),

(6)#\[\boldsymbol{B} = \boldsymbol{B}^0 + e \boldsymbol{A}, \qquad \boldsymbol{D} = \boldsymbol{D}^0 + 2 e \boldsymbol{B}^0 + e^2 \boldsymbol{A}\]

while \(\boldsymbol{A}\) and the transverse shear stiffnesses do not change. An offset couples the membrane and the bending responses as an unsymmetric laminate does, which models e.g. a skin whose nodes are on its inner surface, shared with the beams of a stiffener; it also moves the centre of mass in the mass matrices, see Mass matrices of the shells.

Transverse shear stiffness and shear correction#

The transverse shear strains of the FSDT are constant through the thickness, whereas the transverse shear stresses of a bent plate vanish at its faces, and the stiffness \(\bar{\boldsymbol{A}}_{ts} = \sum_k \boldsymbol{C}_s^{(k)} h_k\), with \(\boldsymbol{C}_s^{(k)} = [[q_{44L}, q_{45L}], [q_{45L}, q_{55L}]]\), overestimates the shear stiffness. The attributes A44, A45 and A55 of a ShellProp are the stiffnesses used by the elements with the correction already applied, and the elements apply no other factor. The correction is selected with shear_correction, see ShellProp.calc_transverse_shear_stiffness().

Equilibrium approach of Rohwer (1988), the default 'rohwer'. The transverse shear stresses are obtained from the equilibrium of two cylindrical bending states with constant membrane forces, one with the resultants depending on \(x\) only, \(M_{xx,x} = Q_x\) and \(M_{xy,x} = Q_y\), and one with the resultants depending on \(y\) only, by integrating \(\tau_{xz,z} = -\sigma_{xx,x}\) and \(\tau_{yz,z} = -\sigma_{yy,y}\) from the bottom face, where the tractions vanish, with the in-plane strains of the inverse of the ABD matrix. The result is a distribution \(\{\tau_{yz}, \tau_{xz}\} = \boldsymbol{f}^{(k)}(z) \{Q_y, Q_x\}\), quadratic in each ply, continuous at the interfaces and zero at both faces, and the stiffness follows from the complementary energy:

(7)#\[\boldsymbol{A}_{ts} = \left[ \sum_k \int_{z_k}^{z_{k+1}} \boldsymbol{f}^{(k)\top} \left( \boldsymbol{C}_s^{(k)} \right)^{-1} \boldsymbol{f}^{(k)} \, dz \right]^{-1}\]

integrated exactly with three Gauss-Legendre points per ply. It is valid for arbitrary anisotropic and unsymmetric laminates, gives the full \(2 \times 2\) matrix and does not depend on offset. It is not invariant to a rotation of the axes, because the two bending states are tied to \(x\) and \(y\): the elements use it re-evaluated with the plies rotated to their own axes, see ShellProp.calc_Ats_element(). The compliance in a frame rotated by \(\theta\) is a trigonometric polynomial of degree 10 with a period of 180 degrees,

(8)#\[\boldsymbol{A}_{ts}^{-1}(\theta) = \boldsymbol{S}_0 + \sum_{n=1}^{5} \left( \boldsymbol{S}_{cn} \cos 2n\theta + \boldsymbol{S}_{sn} \sin 2n\theta \right)\]

whose coefficients are computed exactly, once per laminate, from 16 rotated evaluations, such that the cost per element does not depend on the number of plies.

Scalar factors. 'vlachoutsis' applies the factors of Vlachoutsis (1992) to the constant-strain stiffness, A55 = k13*Abar55, A44 = k23*Abar44 and A45 = (k13 + k23)/2*Abar45; for the direction 1, with \(C_{11} = q_{11L}\) and \(C_{55} = q_{55L}\) of each ply,

(9)#\[z_n = \frac{\int C_{11} z \, dz}{\int C_{11} \, dz}, \quad R = \int C_{11} (z - z_n)^2 dz, \quad g(z) = -\int_{z_1}^{z} C_{11} (\bar{z} - z_n) \, d\bar{z}, \quad k_{13} = \frac{R^2}{\bar{A}_{55} \int g^2/C_{55} \, dz}\]

i.e. the energy of the stress \(\tau_{xz} = Q_x g(z)/R\) of the one-dimensional equilibrium equals that of the FSDT, and analogously \(k_{23}\) with \(C_{22}\), \(C_{44}\). The other options are applied in the same way:

  • 'whitney': Whitney (1973), identical to 'vlachoutsis';

  • 'chow': Chow (1971), for laminates symmetric about the mid-surface, for which it is identical to 'vlachoutsis';

  • 'birman_bert': the average shear strain factors of Birman and Bert (2002);

  • 'thickness_shear': the factors that match the first thickness-shear frequency of the laminate, after Yang, Norris and Stavsky (1966), as described by Sun and Whitney (1973), extending Mindlin (1951); it requires the ply densities;

  • 'constant': \(k = 5/6\);

  • None: no correction, \(\boldsymbol{A}_{ts} = \bar{\boldsymbol{A}}_{ts}\).

For an isotropic plate 'rohwer', 'vlachoutsis', 'whitney', 'chow' and 'constant' give \(5/6 \, G h\), 'birman_bert' gives \(G h\) and 'thickness_shear' gives \(\pi^2/12 \, G h\). Except for 'rohwer', the elements rotate \(\boldsymbol{A}_{ts}\) to their axes as a tensor. The constant-strain Abar44, Abar45, Abar55 and the constant-stress \(\bar{\bar{\boldsymbol{A}}}_{ts} = h^2 [\sum_k (\boldsymbol{C}_s^{(k)})^{-1} h_k]^{-1}\), Abarbar44, Abarbar45, Abarbar55, are kept for comparison, and scf_k13, scf_k23 are the resulting ratios, informative only. A ShellProp created from lamination parameters has no plies and no correction; A44, A45, A55 can then be set directly.

Sandwich sections. For stiff faces on a soft core the factor \(5/6\) is badly wrong: 'rohwer' and 'vlachoutsis' reproduce the factors tabulated by Vlachoutsis (1992), Tables I and II, down to \(K = 0.01964\), for which \(5/6\) overestimates the shear stiffness 42 times, and, for an antiplane core, the shear stiffness \(G_c d^2/c\) of Allen (1969), Eq. (2.15b), with \(d\) the distance between the centroids of the faces, within the bounds given by the local bending and the shear strains of the faces that Allen neglects. The sandwich panel of Rohwer (1988), Sec. 4.3, is not used as a reference, because its printed stiffnesses are not consistent with the stated lay-up, see the docstring of tests/test_shellprop_transverse_shear.py.

Transverse shear stresses. The stresses of the element strains, \(\boldsymbol{C}_s \boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(0)}\), are constant in each ply and non-zero at the faces. The stresses for e.g. a failure criterion are recovered from the shear forces with the distribution of Rohwer, ShellProp.calc_transverse_shear_stress(), which for a homogeneous plate is the parabola \(\tau_{xz} = 3 Q_x/(2h) (1 - 4 \bar{z}^2/h^2)\), or from the derivatives of the generalised strains of a solution, by integrating the three-dimensional equilibrium equations through the thickness, ShellProp.calc_equilibrium_transverse_shear(), as in the a posteriori approach of Noor and Peters (1989), whose energy is given by ShellProp.calc_aposteriori_energy().

Constitutive matrices in the element axes#

The ply angles are measured from the material direction of each element, see Conventions, and the elements rotate the matrices from the material direction to their own axes with m11, m12, m21, m22, \(m_{11} = m_{22} = \cos\theta\), \(m_{21} = -m_{12} = \sin\theta\), \(\theta\) being the angle from the element \(x\) axis to the material direction:

(10)#\[\begin{split}\boldsymbol{A}_e = \boldsymbol{T}_\sigma \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{T}_\sigma^\top, \quad \boldsymbol{T}_\sigma = \begin{bmatrix} m_{11}^2 & m_{12}^2 & 2 m_{11} m_{12} \\ m_{21}^2 & m_{22}^2 & 2 m_{21} m_{22} \\ m_{11} m_{21} & m_{12} m_{22} & m_{11} m_{22} + m_{12} m_{21} \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \boldsymbol{A}_{ts,e} = \boldsymbol{T}_\gamma^\top \boldsymbol{A}_{ts} \boldsymbol{T}_\gamma, \quad \boldsymbol{T}_\gamma = \begin{bmatrix} m_{22} & m_{12} \\ m_{21} & m_{11} \end{bmatrix}\end{split}\]

and the same for \(\boldsymbol{B}\) and \(\boldsymbol{D}\); with 'rohwer' and plies, \(\boldsymbol{A}_{ts,e}\) is (8) instead. The rotated matrices are given by ShellProp.calc_constitutive_element() and ShellProp.calc_Ats_element(): a ply at 0 degrees seen in an element whose material direction is at \(\theta\) from its \(x\) axis has the stiffnesses of a ply at \(\theta\).

Lamination parameters#

With \(\bar{z} = z/h\) measured from the reference surface and the ply angle \(\theta(\bar{z})\), the lamination parameters of ShellProp.calc_lamination_parameters() are

(11)#\[\{\xi_{A1}, \xi_{A2}, \xi_{A3}, \xi_{A4}\} = \int \{\cos 2\theta, \sin 2\theta, \cos 4\theta, \sin 4\theta\} \, d\bar{z}, \qquad \xi_{Bi} = 4 \int (\cdot) \, \bar{z} \, d\bar{z}, \qquad \xi_{Di} = 12 \int (\cdot) \, \bar{z}^2 d\bar{z}\]

with the same four functions, and \(\{\xi_{Ats,1}, \xi_{Ats,2}\} = \int \{\cos 2\theta, \sin 2\theta\} d\bar{z}\); the \(\xi_{Ai}\) and \(\xi_{Di}\) are those of Krupa et al. (2018) with offset = 0. For a laminate of one material the stiffnesses are linear in them, with the invariants (3), e.g.

(12)#\[A_{11} = h (u_1 + u_2 \xi_{A1} + u_3 \xi_{A3}), \quad A_{16} = h \left( \frac{u_2}{2} \xi_{A2} + u_3 \xi_{A4} \right), \quad \bar{A}_{44} = h (u_6 + u_7 \xi_{Ats,1}), \quad D_{11} = \frac{h^3}{12} \left( (1 + 12 \bar{e}^2) u_1 + u_2 \xi_{D1} + u_3 \xi_{D3} \right)\]

with \(\bar{e} = e/h\), see shellprop_from_LaminationParameters(), whose Notes give all the terms, and whose transverse shear stiffness is the uncorrected one, the distribution of the plies being unknown. The lamination parameters include the offset, and shellprop_from_LaminationParameters() needs the same offset to give back the stiffnesses. GradABD gives the derivatives of the stiffnesses with respect to the thickness and the lamination parameters, e.g. for the optimisation of the stiffness distribution.

Idealised laminates. After ShellProp.calc_constitutive_matrix(), ShellProp.make_balanced(), ShellProp.make_orthotropic() and ShellProp.make_symmetric() set to zero the stiffnesses that vanish for a balanced laminate, \(A_{16}\), \(A_{26}\) and \(\bar{A}_{45}\), for an orthotropic one, all the terms 16, 26 and 45, or for a symmetric one, \(\boldsymbol{B}\), and ShellProp.make_smeared() replaces \(\boldsymbol{D}\) by \(h^2 \boldsymbol{A}/12\) and \(\boldsymbol{B}\) by zero; they require offset = 0. In a balanced laminate \(B_{16}\), \(B_{26}\), \(D_{16}\) and \(D_{26}\) depend on the positions of the plies and do not vanish in general, and with 'rohwer' A45 neither. The functions make_balanced_LP(), make_orthotropic_LP() and make_symmetric_LP() do the same with the lamination parameters.

Limits of validity#

  • Equivalent single layer: the laminate is represented by the stiffnesses of its reference surface, with the plane-stress stiffnesses of each ply and an inextensible normal; the transverse shear stresses are recovered a posteriori.

  • The shear correction of 'rohwer' rests on two cylindrical bending states with constant membrane forces; the scalar factors on the one-dimensional equilibrium along each direction, exact only for specially orthotropic plies, and their A45 is an average that cannot represent the coupling of angle-ply laminates with Abar45 = 0.

  • Linear elastic plies, without thermal strains; the strengths of MatLamina are stored and not used.
