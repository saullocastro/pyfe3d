Shell and beam properties#

Shell properties and laminates#

The shell elements read their constitutive matrices and mass integrals from a ShellProp, passed to every update_* method that needs it. The stiffnesses of the first-order shear deformation theory are

\[\begin{split}\begin{Bmatrix} N \\ M \end{Bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} A & B \\ B & D \end{bmatrix} \begin{Bmatrix} \varepsilon^{(0)} \\ \varepsilon^{(1)} \end{Bmatrix}, \qquad \begin{Bmatrix} Q_y \\ Q_x \end{Bmatrix} = \boldsymbol{A}_{ts} \begin{Bmatrix} \gamma_{yz} \\ \gamma_{xz} \end{Bmatrix}\end{split}\]

with \(A_{ij}\), \(B_{ij}\), \(D_{ij}\) the thickness integrals of the ply stiffnesses times \(1\), \(z\) and \(z^2\), and \(\boldsymbol{A}_{ts}\) = [[A44, A45], [A45, A55]], see ShellProp. A property is created by:

  • isotropic_plate(), from thickness, E, nu and, optionally, rho, offset and shear_correction;

  • laminated_plate(), from the stacking sequence stack, the ply angles in degrees from the bottom to the top ply, the ply thickness plyt or plyts, and the ply material laminaprop = (E11, E22, nu12, G12, G13, G23) or laminaprops, see read_laminaprop();

  • shellprop_from_LaminationParameters(), from the thickness, a MatLamina and the LaminationParameters, whose gradients are given by GradABD, e.g. for the optimisation of the stiffness distribution, see the tutorials.

The ply stiffnesses are those of plane stress, \(\sigma_{33} = 0\), see Lamina.rebuild(), which depend on E11, E22, nu12, G12, G13 and G23 only, also when laminaprop gives E33, nu13 and nu23. The invariants of MatLamina, used by shellprop_from_LaminationParameters() and GradABD, are by default those of the same plane-stress stiffnesses, see MatLamina.rebuild(), such that the lamination parameters of ShellProp.calc_lamination_parameters() give back the stiffnesses of laminated_plate(), for any laminaprop:

@pytest.mark.parametrize('layup', LAYUPS.keys())
@pytest.mark.parametrize('material', LAMINAPROPS_9.keys())
def test_lamination_parameters_three_dimensional_constants(material, layup):
    r"""Both routes give the plane-stress stiffnesses when e3 is given

    The shell elements are plane stress, `\sigma_{33} = 0`: the reduced
    stiffnesses depend on E1, E2, nu12, G12, G13 and G23 only (Reddy, 2003,
    Eq. (2.4.4), p. 101; Jones, 1999, Eqs. (2.64) and (2.66), p. 72), and the
    invariants of Tsai and Pagano are those of the reduced stiffnesses
    (Jones, 1999, Eq. (2.99), p. 86). :func:`.laminated_plate` and
    :func:`.shellprop_from_LaminationParameters`, with the lamination
    parameters of the same layup, must give A, B and D of the classical
    lamination theory, ``clt_abd``, and the constant-strain transverse shear
    stiffness ``abar_ts``, to round-off, also for e3 != 0, and the same
    values as for the six-entry ``laminaprop`` without e3, nu13 and nu23.
    """
    lamprop = LAMINAPROPS_9[material]
    stack, plyts = LAYUPS[layup]
    h = sum(plyts)
    # NOTE no shear correction, such that Ats = Abar_ts in both routes
    prop = laminated_plate(stack, plyts=plyts, laminaprop=lamprop,
                           shear_correction=None)
    mat = read_laminaprop(lamprop)
    assert mat.e3 != 0 and mat.plane_stress
    lp = prop.calc_lamination_parameters()
    prop_lp = shellprop_from_LaminationParameters(prop.h, mat, lp)

    A, B, D = clt_abd(stack, plyts, [lamprop]*len(stack))
    Abar = abar_ts(stack, plyts, lamprop[4], lamprop[5])
    for p in (prop, prop_lp):
        assert_abd(p.A, p.B, p.D, A, B, D, h)
        assert np.allclose(p.Abar_ts, Abar, rtol=1e-12,
                           atol=1e-14*np.abs(Abar).max())
    assert np.allclose(prop_lp.Ats, prop.Ats, rtol=1e-12,
                       atol=1e-14*np.abs(Abar).max())

    # NOTE e3, nu13 and nu23 do not change the plane-stress invariants
    mat6 = read_laminaprop(lamprop[:6])
    for name in ('q11', 'q12', 'q21', 'q22', 'q44', 'q55', 'q66', 'u1', 'u2',
                 'u3', 'u4', 'u5', 'u6', 'u7'):
        assert getattr(mat, name) == getattr(mat6, name), name

    # NOTE the gradients use the same invariants: at constant lamination
    #      parameters A is linear in h and D cubic, dA/dh = A/h and dD/dh =
    #      3 D/h
    grad = GradABD()
    grad.calc_LP_grad(prop.h, mat, lp)
    ij = ((0, 0), (0, 1), (0, 2), (1, 1), (1, 2), (2, 2))
    gradA = np.array([A[i, j] for i, j in ij])/h
    gradD = np.array([D[i, j] for i, j in ij])*3/h
    assert np.allclose(np.asarray(grad.gradAij)[:, 0], gradA, rtol=1e-12,
                       atol=1e-14*np.abs(gradA).max())
    assert np.allclose(np.asarray(grad.gradDij)[:, 0], gradD, rtol=1e-12,
                       atol=1e-14*np.abs(gradD).max())

    # NOTE three-dimensional option: stiffer than plane stress, the
    #      lamination-parameter route then no longer gives the stiffnesses of
    #      the shell elements
    mat.rebuild(plane_stress=False)
    prop_3d = shellprop_from_LaminationParameters(prop.h, mat, lp)
    assert prop_3d.A11 > (1 + 1e-3)*prop.A11
    assert prop_3d.A22 > (1 + 1e-3)*prop.A22

MatLamina.rebuild(plane_stress=False) gives the three-dimensional stiffnesses and invariants instead, e.g. for continuum shell elements of other codes; the shell elements of pyfe3d do not use them. Plane strain and thermal expansion are not modelled: the thermal expansion coefficients and the reference temperature of MatLamina are not used.

The ply angles are measured from the material direction of each element, the vector xmati, xmatj, xmatk of update_rotation_matrix projected onto the element, or from the element \(x\) axis when it is not given, see Conventions. The elements rotate the matrices from the material direction to the element axes with m11, m12, m21, m22; ShellProp.calc_constitutive_element() gives the rotated matrices.

Offset of the reference surface#

The nodes of the shell elements are on the reference surface, and \(z\) is measured from it. With offset \(= e\), the laminate occupies \(-h/2 + e \le z \le h/2 + e\), i.e. its mid-surface is at the distance \(e\) from the nodes, along the element \(z\) axis. For a single isotropic layer the integrals give

\[\boldsymbol{B} = e \boldsymbol{A}, \qquad \boldsymbol{D} = \boldsymbol{D}_{e=0} + e^2 \boldsymbol{A}, \qquad \int \rho z \, dz = \rho h e, \qquad \int \rho z^2 \, dz = \rho \left(\frac{h^3}{12} + h e^2\right)\]

such that an offset couples the membrane and bending responses. It models e.g. a skin whose nodes are on its inner surface, shared with the beams of a stiffener. The mass integrals intrho, intrhoz and intrhoz2 enter the mass matrices of the shells.

For any laminate, \(\boldsymbol{B} = \boldsymbol{B}_{e=0} + e \boldsymbol{A}\) and \(\boldsymbol{D} = \boldsymbol{D}_{e=0} + 2 e \boldsymbol{B}_{e=0} + e^2 \boldsymbol{A}\), the shift of the stiffness integrals to another reference plane (Vasiliev and Morozov, Mechanics and Analysis of Composite Materials, Elsevier, 2001, Eq. (5.28), p. 235), while \(\boldsymbol{A}\) and the transverse shear stiffnesses do not change. The lamination parameters of ShellProp.calc_lamination_parameters() are integrated about the reference surface, i.e. they include the offset, and shellprop_from_LaminationParameters() needs the same offset to give back the stiffnesses:

@pytest.mark.parametrize('offset', ['+0.2 mm', '-0.2 mm', 'one ply'])
@pytest.mark.parametrize('layup', LAYUPS.keys())
@pytest.mark.parametrize('material', LAMINAPROPS_9.keys())
def test_lamination_parameters_offset(material, layup, offset):
    r"""Lamination parameters of a laminate with an offset

    With ``offset`` `= e`, the laminate occupies `-h/2 + e \le z \le h/2 +
    e`, and :meth:`.ShellProp.calc_lamination_parameters` integrates about
    the reference surface. :func:`.shellprop_from_LaminationParameters`
    given the same offset must give the A, B and D of ``clt_abd`` with the
    offset, which also satisfy the shift of the stiffness integrals to
    another reference plane, `B = B^0 + e A` and `D = D^0 + 2 e B^0 + e^2
    A`, with `B^0`, `D^0` about the mid-surface (Vasiliev and Morozov,
    Mechanics and Analysis of Composite Materials, Elsevier, 2001, Eq.
    (5.28), p. 235). The constant-strain transverse shear stiffness does not
    depend on the offset. The derivatives of GradABD with respect to the
    thickness, at constant lamination parameters and offset, are compared
    with central differences of the stiffnesses, polynomials of degree 3 in
    h, whose truncation error is `(\Delta h/h)^2 = 10^{-12}` and whose
    round-off error is about `10^{-16} h/\Delta h = 10^{-10}`, relative.
    """
    lamprop = LAMINAPROPS_9[material]
    stack, plyts = LAYUPS[layup]
    e = {'+0.2 mm': 0.2e-3, '-0.2 mm': -0.2e-3, 'one ply': plyts[0]}[offset]
    h = sum(plyts)
    lam = laminated_plate(stack, plyts=plyts, laminaprop=lamprop, offset=e,
                          shear_correction=None)
    mat = read_laminaprop(lamprop)
    lp = lam.calc_lamination_parameters()
    lam_lp = shellprop_from_LaminationParameters(lam.h, mat, lp, e)
    assert lam_lp.offset == e

    A, B, D = clt_abd(stack, plyts, [lamprop]*len(stack), offset=e)
    A0, B0, D0 = clt_abd(stack, plyts, [lamprop]*len(stack))
    assert_abd(A, B, D, A0, B0 + e*A0, D0 + 2*e*B0 + e**2*A0, h)
    Abar = abar_ts(stack, plyts, lamprop[4], lamprop[5])
    for p in (lam, lam_lp):
        assert_abd(p.A, p.B, p.D, A, B, D, h)
        assert np.allclose(p.Abar_ts, Abar, rtol=1e-12,
                           atol=1e-14*np.abs(Abar).max())
    assert np.allclose(lam_lp.Ats, lam.Ats, rtol=1e-12,
                       atol=1e-14*np.abs(Abar).max())

    # NOTE the same through the function with the lamination parameters as
    #      arguments
    names = ['xi%s%d' % (m, i) for m in 'ABD' for i in (1, 2, 3, 4)]
    lam_lp2 = shellprop_from_lamination_parameters(lam.h, mat,
            *[getattr(lp, name) for name in names], lp.xiAts1, lp.xiAts2,
            offset=e)
    assert_abd(lam_lp2.A, lam_lp2.B, lam_lp2.D, A, B, D, h)

    grad = GradABD()
    grad.calc_LP_grad(lam.h, mat, lp, e)
    ij = ('11', '12', '16', '22', '26', '66')
    def stiffnesses(hh):
        p = shellprop_from_LaminationParameters(hh, mat, lp, e)
        return {m: np.array([getattr(p, m + s) for s in ij]) for m in 'ABD'}
    dh = 1e-6*h
    plus, minus = stiffnesses(h + dh), stiffnesses(h - dh)
    for m, g in (('A', grad.gradAij), ('B', grad.gradBij),
                 ('D', grad.gradDij)):
        fd = (plus[m] - minus[m])/(2*dh)
        assert np.allclose(np.asarray(g)[:, 0], fd, rtol=1e-8,
                           atol=1e-8*np.abs(fd).max()), m

Transverse shear correction#

The stiffnesses A44, A45 and A55 of a ShellProp are the ones used by the elements, with the shear correction already applied, and the elements apply no other correction factor. The correction is selected with shear_correction, see ShellProp.calc_transverse_shear_stiffness():

  • 'rohwer', the default: the equilibrium approach of Rohwer (1988), giving the full matrix \(\boldsymbol{A}_{ts}\), re-evaluated in the element axes by ShellProp.calc_Ats_element() when the material direction differs from the element \(x\) axis;

  • 'vlachoutsis': the two factors of Vlachoutsis (1992), doi:10.1002/nme.1620330712, identical to 'whitney' (Whitney, 1973) and, for laminates symmetric about the mid-surface, to 'chow' (Chow, 1971);

  • 'birman_bert': the average shear strain factors of Birman and Bert (2002);

  • 'thickness_shear': the factors that match the first thickness-shear frequency of the laminate, which require the ply densities;

  • 'constant': \(k = 5/6\);

  • None: no correction, \(\boldsymbol{A}_{ts} = \sum_k \boldsymbol{C}_s^{(k)} h_k\).

For an isotropic plate 'rohwer', 'vlachoutsis', 'whitney', 'chow' and 'constant' give \(5/6 \, G h\), 'birman_bert' gives \(G h\) and 'thickness_shear' gives \(\pi^2/12 \, G h\). Except for 'rohwer', the elements rotate \(\boldsymbol{A}_{ts}\) to the element axes as a tensor. None of the corrections depends on offset. The uncorrected stiffnesses Abar44, Abar45, Abar55 and the constant-stress ones Abarbar44, Abarbar45, Abarbar55 are kept for comparison, and scf_k13, scf_k23 are the resulting ratios, informative only. A ShellProp created from lamination parameters has no plies and no correction; A44, A45, A55 can then be set directly.

The transverse shear stresses through the thickness are recovered with ShellProp.calc_transverse_shear_stress(), from the shear forces and the distribution of Rohwer (1988), or with ShellProp.calc_equilibrium_transverse_shear(), from the derivatives of the generalized strains of a solution, whose strain energy is given by ShellProp.calc_aposteriori_energy().

Idealized laminates#

After ShellProp.calc_constitutive_matrix(), the methods ShellProp.make_balanced(), ShellProp.make_orthotropic(), ShellProp.make_symmetric() and ShellProp.make_smeared() set to zero the stiffnesses that vanish for a balanced, orthotropic or symmetric laminate, or replace \(\boldsymbol{D}\) by \(h^2 \boldsymbol{A}/12\), which requires offset = 0. make_balanced_LP(), make_orthotropic_LP() and make_symmetric_LP() do the same with the lamination parameters, such that shellprop_from_LaminationParameters() gives the same stiffnesses:

def test_make_orthotropic_LP_transverse_shear():
    # G13 != G23, such that xiAts2 gives Abar45
    laminaprop = (142.5e9, 8.7e9, 0.28, 5.1e9, 5.1e9, 3.0e9)
    prop = laminated_plate([30, -45, 60, 60, -45, 30], plyt=0.125e-3,
                           laminaprop=laminaprop)
    matlamina = prop.plies[0].matlamina
    lp = prop.calc_lamination_parameters()
    assert abs(lp.xiAts2) > 0
    prop.make_orthotropic()
    make_orthotropic_LP(lp)
    assert lp.xiAts2 == 0
    prop_2 = shellprop_from_LaminationParameters(prop.h, matlamina, lp)
    assert np.allclose(prop_2.A, prop.A)
    assert np.allclose(prop_2.B, prop.B)
    assert np.allclose(prop_2.D, prop.D)
    assert np.allclose(prop_2.Abar_ts, prop.Abar_ts)

Beam properties#

BeamProp holds the cross-section integrals, in the element axes \(y\) and \(z\), of BeamC, BeamLR and Truss: A, Iyy \(= \int z^2 dA\), Izz \(= \int y^2 dA\), Iyz, the torsion constant J, Ay and Az, which are null when the beam axis passes through the centroid, the moduli E and G, and the mass integrals intrho, intrhoy, intrhoz, intrhoy2, intrhoz2 and intrhoyz. Bending in the element \(xy\) plane, i.e. the displacement \(v\), is governed by Izz and by the transverse shear modulus G12, bending in the \(xz\) plane by Iyy and G13, and the torsion by the torsional stiffness GJ. These three attributes follow G until they are set, G12 = G13 = G and GJ = G*J, such that a property given with G and J only keeps its meaning:

def test_defaults_follow_G():
    prop = BeamProp()
    assert prop.G12 == prop.G13 == prop.GJ == 0
    prop.G = 3.
    prop.J = 5.
    assert prop.G12 == 3. and prop.G13 == 3. and prop.GJ == 15.
    prop.G12 = 1.
    prop.G13 = 2.
    prop.GJ = 7.
    prop.G = 4.
    prop.J = 6.
    assert prop.G12 == 1. and prop.G13 == 2. and prop.GJ == 7.
    for prop2 in [pickle.loads(pickle.dumps(prop)), copy.deepcopy(prop)]:
        assert prop2.G12 == 1. and prop2.G13 == 2. and prop2.GJ == 7.
        assert prop2.G == 4. and prop2.J == 6.
    del prop.G12
    del prop.G13
    del prop.GJ
    assert prop.G12 == 4. and prop.G13 == 4. and prop.GJ == 24.

No correction is applied by the elements: the shear correction factors of the two planes, e.g. \(5/6\) for a rectangular section, are applied by the user to G12 and G13. Applied to G, as in some of the examples, they also scale GJ, unless GJ is set.

The elements read these attributes through the sectional stiffness matrix C of BeamProp, of the strains \(\{\bar{\varepsilon}, \bar{\gamma}_y, \bar{\gamma}_z, \kappa_x, \kappa_y, \kappa_z\}\), which is built from them until it is set. Setting C gives the 21 independent entries of a general section, e.g. the extension-twist and bending-shear couplings of a composite box beam, which the 11 stiffness attributes cannot represent; the elements then read only C, and the mass integrals remain those of the attributes. The torsional and shear stiffnesses and the couplings of an arbitrary, possibly composite, cross section are computed by pyfe3d.vabs, and beamprop_from_vabs() sets C, see Cross-sectional analysis of beams.

Examples#

The constitutive matrices of an isotropic plate, and of the same plate with an offset:

def test_isotropic_plate():
    prop = helper_isotropic_plate()
    E = 71e9
    nu = 0.28
    h = 0.000125
    G = E/(2*(1 + nu))
    # NOTE A = E h/(1 - nu^2) [[1, nu, 0], [nu, 1, 0], [0, 0, (1 - nu)/2]],
    #      B = 0 and D = E h^3/(12 (1 - nu^2)) [...]
    Q = E/(1 - nu**2)*np.array([[1., nu, 0.],
                                [nu, 1., 0.],
                                [0., 0., (1 - nu)/2]])
    A = h*Q
    B = np.zeros((3, 3))
    D = h**3/12*Q
    Abar_ts = G*h*np.eye(2)
    assert_abd(prop.A, prop.B, prop.D, A, B, D, h)
    assert np.allclose(prop.Abar_ts, Abar_ts, rtol=1e-12, atol=1e-14*G*h)
    # NOTE Rohwer (1988), DFVLR-FB 88-32, Sec. 4.1, p. 17: for a homogeneous
    #      isotropic plate the improved stiffness is 5/6 of the simple ones,
    #      "independent of the Poisson's ratio or the position of the
    #      reference surface"
    assert prop.shear_correction == 'rohwer'
    assert np.allclose(prop.Ats, 5/6*G*h*np.eye(2), rtol=1e-12,
                       atol=1e-14*G*h)

def test_offset_isotropic_plate():
    r"""With the offset `e`, the plate occupies `-h/2 + e \le z \le h/2 + e`
    and the thickness integrals give `B = e A`, `D = D_0 + e^2 A`,
    ``intrhoz`` `= \rho h e` and ``intrhoz2`` `= \rho (h^3/12 + h e^2)`; the
    transverse shear stiffness does not change"""
    E = 71e9
    nu = 0.28
    rho = 2700.
    h = 0.002
    e = 0.6*h
    prop0 = isotropic_plate(thickness=h, E=E, nu=nu, rho=rho)
    prop = isotropic_plate(thickness=h, E=E, nu=nu, rho=rho, offset=e)
    assert np.allclose(prop.A, prop0.A)
    assert np.allclose(prop.B, e*prop0.A, rtol=1e-12)
    assert np.allclose(prop.D, prop0.D + e**2*prop0.A, rtol=1e-12)
    assert np.allclose(prop.Ats, prop0.Ats, rtol=1e-12)
    assert np.isclose(prop.intrho, rho*h)
    assert np.isclose(prop.intrhoz, rho*h*e)
    assert np.isclose(prop.intrhoz2, rho*(h**3/12 + h*e**2))

The shear correction of an isotropic plate, and the stiffnesses of a cross-ply laminate given by Rohwer (1988):

def test_isotropic_five_sixths():
    E = 71e9
    nu = 0.33
    h = 0.002
    G = E/(2*(1 + nu))
    for offset in [0., 0.37*h]:
        for mode in ['rohwer', 'vlachoutsis', 'constant']:
            prop = isotropic_plate(thickness=h, E=E, nu=nu, offset=offset,
                                   shear_correction=mode)
            assert np.allclose(prop.Ats, 5/6*G*h*np.eye(2), rtol=1e-12)
            assert np.isclose(prop.scf_k13, 5/6)
            assert np.isclose(prop.scf_k23, 5/6)
            assert np.allclose(prop.Abar_ts, G*h*np.eye(2))
            assert np.allclose(prop.Abarbar_ts, G*h*np.eye(2))
        prop = isotropic_plate(thickness=h, E=E, nu=nu, offset=offset,
                               shear_correction=None)
        assert np.allclose(prop.Ats, G*h*np.eye(2))
        assert np.isclose(prop.scf_k13, 1.)

def test_rohwer_cross_ply_reference_values():
    # NOTE Rohwer (1988), [0,90]s of Table 2, p. 19
    ref = ROHWER_1988_TABLE_2
    prop = laminated_plate(stack=CROSS_PLY, plyt=PLYT, laminaprop=CFRP)
    G12, G13, G23 = CFRP[3:]
    h = PLYT*len(CROSS_PLY)
    # NOTE simple stiffnesses, constant strain sum_k G_k h_k and constant
    #      stress h^2/sum_k (h_k/G_k): in the yz plane G23 for the 0 plies
    #      and G13 for the 90 plies, the reverse in the xz plane
    Abar = 2*PLYT*G23 + 2*PLYT*G13
    Abarbar = h**2/(2*PLYT/G23 + 2*PLYT/G13)
    for name in ['Abar44', 'Abar55']:
        assert np.isclose(getattr(prop, name), Abar, rtol=1e-14)
        assert_printed(getattr(prop, name), ref['Abar'])
    for name in ['Abarbar44', 'Abarbar55']:
        assert np.isclose(getattr(prop, name), Abarbar, rtol=1e-14)
        assert_printed(getattr(prop, name), ref['Abarbar'])
    # NOTE improved stiffness, Eq. (43), written out in rohwer_stiffness
    Ats, top = rohwer_stiffness(CROSS_PLY, [PLYT]*4, [CFRP]*4)
    assert np.allclose(top, 0., atol=1e-14)
    assert np.allclose(prop.Ats, Ats, rtol=1e-12, atol=1e-14*Ats.max())
    assert_printed(prop.A55, ref['A55'])
    # NOTE A44 = 2.52525, and the independent rohwer_stiffness gives the same,
    #      which Rohwer prints as H22 = 2.521; the other three values round
    #      correctly. Both 2.52525/3.067 and 2.521/3.067 round to the
    #      reduction 0.82 given for the y-direction on p. 18-19
    assert_printed(prop.A44, ref['A44'], rtol=1.7e-3)
    # NOTE "Coupling terms are zero", p. 18
    assert abs(prop.A45) < 1e-14*prop.A44
    assert np.isclose(prop.scf_k13, prop.A55/prop.Abar55, rtol=1e-14)
    assert np.isclose(prop.scf_k23, prop.A44/prop.Abar44, rtol=1e-14)

Rohwer, K., Improved transverse shear stiffness for layered finite elements, DFVLR-FB 88-32, 1988.

For a sandwich, stiff faces on a soft core, the factor \(5/6\) is badly wrong: 'rohwer' and 'vlachoutsis' reproduce the factors tabulated by Vlachoutsis (1992), Tables I and II, down to \(K = 0.01964\), for which \(5/6\) overestimates the shear stiffness 42 times, and, for an antiplane core, the shear stiffness \(G_c d^2/c\) of Allen (1969), Eq. (2.15b), with \(d\) the distance between the centroids of the faces, within the bounds given by the local bending and the shear strains of the faces that Allen neglects. The antiplane core, with zero moduli in the plane of the faces and finite transverse shear moduli (Allen, 1969, p. 14), is given by laminaprop = (0, 0, nu12, 0, G13, G23), or with nine entries (0, 0, nu12, 0, G13, G23, E33, 0, 0), see read_laminaprop(); it adds only transverse shear stiffness:

def test_sandwich_antiplane_core_converges_to_allen_1969():
    r"""Antiplane core: closed form and convergence to Allen (1969)

    Allen, H. G., "Analysis and Design of Structural Sandwich Panels",
    Pergamon Press, 1969, Chap. 2, pp. 8-47,
    https://doi.org/10.1016/B978-0-08-012870-2.50006-7. An antiplane core is
    "an idealized core in which the modulus of elasticity in planes parallel
    with the faces is zero but the shear modulus in planes perpendicular to
    the faces is finite", ``E_c = 0``, p. 14, given here as ``laminaprop =
    (0, 0, nu, 0, G_c, G_c)``, see :func:`.read_laminaprop`. Then
    the shear stress is constant in the core, Eq. (2.12), p. 13, ``tau_c = Q
    E_f t d/(2 D)``, with ``D = E_f (t^3/6 + t d^2/2)``, Eq. (2.2), p. 9,
    i.e. ``tau_c = Q/(d phi)``, ``phi = 1 + t^2/(3 d^2)``, and parabolic in
    the faces, Eq. (2.9), p. 11, ``tau_f = Q E_f (h^2/4 - z^2)/(2 D)``. The
    complementary energy, Rohwer (1988), Eq. (43), gives::

        A = (G_c d^2/c) phi^2/(1 + eps),
        eps = (G_c/G_f) 2 t (h^2/3 - h t/2 + t^2/5)/(c d^2)

    with eps the ratio of the face to the core shear energy. This is Eq. (45)
    of Vlachoutsis (1992), p. 1545, ``A = K_ant sum_i G_i h_i``, verified
    here to round-off. Allen's shear stiffness ``G_c d^2/c``, Eq. (2.15b),
    p. 17, neglects the local bending stiffness of the faces, the first term
    of Eq. (2.2), "less than 1%" of the second when ``3 (d/t)^2 > 100``, Eq.
    (2.4), p. 10, i.e. when ``phi - 1 < 0.01``, and the shear strains of the
    faces, footnote of p. 17, i.e. eps. Hence the derived bounds, which tend
    to zero as ``d/t`` and ``G_f/G_c`` grow::

        -eps <= A/(G_c d^2/c) - 1 <= phi^2 - 1

    The family below has ``t/c = 10^-k/2`` and ``G_c/G_f = 10^-k/10``, i.e. a
    constant share of the core in ``Abar = sum_i G_i h_i``. Measured, with
    ``E_f = 70 GPa``, ``nu = 0.3``, ``c = 10 mm``:

    ===  =======  =========  =============  ===========  ======
     k    eps      phi^2-1    A/A_Allen - 1  pyfe3d vs    K
                                             Eq. (45)
    ===  =======  =========  =============  ===========  ======
     1    3.4e-4   1.51e-3    1.17e-3        7e-16        0.1003
     2    3.3e-6   1.65e-5    1.32e-5        1e-14        0.0918
     3    3.3e-8   1.67e-7    1.33e-7        7e-14        0.0910
    ===  =======  =========  =============  ===========  ======

    The deviation from Allen decreases by a factor 100 per step, as both
    bounds, while K tends to 1/11, such that the constant 5/6 overestimates
    the shear stiffness 9.2 times. The column "pyfe3d vs Eq. (45)" is the
    largest relative difference of ``A44`` and ``A55`` of ``'rohwer'`` and
    ``'vlachoutsis'`` and of the independent ``rohwer_stiffness`` from
    ``K_ant sum_i G_i h_i``. With ``E_c = 0``, Eq. (44) of Vlachoutsis
    reduces to Eq. (45), ``K = K_ant``, checked exactly, and the only
    allowance added to the derived bounds is 1e-12 for round-off, also the
    tolerance against Eq. (45).
    """
    Ef = 70e3
    nu = 0.3
    Gf = Ef/(2*(1 + nu))
    c = 10.
    lf = (Ef, Ef, nu, Gf, Gf, Gf)
    devs = []
    for k in [1, 2, 3]:
        t = c*10.**(-k)/2
        Gc = Gf*10.**(-k)/10
        # NOTE antiplane core, E_c = 0 in the plane of the faces
        lc = (0., 0., nu, 0., Gc, Gc)
        plyts = [t, c, t]
        h = c + 2*t
        p = c/h
        d = (h + c)/2
        phi = 1 + t**2/(3*d**2)
        eps = Gc/Gf*2*t*(h**2/3 - h*t/2 + t**2/5)/(c*d**2)
        A_allen = Gc*d**2/c
        Abar = 2*t*Gf + c*Gc
        K, K_ant, _ = vlachoutsis_sandwich_K(p, 0., Gc/Gf)
        # NOTE the closed form above is Eq. (45) of Vlachoutsis (1992), and
        #      Eq. (44) reduces to it for Dc/Df = 0
        assert np.isclose(A_allen*phi**2/(1 + eps), K_ant*Abar, rtol=1e-13)
        assert K == K_ant
        slack = 1e-12
        for mode in ['rohwer', 'vlachoutsis']:
            prop = laminated_plate(stack=[0, 0, 0], plyts=plyts,
                                   laminaprops=[lf, lc, lf],
                                   shear_correction=mode)
            assert np.isclose(prop.A55, K*Abar, rtol=1e-12)
            assert np.isclose(prop.A44, K*Abar, rtol=1e-12)
        Ats, top = rohwer_stiffness([0, 0, 0], plyts, [lf, lc, lf])
        assert np.allclose(Ats, K*Abar*np.eye(2), rtol=1e-12,
                           atol=1e-12*K*Abar)
        dev = prop.A55/A_allen - 1
        assert -eps - slack <= dev <= phi**2 - 1 + slack
        devs.append(dev)
        # NOTE Allen's condition (2.4) holds
        assert 3*(d/t)**2 > 100
        assert (5/6)/K > 8
    # NOTE convergence: the deviation from Allen decreases with k, as the
    #      bounds, by a factor 100 per step
    assert devs[0] > 50*devs[1] > 0 and devs[1] > 50*devs[2] > 0
    assert devs[-1] < 2e-7

Rohwer (1988), Sec. 4.3, also gives the stiffnesses of a sandwich panel, Table 4, p. 23, which are not used as a test: its transverse shear stiffnesses are reproduced only with a foam shear modulus of 0.04 kN/mm2, i.e. \(\nu = 0.25\) instead of the stated 0.3, and its bending stiffnesses contradict the stated lay-up, since the ratio \((D_{11} - D_{22})/D_{16}\), which depends on the positions of the layers alone, is 4.216 for the lay-up of his Fig. 9 against 4.71 from the printed values. The details are in the comments of tests/test_shellprop_transverse_shear.py.
