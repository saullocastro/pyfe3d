Truss and spring elements#

Besides the beams of Beam theory and the beam elements, pyfe3d has two simpler two-node elements with the six degrees of freedom per node of every element: Truss, a bar with axial and torsional stiffness, and Spring, six uncoupled springs between two nodes. Both share the nodes of the other elements and the transformation \(\boldsymbol{K} = \boldsymbol{T} \boldsymbol{K}_e \boldsymbol{T}^\top\), \(\boldsymbol{F} = \boldsymbol{T} \boldsymbol{F}_e\) of Conventions.

Truss#

Kinematics and energy. The element \(x\) axis goes from node 1 to node 2; the \(z\) axis is normal to \(x\) and to the cyclic permutation of its components, and \(y\) completes the right-handed system, see Truss.update_rotation_matrix(). The axial displacement \(u\) and the twist \(r_x\) are interpolated linearly, and the strain energy is

(1)#\[U = \frac{1}{2} \int_0^L \left( E A \, u_{,x}^2 + G J \, {r_x}_{,x}^2 \right) dx\]

with \(E A\) and \(G J\) the entries C[0, 0] and C[3, 3] of the sectional stiffness matrix of the BeamProp, i.e. E*A and GJ when C is not set; the other entries, couplings included, are not read. In element coordinates the stiffness matrix has the entries \(\pm E A/L\) between the axial translations and \(\pm G J/L\) between the twist rotations of the two nodes, and the internal forces are, e.g., \(E A (u_2 - u_1)/L\) at node 2, see Truss.update_KC0() and Truss.update_probe_finte(). The transverse translations \(v\), \(w\) and the rotations \(r_y\), \(r_z\) have no stiffness: a node connected only to trusses must have them supported, by other elements or by boundary conditions.

Mass. The rigid cross section has the displacements \(u_x = u\), \(u_y = v - z r_x\) and \(u_z = w + y r_x\), with the three translations and \(r_x\) linear, whose kinetic energy is

(2)#\[T = \frac{1}{2} \int_0^L \Big[ m (\dot{u}^2 + \dot{v}^2 + \dot{w}^2) - 2 m_z \dot{v} \dot{r}_x + 2 m_y \dot{w} \dot{r}_x + (m_{yy} + m_{zz}) \dot{r}_x^2 \Big] dx\]

with the mass integrals of Beam properties. The consistent mass matrix, mtype=0 of Truss.update_M(), is its exact Hessian, \(L m/6\) times \([2, 1; 1, 2]\) for each translation; the lumped one, mtype=1, the two-point Gauss-Lobatto quadrature, diagonal, without the couplings of \(m_y\) and \(m_z\). The rotations \(r_y\) and \(r_z\) have no inertia.

Limits. The element has no geometric stiffness matrix, hence no linear buckling analysis, and its internal forces are linear; for large displacements it is wrapped by CorotationalTruss, which gives the co-rotational truss of Crisfield (1991), see Co-rotational formulation for large rotations. The module pyfe3d.truss recommends BeamC for its better physical representation; the beam also has geometric stiffness and non-linear internal forces.

Spring#

Energy. Spring connects the same degree of freedom of two nodes with the six stiffnesses kxe, kye, kze, krxe, krye, krze, \(k_1, \ldots, k_6\), in the element coordinate system:

(3)#\[\begin{split}U = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{i=1}^{6} k_i \left( d_{2,i} - d_{1,i} \right)^2, \qquad \boldsymbol{K}_e = \begin{bmatrix} \boldsymbol{k} & -\boldsymbol{k} \\ -\boldsymbol{k} & \boldsymbol{k} \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \boldsymbol{k} = \mathrm{diag}(k_1, \ldots, k_6)\end{split}\]

with \(d_{a,i}\) the six displacements \(u, v, w, r_x, r_y, r_z\) of node \(a\) in element coordinates, see Spring.update_KC0(). There is no coupling between translations and rotations, no mass and no geometric stiffness matrix. The element axes are not taken from the nodes, which may coincide: the \(x\) axis is the vector xi, xj, xk and the \(z\) axis is \(x \times v_{xy}\), with the vector vxyi, vxyj, vxyk of Spring.update_rotation_matrix(); by default they are the global axes.

Rigid motions. A rigid translation and equal rotations of the two nodes give no internal forces. Since the nodal coordinates are not used, a rigid rotation \(\boldsymbol{\theta}\) of two nodes at different positions \(\boldsymbol{p}_1\), \(\boldsymbol{p}_2\) produces the relative translation \(\boldsymbol{\theta} \times (\boldsymbol{p}_2 - \boldsymbol{p}_1)\), normal to the line of the nodes, which loads the translational springs in that direction, kye and kze when the \(x\) axis is along the line of the nodes: the linear element does not balance the moment of its transverse forces. A spring is therefore invariant to rigid rotations only when its nodes coincide, e.g. elastic supports and connections, or when it is an axial spring, kye = kze = 0, with its \(x\) axis along the line of its nodes. The co-rotational wrapper CorotationalSpring adds the missing moment through its projector, see Co-rotational formulation for large rotations.
