Non-linear static analyses#

When the displacements change the geometry enough to change the equilibrium, e.g. the membrane stiffening of a plate under pressure, the softening of a compressed shell after buckling, or the snap-through of an arch, the equilibrium equations are non-linear in the displacements and are solved incrementally and iteratively. This chapter states the equations that pyfe3d solves, how a model is passed to the solvers, and how to choose the solution method and the kinematics. The algorithms are in The Newton-Raphson method and Arc-length methods, the stopping test of the iterations in Convergence criteria, and the loads that depend on the configuration or on the load factor in Load- and configuration-dependent loads.

Equilibrium, residual and tangent stiffness#

The external loads are applied proportionally to the load factor \(\lambda\), with \(\boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\) the reference external force vector and \(\lambda = 1\) the full load. An equilibrium configuration \(\boldsymbol{u}\) makes the residual null, the principle of virtual work in discrete form:

(1)#\[\boldsymbol{R}(\boldsymbol{u}, \lambda) = \boldsymbol{F}_{int}(\boldsymbol{u}) - \lambda \boldsymbol{F}_{ext} = \boldsymbol{0}, \qquad \boldsymbol{F}_{int} = \int_{\Omega} (\boldsymbol{B}_L + \boldsymbol{B}_{NL})^\top \boldsymbol{\sigma} \, d\Omega\]

with the strains \(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} = (\boldsymbol{B}_L + \frac{1}{2} \boldsymbol{B}_{NL}) \boldsymbol{u}\) and their variations \(\delta \boldsymbol{\varepsilon} = (\boldsymbol{B}_L + \boldsymbol{B}_{NL}) \delta \boldsymbol{u}\), where \(\boldsymbol{B}_{NL}\) depends on \(\boldsymbol{u}\). The sign convention is that of the residual \(\boldsymbol{R} = \boldsymbol{F}_{int} - \lambda \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\), whose Jacobian is the tangent stiffness matrix

(2)#\[\boldsymbol{K}_T = \frac{\partial \boldsymbol{R}}{\partial \boldsymbol{u}} = \boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + \boldsymbol{K}_{CNL}(\boldsymbol{u}) + \boldsymbol{K}_{GNL}(\boldsymbol{u}) + \boldsymbol{K}_G(\boldsymbol{u}) + \boldsymbol{K}_F\]

with the linear constitutive stiffness matrix \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\) of \(\boldsymbol{B}_L\), the non-linear constitutive stiffness matrix \(\boldsymbol{K}_{CNL}\) of the terms of \(\boldsymbol{B}_{NL}\), the geometric stiffness matrices of the stresses of the linear strains, \(\boldsymbol{K}_G\), and of the non-linear strains, \(\boldsymbol{K}_{GNL}\), and the load stiffness matrix \(\boldsymbol{K}_F = -\partial (\lambda \boldsymbol{F}_{ext})/\partial \boldsymbol{u}\), null for dead loads, see Load- and configuration-dependent loads. Without \(\boldsymbol{K}_F\), \(\boldsymbol{K}_T\) is symmetric: the constitutive terms have the same operator \(\boldsymbol{B}_L + \boldsymbol{B}_{NL}\) on both sides of the symmetric \(\boldsymbol{C}\), and the geometric terms contain the mixed second derivatives of the strains. The elements group the terms as their update_* methods compute them:

  • KC0, of update_KC0, is \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\);

  • KCNL, of update_KCNL, is \(\boldsymbol{K}_{CNL} + \boldsymbol{K}_{GNL}\);

  • KG, of update_KG, is \(\boldsymbol{K}_G\), which is linear in \(\boldsymbol{u}\) and therefore scales with the load multiplier of the linear buckling analysis, see Linear buckling and natural frequencies;

  • fint, of update_fint(..., nonlinear=1), is \(\boldsymbol{F}_{int}\).

Their sum KC0 + KCNL + KG is the exact derivative of fint for every element with update_KCNL, which tests/test_tangent_consistency.py checks with a Taylor test and finite differences on distorted elements in arbitrary orientations. The internal force vector is not \(\boldsymbol{K}_C \boldsymbol{u}\): because of the factor \(\frac{1}{2}\) of the strains, it must be computed from the stresses, as update_fint does. The strains are the von Kármán strains of each element, valid for small strains and small to moderate rotations; for large rotations, see the kinematics below.

Analysis receives the model as four callables and stores the solution in an.increments, the converged load factors, and an.cs, the converged configurations:

The solvers call calc_fext(inc=1.) and scale the vector by the load factor themselves; calc_kC(), the linear stiffness matrix, gives the predictor of the first increment, the scaling of the arc length and the scaling of the convergence criterion; and the tangent stiffness matrix is calc_kC(c, NLgeom=True) + calc_kG(c, NLgeom=True), see pyfe3d.solver.callbacks.calc_kT(). A callable may declare the optional keyword arguments of pyfe3d.solver.callbacks, for loads that depend on the configuration or on the load factor, see Load- and configuration-dependent loads. The Model class of tests/test_solver_nonlinear_static.py wraps a list of elements of one type into these callables.

With Analysis(..., bu=bu, uk=uk), the callables work with the vectors and matrices of all the N dofs and FreeDofs restricts them to the free dofs, with the prescribed displacements \(\lambda \bar{\boldsymbol{u}}_k\) imposed exactly, see Boundary conditions and prescribed displacements; the vectors of an.cs then have size N. Without bu, the callables work with the free dofs only.

Incremental-iterative solution#

Analysis.static() with NLgeom=True traces the equilibrium path \(\boldsymbol{R}(\boldsymbol{u}, \lambda) = \boldsymbol{0}\) from the undeformed state, \(\lambda = 0\), to \(\lambda = 1\), in increments, each corrected by iterations of Newton’s method until the residual is small. an.NL_method selects how the load factor of each increment is set:

  • 'NR', the default, load control: \(\lambda\) is prescribed at each increment and the iterations solve for \(\boldsymbol{u}\), see pyfe3d.solver.newton_raphson._solver_NR() and The Newton-Raphson method;

  • 'arc_length_riks' and 'arc_length_crisfield', path following: \(\lambda\) is an unknown of each increment, together with \(\boldsymbol{u}\), and an arc-length constraint fixes the size of the increment, see pyfe3d.solver.arc_length._solver_arc_length() and Arc-length methods.

Both stop at the load factor 1.0 exactly, or earlier, keeping the converged increments, when the increment falls below an.minInc or, for the arc-length methods, when the cumulative arc length reaches an.maxArcLength; an analysis that did not finish is recognised by an.increments[-1] != 1.. The linear analysis, an.static(NLgeom=False), returns a single increment, see Linear static analyses.

At a limit point of the load, \(\boldsymbol{K}_T\) is singular and the load factor reaches a maximum along the path. Under load control, no equilibrium exists just above the limit load near the current configuration, and the iterations either fail, the increment being cut until an.minInc, or converge to a distant configuration on another part of the path, which hides the snap-through. The arc-length methods pass limit points of the load, snap-through, and also points where the displacements turn back, snap-back, since they do not prescribe either of them.

The tolerance of the iterations, an.relTOL = 1e-9 by default, is that of Crisfield’s criterion with a consistent tangent stiffness matrix, see Convergence criteria. A tight tolerance matters when the converged state feeds an eigenvalue analysis, since the geometric stiffness matrix of Linear buckling and natural frequencies is built from the stresses of that state.

Choosing the kinematics#

The callables of the table above give the von Kármán kinematics of the elements. For large rotations, CorotationalModel of pyfe3d.corotational assembles a model in which each element is added with one of four kinematics, and provides the four callables of Analysis, which is used unchanged:

The rotational dofs of a CorotationalModel are total rotation vectors \(\boldsymbol{\psi}_a\), whose nodal rotation matrices are \(\exp(\mathrm{Spin}(\boldsymbol{\psi}_a))\), see expso3(); the solvers add their corrections like those of the translations, without a state kept between the calls of the callables. Concentrated moments about fixed axes or following the nodal triads, CorotationalModel.add_moment(), are configuration-dependent loads and give an unsymmetric tangent stiffness matrix, which the solvers support, see Load- and configuration-dependent loads. The theory of the co-rotational layer, its frames, its treatment of warped quadrilaterals and its verification are given in Co-rotational formulation for large rotations.

Log messages#

The solvers print the increments and, at each iteration, the largest absolute residual and the \(\zeta\) of Crisfield’s criterion, with pyfe3d.solver.logger, unless silent=True is passed to Analysis.static(); the callables must accept silent. The number of iterations per increment is the first diagnostic of a model: with a consistent tangent, two to four iterations per moderate increment, see Convergence criteria.
