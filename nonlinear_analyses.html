Non-linear static analyses#

The geometrically non-linear static analyses are run with Analysis, which receives four callables of the model and finds the configurations \(\boldsymbol{u}\) in equilibrium with the external load scaled by the load factor \(\lambda\):

\[\boldsymbol{R}(\boldsymbol{u}, \lambda) = \boldsymbol{F}_{int}(\boldsymbol{u}) - \lambda \boldsymbol{F}_{ext} = \boldsymbol{0}\]

The elements give the terms of the residual and of its Jacobian, the solvers of pyfe3d.solver the increments and the iterations.

From the elements to the callables#

Callable

Returns

With the elements

calc_fext(inc=1., silent=True)

\(\boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\), scaled by inc

the nodal loads of the model

calc_fint(c, silent=True)

\(\boldsymbol{F}_{int}(\boldsymbol{c})\)

update_fint(fint, prop, nonlinear=1) of every element, after update_probe_xe(x) and update_probe_ue(c)

calc_kC(c=None, NLgeom=False, silent=True)

\(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\), or \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + \boldsymbol{K}_{CNL}(\boldsymbol{c}) + \boldsymbol{K}_{GNL}(\boldsymbol{c})\) with NLgeom=True

update_KC0 once, update_KCNL at c

calc_kG(c=None, NLgeom=False, silent=True)

\(\boldsymbol{K}_G(\boldsymbol{c})\)

update_KG at c

calc_kC() + calc_kG() is then the exact Jacobian of calc_fint, \(\boldsymbol{K}_T = \partial \boldsymbol{R}/\partial \boldsymbol{u}\), and the full Newton-Raphson method converges quadratically. The class Model of tests/test_solver_nonlinear_static.py, see Non-linear static analysis with pyfe3d.solver, wraps a list of elements of one type into these four callables. calc_kC(), the linear stiffness matrix, also gives the scaling of the convergence criterion.

Boundary conditions and prescribed displacements#

With Analysis(..., bu=bu), the callables receive and return the vectors and matrices of all the N dofs, as assembled by the elements, and FreeDofs restricts them to the free dofs bu. The solution vectors an.cs have size N, with zeros at the constrained dofs.

With uk, the constrained dofs have the prescribed displacements \(\lambda \boldsymbol{u}_k\), imposed exactly, proportional to the load factor: uk[~bu] are the values at \(\lambda = 1\), and a load-free analysis, calc_fext returning zeros, is displacement controlled. The reaction at a prescribed dof is calc_fint(u) at that dof minus the load applied there.

In test_prescribed_displacement_and_reaction of Non-linear static analysis with pyfe3d.solver, a point-loaded plate is first solved under load control; its central deflection w_c, at the dof dof, is then prescribed without load, with the callables of the full model:

bu_d = bu.copy()
bu_d[dof] = False        # the loaded dof becomes a known dof
uk = np.zeros(N)
uk[dof] = w_c            # value at the load factor 1

def calc_fext(inc=1., silent=True):
    return np.zeros(N)   # no load: displacement control

an = Analysis(calc_fext, calc_fint, calc_kC, calc_kG, bu=bu_d, uk=uk)
an.NL_method = 'arc_length_riks'   # or 'NR'
increments, cs = an.static(NLgeom=True)
reaction = calc_fint(cs[-1])[dof]  # minus the load at dof, here none

Each cs[i][dof] is increments[i]*w_c, the displacement field at \(\lambda = 1\) is that of the load-controlled analysis, and the reaction equals the load. The same bu and uk serve the linear analysis, an.static(NLgeom=False), see Linear static analyses and the worked example Tutorial: prescribed displacement at the tip of a cantilever.

The worked example Tutorial: shells pressed down by a prescribed displacement, Riks’ method traces with Riks’ method the reaction of a clamped spherical cap and of a hinged shallow shell pressed down by a deflection prescribed at their crown, and Tutorial: NAFEMS spherical cap restricted to axisymmetric deformations restricts the co-rotational model of the cap to its axisymmetric deformations with a linear constraint u = T q, passing the restricted callables to Analysis.

Without bu, the callables return the vectors and matrices of the free dofs only. The solvers do not remove null rows and columns: a null row or column of the linear stiffness matrix, a dof without stiffness, raises a numpy.linalg.LinAlgError naming the dofs, see check_null_rows_cols().

Solvers#

an.NL_method selects the solver, and Analysis.static() with NLgeom=True runs it, returning the converged load factors an.increments and solutions an.cs:

  • 'NR', the default: load control with the Newton-Raphson method, newton_raphson._solver_NR(), full or modified (an.modified_NR), see Crisfield (1991), Sections 1.2.2 and 1.2.3, with an optional line search (an.line_search). The first increment is an.initialInc; a step that fails is repeated with half the increment, and the increment grows after a step that converged without being cut, up to an.maxInc. Beyond a limit point of the load it may converge to another branch of the equilibrium path.

  • 'arc_length_riks' and 'arc_length_crisfield': the arc-length methods following Riks (1979) and Crisfield (1981), arc_length._solver_arc_length(), in which the load factor is an unknown of each step, such that limit points, snap-through and snap-back are traced. The first arc length corresponds to the load factor increment an.initialInc along the initial tangent.

Both stop at the load factor 1.0, or earlier when the increment falls below an.minInc or, for the arc-length methods, when the cumulative arc length reaches an.maxArcLength. A run that did not finish is detected with:

increments, cs = an.static(NLgeom=True)
if not increments or increments[-1] != 1.:
    ...  # stopped at increments[-1], see the log messages

The linear static analysis, an.static(NLgeom=False), returns a single increment, see Linear static analyses.

Convergence criterion#

The iterations of each increment stop when the residual is small according to an.convergence, see convergence:

  • 'crisfield', the default: the residual scaled with the diagonal of the linear stiffness matrix of the free dofs, \(d_j = K_{{C0}_{jj}}\), the entries of the diagonal scaling vector \(\boldsymbol{d}\),

    \[\zeta = \frac{\|\boldsymbol{R}\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}}{\max( \|\lambda \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}, \|\boldsymbol{F}_{int}\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}, \ldots)} \le \text{relTOL}, \qquad \|v\|_{\boldsymbol{d}} = \sqrt{\sum_j v_j^2/|d_j|}\]

    with an.relTOL = 1e-9 by default, see crisfield_zeta() for the references in the denominator. It does not depend on the units of each dof, e.g. on whether the rotations are in radians or in milliradians, and it ignores the support reactions;

  • 'relative': the Euclidean norm of the residual over the largest Euclidean norm of the references, which adds the squares of forces and of moments.

an.absTOL also accepts the iterations whose largest absolute residual is smaller, in the units of force of the model. With both criteria, an iteration has also converged at the round-off floor of the residual,

\[\|\boldsymbol{R}\|_{\boldsymbol{d}} \le 3 \varepsilon \, \| |\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}| \, |\boldsymbol{u}| \|_{\boldsymbol{d}}\]

with \(\varepsilon\) the machine precision, \(|\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}|\) and \(|\boldsymbol{u}|\) the elementwise absolute values of the linear stiffness matrix and of the displacements of the free dofs, and the Euclidean norm with 'relative', see roundoff_floor(): the residual cannot be computed more accurately, and, under large self-equilibrated axial forces, e.g. of an arch near a null load, \(\zeta\) would otherwise stagnate above relTOL and the analysis stop, see test_fung_kaplan_beam_arch in Non-linear static analysis with pyfe3d.solver. The log messages mark the iterations converged at the round-off floor. With a consistent tangent stiffness matrix and full Newton-Raphson, \(\zeta\) falls quadratically, as verified in Non-linear static analysis with pyfe3d.solver; a slow decrease of \(\zeta\) reveals an inconsistent tangent stiffness matrix, e.g. a missing calc_kG, which changes the iterations but not the converged solution.

Configuration-dependent loads#

A load that depends on the configuration, e.g. a follower pressure or the aerodynamic load of the piston theory, is given through optional keyword arguments, passed only to the callables that declare them, see callbacks:

  • calc_fint(c, inc=...) receives the load factor and includes the configuration-dependent part of the load, \(-\lambda \boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{c})\), and calc_kC(c, NLgeom=True, inc=...) its derivative, the load stiffness matrix \(\boldsymbol{K}_F = \lambda \boldsymbol{K}_{F_{ref}}\), with \(\boldsymbol{K}_{F_{ref}} = -\partial \boldsymbol{F}/\partial \boldsymbol{c}\), which is unsymmetric in general, see below; the solvers use LU factorizations;

  • calc_fext(inc=1., c=...) returns the load vector of the configuration c for a unit load factor, \(-\partial \boldsymbol{R}/\partial \lambda\), used by the arc-length methods.

The aerodynamic load of the piston theory, \(\lambda \beta \boldsymbol{K}_{A_\beta} \boldsymbol{u}\), with update_KA_beta of Quad4R or Quad4, is such a load, see test_piston_theory_load_unsymmetric_tangent in Non-linear static analysis with pyfe3d.solver. The keyword argument lbd of calc_fext gives a load vector that depends on the load factor, which is how FreeDofs imposes the prescribed displacements.

\(\boldsymbol{K}_F\) is symmetric, and the load conservative, only in particular cases. For a pressure normal to the deformed surface, Hibbitt (1979, p. 938) splits the load stiffness of a uniform pressure into a symmetric surface integral and an antisymmetric integral along the boundary of the loaded surface, which vanishes where the displacements are prescribed: a uniform pressure on a fully enclosed volume, or on a surface whose boundaries have prescribed displacements, gives a symmetric \(\boldsymbol{K}_F\), whereas free edges, where the pressure changes, and a non-uniform pressure give unsymmetric terms (p. 939). In Table 2 of Schweizerhof and Ramm (1984), the domain terms are symmetric for a uniform pressure, and for a non-uniform one only when the pressure field is fixed in space, and the boundary terms vanish under one of the conditions of their Sec. 3.2.1, Eqs. (32)-(35): the pressure is zero on the whole boundary; at least two displacement components are prescribed at the edge; the edge is restrained normal to a continuous surface, e.g. the undeformed surface, \(w = 0\) for a plate on the \(XY\) plane; or the boundary terms add up to zero. pyfe3d does not symmetrise \(\boldsymbol{K}_F\): the tangent stiffness matrix, with the matrix returned by calc_kC, is factorized with scipy.sparse.linalg.splu(), as in solve(), such that a consistent unsymmetric \(\boldsymbol{K}_F\) keeps the quadratic convergence of the Newton-Raphson method, as verified by test_newton_raphson_quadratic_with_follower_load of tests/test_solver_follower_loads.py.

Choosing the kinematics#

The callables of the table above give the von Kármán kinematics of the elements. For large rotations, CorotationalModel of pyfe3d.corotational assembles a model in which each element is added with one of four kinematics, and provides the four callables of Analysis, which is used unchanged:

kinematics

Assumes

Limits

Elements

'linear'

small displacements, \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} \boldsymbol{u}\)

small rotations

all

'von_karman'

the von Kármán strains of each element, update_fint(..., nonlinear=1), update_KCNL and update_KG, in the fixed element axes; small strains

small to moderate rotations

Quad4, Quad4R, Tria3R, Tria3DSG, BeamC, BeamLR

'corotational'

the element-independent co-rotational (EICR) formulation of Felippa and Haugen (2005): the rigid motion of each element is removed, and its local linear stiffness matrix acts on the deformational displacements; small strains

large displacements and rotations, each nodal rotation vector below one full revolution, \(|\boldsymbol{\psi}_a| < 2 \pi\)

all, Truss and Spring included

'corotational_von_karman'

as 'corotational', with the von Kármán strains of the element in its co-rotational frame instead of its linear strains, Crisfield (1997), Section 17.1.5

as 'corotational'

as 'corotational'; it differs only for BeamC

The von Kármán terms of the shells and of BeamLR vanish in their co-rotational frames, where the transverse translations of a warped quadrilateral are the hourglass pattern of its warp and Quad4 takes the slopes of \(w\) at the centroid, see CorotationalShell, so 'corotational_von_karman' gives the results of 'corotational' for them, to round-off; Truss and Spring, which have no von Kármán strains, take it as 'corotational'. For BeamC it adds the higher-order terms of the local element, with its element-averaged axial strain, see BeamC: on the cantilever under an end force of Sze, Liu and Lo (2004), Section 3.1, the tip deflections \(-U\) and \(W\) of 16 elements differ from the elastica by -0.02 % and +0.05 % with 'corotational', and by +0.002 % and +0.007 % with 'corotational_von_karman', see CorotationalBeam. 'von_karman' follows the elastica only up to moderate rotations: on the cantilever rolled up by an end moment, Sze, Liu and Lo (2004), Section 3.2, its tip leaves the exact one by 0.6 % of the length at a tenth of the moment that closes the circle, see test_cantilever_end_moment_full_circle of tests/test_corotational_benchmarks.py.

The rotational dofs of a CorotationalModel are total rotation vectors \(\boldsymbol{\psi}_a\), whose nodal rotation matrices are \(\exp(\mathrm{Spin}(\boldsymbol{\psi}_a))\), see expso3(), added by the solvers like the translations, without a state kept between the calls. The map is singular at \(|\boldsymbol{\psi}_a| = 2 \pi\); the benchmark above reaches it at the tip only because the out-of-plane rotations are restrained, which is exact for that plane problem. A node shared by a co-rotational element and an element of another kinematics is valid only while its rotations remain small, and a warning is issued.

A model is built as with the elements alone: set the connectivity, call update_rotation_matrix with the coordinates x of the model, and pass each element to CorotationalModel.add(); the init_k_* attributes are not needed. The cantilever of Sze, Liu and Lo (2004), Section 3.2, with 16 BeamC elements, rolled up into half a circle by an end moment that follows the tip:

import numpy as np
from pyfe3d import BeamC, BeamCProbe, DOF
from pyfe3d.beamprop import BeamProp
from pyfe3d.solver import Analysis
from pyfe3d.corotational import CorotationalModel

L, b, h, E = 12., 1., 0.1, 1.2e6
prop = BeamProp()
prop.A, prop.E, prop.G = b*h, E, E/2
prop.Iyy, prop.Izz, prop.J = b*h**3/12, h*b**3/12, b*h**3/3
n = 16
xs = np.linspace(0, L, n + 1)
x = np.vstack((xs, 0*xs, 0*xs)).T.ravel()
model = CorotationalModel(x)
probe = BeamCProbe()
for k in range(n):
    elem = BeamC(probe)
    elem.n1, elem.n2 = k, k + 1
    elem.c1, elem.c2 = DOF*k, DOF*(k + 1)
    elem.update_rotation_matrix(0., 1., 0., x)
    model.add(elem, prop, kinematics='corotational')

M = np.pi*E*prop.Iyy/L   # half of the moment that closes the circle
model.add_moment(n, [0., -M, 0.], kind='follower')
bu = np.ones(model.N, dtype=bool)
bu[:DOF] = False         # clamped at X = 0

an = Analysis(model.calc_fext, model.calc_fint, model.calc_kC,
              model.calc_kG, bu=bu)
an.initialInc = an.maxInc = 0.1
increments, cs = an.static(NLgeom=True, silent=True)
tip = cs[-1][DOF*n:DOF*(n + 1)]   # -L, 0, 7.6517, 0, -pi, 0

The tip reaches the end of the polygon of 16 equal chords inscribed in the circle of radius \(L/\pi\), \(W = 7.6517\), to round-off, against \(2 L/\pi = 7.6394\) for the exact circle, and turns by \(-\pi\) about \(Y\).

Applied moments. The forces and moments in the attribute fext of the model are constant; a moment in fext is conjugate to the rotation vector, and equals a moment about fixed axes only while the rotation vector keeps a fixed direction, e.g. in plane problems. CorotationalModel.add_moment() adds a concentrated moment with kind='rotation_vector' (into fext), 'fixed_axes', a moment about the fixed global axes, which is non-conservative and gives an unsymmetric tangent stiffness matrix, or 'follower', a moment that rotates with the nodal triad. The last two depend on the configuration and enter calc_fint, calc_kG and calc_fext(c=...) as described in the section above; pass model.calc_fext to Analysis after adding them, since the callable it returns depends on them.

Shells and warping. CorotationalShell links a warped quadrilateral to its nodes with rigid offsets, warping='offsets' by default, and warns above the warp ratio of 0.07, up to which the offsets were verified; with warping=None it warns above 0.01, see Choosing an element. The frame of the triangles is along their side 1-2, so that their solution depends on the numbering of the nodes, see the Notes of CorotationalShell. The keyword arguments of CorotationalModel.add() reach the wrappers, e.g. warping and the frame of CorotationalTruss and CorotationalSpring, and the update_KC0 of the element, e.g. the hgfactor_* of Quad4R.

Convergence tolerance. The default criterion, relTOL = 1e-9 with the round-off floor of the section above, applies without absTOL. The co-rotational elements compute the current nodal positions relative to the base centroid of each element, see the Notes of CorotationalShell, so that the round-off of the internal force does not depend on the origin of the coordinates, which changes neither \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\) nor the force vectors of the criterion. Computed as \(\boldsymbol{X} + \boldsymbol{u}\), with \(\boldsymbol{X}\) the nodal coordinates, the positions had a round-off of the order of \(\varepsilon |\boldsymbol{X}|\), above the round-off floor \(3 \varepsilon \| |\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}| |\boldsymbol{u}| \|_{\boldsymbol{d}}\) on fine meshes: up to 2026-10-30, the iterations of the cantilever with 64 BeamC elements stagnated at \(\zeta \approx 10^{-8}\) and needed an.absTOL, and with its root at 1000 from the origin at \(\zeta \approx 10^{-6}\), see test_cantilever_end_shear_translated in tests/test_corotational_benchmarks.py.

Post-processing. CorotationalModel.nodal_rotations() gives the rotation matrices of the nodes and CorotationalModel.deformational_displacements() the deformational displacements of each co-rotational element in its frame; CorotationalShell.update_probe() writes them into the probe of the element, whose own routines then recover the stresses. model.KC0 is the linear stiffness matrix of the elements; for a warped quadrilateral added with 'corotational' or 'corotational_von_karman' it is that of the flat element, since the wrapper switches off the offsets of the element, see the Notes of CorotationalShell.

Benchmarks. tests/test_corotational_benchmarks.py reproduces cases 3.1 (cantilever under an end force), 3.2 (cantilever rolled up into a full circle by an end moment), 3.3 (slit annular plate), 3.4 (hemispherical shell) and 3.8 (hinged cylindrical roof, with the arc-length method) of Sze, Liu and Lo (2004) with BeamC, Quad4R and Tria3R, and tests/test_corotational_core.py checks the finite-rotation utilities, the wrappers and their tangent stiffness matrices by finite differences. The worked example Tutorial: large rotations with the co-rotational kinematics shows such analyses step by step.

Log messages#

The solvers print their progress, the increments and the \(\zeta\) of each iteration, with pyfe3d.solver.logger, unless silent=True is passed to Analysis.static(). The callables must accept silent.
