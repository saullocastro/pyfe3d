Tria3R - Triangular element with reduced integration (pyfe3d.tria3r)#

The module has three classes: Tria3RData, with the sizes of the sparse arrays that one element fills; Tria3RProbe, with the buffers in element coordinates that one or more elements share; and Tria3R, the element. The update_* methods of Tria3R write the element contributions in sparse row, column and value arrays, from the positions given by its init_k_* attributes, or add them to a global internal force vector.

Three-node triangular shell element with 6 degrees-of-freedom per node, \(u\), \(v\), \(w\), \(r_x\), \(r_y\), \(r_z\), with reduced integration.

The transverse shear stiffnesses A44, A45 and A55 of the pyfe3d.shellprop.ShellProp are reduced by a factor controlled by the alpha_shear_locking attribute of Tria3R, 0.7 by default, which is a parameter to be verified per problem class and not a constant of the element. pyfe3d.tria3dsg.Tria3DSG takes no such parameter and is the triangle to reach for by default; this one is kept for continuity with results obtained before it, and for the comparison itself.

The drilling rotation \(r_z\) is given stiffness by one of two models, selected with the drilling_model attribute of Tria3R: the physics-based default, for which the nodal moments about the shell normal recovered in the internal force vector are physical and no user parameter is involved, and a fictitious penalty scaled by K6ROT, the default before version 0.10.0, whose only purpose is to remove the singularity of the drilling degree-of-freedom. Geometrically nonlinear analyses with the von Karman membrane strains are supported by Tria3R.update_fint() with nonlinear=1, Tria3R.update_KCNL() and Tria3R.update_KG().

Notes#

Triangular element with reduced integration, where a single point at the centroid (\(S1=S2=S3=1/3\)) is evaluated with the weight \(1/2\) times the Jacobian determinant \(2 A_e\), i.e. the area \(A_e\), preventing shear locking. The drilling stiffness is evaluated with full integration. Under the default drilling_model = 0 this applies to the terms of the Allman enrichment, the Hughes-Brezzi term being integrated with a single point at the centroid, see the drilling stiffness below.

The transverse shear stiffnesses \(A_{44}\), \(A_{45}\) and \(A_{55}\) are stabilised with the scalar factor that Lyly, Stenberg and Vihinen [1] proposed for the MITC4 quadrilateral, with the longest edge of the element as its size (their Eq. (3.12)), called Stenberg’s method by Bischoff and Bletzinger [2] (Sec. 3.1), in the form adopted by Castro et al. [3], with the longest edge of the triangle as the element size, where the transverse shear terms are changed as per Eq. (26) in Castro et al., here repeated for convenience:

\[\left[\begin{matrix}\hat{A}_{44}, \hat{A}_{45} \ \hat{A}_{45}, \hat{A}_{55}\end{matrix}\right] = \frac{1}{1 + factor}\left[\begin{matrix}A_{44}, A_{45} \ A_{45}, A_{55}\end{matrix}\right]\]

with \(factor\) defined as:

factor = frac{alpha ell^2}{h^2}

where \(\alpha\) is a positive constant parameters (see the alpha_shear_locking attribute), \(\ell\) is the longest edge of the corresponding triangle, and \(h\) the total thickness of the element.

Note that \(factor \rightarrow 0\) as the element size shrinks, so the true stiffness is recovered under mesh refinement and the scheme is consistent. The rate matters though: \(\ell < 0.12 h\) is needed for the stabilised stiffness to be within 1 per cent of the true one, which for a thin shell is not a mesh anyone would build. What makes the scheme legitimate all the same is that in a load-driven problem the error it introduces is \(factor \times f_s\), with \(f_s\) the shear fraction of the response, and for a plate or beam of span \(L\) discretised with \(n\) elements

\[factor \times f_s \sim \frac{\alpha \ell^2}{h^2} c \frac{h^2}{L^2} = \frac{\alpha c}{n^2}\]

so the thickness cancels and the error behaves as an ordinary \(O(1/n^2)\) discretisation error. This is measured in tests/test_transverse_shear_stiffness.py.

Warning

The default \(\alpha = 0.7\) is roughly seven times the value of Stenberg’s method in the references above, which is near \(0.1\): Lyly et al. chose \(0.1\), the value Bischoff and Bletzinger quote from them, and Castro et al. investigate \(0.05\) to \(0.15\) and recommend \(0.07\) to \(0.09\), reporting that above \(0.09\) the linear buckling behaviour becomes overly soft and converges from below. Bischoff and Bletzinger show, however, that matching the deflection of a Timoshenko beam element gives \(\alpha = \kappa/(2(1+\nu))\), i.e. \(0.32\) for \(\kappa = 5/6\) and \(\nu = 0.3\) (their Eq. (22)), about half the default, a value that works well when bending is dominant in one direction but that they find too flexible as soon as bending in two directions and twist are present (their Sec. 4.2). Their own algebraic subgrid scale stabilisation uses one parameter per direction of the element, \(\alpha_\xi = 0.17 (\ell_\eta/\ell_\xi)/(2(1+\nu))\) and likewise \(\alpha_\eta\) (their Eqs. (23) and (24)), i.e. \(0.065\) for a square element with \(\nu = 0.3\).

The difference is not a retune but a consequence of where the factor is applied. In all three references it sits on top of an assumed-strain or discrete shear gap (DSG) field, the MITC4 field for Lyly et al. and a DSG field for Castro et al., whose operator already contains the cure of the shear locking, so there the factor is a mild stabilisation that improves stability and coarse-mesh accuracy. This element has no DSG: the transverse shear is taken from a single point at the centroid, which still locks, and the factor is therefore doing the unlocking. Reducing \(\alpha\) to the literature value makes this element stiffen sharply. On the plate of test_tria3r_natural_freq.py, with the consistent mass matrix, the error in the first natural frequency goes

\[+0.6\% \ (\alpha = 0.7) \quad +30.6\% \ (\alpha = 0.1) \quad +77.3\% \ (\alpha = 0.01) \quad +96.1\% \ (\alpha = 0)\]

so with the stabilisation removed the element is nearly twice as stiff as it should be, which is the locking itself. For scale, pyfe3d.tria3dsg.Tria3DSG on the identical mesh, with no parameter of any kind, gives \(+2.8\%\).

The price of the large \(\alpha\) is paid on the transverse shear itself. A field with \(w\) varying and all rotations zero has identically zero curvature, so it is resisted by transverse shear alone, and the stabilisation divides that resistance by \(1 + factor\). Measured on an 8 by 8 patch, this element’s stiffness in that subspace is 0.33, 0.019 and 0.0012 of the Quad4 and Quad4R value at \(\ell/h\) of 1.25, 6.25 and 25. A Donnell geometric stiffness matrix works on \(\partial w/\partial x\), which is exactly that subspace, so thin-shell buckling is where the scheme shows its cost, as measured for a cylinder in tests/test_quad4_linear_buckling_cylinder_displ.py. The failure there is not a uniformly wrong load but a change of critical mode: with \(factor\) at 1852 the element admits a mode at two elements per wavelength, the mesh Nyquist limit, carrying 36.6 per cent of its energy in transverse shear, and that mesh artefact undercuts the physical one. At the literature \(\alpha = 0.1\) the physical mode is critical again and this element agrees with the quadrilateral and with Tria3DSG to 3 per cent on it, while that same \(\alpha = 0.1\) makes the plate above 30.6 per cent too stiff. No single value serves both problem classes.

The proper fix is to give the element a locking-free transverse shear field, the DSG of Bletzinger, Bischoff and Ramm (2000) [8] being the triangular counterpart of the MITC4 treatment used for quadrilaterals, after which no \(\alpha\) is needed at all. That element now exists as pyfe3d.tria3dsg, and it is the triangle to reach for by default; this one is kept for continuity with results obtained before it, and for the comparison itself. Where it is used, alpha_shear_locking is a parameter to be verified per problem class and not a constant of the element.

The transverse shear stiffnesses \(A_{44}\), \(A_{45}\) and \(A_{55}\) are read from the pyfe3d.shellprop.ShellProp object with the shear correction already applied, see pyfe3d.shellprop.ShellProp.calc_transverse_shear_stiffness(), and brought to the element coordinate system with pyfe3d.shellprop.ShellProp.calc_Ats_element(), before the stabilisation above is applied. With shear_correction='rohwer' and the plies available, that method re-evaluates the equilibrium-based stiffness of Rohwer (1988) [9] with the plies rotated to the element coordinate system. As concluded by Lyly et al. (Sec. 5) and recalled by Castro et al., the shear correction is no longer a very relevant parameter when the stabilisation scheme presented above is used.

Drilling stiffness

The FSDT kinematics contains no strain measure associated with \(r_z\), so the rows and columns of the drilling degree-of-freedom would be empty and a mesh of coplanar elements would give a singular global stiffness matrix. Two models are available, selected with the drilling_model attribute, and the formulation of each is the one documented for pyfe3d.quad4, to which the reader is referred for the derivation.

Physics-based, the default (drilling_model = 0). The in-plane displacement field is enriched with the hierarchical quadratic edge modes of Allman [4], so that the drilling rotations produce membrane strain energy, and the independently interpolated \(r_z\) is tied to the rotation of the membrane field by the regularised functional of Hughes and Brezzi [5].

The amplitude of the mode of the edge \(k\) joining nodes \(i\) and \(j\) is \(a_k = \frac{\ell_k}{8}\left({r_z}_i - {r_z}_j\right)\), so the enrichment is the difference of the two drilling rotations already present at the ends of the edge and introduces no new degree-of-freedom. For the triangle the hierarchical bubble of that edge is

\[\hat{S}_k = 4 S_i S_j\]

with \(S_i\) the area coordinates, equal to unity at the mid-point of the edge and zero at every vertex. The enrichment populates the drilling columns of the membrane operator, giving \(\tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}_m\), and the drilling residual becomes \(\tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}_{r_z} = \boldsymbol{S}^{r_z} + \frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\tilde S}^u_{,y} - \frac{1}{2}\tilde{\boldsymbol{S}}^v_{,x}\), whose contribution to the element stiffness matrix is \(\gamma_{r_z} \int_A \tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}_{r_z}^\top \tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}_{r_z} dA\) with \(\gamma_{r_z} = A_{66}\), a modulus and not a user parameter. The curvature and transverse shear operators are untouched, the edge modes acting only on the in-plane translations, so the bending and the transverse shear response of the element are identical for the two drilling models.

Two quadrature choices matter. Because the derivatives of the bubbles are linear in the area coordinates, the drilling columns of \(\tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}_m\) vary linearly, and the terms quadratic in them are integrated with the three-point rule of degree of precision 2, first row of Table I in Cowper [10], the same one the element already used for its drilling terms. A single point at the centroid would leave those terms unsampled and would give the element five zero eigenvalues over its nine in-plane degrees-of-freedom instead of three. The Hughes-Brezzi term itself is integrated with a single point at the centroid, following Ibrahimbegovic et al. (1990) [7], which is what makes the element insensitive to \(\gamma_{r_z}\).

Unlike the penalty below, the added term is consistent rather than artificial: stationarity with respect to \(r_z\) gives \(r_z = \theta_z\) pointwise, so it contributes no energy at the exact solution for any positive \(\gamma_{r_z}\), and the nodal moments about the shell normal recovered in the internal force vector are physical. Note that the transverse shear stabilisation documented above scales the transverse shear stiffness only and does not interact with the drilling term, which draws its scale from \(A_{66}\) of the extensional stiffness matrix.

Fictitious penalty (drilling_model = 1), the default before version 0.10.0, following the approach adopted in MSC Nastran and Autodesk Nastran through their K6ROT parameter. It provides a small artificial stiffness whose only purpose is to remove the singularity, so the forces associated with it are spurious and any moment recovered about the shell normal is meaningless. The penalty energy is defined per element as:

\[U_{r_z} = \frac{1}{2} K6ROT \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot \int_A A_{66} (r_z - \theta_z)^2 dA\]

where \(10^{-6}\) is a scaling factor suggested by MSC Nastran’s approach (CQUAD4) to make the artificial drilling stiffness sufficiently small. AUTODESK NASTRAN’s quick reference guide recommends \(K6ROT = 100\) for static analysis. For modal solutions, \(K6ROT = 10^4\) is suggested. MSC NASTRAN’s quick reference guide states that \(K6ROT > 100\) should not be used, thus contradicting AUTODESK NASTRAN. The rotation \(r_z\) represents the drilling degree-of-freedom in element’s coordinates, whereas \(\theta_z\) the in-plane rotation strain, defined as \(\theta_z = \frac{1}{2}\left(v_{,x} - u_{,y}\right)\), such that the penalty is built from the operator

\[B_{r_z} = S^{r_z} + 1/2 S^u_{,y} - 1/2 S^v_{,x}\]

which is the same operator as \(\tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}_{r_z}\) above, evaluated on the unenriched field. Being built from that operator and not from an addition on the diagonal terms is what keeps the penalty from stiffening a rigid rotation of the element about its normal, so both models represent all rigid-body motions and all constant-strain states exactly. \(A_{66}\) is assumed constant over the element. This term is integrated with the three-point rule. The approach herein presented is very similar to the one presented in Eq. 2.20 of [6].

Choosing between them. The physics-based model is the default because it is the one that is correct when the drilling moment is part of the load path, when shells are connected to beams or stiffeners that must transmit in-plane moments, or when the mesh is too coarse for the unenriched membrane response to be trusted. It is markedly more accurate in in-plane bending: on Cook’s skew membrane with a four by four mesh of split quadrilaterals it gives 20.7 against the reference 23.9, where the penalty gives 11.3. The penalty remains available for reproducing results obtained before 0.10.0.

class pyfe3d.tria3r.Tria3R#

Three-node triangular shell element with reduced integration

Nodal connectivity for the triangular element similar to Nastran’s CTRIA3:

3
|\
| \    positive normal in CCW
|  \
|___\
1    2

The element coordinate system is determined identically what is explained in Nastran’s quick reference guide for the CTRIA3 element, as illustrated below.

The constructor stores the probe and sets the identification numbers and the positions c1, c2, c3 to -1, the init_k_* positions to 0, the rotation matrix r11, ..., r33 to zero and m11, m12, m21, m22 to the identity. Before computing any element matrix, the caller sets c1, c2, c3 and the init_k_* positions, and calls update_rotation_matrix() and update_probe_xe().

Parameters:
pTria3RProbe

Probe used by the element, stored in probe. One probe can be shared by many elements.

Attributes:
eid,int

Element identification number.

pid,int

Property identification number.

area,double

Element area. Dimension of length squared. Updated by update_area().

alpha_shear_locking,double

Factor used to prevent shear locking, affecting the transverse shear stiffness terms A44, A45, A55, already in the element coordinate system and with the shear correction applied (see ShellProp.calc_Ats_element()), which are reduced by a factor that grows with the square of the ratio of the longest edge to the thickness. 0. disables the reduction. The adopted default is alpha_shear_locking = 0.7, chosen such that the linear buckling load of a simply supported plate approaches the one of the Quad4R element for an equivalent mesh (see the test case test_tria3r_linear_buckling_plate.py). No single value serves every problem class, so this is a parameter to be verified per problem and not a constant of the element; Tria3DSG takes no such parameter. Dimensionless.

drilling_model,int

Selects how the drilling degree-of-freedom \(r_z\) is given stiffness. The default 0 is the physics-based stiffness, for which the recovered nodal moments about the shell normal are physical, and no user parameter is involved. Any other value selects the fictitious penalty of MSC Nastran and Autodesk Nastran, which was the default up to version 0.9.0 and is controlled by K6ROT. Setting elem.drilling_model = 1 before calling update_KC0() is the way to reproduce results obtained before 0.10.0.

K6ROT,double

Dimensionless multiplier for the fictitious drilling stiffness, only read when drilling_model is not 0. It has no effect under the default physics-based model, which takes its coefficient from the laminate stiffness instead, see gamma_rz. Default 100..

gamma_rz,double

Coefficient \(\gamma_{r_z}\) of the physics-based drilling stiffness, only read when drilling_model is 0. The default is a negative value, which means that A66 of the laminate extensional stiffness matrix is used. The attribute is exposed for sensitivity studies rather than for normal use. Dimension of force per unit length, as A66.

Entries \(T_{ij}\) of the transformation matrix \(\boldsymbol{T}_{3\times 3}\) from element to global coordinates, whose columns are the element axes.

Rotation matrix only for the constitutive relations. Used when a material direction is used instead of the element local coordinates.

c1, c2, c3int

Position of each node in the global stiffness matrix. The coordinates of a node are read at the positions c1//2, c2//2 and c3//2 of the coordinate array.

n1, n2, n3int

Node identification number.

init_k_KC0, init_k_KCNL, init_k_KG, init_k_Mint

Position in the arrays storing the sparse data for the structural matrices.

Pointer to the probe.

Notes

The factor used to prevent shear locking, alpha_shear_locking, is adopted from the stabilised assumed-strain and discrete shear gap (DSG) elements: it is the factor of Lyly, Stenberg and Vihinen, called Stenberg’s method by Bischoff and Bletzinger [1] (Sec. 3.1), in the form of Castro et al. [2] (Eq. (26)), see the module documentation, and scales the transverse shear stiffnesses as:

maxl = max(edge_12, edge_23, edge_31)
factor = alpha_shear_locking*maxl**2/thickness**2
A44 = 1 / (1 + factor) * A44
A45 = 1 / (1 + factor) * A45
A55 = 1 / (1 + factor) * A55

Warning

\(\alpha = 0.7\) is about seven times the value used in the references this scheme comes from, and it is the dominant error term on problems where transverse shear carries load. The reason, and the measurements, are in the Notes of the module documentation.

No single value serves every problem class, so this is a parameter to be verified per problem and not a constant of the element. Two measurements bracket it, and they pull in opposite directions:

  • on the plate of tests/test_tria3r_natural_freq.py with the consistent mass matrix, \(\alpha = 0.7\) gives a first natural frequency 0.6 per cent above the analytical value while the literature \(\alpha = 0.1\) gives one 30.6 per cent above it, so here the default is much the better of the two. With the stabilisation off the error is 96.1 per cent, which is how much of this element’s accuracy rests on \(\alpha\);

  • on the cylinder of tests/test_quad4_linear_buckling_cylinder_displ.py at ntheta = 60, where \(factor\) reaches 1852, the ordering reverses. At \(\alpha = 0.7\) the critical eigenvalue belongs to a mode at two elements per wavelength, the mesh Nyquist limit, carrying 36.6 per cent of its energy in transverse shear: a numerical mechanism rather than a physical mode. At \(\alpha = 0.1\) the physical eight-wave mode is critical instead, and there this element agrees with the quadrilateral and with the discrete shear gap triangle to 3 per cent, 2.520 against 2.453 and 2.534 times the reference load, all three overpredicting at so coarse a mesh.

So on a thin shell in buckling the default is not merely inaccurate, it can change which mode is critical, and a plausible-looking eigenvalue can belong to a mesh artefact. Where the answer matters, either sweep \(\alpha\) and confirm that the critical mode is physical and resolved by several elements per wavelength, or use Tria3DSG, whose discrete shear gap transverse shear field is locking-free and takes no such parameter.

Under the default drilling_model = 0, the physics-based stiffness is the one of Allman (1984) [3] and Hughes and Brezzi (1989) [4], for which the drilling rotation is a kinematic variable that carries strain energy, see the module documentation.

For K6ROT, AUTODESK NASTRAN’s quick reference guide recommends K6ROT = 100. for static analysis. For modal solutions, K6ROT=1.e4 is suggested. MSC NASTRAN’s quick reference guide states that K6ROT > 100. should not be used, but this is contradicting AUTODESK NASTRAN.

The default of gamma_rz, \(\gamma_{r_z} = A_{66}\), is the value identified by Hughes and Brezzi (1989) [4]. This is a modulus and not a parameter that needs tuning: the element response has a broad plateau of insensitivity around it, and the attribute is exposed for the sensitivity study that the literature recommends rather than for normal use. Very large values over-constrain \(r_z = \theta_z\), and a zero value leaves the Allman enrichment rank-deficient by one.

Update sparse vectors for linear constitutive stiffness matrix KC0

The element matrix is computed from the coordinates probe.xe, the area and the constitutive matrices of prop rotated with m11, m12, m21, m22, the transverse shear stiffnesses being scaled according to alpha_shear_locking. Notes

The membrane, bending, extension-bending coupling and transverse shear terms are integrated with a single point at the centroid, with the weight 0.5 times detJ = 2*area, the transverse shear stiffnesses \(A_{44}\), \(A_{45}\), \(A_{55}\) divided by \(1 + \alpha \ell^2/h^2\), with \(\alpha\) given by alpha_shear_locking, \(\ell\) the longest edge and \(h\) the total thickness prop.h, see the module documentation. The fictitious drilling penalty,

\[K6ROT \cdot 10^{-6} A_{66} \int_A B_{r_z}^\top B_{r_z} dA, \qquad B_{r_z} = S^{r_z} + 1/2 S^u_{,y} - 1/2 S^v_{,x}\]

is integrated with the three-point rule of degree of precision 2, first row of Table I in Cowper [1], with the area coordinates \((2/3, 1/6, 1/6)\) and their permutations, each with the weight area/3. With drilling_model = 0 this penalty is switched off, K6ROT being replaced by zero, and the physics-based drilling stiffness of allman_delta_Ke() is added instead, rotated to global coordinates with \(K_{g,mn} = T_{mi} K_{e,ij} T_{nj}\). It is added separately because the enrichment couples the drilling rotation to the curvatures whenever the extension-bending matrix \(B\) is not zero.

update_KCNL(self, long[: :1] KCNLr, long[: :1] KCNLc, double[: :1] KCNLv, ShellProp prop, int update_KCNLv_only=0) → void#

Update sparse vectors for the nonlinear stiffness matrix KCNL

The nonlinear stiffness matrix KCNL is the part of the tangent stiffness matrix, besides update_KC0() and update_KG(), that depends on the displacements through the von Karman membrane strains, evaluated at the displacements ue of the probe. The sum of the three is the tangent stiffness matrix of the internal forces of update_fint() with nonlinear=1. With drilling_model = 0 the membrane rows include the Allman enrichment, see _update_probe_BL_G().

Before this function is called, the probe Tria3RProbe attribute of the Tria3R object must be updated using update_probe_ue() with the current displacements; and update_probe_xe() with the node coordinates.

The element matrix is computed in element coordinates into the probe attribute KCNLve, rotated to global coordinates, and its values are added to KCNLv at the positions init_k_KCNL to init_k_KCNL + KCNL_SPARSE_SIZE - 1. With update_KCNLv_only=0 the global row and column indices are written to KCNLr and KCNLc at the same positions.

Parameters:
KCNLrnp.array

Array to store row positions of sparse values 1-D array of integers (INT) of size at least init_k_KCNL + KCNL_SPARSE_SIZE.

KCNLcnp.array

Array to store column positions of sparse values Same type and size as KCNLr.

KCNLvnp.array

Array to store sparse values 1-D array of DOUBLE, same size as KCNLr. The values are added to the existing ones, with the dimensions given for KC0v in update_KC0().

propShellProp object

Shell property object from where the stiffness and mass attributes are read from.

update_KCNLv_onlyint, optional

The default 0 means that the row and column indices KCNLr and KCNLc should also be updated. Any other value will only update the stiffness matrix values KCNLv.

Notes

Assuming that KCNL = KC0L + KCL0 + KCLL + KGNL, that is \(\boldsymbol{K}_{CNL} + \boldsymbol{K}_{GNL}\), the book’s nonlinear constitutive stiffness matrix \(\boldsymbol{K}_{CNL}\) of the terms of \(\boldsymbol{B}_{NL}\) plus the geometric \(\boldsymbol{K}_{GNL}\), built from the von Karman membrane strains

\[\epsilon_{xx} = u_{,x} + \frac{1}{2} w_{,x}^2, \quad \epsilon_{yy} = v_{,y} + \frac{1}{2} w_{,y}^2, \quad \gamma_{xy} = u_{,y} + v_{,x} + w_{,x} w_{,y}\]

whose nonlinear part is \(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{NL} = \frac{1}{2} \boldsymbol{B}_{NL} \boldsymbol{u}_e\), with \(\boldsymbol{B}_{NL}\) its variation. With \(\boldsymbol{B}_m\) and \(\boldsymbol{B}_b\) the linear membrane and bending strain-displacement matrices, \(\boldsymbol{G}_x = \partial \boldsymbol{S}^w / \partial x\) and \(\boldsymbol{G}_y = \partial \boldsymbol{S}^w / \partial y\) the gradients of \(w\), and \(\boldsymbol{A}\), \(\boldsymbol{B}\) the laminate matrices:

  • KC0L = \(\boldsymbol{B}_m^\top \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{B}_{NL} + \boldsymbol{B}_b^\top \boldsymbol{B} \boldsymbol{B}_{NL}\)

  • KCL0 = KC0L`^top`

  • KCLL = \(\boldsymbol{B}_{NL}^\top \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{B}_{NL}\)

  • KGNL = \(N_{NL,xx} \boldsymbol{G}_x^\top \boldsymbol{G}_x + N_{NL,yy} \boldsymbol{G}_y^\top \boldsymbol{G}_y + N_{NL,xy} (\boldsymbol{G}_x^\top \boldsymbol{G}_y + \boldsymbol{G}_y^\top \boldsymbol{G}_x)\), with \(\boldsymbol{N}_{NL} = \{N_{NL,xx}, N_{NL,yy}, N_{NL,xy}\}^\top = \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{NL}\)

The first three groups are the constitutive terms coupling the linear and the nonlinear parts of the membrane strain. KGNL is geometric, carrying the stress of the nonlinear membrane strain. It is collected here so that update_KG() stays homogeneous of degree one in the displacements, which is what a linear buckling analysis needs. With it here,

\[\boldsymbol{K}_T = \boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + \boldsymbol{K}_{CNL}(\boldsymbol{u}) + \boldsymbol{K}_{GNL}(\boldsymbol{u}) + \boldsymbol{K}_G(\boldsymbol{u})\]

is the exact Jacobian of the internal forces of update_fint() with nonlinear=1, and a Newton-Raphson iteration built on them converges quadratically. The quadrature of update_KG() is used.

update_M(self, long[: :1] Mr, long[: :1] Mc, double[: :1] Mv, ShellProp prop, int mtype=0) → void#

Update sparse vectors for mass matrix M

Different integration schemes are available by means of the mtype parameter.

The element matrix is computed from intrho, intrhoz and intrhoz2 of prop, the coordinates probe.xe and the area, rotated to global coordinates, and its values are added to Mv at the positions starting at init_k_M; the global row and column indices are always written to Mr and Mc at the same positions. mtype=0 and mtype=2 write M_SPARSE_SIZE entries and mtype=1 writes the first 90 of them. Before this method is called, update_rotation_matrix() and update_probe_xe() must have been used.

Parameters:
Mrnp.array

Array to store row positions of sparse values 1-D array of integers (INT) of size at least init_k_M + M_SPARSE_SIZE.

Mcnp.array

Array to store column positions of sparse values Same type and size as Mr.

Mvnp.array

Array to store sparse values 1-D array of DOUBLE, same size as Mr. The values are added to the existing ones. Dimension of mass between translations, of mass times length between a translation and a rotation, and of mass times length squared between rotations.

propShellProp object

Shell property object, from which intrho, intrhoz and intrhoz2 are read.

mtypeint, optional

0 for consistent mass matrix 1 for lumped mass matrix 2 for reduced integration mass matrix Default 0.

Raises:
ValueError

If mtype is not 0, 1 or 2.

Notes

The element matrices follow from the kinetic energy of Brockman [1], Eq. (20), with the displacements \(u + z r_y\), \(v - z r_x\) and \(w\) through the thickness,

\[T = \frac{1}{2} \int_A \left[ I_\rho (\dot{u}^2 + \dot{v}^2 + \dot{w}^2) + 2 I_{\rho z} (\dot{u} \dot{r}_y - \dot{v} \dot{r}_x) + I_{\rho z^2} (\dot{r}_x^2 + \dot{r}_y^2) \right] dA\]

with \(I_\rho\), \(I_{\rho z}\) and \(I_{\rho z^2}\) given by intrho, intrhoz and intrhoz2, which include the offset. With \(h_{ij}\) the integral of the product of the shape functions \(S_i S_j\) over the element, the element matrix has, as in Eq. (21) of [1], \(I_\rho h_{ij}\) between the translations of nodes \(i\) and \(j\), \(I_{\rho z^2} h_{ij}\) between the rotations \(r_x\) and between the rotations \(r_y\), and \(I_{\rho z} h_{ij}\) coupling the translations to the rotations. The drilling rotation \(r_z\) has no inertia. The schemes differ in how \(h_{ij}\) is integrated:

  • mtype=0: the three-point rule of degree of precision 2, first row of Table I in Cowper [2], with the area coordinates \((2/3, 1/6, 1/6)\) and their permutations, each with the weight \(A_e/3\), which is exact for the quadratic \(S_i S_j\), \(h_{ii} = A_e/6\) and \(h_{ij} = A_e/12\)

  • mtype=1: the vertex rule, the three nodes, each with the weight \(A_e/3\), \(h_{ii} = A_e/3\) and \(h_{ij} = 0\)

  • mtype=2: a single point at the centroid, \(h_{ij} = A_e/9\)

with \(A_e\) the element area. In element coordinates the coupling terms are \(+I_{\rho z} h_{ij}\) between \(u\) and \(r_y\) and \(-I_{\rho z} h_{ij}\) between \(v\) and \(r_x\), consistently with the curvatures of _update_probe_BL_G().

Lumped mass matrix

The vertex rule is a nodal quadrature. Applied to the whole integrand of \(T\), in which \(S_i\) is 1 at node \(i\) and 0 at the other nodes, it gives no coupling between different nodes and, for node \(i\), the block

\[\begin{split}\frac{A_e}{3} \begin{bmatrix} I_\rho & 0 & 0 & 0 & I_{\rho z} & 0 \\ 0 & I_\rho & 0 & -I_{\rho z} & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & I_\rho & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & -I_{\rho z} & 0 & I_{\rho z^2} & 0 & 0 \\ I_{\rho z} & 0 & 0 & 0 & I_{\rho z^2} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}\end{split}\]

for the DOF \(u, v, w, r_x, r_y, r_z\). The coupling through \(I_{\rho z}\) stays in the nodal block: the matrix is block-diagonal per node, and diagonal only when \(I_{\rho z} = 0\), as Brockman [1] notes for the Lobatto integration at the nodes of the quadrilateral. Since the weights sum to \(A_e\) and \(\sum_i (A_e/3) \boldsymbol{x}_i = \int_A \boldsymbol{x} dA\), with \(\boldsymbol{x}_i\) the position of node \(i\), the lumped matrix has the exact mass and the exact first moment of the mass, including the term \(I_{\rho z} A_e\) that places the centre of mass at the offset from the reference surface. Of the rotary inertia, the term \(I_{\rho z^2} A_e\) about the axes in the plane of the element is exact, while the part from the distribution of \(I_\rho\) over the area, about any axis, is concentrated at the nodes: 4 times the exact value about the centroid of the element, an error that decreases with the size of the elements.

References

[1] (1,2,3)

Brockman, R. A., Dynamics of the Bilinear Mindlin Plate Element, International Journal for Numerical Methods in Engineering, 24(12), 2343-2356, 1987. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1002/nme.1620241208

[2]

Cowper, G. R., Gaussian quadrature formulas for triangles, International Journal for Numerical Methods in Engineering, 7(3), 405-408, 1973. https://doi.org/10.1002/nme.1620070316

Array of size NUM_NODES*DOF=18 containing the element internal forces corresponding to the degrees-of-freedom described by ue. Forces, and moments (force times length) for the rotations. Updated by Tria3R.update_probe_finte().

BLexx, BLeyy, BLgxy, BLkxx, BLkyy, BLkxyarray-like

Arrays of size NUM_NODES*DOF=18 with the rows of the linear strain-displacement matrix for the membrane strains and curvatures, at the last evaluated integration point. Their products with ue give the membrane strains, dimensionless, and the curvatures, with dimension of one over length.

Sw_x, Sw_yarray-like

Arrays of size NUM_NODES*DOF=18 with the rows giving \(w_{,x}\) and \(w_{,y}\), at the last evaluated integration point. Sw_x holds \(\boldsymbol{G}_x = \partial \boldsymbol{S}^w/\partial x\) and Sw_y holds \(\boldsymbol{G}_y = \partial \boldsymbol{S}^w/\partial y\), the underscore denoting the partial derivative. Their products with ue are dimensionless.
