Tria3R - Triangular element with reduced integration (pyfe3d.tria3r)#

The module has three classes: Tria3RData, with the sizes of the sparse arrays that one element fills; Tria3RProbe, with the buffers in element coordinates that one or more elements share; and Tria3R, the element. The update_* methods of Tria3R write the element contributions in sparse row, column and value arrays, from the positions given by its init_k_* attributes, or add them to a global internal force vector.

Three-node triangular shell element with 6 degrees-of-freedom per node, \(u\), \(v\), \(w\), \(r_x\), \(r_y\), \(r_z\), first-order shear deformation theory.

The transverse shear strains are, by default, those of the discrete shear gap (DSG) field of pyfe3d.tria3dsg.Tria3DSG, selected by the shear_model attribute of Tria3R, 1 by default, with the alpha_shear_locking attribute 0. by default. The membrane field is enriched with the drilling rotations of Allman, which is what distinguishes this element from pyfe3d.tria3dsg.Tria3DSG. With shear_model = 0 the transverse shear is sampled at the centroid and its stiffness divided by \(1 + \alpha \ell^2/h^2\), e.g. with alpha_shear_locking = 0.7; that field does not converge on thin plates and shells and is not recommended, see the Notes.

Tria3R is the recommended triangle of pyfe3d. With the same transverse shear field, it gives the results of pyfe3d.tria3dsg.Tria3DSG on flat plates in bending, buckling and vibration, is much better in in-plane bending, better on shells whose response mixes membrane and bending, and cheaper to assemble. The exception is a coarse mesh of a doubly curved shell, where its drilling enrichment makes it stiffer than pyfe3d.tria3dsg.Tria3DSG, which is then the better choice; the measurements are in the Notes.

The drilling rotation \(r_z\) is given stiffness by one of two models, selected with the drilling_model attribute of Tria3R: the physics-based default, for which the nodal moments about the shell normal recovered in the internal force vector are physical and no user parameter is involved, and a fictitious penalty scaled by K6ROT, whose only purpose is to remove the singularity of the drilling degree-of-freedom. Geometrically nonlinear analyses with the von Kármán membrane strains are supported by Tria3R.update_fint() with nonlinear=1, Tria3R.update_KCNL() and Tria3R.update_KG().

Notes#

The membrane, bending and extension-bending coupling terms, constant over the linear triangle, are integrated with a single point at the centroid (\(S1=S2=S3=1/3\)), with the weight \(1/2\) times the Jacobian determinant \(2 A_e\), i.e. the area \(A_e\). The drilling stiffness is evaluated with full integration. Under the default drilling_model = 0 this applies to the terms of the Allman enrichment, the Hughes-Brezzi term being integrated with a single point at the centroid, see the drilling stiffness below.

Transverse shear, the default shear_model = 1

The transverse shear strains are the gradient of the interpolated shear gap of Bletzinger, Bischoff and Ramm [8], averaged over the three choices of the starting node, the operator of pyfe3d.tria3dsg.Tria3DSG, whose Notes give its derivation and properties: the rigid-body modes, a state of constant transverse shear and every state of constant curvature are reproduced exactly, the last being the locking-free property. The operator is added to the element matrix through the same 18 by 18 block as the Allman term, see dsg_shear_Ke(). The bending, transverse shear, geometric stiffness and mass matrices of the two triangles are then identical, and on a flat plate in bending, buckling or vibration, where the membrane is decoupled from the bending, the two give the same results to round-off, measured at \(2 \times 10^{-10}\). alpha_shear_locking defaults to 0.; a non-zero value applies the factor below on top of the DSG field, exactly as Tria3DSG does, whose Notes give the measurements.

Compared with Tria3DSG, the Allman membrane makes this element much better in in-plane bending, better on shells whose response mixes membrane and bending, and no worse on plates; on the coarse meshes of a doubly curved shell in inextensional bending it is stiffer. Measured with quadrilateral meshes split along the diagonal 1-3, normalised by the reference:

  • Cook’s skew membrane, reference 23.956 of Felippa [11] (Sec. 9.3), \(n \times n\) cells: 0.864, 0.948, 0.982 and 0.994 for \(n\) = 4, 8, 16 and 32, against 0.457, 0.713, 0.896 and 0.970 for Tria3DSG;

  • the Scordelis-Lo roof of Belytschko et al. [12] (Table 3), reference 0.3024, \(N \times N\) on the quarter: 0.975, 0.989, 0.994 and 0.996 for \(N\) = 8, 16, 32 and 64, against 0.853, 0.952, 0.984 and 0.994;

  • the pinched cylinder of [12]: 0.564, 0.852, 0.961 and 0.996, against 0.545, 0.843, 0.958 and 0.996;

  • the twisted beam of MacNeal and Harder [13] (Table 3), out-of-plane load: 0.933, 0.988 and 0.997 with 12x2, 48x4 and 192x8 cells, against 0.817, 0.954 and 0.990;

  • the pinched hemisphere with an 18 degree hole of [12], reference 0.0924: 0.288, 0.862, 0.998 and 1.011 for \(N\) = 8, 16, 32 and 64, against 0.451, 0.926, 0.998 and 1.009. On these coarse meshes of a doubly curved surface the drilling rotation of one facet is made of the rotations that bend its neighbours, and the edge modes of the Allman field turn them into membrane strain, which stiffens an inextensional deformation. The penalty drilling model, drilling_model = 1, which has no Allman field, gives 0.929 and 0.988 at \(N\) = 8 and 16, whereas reducing gamma_rz hundredfold leaves this element at 0.309 at \(N\) = 8: the stiffening is in the enrichment, not in the Hughes-Brezzi coefficient. It is gone at \(N = 32\).

The assembly of Tria3R.update_KC0() takes about half the time of the one of Tria3DSG, 0.64 against 1.36 s for 80000 elements on one thread.

These measurements are why Tria3R is the recommended triangle, and why Tria3DSG is the better choice on a coarse mesh of a doubly curved shell.

Transverse shear with shear_model = 0

The transverse shear strains are sampled at the centroid, and the transverse shear stiffnesses \(A_{44}\), \(A_{45}\) and \(A_{55}\) are divided by \(1 + factor\), with

\[factor = \frac{\alpha \ell^2}{h^2}\]

where \(\alpha\) is the alpha_shear_locking attribute, \(\ell\) the longest edge of the triangle and \(h\) the total thickness. It is the scalar factor that Lyly, Stenberg and Vihinen [1] proposed for the MITC4 quadrilateral, their Eq. (3.12), called Stenberg’s method by Bischoff and Bletzinger [2] (Sec. 3.1), in the form of Castro et al. [3] (Eq. (26)), with the longest edge of the triangle as the element size. The values of those references are near \(0.1\), roughly seven times smaller than \(\alpha = 0.7\) of the measurements below: in all of them the factor sits on top of an assumed-strain or DSG field, which already removes the locking, whereas the centroid sample locks, and here the factor has to do the unlocking.

Warning

With shear_model = 0 the element does not converge on thin plates and shells. The factor tends to zero only when \(\ell\) falls well below \(h\), \(\ell < 0.12 h\) for 1 per cent; before that, the centroid-sampled field it scales still locks, and the error stalls at a value set by \(\alpha\) and by the mesh pattern. Lyly, Stenberg and Vihinen [1] (p. 348) state that the stabilisation was first introduced for a piecewise quadratic deflection, and that a linear or bilinear deflection needs an MITC reduction operator with it; the centroid sample is neither. Measured on a simply supported square plate under a uniform pressure, with \(n \times n\) squares cut along one diagonal, against the closed form of Timoshenko and Woinowsky-Krieger [14] (Table 8, p. 120), the error of the centre deflection, in per cent, is:

The buckling load of that plate under a uniform compression, \(a/h = 1000\), stalls at +16.4 per cent and its first frequency at +8.0 per cent with \(\alpha = 0.7\), where the default converges to +0.02 per cent. On shells, with \(\alpha = 0.7\), the hemisphere above stalls at 0.826 from \(N = 32\) to 64 and the Scordelis-Lo roof is at 0.894 with \(N = 64\). A larger \(\alpha\) moves the stall at the price of a much too flexible coarse mesh, and on a thin cylinder in buckling the large factor admits a mode at two elements per wavelength, the mesh Nyquist limit, that undercuts the physical one, see tests/test_quad4_linear_buckling_cylinder_displ.py; no single value serves every problem or every mesh. On the coarse meshes of very thin plates, where the DSG field of the default is itself stiff, e.g. the clamped plates of MacNeal and Harder [13], this field gives a closer answer, but it does not converge to it.

The transverse shear stiffnesses \(A_{44}\), \(A_{45}\) and \(A_{55}\) are read from the pyfe3d.shellprop.ShellProp object with the shear correction already applied, see pyfe3d.shellprop.ShellProp.calc_transverse_shear_stiffness(), and brought to the element coordinate system with pyfe3d.shellprop.ShellProp.calc_Ats_element(), before any factor of alpha_shear_locking is applied. With shear_correction='rohwer' and the plies available, that method re-evaluates the equilibrium-based stiffness of Rohwer (1988) [9] with the plies rotated to the element coordinate system.

Drilling stiffness

The FSDT kinematics contains no strain measure associated with \(r_z\), so the rows and columns of the drilling degree-of-freedom would be empty and a mesh of coplanar elements would give a singular global stiffness matrix. Two models are available, selected with the drilling_model attribute, and the formulation of each is the one documented for pyfe3d.quad4, to which the reader is referred for the derivation.

Physics-based, the default (drilling_model = 0). The in-plane displacement field is enriched with the hierarchical quadratic edge modes of Allman [4], so that the drilling rotations produce membrane strain energy, and the independently interpolated \(r_z\) is tied to the rotation of the membrane field by the regularised functional of Hughes and Brezzi [5].

The amplitude of the mode of the edge \(k\) joining nodes \(i\) and \(j\) is \(a_k = \frac{\ell_k}{8}\left({r_z}_i - {r_z}_j\right)\), so the enrichment is the difference of the two drilling rotations already present at the ends of the edge and introduces no new degree-of-freedom. For the triangle the hierarchical bubble of that edge is

\[\hat{S}_k = 4 S_i S_j\]

with \(S_i\) the area coordinates, equal to unity at the mid-point of the edge and zero at every vertex. The enrichment populates the drilling columns of the membrane operator, giving \(\tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}_m\), and the drilling residual becomes \(\tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}_{r_z} = \boldsymbol{S}^{r_z} + \frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\tilde S}^u_{,y} - \frac{1}{2}\tilde{\boldsymbol{S}}^v_{,x}\), whose contribution to the element stiffness matrix is \(\gamma_{r_z} \int_A \tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}_{r_z}^\top \tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}_{r_z} dA\) with \(\gamma_{r_z} = A_{66}\), a stiffness per unit length taken from the laminate, and not a user parameter. The curvature and transverse shear operators are untouched, the edge modes acting only on the in-plane translations, so the bending and the transverse shear response of the element are identical for the two drilling models.

Two quadrature choices matter. Because the derivatives of the bubbles are linear in the area coordinates, the drilling columns of \(\tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}_m\) vary linearly, and the terms quadratic in them are integrated with the three-point rule of degree of precision 2, first row of Table I in Cowper [10], the same one the element already used for its drilling terms. A single point at the centroid would leave those terms unsampled and would give the element five zero eigenvalues over its nine in-plane degrees-of-freedom instead of three. The Hughes-Brezzi term itself is integrated with a single point at the centroid, following Ibrahimbegovic et al. (1990) [7], which is what makes the element insensitive to \(\gamma_{r_z}\).

Unlike the penalty below, the added term is consistent rather than artificial: stationarity with respect to \(r_z\) gives \(r_z = \theta_z\) pointwise, so it contributes no energy at the exact solution for any positive \(\gamma_{r_z}\), and the nodal moments about the shell normal recovered in the internal force vector are physical. Note that the transverse shear stabilisation documented above scales the transverse shear stiffness only and does not interact with the drilling term, which draws its scale from \(A_{66}\) of the extensional stiffness matrix.

Fictitious penalty (drilling_model = 1), following the approach adopted in MSC Nastran and Autodesk Nastran through their K6ROT parameter. It provides a small artificial stiffness whose only purpose is to remove the singularity, so the forces associated with it are spurious and any moment recovered about the shell normal is meaningless. The penalty energy is defined per element as:

\[U_{r_z} = \frac{1}{2} K6ROT \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot \int_A A_{66} (r_z - \theta_z)^2 dA\]

where \(10^{-6}\) is a scaling factor suggested by MSC Nastran’s approach (CQUAD4) to make the artificial drilling stiffness sufficiently small. AUTODESK NASTRAN’s quick reference guide recommends \(K6ROT = 100\) for static analysis. For modal solutions, \(K6ROT = 10^4\) is suggested. MSC NASTRAN’s quick reference guide states that \(K6ROT > 100\) should not be used, thus contradicting AUTODESK NASTRAN. The rotation \(r_z\) represents the drilling degree-of-freedom in element’s coordinates, whereas \(\theta_z\) the in-plane rotation strain, defined as \(\theta_z = \frac{1}{2}\left(v_{,x} - u_{,y}\right)\), such that the penalty is built from the operator

\[B_{r_z} = S^{r_z} + 1/2 S^u_{,y} - 1/2 S^v_{,x}\]

which is the same operator as \(\tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}_{r_z}\) above, evaluated on the unenriched field. Being built from that operator and not from an addition on the diagonal terms is what keeps the penalty from stiffening a rigid rotation of the element about its normal, so both models represent all rigid-body motions and all constant-strain states exactly. \(A_{66}\) is assumed constant over the element. This term is integrated with the three-point rule. The approach herein presented is very similar to the one presented in Eq. 2.20 of [6].

Choosing between them. The physics-based model is the default because it is the one that is correct when the drilling moment is part of the load path, when shells are connected to beams or stiffeners that must transmit in-plane moments, or when the mesh is too coarse for the unenriched membrane response to be trusted. It is markedly more accurate in in-plane bending: on Cook’s skew membrane with a four by four mesh of split quadrilaterals it gives 20.7 against the reference 23.9, where the penalty gives 11.3.

Zero-energy modes of a single element

The stiffness matrix KC0 of a single free element has seven zero eigenvalues: the six rigid-body motions and one mode of the rotations,

\[u = v = w = r_z = 0, \qquad r_x = c \, (x - x_c), \qquad r_y = c \, (y - y_c)\]

in the element coordinates, \((x_c, y_c)\) being the centroid and \(c\) an arbitrary constant. Its curvatures vanish, \(\kappa_{xx} = {r_y}_{,x} = 0\), \(\kappa_{yy} = -{r_x}_{,y} = 0\) and \(\kappa_{xy} = -{r_x}_{,x} + {r_y}_{,y} = c - c = 0\), and so does its transverse shear strain, constant with either shear_model. The bending and the transverse shear have nine degrees-of-freedom, \(w\), \(r_x\) and \(r_y\) of the three nodes, and at most five independent strains, the three constant curvatures and the two constant transverse shear strains, hence at least four zero modes, three of which are the rigid-body motions \(w = \text{const.}\) and the rotations about the in-plane axes; the membrane with its drilling term has only the three in-plane rigid-body motions. The mode is the triangular counterpart of the spurious twisting mode of the one-point quadrilateral, Eq. (13) of Brockman [15], p. 2347, which exists for a single element but not in a mesh of two or more elements. Measured with both shear_model and both drilling_model, thicknesses of 0.001 to 0.1 times the size of the triangle, in the \(xy\) plane and rotated out of it: seven zero eigenvalues, below \(10^{-15}\) times the largest, with the eighth above \(10^{-8}\) times it; assembled, two triangles of a square and four triangles about an interior node have only the six rigid-body modes.

Notes

With alpha_shear_locking \(= \alpha \neq 0\), the factor is the one of Lyly, Stenberg and Vihinen, called Stenberg’s method by Bischoff and Bletzinger [1] (Sec. 3.1), in the form of Castro et al. [2] (Eq. (26)), see the module documentation, and scales the transverse shear stiffnesses as:

maxl = max(edge_12, edge_23, edge_31)
factor = alpha_shear_locking*maxl**2/thickness**2
A44 = 1 / (1 + factor) * A44
A45 = 1 / (1 + factor) * A45
A55 = 1 / (1 + factor) * A55

Warning

shear_model = 0, with alpha_shear_locking = 0.7, does not converge on thin plates and shells: on a simply supported square plate with \(a/h = 1000\) the error of the centre deflection stalls at -14 per cent, of the buckling load at +16 per cent and of the first frequency at +8 per cent under refinement, and on a thin cylinder in buckling the factor can make a mesh artefact the critical mode. The measurements and the reason, given by Lyly et al. (1993), p. 348, are in the Notes of the module documentation.

Under the default drilling_model = 0, the physics-based stiffness is the one of Allman (1984) [3] and Hughes and Brezzi (1989) [4], for which the drilling rotation is a kinematic variable that carries strain energy, see the module documentation.

For K6ROT, AUTODESK NASTRAN’s quick reference guide recommends K6ROT = 100. for static analysis. For modal solutions, K6ROT=1.e4 is suggested. MSC NASTRAN’s quick reference guide states that K6ROT > 100. should not be used, but this is contradicting AUTODESK NASTRAN.

The default of gamma_rz, \(\gamma_{r_z} = A_{66}\), is the value identified by Hughes and Brezzi (1989) [4]. This is a stiffness per unit length taken from the laminate, and not a parameter that needs tuning: the element response has a broad plateau of insensitivity around it, and the attribute is exposed for the sensitivity study that the literature recommends rather than for normal use. Very large values over-constrain \(r_z = \theta_z\), and a zero value leaves the Allman enrichment rank-deficient by one.

update_KC0(self, long[: :1] KC0r, long[: :1] KC0c, double[: :1] KC0v, ShellProp prop, int update_KC0v_only=0) → void#

Update sparse vectors for linear constitutive stiffness matrix KC0

The element matrix is computed from the coordinates probe.xe, the area and the constitutive matrices of prop rotated with m11, m12, m21, m22, the transverse shear field being selected by shear_model and its stiffnesses scaled according to alpha_shear_locking. Its values, in global coordinates, are added to KC0v at the positions init_k_KC0 to init_k_KC0 + KC0_SPARSE_SIZE - 1. With update_KC0v_only=0 the global row and column indices are written to KC0r and KC0c at the same positions. Before this method is called, update_rotation_matrix() and update_probe_xe() must have been used.

Parameters:
KC0rnp.array

Array to store row positions of sparse values 1-D array of integers (INT) of size at least init_k_KC0 + KC0_SPARSE_SIZE.

KC0cnp.array

Array to store column positions of sparse values Same type and size as KC0r.

KC0vnp.array

Array to store sparse values 1-D array of DOUBLE, same size as KC0r. The values are added to the existing ones. Dimension of force per unit length between translations, of force between a translation and a rotation, and of force times length between rotations.

propShellProp object

Shell property object from where the stiffness and mass attributes are read from.

update_KC0v_onlyint, optional

The default 0 means that the row and column indices KC0r and KC0c should also be updated. Any other value will only update the stiffness matrix values KC0v.

Notes

The membrane, bending and extension-bending coupling terms are integrated with a single point at the centroid, with the weight 0.5 times detJ = 2*area. The transverse shear stiffnesses \(A_{44}\), \(A_{45}\), \(A_{55}\) are divided by \(1 + \alpha \ell^2/h^2\), the factor of Lyly et al. [2], with \(\alpha\) given by alpha_shear_locking, 0. by default, \(\ell\) the longest edge and \(h\) the total thickness prop.h. With the default shear_model = 1 they enter the discrete shear gap field of dsg_shear_Ke(), added with the Allman block below; with shear_model = 0 the transverse shear strains are sampled at the centroid by the generated terms, see the module documentation. The fictitious drilling penalty,

\[K6ROT \cdot 10^{-6} A_{66} \int_A B_{r_z}^\top B_{r_z} dA, \qquad B_{r_z} = S^{r_z} + 1/2 S^u_{,y} - 1/2 S^v_{,x}\]

is integrated with the three-point rule of degree of precision 2, first row of Table I in Cowper [1], with the area coordinates \((2/3, 1/6, 1/6)\) and their permutations, each with the weight area/3. With drilling_model = 0 this penalty is switched off, K6ROT being replaced by zero, and the physics-based drilling stiffness of allman_delta_Ke() is added instead, rotated to global coordinates with \(K_{g,mn} = T_{mi} K_{e,ij} T_{nj}\), together with the transverse shear of dsg_shear_Ke() when shear_model = 1. It is added separately because the enrichment couples the drilling rotation to the curvatures whenever the extension-bending matrix \(B\) is not zero.

update_KCNL(self, long[: :1] KCNLr, long[: :1] KCNLc, double[: :1] KCNLv, ShellProp prop, int update_KCNLv_only=0) → void#

Update sparse vectors for the nonlinear stiffness matrix KCNL

The nonlinear stiffness matrix KCNL is the part of the tangent stiffness matrix, besides update_KC0() and update_KG(), that depends on the displacements through the von Kármán membrane strains, evaluated at the displacements ue of the probe. The sum of the three is the tangent stiffness matrix of the internal forces of update_fint() with nonlinear=1. With drilling_model = 0 the membrane rows include the Allman enrichment, see _update_probe_BL_G().

Before this function is called, the probe Tria3RProbe attribute of the Tria3R object must be updated using update_probe_ue() with the current displacements; and update_probe_xe() with the node coordinates.

The element matrix is computed in element coordinates into the probe attribute KCNLve, rotated to global coordinates, and its values are added to KCNLv at the positions init_k_KCNL to init_k_KCNL + KCNL_SPARSE_SIZE - 1. With update_KCNLv_only=0 the global row and column indices are written to KCNLr and KCNLc at the same positions.

Parameters:
KCNLrnp.array

Array to store row positions of sparse values 1-D array of integers (INT) of size at least init_k_KCNL + KCNL_SPARSE_SIZE.

KCNLcnp.array

Array to store column positions of sparse values Same type and size as KCNLr.

KCNLvnp.array

Array to store sparse values 1-D array of DOUBLE, same size as KCNLr. The values are added to the existing ones, with the dimensions given for KC0v in update_KC0().

propShellProp object

Shell property object from where the stiffness and mass attributes are read from.

update_KCNLv_onlyint, optional

The default 0 means that the row and column indices KCNLr and KCNLc should also be updated. Any other value will only update the stiffness matrix values KCNLv.

Notes

KCNL = KC0L + KCL0 + KCLL + KGNL, that is \(\boldsymbol{K}_{CNL} + \boldsymbol{K}_{GNL}\): the nonlinear constitutive stiffness matrix \(\boldsymbol{K}_{CNL}\), the terms of \(\int (\boldsymbol{B}_L + \boldsymbol{B}_{NL})^\top \boldsymbol{C} (\boldsymbol{B}_L + \boldsymbol{B}_{NL}) d\Omega\) that contain \(\boldsymbol{B}_{NL}\), plus the geometric stiffness matrix \(\boldsymbol{K}_{GNL}\) of the stress resultants of the nonlinear strains. Crisfield [1], Section 8.1.5, Eqs. (8.26)-(8.31), groups the same terms differently: his \(\boldsymbol{K}_{t1}\) is \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + \boldsymbol{K}_{CNL}\), and his initial-stress matrix \(\boldsymbol{K}_{t\sigma}\), built with the total membrane forces, is \(\boldsymbol{K}_G + \boldsymbol{K}_{GNL}\); the tangent stiffness matrix is the same. The matrices are built from the von Kármán membrane strains

\[\epsilon_{xx} = u_{,x} + \frac{1}{2} w_{,x}^2, \quad \epsilon_{yy} = v_{,y} + \frac{1}{2} w_{,y}^2, \quad \gamma_{xy} = u_{,y} + v_{,x} + w_{,x} w_{,y}\]

whose nonlinear part is \(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{NL} = \frac{1}{2} \boldsymbol{B}_{NL} \boldsymbol{u}_e\), with \(\boldsymbol{B}_{NL}\) its variation. With \(\boldsymbol{B}_m\) and \(\boldsymbol{B}_b\) the linear membrane and bending strain-displacement matrices, \(\boldsymbol{G}_x = \partial \boldsymbol{S}^w / \partial x\) and \(\boldsymbol{G}_y = \partial \boldsymbol{S}^w / \partial y\) the gradients of \(w\), and \(\boldsymbol{A}\), \(\boldsymbol{B}\) the laminate matrices:

  • KC0L = \(\boldsymbol{B}_m^\top \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{B}_{NL} + \boldsymbol{B}_b^\top \boldsymbol{B} \boldsymbol{B}_{NL}\)

  • KCL0 = KC0L`^top`

  • KCLL = \(\boldsymbol{B}_{NL}^\top \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{B}_{NL}\)

  • KGNL = \(N_{NL,xx} \boldsymbol{G}_x^\top \boldsymbol{G}_x + N_{NL,yy} \boldsymbol{G}_y^\top \boldsymbol{G}_y + N_{NL,xy} (\boldsymbol{G}_x^\top \boldsymbol{G}_y + \boldsymbol{G}_y^\top \boldsymbol{G}_x)\), with \(\boldsymbol{N}_{NL} = \{N_{NL,xx}, N_{NL,yy}, N_{NL,xy}\}^\top = \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{NL}\)

The first three groups are the constitutive terms coupling the linear and the nonlinear parts of the membrane strain. KGNL is geometric, carrying the stress of the nonlinear membrane strain. It is collected here so that update_KG() stays homogeneous of degree one in the displacements, which is what a linear buckling analysis needs. With it here,

\[\boldsymbol{K}_T = \boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + \boldsymbol{K}_{CNL}(\boldsymbol{u}) + \boldsymbol{K}_{GNL}(\boldsymbol{u}) + \boldsymbol{K}_G(\boldsymbol{u})\]

is the exact Jacobian of the internal forces of update_fint() with nonlinear=1, and a Newton-Raphson iteration built on them converges quadratically. The quadrature of update_KG() is used.

Update sparse vectors for mass matrix M

Different integration schemes are available by means of the mtype parameter.

The element matrix is computed from intrho, intrhoz and intrhoz2 of prop, the coordinates probe.xe and the area, rotated to global coordinates, and its values are added to Mv at the positions starting at init_k_M; the global row and column indices are always written to Mr and Mc at the same positions. mtype=0 and mtype=2 write M_SPARSE_SIZE entries and mtype=1 writes the first 90 of them. Before this method is called, update_rotation_matrix() and update_probe_xe() must have been used.

Parameters:
Mrnp.array

Array to store row positions of sparse values 1-D array of integers (INT) of size at least init_k_M + M_SPARSE_SIZE.

Mcnp.array

Array to store column positions of sparse values Same type and size as Mr.

Mvnp.array

Array to store sparse values 1-D array of DOUBLE, same size as Mr. The values are added to the existing ones. Dimension of mass between translations, of mass times length between a translation and a rotation, and of mass times length squared between rotations.

propShellProp object

Shell property object, from which intrho, intrhoz and intrhoz2 are read.

mtypeint, optional

0 for consistent mass matrix 1 for lumped mass matrix 2 for reduced integration mass matrix Default 0.

Raises:
ValueError

If mtype is not 0, 1 or 2.

Notes

The element matrices follow from the kinetic energy of Brockman [1], Eq. (20), with the displacements \(u + z r_y\), \(v - z r_x\) and \(w\) through the thickness,

\[T = \frac{1}{2} \int_A \left[ I_\rho (\dot{u}^2 + \dot{v}^2 + \dot{w}^2) + 2 I_{\rho z} (\dot{u} \dot{r}_y - \dot{v} \dot{r}_x) + I_{\rho z^2} (\dot{r}_x^2 + \dot{r}_y^2) \right] dA\]

with \(I_\rho\), \(I_{\rho z}\) and \(I_{\rho z^2}\) given by intrho, intrhoz and intrhoz2, which include the offset. With \(h_{ij}\) the integral of the product of the shape functions \(S_i S_j\) over the element, the element matrix has, as in Eq. (21) of [1], \(I_\rho h_{ij}\) between the translations of nodes \(i\) and \(j\), \(I_{\rho z^2} h_{ij}\) between the rotations \(r_x\) and between the rotations \(r_y\), and \(I_{\rho z} h_{ij}\) coupling the translations to the rotations. The drilling rotation \(r_z\) has no inertia. The schemes differ in how \(h_{ij}\) is integrated:

  • mtype=0: the three-point rule of degree of precision 2, first row of Table I in Cowper [2], with the area coordinates \((2/3, 1/6, 1/6)\) and their permutations, each with the weight \(A_e/3\), which is exact for the quadratic \(S_i S_j\), \(h_{ii} = A_e/6\) and \(h_{ij} = A_e/12\)

  • mtype=1: the vertex rule, the three nodes, each with the weight \(A_e/3\), \(h_{ii} = A_e/3\) and \(h_{ij} = 0\)

  • mtype=2: a single point at the centroid, \(h_{ij} = A_e/9\)

with \(A_e\) the element area. In element coordinates the coupling terms are \(+I_{\rho z} h_{ij}\) between \(u\) and \(r_y\) and \(-I_{\rho z} h_{ij}\) between \(v\) and \(r_x\), consistently with the curvatures of _update_probe_BL_G().

Lumped mass matrix

The vertex rule is a nodal quadrature. Applied to the whole integrand of \(T\), in which \(S_i\) is 1 at node \(i\) and 0 at the other nodes, it gives no coupling between different nodes and, for node \(i\), the block

\[\begin{split}\frac{A_e}{3} \begin{bmatrix} I_\rho & 0 & 0 & 0 & I_{\rho z} & 0 \\ 0 & I_\rho & 0 & -I_{\rho z} & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & I_\rho & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & -I_{\rho z} & 0 & I_{\rho z^2} & 0 & 0 \\ I_{\rho z} & 0 & 0 & 0 & I_{\rho z^2} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}\end{split}\]

for the DOF \(u, v, w, r_x, r_y, r_z\). The coupling through \(I_{\rho z}\) stays in the nodal block: the matrix is block-diagonal per node, and diagonal only when \(I_{\rho z} = 0\), as Brockman [1] notes for the Lobatto integration at the nodes of the quadrilateral. Since the weights sum to \(A_e\) and \(\sum_i (A_e/3) \boldsymbol{x}_i = \int_A \boldsymbol{x} dA\), with \(\boldsymbol{x}_i\) the position of node \(i\), the lumped matrix has the exact mass and the exact first moment of the mass, including the term \(I_{\rho z} A_e\) that places the centre of mass at the offset from the reference surface. Of the rotary inertia, the term \(I_{\rho z^2} A_e\) about the axes in the plane of the element is exact, while the part from the distribution of \(I_\rho\) over the area, about any axis, is concentrated at the nodes: 4 times the exact value about the centroid of the element, an error that decreases with the size of the elements.

update_rotation_matrix(self, double[: :1] x, double xmati=0., double xmatj=0., double xmatk=0.) → void#

Update the rotation matrix of the element

Attributes r11,r12,r13,r21,r22,r23,r31,r32,r33 are updated, with the entries \(T_{ij}\) of the transformation matrix \(\boldsymbol{T}_{3\times 3}\) from element to global coordinates, whose columns are the element axes.

The element coordinate system is determined, identifying the \(ijk\) components of each axis: \({x_e}_i, {x_e}_j, {x_e}_k\); \({y_e}_i, {y_e}_j, {y_e}_k\); \({z_e}_i, {z_e}_j, {z_e}_k\).

The element x axis runs from node 1 to node 2 and the element z axis is along the cross product of the vectors from node 1 to node 2 and from node 1 to node 3. When a material direction is given, m11, m12, m21, m22 are updated with the rotation from the material direction projected onto the element to the element x axis. When it is the zero vector, the default, or parallel to the element normal, m11, m12, m21, m22 are not changed.

Parameters:
xarray-like

Array with global nodal coordinates, for a total of \(M\) nodes in the model, this array will be arranged as: \(x_1, y_1, z_1, x_2, y_2, z_2, ..., x_M, y_M, z_M\). 1-D array of size 3*M, dimension of length. The coordinates of each node are read from the positions c1//2, c2//2 and c3//2.

xmati, xmatj, xmatkdouble, optional

Vector in global coordinates representing the material direction. This vector is projected onto the plate element, thus becoming the material direction. The \(ABD\) matrix defining the constitutive behavior of the element is rotated from the material direction to the element \(x\) axis while calculating the stiffness matrices. Only the direction of the vector is used. Default 0., the zero vector, which leaves m11, m12, m21, m22 unchanged.

Notes

With \(\boldsymbol{v}_{12} = \boldsymbol{x}_2 - \boldsymbol{x}_1\) and \(\boldsymbol{v}_{13} = \boldsymbol{x}_3 - \boldsymbol{x}_1\), the element axes are

\[\boldsymbol{e}_x = \frac{\boldsymbol{v}_{12}}{\|\boldsymbol{v}_{12}\|}, \qquad \boldsymbol{e}_z = \frac{\boldsymbol{v}_{12} \times \boldsymbol{v}_{13}}{\|\boldsymbol{v}_{12} \times \boldsymbol{v}_{13}\|}, \qquad \boldsymbol{e}_y = \boldsymbol{e}_z \times \boldsymbol{e}_x\]

and \(\boldsymbol{T}_{3\times 3} = [\boldsymbol{e}_x, \boldsymbol{e}_y, \boldsymbol{e}_z]\). The material direction \(\boldsymbol{x}_m\) is projected onto the element plane as \(\boldsymbol{x}_m' = (\boldsymbol{e}_z \times \boldsymbol{x}_m) \times \boldsymbol{e}_z\), normalized, and with \(\theta\) the angle from \(\boldsymbol{e}_x\) to \(\boldsymbol{x}_m'\), positive towards \(\boldsymbol{e}_y\), \(m_{11} = m_{22} = \cos\theta = \boldsymbol{x}_m' \cdot \boldsymbol{e}_x\), \(m_{21} = -m_{12} = \sin\theta\), the sign of \(\sin\theta\) being that of \(\boldsymbol{x}_m \cdot \boldsymbol{e}_y\). The material direction is ignored when its norm, or the norm of \(\boldsymbol{e}_z \times \boldsymbol{x}_m\) after its normalization, is not larger than \(10^{-10} \|\boldsymbol{v}_{12} \times \boldsymbol{v}_{13}\|\).

class pyfe3d.tria3r.Tria3RProbe#

Probe used for local coordinates, local displacements, local stresses etc

The idea behind using a probe is to avoid allocating larger memory buffers per finite element. The memory buffers are allocated per probe, and one probe can be shared amongst many finite elements, with the information being updated and retrieved on demand.

Note

The probe can be shared amongst more than one finite element, depending on how you defined them. Mind that the probe will always save the values from the last update.

The constructor takes no parameters and allocates every array filled with zeros.
